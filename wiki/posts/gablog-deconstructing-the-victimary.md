## Summary

This post analyzes the political paralysis surrounding victimary claims—examining why both left and right politicians surrender to sacred imperatives even when they contradict electoral logic or truth. The author argues that victimary behavior functions as ritual re-enactment rather than rational belief, grounded in the [[originary event]] of the Holocaust. Rather than explain victimary logic through declarative propositions, we must understand it as performative: a promissory gesture that reconciles competing domains of consciousness without requiring coherent belief.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Victimary]]**: A political and social order organized around the sacred status of victimhood, overriding rational calculation
- **[[Originary event]]**: The generative scene of human culture; here, the Holocaust as the signal victimary originary
- **[[Declarative culture]]**: Systems of explicit belief and argument; distinguished from performative imperatives
- **[[Ritual]]**: Repeated enactment that precedes and supersedes explanation; the form victimary politics takes
- **[[Sacred]]**: That which commands obedience beyond rational justification; the victimary operates in sacred register
- **[[Sovereignty]]**: Political authority; here, compromised by subordination to victimary imperatives over truth-telling

## Notable Passages

> "Very few people actually believe that black bodies are maimed from birth to death...and yet on a level deeper than 'opinions' a substantial majority of Americans act as if they do believe such things."

> "Those who repeat the originary event through ritual don't know and can't say why they do so, but eventually they arrive at explanatory myths and other discourses. But the explanations don't affect the ritual."

> "We must see the effects of the Holocaust in its revelation, not in its intrinsic character...much of its intrinsic character may lie in its 'revealability.'"

## Questions Raised

1. If victimary imperatives operate at a level "deeper than opinions," what mechanism prevents the [[declarative]] articulation of victimary claims from actually changing behavior—and could self-awareness of this split enable political resistance?

2. Why the Holocaust specifically as the originary event for contemporary victimary politics, rather than other catastrophes? Is its "revealability" unique, or does the answer lie in historical contingency?

3. Can a conservative or leftist politician break the victimary ritual without first articulating its performative rather than propositional nature—or does naming it risk sacrilege that would only strengthen its hold?
