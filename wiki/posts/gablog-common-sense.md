## Summary

This post argues that [[resentment]] is not a flaw to transcend but a fundamental structuring principle of human meaning-making. Drawing on Thomas Reid's common sense philosophy and GA's [[originary hypothesis]], the author shows how [[resentment]] operates as a calculus through which we constantly adjust ourselves to present social configurations. Rather than seeking to eliminate resentment, we can understand it as the basis for [[shared presence]] and genuine communication—making it irreducibly [[auto-probatory]], something that cannot be denied without paradoxically affirming it.

## Key Concepts

- [[Resentment]] – The imperative to even out imbalances; the fundamental mechanism by which we claim our due within social scenes
- [[Auto-probatory]] – An experience that proves itself by the act of utterance; something that cannot be said without having already lived it
- [[Common sense]] – A calculus of resentment; the practical wisdom of fitting one's claims within present configurations without being excluded
- [[Originary hypothesis]] – The framework for generating a phenomenology of everyday life that respects how people actually live, not abstract models
- [[Shared presence]] – The infinitesimal balancing achieved when resentments are given shape and articulated within a social space
- [[Self-evident truths]] – Axiomatic access to reality grounded in experience itself, as foundational to Declaration of Independence thinking
- [[Counter-resentment]] – The recognition that all resentments respond to prior resentments, creating chains of justification and reciprocal claim-making

## Notable Passages

> "Common sense as a kind of calculus of resentment—each of us has to figure out ways of 'fitting' our resentments within a present configuration that always threatens, however implicitly or distantly, to exclude our own."

> "Resentment itself is the most immediate auto-probatory experience—everyone has experienced resentment, and everyone can acknowledge anyone else's resentment... to deny this would be to affirm it."

> "Every resentment, insofar as it is given shape, does represent, in however small or imaginary a space, an infinitesimal balancing out that sustains some presence and to that extent can be shared and 'transcendent.'"

## Questions Raised

1. If all resentments are equally "true and false" in their counter-resentment logic, how do we distinguish between resentments that sustain genuine reciprocity versus those that calcify into victimary rigidity?

2. Can the notion of resentment-as-calculus account for moments of genuine forgiveness or grace, or does it risk reducing all human reconciliation to mere "evening out"?

3. How does recognizing resentment as [[auto-probatory]] change our approach to political discourse and moral education in ways that differ from both elite social engineering and pure relativism?
