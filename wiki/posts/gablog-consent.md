## Summary

This essay traces consent's rise as modernity's sole remaining value—replacing inherited norms like loyalty and honor with a principle that any act without explicit agreement is evil. Yet the author argues that consent, when "unmoored from any other moral or ethical terms," tends toward incoherence and pathology, exemplified by "affirmative consent" laws requiring explicit "yes" at each step of intimate encounter. The paradox is that consent begins as a liberating critique of corrupt institutions but metastasizes into an unsolvable metaphysical minefield ripe for weaponization by victimary movements.

## Key Concepts

- [[Consent]] — the sole surviving moral principle of modernity, replacing status, hierarchy, and inherited virtues
- [[Modernity]] — historical trajectory from status to contract, tribe to individual, established norms to unrestricted movement
- [[Victimocracy]] — empowerment of protected victim groups as a political tool, using consent doctrine punitively

- [[No harm principle]] — libertarian ethics limiting freedom only where it initiates physical force on others

- [[Inherited virtues]] — loyalty, honor, courage, faith—eroded by consent's logic

## Notable Passages

> "To do something to someone without their consent is evil; to prevent someone from doing what they want to do is evil."

> "Ground zero here is, of course, contemporary sexual relations. More and more states are now passing 'affirmative consent' laws, replacing the original anti-'rape culture' epithet 'no means no' with 'yes means yes.'"

> "Consent has an immaculate birth, and can stand as a pristine alternative to the complicated and corrupt machinations of established institutions ruling through force."
