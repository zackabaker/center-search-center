# Language Paradoxed

## Summary

This post argues that linguistic and analytical discourse inevitably embed the speaker's own [[resentment]] and [[mimesis]] while claiming objectivity. The author proposes that authentic [[center]] thinking begins by acknowledging how resentment structures all thought—the conviction that "the other is in my place." Rather than pursuing critical analysis to extract rivals from stolen positions, maturity involves formalizing status distinctions explicitly, converting invidious comparison into transparent order. The paradox is that we cannot escape resentment-driven thinking, only render it more conscious and ritualized.

## Key Concepts

- [[Resentment]]: The elemental sense that another occupies one's rightful place, detectable in the texture of all language and thought
- [[Mimesis]]: The modeling of desire after others; the infection of one's own examination by one's own mimetic practices
- [[Center]]: The originary locus from which order emerges and is sensed; what calls to indicate disorder
- [[Declarative language]]: Formal utterance that distances the speaker while claiming to reveal truth; a centering practice itself
- [[Status distinction]]: The inevitable hierarchies that emerge even in equal conditions; can be formalized into order or remain sources of invidious comparison
- [[Ascesis]]: Spiritual discipline required to move beyond resentment toward compassion (as exemplified in Jesus or Dostoevsky's Alyosha)
- [[Formalization]]: The ritualization of distinctions so their meaning becomes explicit and orderly rather than chaotic

## Notable Passages

> "Someone positioned where they 'shouldn't' be; to be even more direct, someone positioned where you should be. That's the elemental core of resentment: the other is in your place."

> "The most egalitarian world possible would instantly turn into the most inegalitarian one: as soon as we got started doing something, status distinctions would emerge."

> "Maturity means being able to notice more and more distinctions—and all distinctions are on some level invidious—while attending more and more to the formalizing of those distinctions, rather than to seeking out their justifications."

## Questions Raised

1. If all thinking embeds resentment and we cannot invent another way of thinking, what makes [[formalization]] of distinctions actually superior to pursuing them critically—or is it merely less transparent about its own resentment?

2. Can a society actually function with members "ever more time anticipating potential disorders than addressing immediate ones," or does this aspiration itself become a source of new resentments?

3. What distinguishes genuine [[ascesis]] (as in religious exemplars) from merely rhetorical acknowledgment of one's own resentment, and is the latter anything but sophisticated self-deception?
