## Summary

This foundational text interrogates the [[center]] as the organizing principle of all structure and hierarchy, beginning with Gertrude Stein's paradoxical injunction to "act so that there is no use in a centre." The author argues that any attempt to dissolve the center must paradoxically place that attempt at the center itself, making the elimination of centers a discovery procedure for revealing their multiple uses. Through close reading of Derrida's work on centered structures, the text exposes how the center operates simultaneously inside and outside structure—a "coherence in contradiction" that masks an underlying [[desire]] for certitude and the mastery of anxiety. The project repositions [[anthropomorphics]] as the originary grammar for understanding how human meaning-making fundamentally depends on centering practices.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Center]]**: The paradoxical origin-point that structures all hierarchy, simultaneously inside and outside the system it organizes
- **[[Hierarchy]]**: The fundamental human ordering principle, modeled on the relationship between center and margin
- **[[Desire]]**: The force underlying the seemingly rational need for centered structures; expresses itself as anxiety-mastery
- **[[Structure]]**: Any organized whole dependent on a center for coherence, yet destabilized by that same center's paradoxical status
- **[[Originary Thinking]]**: The method of tracing hierarchies back to their foundational centering acts
- **[[Free Play]]**: The movement of elements within a structure, simultaneously enabled and constrained by the center
- **[[Coherence in Contradiction]]**: The paradoxical logic by which centers maintain systems despite their logical impossibility

## Notable Passages

> "Acting so that there is no use in a center is, in fact, a discovery procedure for revealing and naming all the uses of a center."

> "The center is, paradoxically, within the structure and outside it... The center is not the center."

> "Coherence in contradiction expresses the force of a desire... a desire for certitude, a mastering of anxiety."

## Questions Raised

1. If the center is inherently paradoxical—both inside and outside structure—can we ever escape centering logic, or is the attempt to do so merely another form of centering?

2. How does the anxiety masked by the desire for centered structures manifest in contemporary institutions and social formations?

3. What would an explicitly anthropomorphic grammar reveal about the uses of the center that deconstructive or structuralist approaches leave concealed?
