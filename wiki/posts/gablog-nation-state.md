## Summary

This post distinguishes between nationalism and imperialism by analyzing how victimocracy functions as the most efficient empire-building mechanism yet devised. Rather than rule *by* victims, victimocracy means rule *in the name of* victims by a central authority that expands its mediatory reach by continually identifying local relationships as oppressive. The author argues that nations are mini-empires in perpetual tension with states, and that strong nations deploy their minorities creatively rather than expelling them. The post concludes by situating contemporary resistance to globalism within a larger struggle against imperial reduction of all individuals to their relation to a single center.

## Key Concepts

- [[Empire]]: the reduction of all individuals to their relation to a single center; can be large or small, comprehensive or partial
- [[Victimocracy]]: rule exercised in the name of victims by a central authority; the most efficient imperial mechanism for expanding mediation into every social capillary
- [[Nation]]: a mini-empire that orients populations toward a state; born through revolt against monarchy but perpetually vulnerable to imperial reversion
- [[State]]: the apparatus claiming to represent the nation; tends toward imperial strategies like regional favoritism and minority elevation
- [[Centrality]]: the organizing principle by which individuals relate to a unified center; the structural basis of imperial power
- [[Anti-discrimination regime]]: the institutional mechanism through which victimocracy expands state mediation while appearing egalitarian
- [[Marginality]]: a generative position from which minorities can contribute insights and creations suited to the national archive

## Notable Passages

"The victimocracy, ruling in the name of victims, is the most efficient empire-building mechanism yet invented."

"The more local relationships are shown to violate the equality of all in relation to the center the more the center intervenes to remedy the violation—and thereby lay the groundwork for new ones."

"A stronger nation deploys its minorities, more or less deliberately, as a source of insights and creations more available to those on the margin than those at the center."

## Questions Raised

1. If nations necessarily contain or invent minorities and regional differences to maintain coherence, what distinguishes a healthy national relationship to difference from a weak nation that merely scapegoats?

2. How can resistance to globalist imperialism avoid simply reconstructing national-imperial forms—is there a politics beyond the nation/state binary?

3. What would it mean for a national "archive" to genuinely incorporate minority insights without converting them into imperial fodder?
