## Summary

Centerism is a political-ontological position distinct from centrist compromise, grounded in the principle of directing [[resentment]] toward sustainable centers of meaning and order rather than marginal concerns. Every utterance, thought, and social arrangement orbits a center; the centerist task is identifying which center can best sustain itself and redirecting collective attention and resentment toward it. Drawing on [[generative anthropology]], the text argues that centers emerge from [[originary scene]] dynamics and must be evaluated not by ideological purity but by their capacity to order human cooperation and self-reformation.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Center]]** — The focal point of significance around which all utterance and thought organize; always present but variable in stability
- **[[Resentment]]** — The emotional energy that must be "donated" to centers in order to strengthen them rather than undermining them
- **[[Originary scene]]** — The foundational human scene of mimetic desire around an object, establishing the structure of all subsequent centers
- **[[Centerity]]** — The quality of being central or significant; identical to what matters or demands attention
- **[[Sustainable center]]** — A focus of social organization capable of ordering cooperation and self-reformation over time
- **[[Liberal vs. absolutist GA]]** — Two frames of the originary scene that compete to encompass each other; the text suggests absolutism better explains center-formation
- **[[Mimetic desire]]** — The mechanism by which one's hunger inflames another's, creating hierarchical pressure around focal objects

## Notable Passages

> "We are never centerless, but one center is marginal relative to another. So the problem is distinguishing the central from the marginal."

> "To be a centerist is to seek out the more sustainable center, and do your part to make it even more sustainable."

> "Their resentment toward law, or order, or civilization, or respectability, or whatever it was, must now be donated toward that center in order to make it more capable of ordering such self-reformation projects."
