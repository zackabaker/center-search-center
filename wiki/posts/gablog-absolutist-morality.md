## Summary

The destruction of [[sacral kingship]] as the organizing principle of human communities has left us without a functional [[political center]], driving modern societies into what the author calls "madness." While we cannot restore sacral kingship's blanket accountability (which enabled arbitrary scapegoating), we must recover some framework for establishing clear chains of command and communal meaning. The author argues that progress—from hygiene practices to scientific knowledge—emerged precisely by resisting the compulsion to attribute all events to the sacred center, yet we cannot judge pre-modern societies by post-hoc standards without obscuring our own unstable assumptions about rationality and knowledge.

## Key Concepts

- [[Sacred center]]: The focal point of community meaning and political legitimacy; defines the community's very existence through language and ritual
- [[Sacral kingship]]: Historical system where the king bore responsibility for all community outcomes, enabling both order and indiscriminate scapegoating
- [[Political common sense]]: Functioning shared frameworks for attribution, causality, and order—now destroyed, leaving only accusatory frenzy
- [[Scapegoating]]: The mechanism by which communities deflect responsibility onto marginalized figures when control fails
- [[Observation and trial]]: Proto-scientific method that developed alongside (not against) ritual frameworks in traditional societies
- [[Shaman/priestly figure]]: Subordinate authority figure who developed empirical practices within the sacral order
- [[Community transcendence]]: The capacity of traditions to survive local conquest and mass killing—a problem sacral kingship could not solve

## Notable Passages

> "Language is only meaningful insofar as it refers to the center, which means that the history of humanity, considered as a (language) learning process, is the history of dialogue with the center."

> "There's no position outside of this process from which we can say 'this is irrational'... all you're doing is removing from scrutiny your own assumptions about rationality, intelligence, common sense."

> "Keeping in mind both the difficulty of doing all this and the frenzy that this uncertainty can drive us to is the best way of restoring some sanity."

## Questions Raised

1. If progress required resisting attribution to the sacred center, what replaces that center's function in maintaining community coherence without collapsing into scapegoating?

2. How do we distinguish between legitimate accountability structures and the indiscriminate blame-assignment that sacral kingship enabled—without importing present-day epistemic standards into past contexts?

3. Can ritual practices (like those surrounding medicine) remain therapeutically effective once their sacred cosmology is intellectually abandoned?
