## Summary

Gans argues that White Guilt's grip on contemporary thought stems from a paradoxical omnipotence fantasy: the guilty subject claims total responsibility for others' suffering, implying complete power to rectify it. Unlike the colonial "White Man's Burden," which asserted civilizational superiority, White Guilt denies moral authority while maintaining causal control. This configuration renders the Other blameless and impotent, unable to be held accountable for their actions. From an originary perspective, such omnipotence is unstable—it mimics the Big Man's usurpation of the [[center]] rather than the center's original equilibrium. Understanding this dynamic is essential to transcending guilt-based politics.

## Key Concepts

- [[Victimary thought]]: The peripheral configuration that sees all suffering as evidence of victimization and claims the responsibility to rectify it
- [[Omnipotence]]: The paradoxical fantasy underlying guilt, wherein accepting blame means controlling causality
- [[Center and periphery]]: The structural opposition between originary/centered thinking and victimary/peripheral consciousness
- [[Resentment]]: The emotional fuel of victimary discourse, the Other's refusal to accept their position
- [[Cultural scene]]: The domain of human meaning-making, distinct from natural processes but often confused with them
- [[The Big Man]]: The figure who usurps the center's authority and controls ritual redistribution
- [[Originary thinker]]: One who respects the boundary between culture and nature, rejecting victimary conflation

## Notable Passages

"To claim all responsibility is to presuppose one's own omnipotence; to affirm one's guilt is to take charge of causality."

"White Guilt sees this relationship from the side of the resentful Other as inherently exploitative and humiliating... giving to the Other is merely returning a necessarily inadequate part of what we still owe him."

"His actions, however hostile and even injurious, are not to be taken seriously... like the acts of a child in a tantrum flailing at his parent, they are something to be smiled at rather than avenged."

## Questions Raised

1. If White Guilt's omnipotence fantasy is structurally unstable, what conditions would need to change for societies to abandon it without collapsing into reactive violence?

2. How does the conflation of natural processes with the [[cultural scene]] (as in climate change discourse) systematically distort anthropological understanding, and what are the practical consequences?

3. Can the center ever truly be restored once usurped by the Big Man configuration, or is stable reciprocal authority permanently lost in complex societies?
