## Summary

This brief post uses a classic joke to expose the logical fallacy underlying claims that Republican anti-terror strategies are effective. By citing the absence of major terrorist attacks as proof of policy success, commentators commit a post hoc ergo propter hoc error—confusing correlation with causation. Gans's reposting highlights how [[declarative culture]] allows unfalsifiable claims to circulate unchallenged, revealing deeper patterns in how power justifies itself through rhetorical sleight-of-hand rather than rational argument.

## Key Concepts

- [[Declarative culture]]: Systems of meaning maintained through assertion rather than evidence
- [[Victimary logic]]: The rhetorical strategy of positioning oneself as protected/victim to justify action
- [[Mimetic desire]]: The tendency to adopt others' fears and desires without independent evaluation
- [[Political rhetoric]]: Language used to legitimize power through non-rational persuasion
- [[Resentment]]: The underlying emotional structure that sustains failed arguments through repetition

## Notable Passages
