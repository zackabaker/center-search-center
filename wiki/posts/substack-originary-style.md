## Summary

This post proposes that [[originary style]] is fundamentally pedagogical—a method for teaching language and thought through active manipulation of existing discursive chunks rather than passive "understanding." The author argues that everyone says the same thing (naming the restraining other), but under different infrastructural conditions that make each utterance unique. The core insight is ergodic: all ideas are reworkings of existing language through replacement, modification, and combination. Modern literary criticism erred by treating texts as special artifacts rather than filtering and rearranging disciplinary discourses available to authors.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary scene]]: The founding moment where everyone says the same thing to prevent everyone doing the same thing simultaneously
- [[ESTSTAEE]]: "Everyone says the same thing as everybody else"—the paradoxical foundation of language and difference
- [[Ergodic proposition]]: The principle that we only work with existing language chunks, endlessly recombined
- [[Scenic/infrastructural conditions]]: The material and social contexts that shape utterance while appearing in what is said
- [[Say/do distinction]]: The pedagogical undecidability between utterance and action that all languages preserve
- [[Philological method]]: Treating texts as filtered, rearranged samples from the vast body of disciplinary discourses
- [[Declarative world]]: The digital, discretized realm of same/other distinction versus the continuous analogue
- [[Originary pedagogy]]: Teaching through active rewriting—negation, conjunction, syntactic transformation

## Notable Passages

> "We say the same thing as everybody else so that we don't do the same thing as everybody else at the same time."

> "Anything we might talk about as an 'idea,' or a 'belief' or an 'argument' is to be directly and exclusively treated as a reworking, through replacements, modifications and combinations, of existing discourses."

> "It was a colossal mistake of modern literary criticism...to treat literary texts as very special artifacts irreducible to any extrinsic conditions rather than radicalizing philological practices."

## Questions Raised

1. How can a pedagogy based on active syntactic manipulation of existing sentences avoid becoming mechanical formalism, and what would distinguish genuine understanding from sophisticated recombination?

2. If all discourse is ergodic reworking, how do we account for genuine innovation or thought that exceeds its available language—or is such transcendence itself an illusion?

3. What changes in digital textuality (datafication, searchability) actually enable this philological method in practice, and could it become a new form of instrumental reduction?
