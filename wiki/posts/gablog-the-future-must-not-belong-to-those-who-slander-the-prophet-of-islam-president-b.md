## Summary

This post critiques how Western elite accommodation of Muslim sensitivities paradoxically encourages rather than prevents terrorism. The author argues that self-policing against perceived insults stems from a inverted moral logic: civilized elites blame themselves and Western "barbarism" for terrorist acts, thereby absolving actual perpetrators of responsibility. The piece suggests that genuine Muslim integration into modernity would require public disavowal of violence and active policing of extremist leaders—actions largely absent. White guilt, rather than legitimate fear, prevents Western institutions from defending free expression.

## Key Concepts

- [[victimary progressivism]]: The inversion of blame wherein elites blame themselves and their civilization for the violence of others
- [[white guilt]]: The shame felt by Western elites that becomes the primary constraint on their decision-making
- [[declarative culture]]: The public utterances and commitments (cartoons, films, books) that test civilizational integration
- [[center and periphery]]: The dynamic between civilized elites who distinguish themselves from "middle class barbarians"
- [[resentment]]: The underlying grammar driving both elite self-flagellation and demands for apology
- [[center study]]: The examination of how centers maintain or lose authority through crisis response

## Notable Passages

"The most civilized among us who can't imagine anyone having recourse to barbarism and savagery without having suffered unspeakably...In order to make this distinction absolute, reality must be inverted, and the victims of terrorism must be its cause."

"If Muslims genuinely want to be full participants in the modern world, they would urge on such a universal snub of the 'prophet,' precisely so that they can show that they can respond to it in a civilized manner."

"White guilt is the reason that fear is the only response available to them...shame they would feel in the face of those people with whom they have exchanged promises."

## Questions Raised

1. Can a civilization maintain its declarative culture (freedom of expression) while accommodating groups whose members respond to perceived insults with violence—and what does that choice reveal about its actual strength?

2. How does distinguishing between "extremists" and authentic Islam serve the psychological needs of Western elites, and does this distinction obscure rather than clarify the problem?

3. What would it mean for Western institutions to act from genuine confidence rather than from guilt or fear, and what would that look like institutionally?
