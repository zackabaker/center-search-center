## Summary

Established academic disciplines are structurally designed to neutralize revolutionary or transformative thinking by fragmenting unified critiques into specialized domains. The author argues that genuine world-changing thought must be **transdisciplinary**—operating across all fields while maintaining its own coherent base. Generative Anthropology exemplifies such a discourse, functioning as an "initiator of discursive practices" that cannot be domesticated by existing institutions. However, GA's unique challenge is that unlike Marxism, psychoanalysis, or cybernetics, it lacks an independent institutional base (revolutionary movements, clinics, military apparatus) from which to conduct its insurgency. Its proper clientele is a new [[officer class]] capable of navigating technological modernity with unapologetic awareness of its own power and responsibility.

## Key Concepts

- [[Transdisciplinarity]]: Thinking that extends across all disciplinary subjects while maintaining its own irreducible foundation and transformative capacity
- [[Originary thinking]]: Mode of thought requiring fields to be rethought from their origins rather than offering mere perspective shifts
- [[Initiator of discursive practices]]: Foucault's term for foundational discourse that establishes new ways of thinking indigestible to surrounding institutions
- [[Officer class]]: Emerging managerial-intellectual stratum capable of managing advanced technological orders and mass grievance
- [[Disciplines]]: Institutional structures that fragment totalizing critiques into specialized compartments to prevent systemic transformation
- [[Domestication]]: Process by which revolutionary discourses (Marxism, psychoanalysis) lose insurgent power through academic incorporation

## Questions Raised

1. What would constitute an adequate institutional base for GA's insurgency into the disciplines—can a purely intellectual movement sustain itself without the backing of movements, clinics, or military structures?

2. How can originary thinking maintain its transdisciplinary coherence while "infiltrating" separate disciplinary vocabularies without itself becoming fragmented or domesticated?

3. What specific qualities distinguish an "officer class worth serving," and what are the ethical implications of GA's explicit alignment with managerial power rather than revolutionary movements?
