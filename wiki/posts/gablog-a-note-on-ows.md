# A Note on OWS

## Summary

This essay argues that Occupy Wall Street represents a dangerous opening toward American terrorism by adopting a confrontational model that leaves antagonists only two choices: capitulation or violent suppression. The movement's choice of "occupy" as its tactic—borrowed from 1960s student activism—creates an inherently unstable dynamic: it cannot be sustained in open urban space, must either fizzle or spiral into violence, and already embeds the vanguardist logic of anarchist terrorism. Gans warns that OWS provides both rationale and template for future escalation toward organized domestic violence.

## Key Concepts

- [[Occupational tactic]]: Forcibly seizing institutional or public space until demands are met or force is used
- [[Vanguardism]]: The assumption that a revolutionary minority must provoke violence to "wake" a passive majority
- [[Declarative culture]]: The collapsing of dialogue into absolute demand-making with no room for negotiation
- [[Institutional space]]: A delineated domain (like a university) where occupation can be temporally bounded, unlike a city
- [[Anarchist logic]]: The binary positioning of oppressor vs. oppressed with no middle ground for dialogue
- [[Spectacular violence]]: Acts designed to provoke state response and prove the oppressive nature of the system

## Notable Passages

> "The point is to leave your antagonists with only those two alternatives: capitulation or the use of force, with the latter revealing one's intrinsic, if often so well concealed, fascistic nature."

> "It is both absolutist and utterly confused...it must both fizzle out and spiral out of control, simply because it can have no sense of what its objects or objectives are."

> "The sleepers must be woken up by provoking the minority and their lapdogs to commit spectacular acts of violence upon the vanguard."

## Questions Raised

1. Does the occupational tactic necessarily embed vanguardist terrorism, or can mass movements adopt confrontational space-seizure without requiring escalation?

2. How does the distinction between [[institutional space]] (university office) and public space (city streets) clarify when such tactics become incoherent vs. viable?

3. If electoral politics is "irrelevant" and protest is "irrelevant," what political forms remain that don't assume the oppressor-oppressed binary requiring spectacular violence?
