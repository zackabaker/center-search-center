## Summary

This essay grounds all social phenomena in three fundamental attentional dispositions derived from [[joint attention]]: directing attention toward oneself, toward one's products, and toward external objects controlled by neither party. The author maps these dispositions onto gender (self-centering as feminine, product-centering as masculine) and argues that hierarchical, patriarchal orders emerge when the first two dominate. The path toward more complex social organization requires the third disposition—shared attention to the transcendent or shared—which restrains destructive competition and enables negotiation and cultural development.

## Key Concepts

- [[Joint Attention]]: The capacity to direct shared focus on an object while knowing others share it, grounding human language and sociality.
- [[Attentional Disposition]]: A fundamental mode of directing or receiving attention that structures social relationships and hierarchies.
- [[Self-Centering Attention]]: The feminine attentional mode; making oneself the focus of others' regard, ranging from narcissism to self-sacrifice.
- [[Product-Centering Attention]]: The masculine attentional mode; directing attention toward one's creations, skills, or accomplishments.
- [[Originary Disposition]]: Attention directed toward shared objects external to both parties—the foundational mechanism enabling escape from purely hierarchical orders.
- [[Sovereignty]]: The power to direct collective attention; structured through attentional hierarchy and the contest over what receives focus.

## Notable Passages

> "Making oneself the center of attention is the basic feminine disposition and making one's products the center the basic masculine one."

> "The originary disposition...that of having attention directed towards something...that is attached to neither of the 'attenders' in particular" enables escape from purely hierarchical, tribal orders.

> "There must have often been times when physical confrontations led to mutual destruction...a different approach (retreat, surrender, negotiation) might sometimes be preferable."

## Questions Raised

1. How do modern media and digital platforms restructure these three attentional dispositions? Does social media collapse all three modes into compulsive self-centering?

2. Can this framework account for the emergence of [[declarative culture]] and shared symbolic attention beyond the tribal-hierarchical order? What historical conditions enable the stabilization of the third disposition?

3. If sovereignty is fundamentally about directing attention, what happens to political order when no object can command shared attention—when competing attention markets fragment the social altogether?
