## Summary

Drawing on Marx's analysis of French peasants, the author argues that the white working class cannot represent itself and must be represented—but lacks clear objects for its resentments and possesses no real threat to existing power structures. While manufacturing revival and nationalist trade deals are incoherent fantasies, immigration and Islam remain the only directly actionable issues where working-class preferences could materialize into actual policy. The contradiction between what the working class wants and what's politically possible reveals the deeper problem: the working class is "boxed in" by cultural consensus that no longer supports straightforward patriotism.

## Key Concepts

- [[Representation]] — the mediation between a group's actual interests and their political expression
- [[Resentment]] — diffuse anger without clear objects or paths to resolution
- [[Victimary Politics]] — the paralysis of groups that see themselves as wronged but lack power to act
- [[Declarative Culture]] — the power to establish what is sayable and believable in public discourse
- [[Sovereignty]] — the capacity to actually impose preferences and enforce decisions
- [[Patriotism]] — cultural consensus around national identity that requires overwhelming agreement to sustain
- [[Establishment]] — the coalition of classes aligned against working-class preferences through indifference or opposition

## Notable Passages

> "The problem with the white working class is that their resentments have no clear object, there is no clear way to remedy or defer them; and, at the same time, those resentments are not a particular threat to anyone either—no one needs to be afraid of the white working class."

> "If it can't eliminate illegal immigration, cut legal immigration, and forbid entry by Muslims, there is absolutely no reason to believe it capable of doing anything else."

> "The straightforward, unapologetic patriotism and America-firstism that resonates with the working class requires an overwhelming cultural consensus to be sustained, and we simply don't have that any more."

## Questions Raised

1. If working-class resentments are genuinely actionable only on immigration and Islam, what does this reveal about the actual structure of state capacity versus ideological promise?

2. Can a group achieve political representation without either coherent ideology or real coercive power, or is the white working class structurally consigned to perpetual non-representation?

3. How does the collapse of cultural consensus around patriotism relate to the [[Declarative Culture]] that once sustained it, and is such consensus recoverable?
