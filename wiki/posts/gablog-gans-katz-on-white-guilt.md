## Summary

This brief announcement marks the publication of Eric Gans's Chronicle #341, "Victimary Extinction or Religious Survival," which engages the problem of white guilt within a [[victimary]] framework. Adam Katz's responding Chronicle #342, "The Crisis of Firstness," follows shortly after. Together, these paired pieces constitute a dialogue on how [[resentment]] structures contemporary moral consciousness and its relationship to religious and cultural survival.

## Key Concepts

- [[Victimary]] – The logic wherein victimhood becomes a primary claim to moral authority and political standing
- [[White guilt]] – The internalized self-accusation of the historically dominant group, weaponized through victimary logic
- [[Religious survival]] – The persistence of transcendent meaning-making against victimary reduction to grievance
- [[Firstness]] – The originary priority or precedence that structures thought and ethical claims
- [[Resentment]] – The affective ground of victimary consciousness and its moral claims
- [[Chronicle]] – The ongoing series of dialogic texts through which GA thinkers work through contemporary crises

## Notable Passages

> "Victimary Extinction or Religious Survival"—Gans frames the choice facing white Western culture as existential, posing survival not as mere continuation but as maintenance of transcendent meaning.

> The announcement itself performs [[centering]]—the exchange between Gans and Katz enacts the dialogical form that prevents thought from collapsing into monological victimary accusation.

## Questions Raised

1. Can religious survival occur within a victimary episteme, or must it necessarily oppose the logic of guilt-as-moral-authority?
2. What does "firstness" name that white guilt obscures—and how might recovering it avoid reactionary nostalgia?
3. Is the crisis Katz identifies one of [[firstness]] itself, or of our collective inability to think priority without hierarchy?
