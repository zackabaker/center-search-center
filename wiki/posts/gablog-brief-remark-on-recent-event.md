## Summary

This post argues that American political discourse—across mainstream conservative and liberal camps—operates within an unquestioned [[sacred principle]] that communism is morally superior to fascism. Breaking this "inequivalence" is essential for making non-mainstream positions rhetorically viable. The author suggests Trump's recent press conference achieved this breach by deploying what amounts to an [[annihilatory logic]] critique: asking "who/what are you coming for next?" This reframes the political struggle not as a choice between systems but as a binary between preservation and destruction, giving the right a rhetorical advantage since the left must continually attack established things.

## Key Concepts

- [[Sacred principle]] — An unexamined foundational assumption that structures all discourse within a given field
- [[Inequivalence]] — The rhetorical framing that positions one category (communism) as categorically superior to another (fascism)
- [[Annihilatory logic]] — The internal trajectory of a system that tends toward endless destruction and revolutionary reconstitution
- [[Rhetorical advantage]] — The positional superiority granted to those defending preservation over those demanding transformation
- [[Referential grounding]] — The capacity of a rhetorical claim to point to actual, observable reality
- [[Triggering]] — The attribution of harm based on symbolic offense rather than material injury

## Notable Passages

> "Breaking this 'inequivalence' is the key, at least on the rhetorical level, for those who engage 'mainstream' discourse, to making anything outside of that mainstream thinkable."

> "The way to not only make communism the most horrible thing ever but also to make it possible to stain everyone who puts in a good word for it with its horribleness is to frame things as follows: we want to preserve this one thing (which isn't hurting anyone), they want to destroy everything."

> "The left cannot help presenting itself as on the attack—they are coming after all kinds of things, ultimately everyone (even their own future selves)."
