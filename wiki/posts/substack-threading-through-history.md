## Summary

This post addresses the logical trap facing reactionary thought: each contemporary "liberation" traces back to prior ones, creating an infinite regress that either commits one to defending indefensible positions or requires abandoning critique altogether. Gans proposes that genuine reactionary thinking must be rooted in the [[Big Man revolution]]—humanity's occupation of the [[center]]—as the only historically defensible endpoint. By reading history "emically" through the perspective and responsibilities of successive center-occupants, one can distinguish good from bad changes without falling into either progressivism or pre-human nostalgia.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Big Man revolution]]** – The usurpation of ritual center by human agent; the most consequential revolution enabling all subsequent history
- **[[Center]]** – The ritual and mythic locus of human authority and decision-making that occupants must steward responsibly
- **[[Singularized succession in perpetuity]]** – A framework for reading historical change that avoids both determinism and reactionary infinite regress
- **[[Emic perspective]]** – Narrating history from within the decision-making context of center-occupants rather than external judgment
- **[[Reactionary]]** – One who operates outside liberalism by reading history through center-occupants' highest responsibilities
- **[[Ritual and myth]]** – The pre-human basis of social order before the Big Man's occupancy of the center

## Notable Passages

"We are defenders of the Big Man revolution! I would wager that not even the most reactionary reactionary wants to restore pre-Big Man conditions."

"One is reactionary insofar as one hypothetically narrates the occupation of the center 'emically,' that is, sharing the perspective and entering the terms of decision making confronting the successive occupants."

"We are therefore reading history through the highest responsibilities imposed upon and accepted by humans, and this is the thread that takes us from this first revolution through all the subsequent ones."
