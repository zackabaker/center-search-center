# Modeling Thirdness

## Summary

Thirdness proposes a transition from [[juridical]] adjudication (punishment/damages) toward [[data exchange]] as a mechanism for resolving conflicts. The project recognizes the juridical as an irreducible middle term between [[ritual]] (originary distribution) and [[disciplinary]] modes, and seeks to reintegrate it closer to both through centering. By rendering all human conflicts—ultimately grounded in elementary [[mimetic emotions]] like envy and resentment—submitable to data analysis and center idioms, Thirdness aims to pacify modern life through anticipation rather than intensify hyper-juridification. The work requires grounding technology itself in this political antagonism: as either deepening juridical penetration or resisting orderly succession.

## Key Concepts

- [[Thirdness]]: A political project transitioning juridical resolution into data exchange and center-based mediation
- [[Juridical]]: The middle term between ritual and disciplinary orders; adjudication by enforcing third parties
- [[Data exchange]]: Opposing parties donating/receiving data from center in lieu of punishment or damages
- [[Mimetic emotions]]: Elementary human desires (envy, desire, resentment) made adjudicable through center idioms
- [[Scenic design]]: Technology understood as constitution of scenes, governance, and pedagogical platforms
- [[Dual use]]: Technologies deployed either to deepen juridical penetration or resist orderly succession
- [[Center]]: The locus where conflicts are resolved and reality constructs are maintained

## Notable Passages

> "Not only every conflict, but every imaginable or hypothetical conflict, must be figurable in this way—this is a construction of reality, one which departs from while being continuous with existing constructs."

> "We would be giving more substance to concepts such as 'fairness' as buffers protecting cooperative, norm-governed endeavors."

> "Techno-scenes can serve to display human gestures and mimisms so as to allow for the further penetration and self-effacement of the juridical or can be deployed to wage war on those parts of the population seeking to maintain orderly successions."

## Questions Raised

1. How can we practically render elementary mimetic conflicts (envy, desire) into data-exchangeable forms without collapsing the [[originary scene]] itself into pure technique?

2. If all relationships bear conflict-potential drilling down to mimetic substructure, doesn't universalizing [[data exchange]] risk creating the very total surveillance state it claims to prevent?

3. What distinguishes Thirdness's "anticipatory" pacification from the preemptive juridification it opposes—where does the boundary lie?
