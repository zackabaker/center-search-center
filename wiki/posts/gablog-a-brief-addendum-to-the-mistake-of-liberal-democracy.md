## Summary

This brief post refines the concept of [[sovereign equality]] as a corrective to liberal democracy's failed promise of "equality of outcome." Rather than claiming Bill Gates and a homeless person are equal in meaningful ways, the author argues that [[sovereign]] power must superintend competing resentments to prevent obstruction of the [[center]]'s foundational resentment. [[Sovereign equality]] thus becomes public recognition of each individual's resentments, displacing false universalism. The wealthy paradoxically invoke sovereign power to escape competitive freedom once victory is secured, creating the massive inequalities we observe today.

## Key Concepts

- [[Sovereign equality]]: Public recognition of each person's resentments, replacing outcome-equality
- [[Sovereign power]]: The superintending force that mediates between freedom and competing resentments
- [[Equaliberty]]: The indivisible pairing of freedom and equality, inherently in tension with sovereignty
- [[Center]]: The foundational resentment structure that must remain intelligible amid individual claims
- [[Resentment]]: The generative mechanism underlying both individual and collective claims to recognition
- [[Liberal democracy]]: The failed attempt to sever freedom from equality through outcome-equality doctrine

## Notable Passages

> "Sovereign power stakes its claim where equaliberty demands too much rigor from its participants, and that there are many such sites our current crisis testifies."

> "The wealthy who felt that competition had outlived its usefulness once they had won, but also sanctioned by the claims of sovereign equality."
