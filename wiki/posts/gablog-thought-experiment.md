## Summary

This thought experiment explores whether complete neurological monitoring could allow us to predict human consciousness and behavior from brain activity alone. By analogizing the brain to a computer with decodable internal states, the author questions whether consciousness follows a fixed "code" or continuously reprograms itself. The piece critiques neuroscientific claims about free will while acknowledging the genuine medical value of brain research, ultimately suggesting that consciousness cannot be reduced to mere electro-chemical correlation.

## Key Concepts

- [[Consciousness]] — subjective experience requiring explanation beyond physical brain states
- [[Mediation]] — the gap between internal neural activity and external representation
- [[Code]] — fixed systems of translation (like ASCII) versus self-modifying systems
- [[Representation]] — how internal states map onto observable outputs and reports
- [[Free will]] — whether neural precedence of activity undermines human agency
- [[Mimesis]] — the relationship between reported experience and actual neurological substrate

## Notable Passages

"The brain doesn't operate according to a fixed code like a computer. The brain is, in effect, constantly reprogramming itself."

"We would be able to predict, purely on the basis of recorded brain activity, what the person is feeling or thinking—but only, I would guess, in a general way, not precisely."

"There is the problem of identifying which particular neurons actually 'cause' a decision."

## Questions Raised

1. If the brain continuously reprograms itself based on environmental input, does this mean consciousness is fundamentally **non-predictable** even in principle? What does this imply for determinism vs. agency?

2. How does the gap between reported consciousness and actual neural activity map onto the [[originary scene]]'s gap between gesture and meaning—is consciousness itself a form of deferred representation?

3. If we could only predict consciousness "in a general way, not precisely," what constitutes the remainder? Is individual human consciousness necessarily irreducible to any complete mapping?
