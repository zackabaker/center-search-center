## Summary

This essay diagnoses contemporary Left ideology as a perpetual war against [[marking]]—the process by which communities consolidate victims for sacrifice. Drawing on [[generative anthropology]] and Girardian thought, the author argues that modern institutions (nation-states, markets, individual rights) are merely concealments of global scapegoating. The Left's tactical response is to prevent any mark from achieving closure while simultaneously marking the "unmarked markers" (those who initiate marking). This produces a self-contradictory but exhausting politics dedicated solely to disrupting [[scapegoating]] mechanisms and discrediting those who perform them.

## Key Concepts

- [[Scapegoating]]: The originary communal mechanism of marking and sacrificing a victim to resolve crisis and create social cohesion
- [[Marking]]: The attribution of a distinctive sign to individuals or groups that singles them out as dangerous or subversive
- [[Scenic closure]]: The moment when a mark crystallizes and a community reaches consensus for collective action against the marked victim
- [[Declarative culture]]: Culture structured by the power to name, define, and mark categories of people and meaning
- [[Victimary progressivism]]: Left ideology that positions itself against marking while marking the markers, creating perpetual moral inversion
- [[The contemporary Left]]: Political movement dedicated exclusively to disrupting marking and preventing scenic consolidation
- [[Unmarked markers]]: Those with the power and expertise to initiate marking (historically "white" agents)

## Notable Passages

> "If you believe all these things, where do you look for political salvation… Your mission must be to prevent any scene from arriving at closure."

> "You must simply reject any marking–wherever some marking is starting to take shape, you must foreground whatever in the potentially marked group resists such marking."

> "At the same time, there is one exception to your absolute opposition to marking: it is essential that you mark the unmarked markers."

## Questions Raised

1. Can the Left's strategy of perpetually blocking scenic closure actually prevent catastrophic violence, or does it merely defer and distribute scapegoating pressure unpredictably across society?

2. Is marking the markers a coherent political position, or does it inevitably collapse into the very scapegoating mechanism it claims to oppose?

3. What would positive (non-reactive) political creativity look like for a movement committed exclusively to prevention and disruption?
