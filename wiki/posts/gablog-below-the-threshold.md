## Summary

This post explores the structural asymmetry between left and right political movements in contemporary discourse, arguing that the Left has monopolized the modern Western narrative of liberation through its ability to marshal [[scenic representation]] around recognizable victim-types. The author, while sympathetic to free market economics and critical of state overreach, observes that principled defense of markets and liberty cannot compete theatrically with victimary progressivism. The core problem is that market actors—entrepreneurs harmed by regulation, workers accepting wage cuts—lack the dramatic visibility required for political narrative, leaving the Right vulnerable to appropriation by extremists and marginalization by mainstream media.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Victimary regime]]** – The contemporary political order structured around the perpetual identification and elevation of victim-types
- **[[Scenic representation]]** – The ability to stage political narratives visually and emotionally for public consumption
- **[[Resentment]]** – The foundational emotional logic of both Left and Right when pushed to extremes
- **[[Democratic sentiment]]** – Public opinion that constrains but should not wholly determine statecraft
- **[[Market logic]]** – The invisible mechanisms of exchange that resist theatrical visibility
- **[[Conspiracy thinking]]** – The rhetorical tendency to read structural problems as intentional malice
