# (P)ower

## Summary

Power originates in the [[credit]] granted by a community to those who successfully coordinate collective action—first on the [[originary scene]] where unified attention prevented sacrificial violence, then extended to hunts and defenses. The essay interrogates whether power grows because it is fundamentally *insecure* (always seeking to eliminate gaps in its reach) or simply *insatiable* (hungry by nature). This distinction matters enormously for understanding modern state expansion, yet proves conceptually slippery: any power extension can be rationalized either way, suggesting the framework we choose determines what empirical facts we observe.

## Key Concepts

- [[Credit]]: The delegation of decision-making authority granted by a group to a capable leader
- [[Originary scene]]: The founding moment where humans chose unified attention over rivalrous violence
- [[Attention structure]]: The ordering of collective focus to prevent panic and coordinate action
- [[Absolutism]]: Political systems where power seeks to flatten intermediary layers and rule directly
- [[Power hunger vs. insecurity]]: Two interpretive frames for explaining power's expansion
- [[Spontaneity]]: The irreducible human freedom that power registers as "anomalous"

## Notable Passages

> "Power, then, first of all means structuring attention so as to quell or preferably pre-empt the panic that results from a collapse of shared attention into upwardly spiraling rivalries."

> "An early medieval king...may consider his power quite secure if he can on occasion rouse his lords...the modern state apparently feels its power is insecure if there is a single 'white supremacist' who can hold down a job."

> "The determination can't be made on empirical grounds, because the conceptual order will lead to the empirical observation."

## Questions Raised

1. If power's insecurity and power's insatiability are empirically indistinguishable, can we ever determine which actually drives state expansion—or is this a permanent aporia in political analysis?

2. Does the shift from leaders earning credit through concrete protective services (hunts, defenses) to modern states demanding compliance for abstract safety indicate a fundamental transformation in the legitimacy structure, or merely its degradation?

3. How would an originary thinker distinguish between power legitimately preserving the [[attention structure]] that prevents mimetic violence versus power pathologically extending itself into domains where no such coordination is needed?
