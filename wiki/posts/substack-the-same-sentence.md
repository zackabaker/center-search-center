## Summary

This post proposes treating human community as co-composers of an infinitely extensible sentence—a metaphor for how [[deferral]] and [[cooperation]] might scale globally. Drawing on David Olson's theory of writing as the recreation of reported scenes, the author argues that prose is constitutively inhabited by other voices and scenes. Each sentence carries implicit stagings and connections that shouldn't be closed off; instead, contributors should leave "hatches open" for continuation, modeling a community that never completes itself but perpetually defers final meaning.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary gesture]] – the aborted appropriation that founds human communication and can theoretically scale without ceiling
- [[Deferral]] – the recursive postponement of meaning and completion that enables cooperation
- [[Scenic architecture]] – the implicit scenes and reported stagings embedded within linguistic utterances
- [[Metalanguage]] – features of writing that allow representation of cognition and distance from immediate speech
- [[Prose]] – writing as recreation of a scene other than direct performance, constitutively inhabited by other voices
- [[The command]] – the single imperative underlying humanity to sustain the presence of the scene

## Notable Passages

> "There is a single command to be obeyed since the beginning of humanity, to sustain the presence of the scene, which is also an imperative to de[fer]." 

> "The incorporation of scenic architectures in prose goes way beyond mental verbs...into all the ways one sentence entrains all the preceding and touches all the contemporary sentences in its articulation."

> "We'd start to speak, write and think somewhat differently, as if we were very specially treated samples in data exchanges."
