## Summary

This essay critiques Bernard Stiegler's philosophy of technics and memory while advancing a distinctly GA alternative. Stiegler correctly identifies technics as externalized memory but remains trapped in phenomenological metaphysics when trying to explain how the first inscription could be recognized as an inscription. The author proposes replacing Stiegler's "default of origin" with an [[event|event-al anthropogenics]] that grounds meaning not in consciousness but in the [[scene]] itself—where inscriptions are "conscripted" into use and participants are always already oriented toward a [[center]].

## Key Concepts

- **[[Tertiary retention]]** — Human memory externalized through inscription; the specifically human form of memory that depends on material marks.
- **[[Default of origin]]** — Stiegler's paradox: the first inscription must be both accidental and necessary, yet this origin cannot be explained without metaphysics.
- **[[Event-al anthropogenics]]** — Grounding human meaning in actual scenes and events rather than individual consciousness or philosophical discourse.
- **[[Scene]]** — The shared spatial-temporal configuration where inscriptions are read and attention is directed toward a center.
- **[[Center]]** — The focal point organizing attention and meaning within any scene.
- **[[Technics]]** — The systematic externalization of memory through tools, writing, and media—what metaphysics has systematically suppressed.
- **[[Conscription]]** — The way an accidental mark becomes a meaningful sign through its uptake and use within an event.

## Notable Passages

> "How could the first sign be recognized as a sign without there already being signs, articulated within a system, that allows us to recognize some mark as a sign?"

> "The 'accidental' inscription is 'conscripted' into use upon a scene, in an event—in that case, we have an 'event-al anthropogenics' instead of philosophy."

> "We are always on the margin of some scene, however configured, in accord with whatever temporality, in terms of whatever mode of participation in that scene we find imaginable."

## Questions Raised

1. If all meaning is [[scene]]-dependent and origin-grounded in events rather than consciousness, how do we account for the portability of inscriptions across radically different scenes?
2. Does acknowledging that we are "always on the margin of some scene" resolve the problem of [[differance]], or merely defer it to the scene itself?
3. What makes a particular [[conscription]] of an accidental mark into necessity rather than merely habitual repetition?
