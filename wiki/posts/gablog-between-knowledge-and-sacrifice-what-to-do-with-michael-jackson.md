## Summary

This essay applies [[generative anthropology]] and [[René Girard]]'s sacrificial theory to understand Michael Jackson as a post-modern sacrificial figure occupying the [[centre-periphery]] configuration. Jackson embodies simultaneous objects of envy and scorn, positioned centrally in consumer culture while subjected to public resentment and fixation. The author argues that Jackson's trajectory exemplifies how modern celebrity functions as a safety valve for mimetic violence, and how the pursuit of differentiation in mass society paradoxically produces [[monstrous doubling]] and sacrificial vulnerability. Jefferson's cultural critique becomes a lens for understanding how America's simultaneous horror at and promotion of "freakishness" creates the conditions for sacrificial crisis.

## Key Concepts

- [[Centre-periphery]]: The structural configuration where a central figure attracts simultaneous admiration and resentment from the periphery
- [[Sacrificial crisis]]: A state where mimetic violence and resentment converge on a designated victim
- [[Mimetic desire]]: The tendency to desire what others desire, generating rivalry and violence
- [[Differentiation]]: The individual's drive to distinguish themselves from the community, risking identity crisis when uniqueness fades
- [[Monstrous doubling]]: The paradoxical process where attempts at radical differentiation produce uncanny similarity and violent rejection
- [[Consumer culture]]: The modern system that simultaneously promotes and horrifies at transgression and "freakishness"

## Notable Passages

> "Michael Jackson occupies a status more central in today's decentralized market society" than any institutional figure—he is the object upon whom "all desires on the periphery converge."

> "A young Michael Jackson had to make his mark in order to stand apart not only from his brothers, but from the other acts Motown was propagating"—yet uniqueness, once granted, wanes dangerously.

> American culture is "horrified at freaks and which, simultaneously, promotes freakishness as a legitimate stepping stone in pursuit of the American dream."

## Questions Raised

1. How does the market structure of celebrity production differ from pre-modern sacrificial mechanisms, and does it actually contain mimetic violence or merely defer it?

2. Can figures like Jackson escape the sacrificial role once positioned centrally, or is the centre-periphery configuration inherently unstable and violent?

3. What is the relationship between the pursuit of extreme differentiation in consumer culture and the production of monstrous doubling that invites public destruction?
