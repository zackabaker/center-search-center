## Summary

This post critiques progressive foreign policy ideology, particularly Rosa Brooks's argument that asymmetric warfare is unwinnable and that addressing "legitimate grievances" is key to eliminating conflict. The author exposes a fundamental logical flaw: when an enemy's grievance is your existence itself, the framework of rational negotiation collapses. The piece traces how the Democratic party's vision shifted from FDR's unconditional-surrender realism to a UN-as-lowest-common-denominator approach that treats all parties as morally equivalent interlocutors.

## Key Concepts

- [[Asymmetric warfare]]: Conflict methods used by the weak against the strong; the post questions whether recognizing grievances can resolve such conflicts
- [[Victimary progressivism]]: A ideological posture that assumes all parties are reasonable and possess legitimate grievances worth accommodating
- [[Declarative culture]]: The belief that naming, discussing, and institutionalizing problems can solve them (here applied to international conflict)
- [[Enemy recognition]]: The capacity to identify an adversary whose stated goal is your annihilation, not negotiation
- [[Institutional decline]]: The erosion of principled institutions (UN) into venues for moral relativism
- [[Resentment as ground]]: The structural problem where one's existence becomes the source of another's grievance
