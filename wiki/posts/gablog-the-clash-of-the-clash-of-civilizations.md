## Summary

This piece reframes the "clash of civilizations" debate by arguing that the real conflict is between those who *believe* a civilizational clash exists and those who deny it. While the West hopes inclusion and tolerance can accommodate even radical Islam, Islamists explicitly reject Western civilization and seek its destruction. Gans argues that resentment, once committed to destruction rather than productive activity, cannot be contained through dialogue or institutional tolerance—and that the West's reluctance to deploy collective force in response may prove fatal to its survival.

## Key Concepts

- [[Resentment]]: The fundamental human tendency toward intraspecific violence, deferred through culture but capable of reverting to raw hostility when it cannot be harnessed productively.
- [[Deferral]]: The mechanism by which human culture postpones immediate violence through ritual, law, and institutional mediation.
- [[Victimary]]: The ideological position that frames tolerance and inclusion as moral absolutes, even when directed at those explicitly committed to destruction.
- [[Collective retribution]]: The crude but sometimes necessary exercise of group-level force as a deterrent when individual justice and institutional remedies fail.
- [[Market]]: Humanity's best available system for channeling competitive energies into productive rather than destructive channels.
- [[Intraspecific violence]]: Aggression within the human species, the primordial danger that culture evolved to defer.

## Notable Passages

> "This is a conflict between one side that believes there is a 'clash of civilizations' and one that does not. To wit, the Islamists believe that modern civilization is evil and that its members should be either annihilated or converted."

> "In the contest between those who believe in a clash of civilizations and those who do not, it is the first group that decides for what stakes the game is to be played."

> "The market and its political support system is humanity's best hope for survival. But it has no magic formula for the indefinite deferral of violence."

## Questions Raised

1. If resentment committed to destruction cannot be recuperated through institutions, what conditions allow a civilization to accurately diagnose when dialogue has become futile—and to act decisively without itself becoming what it opposes?

2. How does Gans's framework distinguish between *legitimate* uses of collective retribution and mere cycles of reciprocal violence justified by the same logic?

3. Can the [[market]] truly channel destructive resentment toward productive ends, or does it merely defer the problem until conditions make violence again "rational"?
