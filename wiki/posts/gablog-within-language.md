## Summary

This essay explores how language functions as the transparent medium of thought and social coordination until literacy makes it visible as an object of study. By examining the comic device of the anachronistic character, the author argues that we are inevitably "locked into" linguistic systems we cannot fully escape. The emergence of literacy creates metalanguages—ways of thinking *about* language—which grants epistemic and social power, yet no metalanguage can fully transcend language itself. The text concludes (fragmentarily) that access to metalanguage constitutes a form of power that remains perpetually vulnerable to displacement.

## Notable Passages

> "We are so much within language, moving in it, 'breathing' it, certainly thinking in it, that we don't even notice it."

> "Any metalanguage is just waiting to be swallowed up by some meta-metalanguage or dissolved back into language itself."

> "Everyone not inside our language is a barbarian. When we become aware of language, formalistic and seemingly niggling disputes ensue."

## Questions Raised

1. If no metalanguage can fully escape language itself, what is the status of claims made *within* a metalanguage about power or truth? Are they necessarily compromised?

2. Does the shift from transparent to opaque language (enabled by literacy) represent progress toward clarity, or a kind of fall into endless reflexive regress that undermines action?

3. How does the author's incomplete final sentence on power suggest that metalinguistic access is ultimately unstable—and what political implications follow?
