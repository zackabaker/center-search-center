## Summary

Power operates fundamentally through [[centered ordinality]]—the ability to attract contending parties to bring their grievances to a center rather than resort to vendetta. The author argues that all legitimate power must be exercised through [[judgment]] and presents itself as juridical, even conquest. Liberalism fills the [[imperative gap]] (the distance between issued and obeyed command) through [[supersovereignties]] drawn from multiple disciplines, particularly law. The US's recent geopolitical failures stem from abandoning its role as "honest broker" and mediator—the very function that would maximize actual power by converting [[resentment]] into [[debt servicing]] to the center.

## Key Concepts

- [[Centered ordinality]]: Power ordered through imperatives and command within teams organized around shared purpose
- [[Imperative gap]]: The distance between the imperative issued and the imperative obeyed; must be ritually reconciled
- [[Juridical order]]: The framework in which power legitimates itself through claims of justice and judgment
- [[Supersovereignties]]: Liberal mechanisms (legal, scientific, social scientific) that fill the imperative gap and distribute authority
- [[Judgment]]: The fundamental exercise of power through settling grievances and disputes
- [[Debt servicing]]: The conversion of resentment into loyalty and obedience to the center through acknowledgment of obligation
- [[Honest broker]]: A mediating authority that increases power by remaining trusted by contending parties
- [[Naked conquest]]: (Hypothetical) conquest presented without juridical justification—likely impossible in actual empire

## Questions Raised

1. If all legitimate power requires juridical presentation, what happens to the concept of power itself when juridical order itself becomes contested or delegitimized? Does the center lose power, or does conflict simply move to a meta-level about the nature of judgment itself?

2. Can a mediating power like the US restore [[centered ordinality]] through "honest broker" functions without first addressing the resentment it has already generated through its own sabotage—or does the attempt itself constitute another judgment that requires acceptance?

3. How does the shift from [[imperative gap]] management through ritual succession to management through disciplinary supersovereignties change the vulnerability of power to [[dedifferentiation]] or civilizational collapse?
