## Summary

This post examines the paradox of Western moral discourse around colonialism and development: while the end of colonialism is deemed morally legitimate, it has left vast regions ungovernable and unable to integrate into the global economy. The author argues that the assumption of fundamental human equality—itself presented as moral—forces the West into increasingly contradictory "neocolonial" aid postures while preventing honest acknowledgment that societies organize at different levels of complexity. As digital capitalism privileges symbol-manipulation intelligence, the conflict between [[liberal democracy|liberal-democratic]] equality doctrine and economic reality becomes untenable.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Morality]]** — the unstable set of assumptions guiding collective action, often masking deeper tensions between stated values and actual practice
- **[[Equality]]** — the originary principle that becomes incoherent when applied universally without acknowledging functional hierarchy
- **[[Colonialism]]** — governance imposed according to different norms; its critique assumes colonizers acted against colonized interests, yet remains arbitrary
- **[[Neocolonialism]]** — the involuntary repetition of colonial gestures (aid, intervention) under the guise of moral obligation
- **[[Liberal democracy]]** — a system whose faith in originary equality grows incompatible with digital-age economic requirements for IQ-type intelligence
- **[[Victimary logic]]** — the compulsion to blame colonialism itself rather than acknowledge differential capacity for integrating technological modernity
- **[[Social organization]]** — the observable fact that societies operate at different levels of complexity, which morality forbids naming directly

## Notable Passages

> "The most moral gestures of the modern West are, it seems, its most colonial ones. And what could more disastrously interfere with this moral impulse than the assumption that 'all human beings have fundamentally the same capabilities'?"

> "If that is your assumption, you can only behave morally—i.e., actually treat other people as needing your help—by finding some roundabout way of claiming that that is not what you're doing."

> "Morality and immorality seem to have switched places."
