## Summary

This essay argues that "meaning" operates on a single fundamental principle across both linguistic and existential registers. Whether we speak of a word's definition or life's purpose, meaning ultimately depends on [[ostensivity]]—utterances pointing toward a shared center of attention that grounds reference in a community. The author contends that existential meaninglessness is not metaphorical but literal: when people feel their lives lack purpose, they experience the breakdown of linguistic coherence in their social institutions, relationships, and work. Meaning requires an entire infrastructure of language, community, history, and shared purpose to function; its absence signals the failure of these systems.

## Key Concepts

- [[Ostensivity]]: The primitive gesture of pointing that grounds all linguistic meaning; any utterance directs attention toward something to be noticed.
- [[Center of attention]]: The shared focal point required for an utterance to be intelligible within a given context or community.
- [[Linguistic meaning]]: The conventional, shared use of signs within a language community that enables mutual understanding.
- [[Existential meaning]]: The felt sense of purpose and coherence in life, grounded ultimately in linguistic coherence, not metaphysically separate.
- [[Community]]: The institutional and historical infrastructure that stabilizes linguistic reference and enables signs to "mean."
- [[Sign degradation]]: The collapse of meaning when words no longer reliably point toward shared referents or lived realities.

## Notable Passages

> "Meaning is ultimately grounded in ostensivity—to put it crudely, any utterance points at something, tells you what to notice."

> "If all those things—a language, a community, a family, an institution, a vocation, and ways of thinking about all these things that don't insult our morals and intelligence—are all we want, then all we want is for all of the linguistic acts we perform and witness to mean."

> "Words like 'job' and 'family,' and other associated ones like 'love' and 'purpose' literally don't mean anything, or perhaps, sinisterly, mean the opposite of what they are supposed to."

## Questions Raised

1. If existential meaninglessness is really linguistic meaninglessness, what role does genuine [[sign degradation]] play in social collapse—is it cause or symptom of institutional breakdown?

2. Can a community sustain shared ostensives without shared moral frameworks, or does the attempt to build meaning-systems on purely technical or procedural grounds necessarily fail?

3. How do we distinguish between legitimate shifts in meaning (semantic evolution) and pathological sign-degradation where words become disconningly decoupled from referents?
