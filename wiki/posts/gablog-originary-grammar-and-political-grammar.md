## Summary

This post argues that political discourse's highest purpose is exposing the [[political imaginary|political imaginaries]] lurking beneath stated desires. By asking "what kind of sovereign would have to exist for you to get what you want?" we can reverse-engineer any political vision. The author demonstrates how progressive demands (ending racism, ensuring equality) necessarily presuppose totalitarian sovereigns engaged in infinite surveillance and correction—an ultimately anarchist imaginary masquerading as liberalism. By contrast, the [[absolutist imaginary]] is simpler: a desire for a sovereign who is actually sovereign, whose subordinates fulfill institutional purposes in coherent chains.

## Key Concepts

- [[Political imaginary]] – The implicit sovereign and social order one envisions to achieve one's stated political desires
- [[Sovereign]] – The organizing center of power whose existence and character structures all other social relations
- [[Declarative culture]] – Culture organized through explicit declaration and enforcement of rules rather than organic emergence
- [[Anarchism|Anarchist imaginary]] – The presupposition of equal, undifferentiated units prior to authority; equality as the original state
- [[Absolutism|Absolutist imaginary]] – The desire for a sovereign who is genuinely sovereign, enabling cascading institutional purposes
- [[Victimocracy]] – A system organized around identifying and remedying victimhood; the machinery that produces rage
- [[Marker]] – Detectable signs of inequality, racism, or deviation that must be eliminated or corrected

## Notable Passages

"What kind of sovereign are you then imagining? One who commands a vast guerilla army of mindless, heartless human resources drones following a rigid playbook that gets rewritten constantly…"

"A sovereign, in other words, which is the enemy of all the people it governs (in differing degrees, at different times). You would have to be constantly enraged, inhabiting such a vicious imaginary."

"The absolutist imaginary is a thing of simple, almost tautological beauty: all of our wants translate into a desire for a sovereign that is sovereign."

## Questions Raised

1. How can progressive movements recognize they are imagining totalitarian sovereigns without abandoning their stated goals? Is there a coherent middle ground, or does any attempt at systematic equality inevitably presuppose infinite surveillance?

2. If the absolutist imaginary is "simpler," why is it politically inert or regressive in modern contexts? What makes simplicity beautiful in theory but apparently unlivable in practice?

3. Can we construct political discourse that exposes imaginaries without either descending into rage or retreating into the absolutist fantasy?
