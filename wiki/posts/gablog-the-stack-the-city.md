## Summary

This essay engages Benjamin Bratton's *The Stack* (2016) to explore how planetary-scale computation challenges and potentially reorganizes traditional sovereignty. Rather than dwelling on victimary progressive concerns, the author finds Bratton's "absolutist" design politics—which accepts climate catastrophe and redesigns human habitat accordingly—more intellectually honest and ambitious. The Stack's vertical articulation of Cloud, Earth, City, Address, Interface, and User layers offers a new topology for thinking sovereignty, power, and political will in the age of computational ubiquity. The essay suggests that these layers are less stacked than "wedged" into one another, opening space for traditional political sovereignty to resurface as necessary for coherent coordination.

## Key Concepts

- [[sovereignty]]: Political will and legitimate power; reconceived through computational strata rather than territorial nation-states
- [[the stack]]: Bratton's model of six vertically articulated layers (Cloud, Earth, City, Address, Interface, User) replacing outdated nation-state frameworks
- [[planetary computation]]: Planetary-scale data accumulation and processing that exceeds and potentially supersedes traditional political authority
- [[cloud capital]]: Data-processing polities (Google, Apple, Amazon, Facebook) that function as new sovereigns above nation-states
- [[design]]: Large-scale, ambitious material and political reorganization of human habitat in response to planetary conditions
- [[nomos]]: Schmitt's concept of foundational ordering; here shifted from earth to "cloud" as the new basis of rule
- [[address layer]]: The institutional and individual nodes where sovereignty and computation actually touch the social world
- [[absolutist politics]]: Unflinching acceptance of catastrophe paired with ambitious redesign—unconcerned with present pieties

## Notable Passages

> "Rather than obsessing over trying to minimize carbon, we could accept that climate catastrophe will happen and redesign our human habitat in response—an 'absolutist' project precisely parallel to Bratton's in scope, ambition, and disregard for present political pieties."

> "The problem of articulating all these levels coherently leaves open the possibility that some kind of traditionally conceived sovereignty (political will) might be beneficial or even necessary to help create the 'stack of the future.'"

> "With the exception of the Cloud and Earth layers, the layers seem less piled on top of than wedged (in very complicated and uneven ways) into each other."

## Questions Raised

1. If the Cloud layer (Google, Amazon, Facebook) now functions as a genuine sovereign above traditional nation-states, what mechanisms of legitimacy or accountability could possibly constrain it?

2. Can traditional political will and decision-making actually coordinate the Stack's six layers coherently, or does the complexity necessarily require some form of post-human governance?

3. What would genuine "absolutist" politics—accepting catastrophe and redesigning without regard for victimary concerns—actually demand of us socially and architecturally?
