## Summary

This post uses David Graeber's *Debt: The First 5,000 Years* to dismantle liberal economic mythology about barter-to-money evolution. The author argues that markets are state creations designed to maintain ritual and military institutions, not organic outgrowths of exchange. Traditional [[moral economies]] operated through personal credit without currency, while modern marketization violently replaces these with [[debt]] regimes where individuals become alienable property. The text traces how [[hierarchy]] (particularly kingship) mediates between [[communist]] relations and exchange relations, using currency to extract taxes and control subjects through debt.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Moral economy]]**: Economic relations embedded in personal obligation where debts never completely expropriate debtors; presupposes inclusion of all parties in the system.
- **[[Debt]]**: An antagonistic obligation divorced from moral frameworks; the foundation of marketized social order where individuals treat each other as potential adversaries.
- **[[Communist relations]]** (Graeber's sense): Everyday reciprocity based on need and ability; the originary form underlying all social organization.
- **[[Exchange]]**: Commensurable relations treating objects as equivalent; disembedded from moral contexts through state intervention.
- **[[Hierarchy]]**: Social organization by precedent without commensurability; the mediating form through which states create currency and extract wealth.
- **[[Market]]**: A state-managed violent disruption replacing personal credit economies with depersonalized debt regimes.
- **[[Currency]]**: Invented by states (particularly kings) to pay soldiers and tax subjects, enabling antagonistic debt extraction.

## Notable Passages

> "Markets have historically been created and managed by states, for the purpose of maintaining ritual and military institutions. A fully marketized order, meanwhile, involves the violent disruption of personal and moral economies of credit."

> "Traditional debt regimes, in which economies are always moral economies, presuppose the inclusion of everyone within the system—debts never completely expropriate the debtors. The market economy has everyone treating everyone else as outside of the system of obligations, as a potential adversary."

> "We need currency in order to pay such 'antagonistic' debts."

## Questions Raised

1. If [[moral economy]] remains the baseline of everyday human interaction, why has marketization proven so durable and difficult to resist across centuries?

2. How does the sacral language of debt (redemption, obligation to God) both conceal and enable state violence in wealth extraction?

3. What would the reinhabitation of [[communist relations]] at scale require, and at what cost to contemporary institutions?
