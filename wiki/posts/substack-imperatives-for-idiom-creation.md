## Summary

This essay argues that writing should move toward "total, wall-to-wall idiom"—a self-enclosed, recycled linguistic system that paradoxically generates without limits. The author advocates battling the metalanguage of literacy (hedging phrases like "suggests," "might," "on the other hand") to initiate direct, staggered scenes rather than deferential writerly pretense. By placing opposites in causal relation, eliminating optativity, and embracing constraint, writers can forge new disciplinary pathways and achieve a kind of generative freedom through ergodic linguistic sealing.

## Key Concepts

- [[Idiom]]: A self-sustaining linguistic system that recycles phrases ergodically while remaining generative without preset limits
- [[Metalanguage of literacy]]: Hedge words and distancing formulas ("suggests," "might," "on the one hand") that fake a neutral scene of speech
- [[Ergodic sealing]]: Constraint through recycled, circulating language that paradoxically enables generation
- [[Scenic structure]]: Staggered, direct scenes initiated by eschewing deferential writerly distance
- [[Optativity]]: Hoping and wishing in writing—a weakness that abandons inquiry for prayer-substitute; should be replaced with actual prayer or planning
- [[Selving]]: Creating deliberate distance between successive "I's" through alert repetition and recycling

## Notable Passages

> "Place opposites in causal relations with each other; figure out how they lead into and convert each other. Do that before you have the 'ideas' all worked out—only by creating the new relationship will you work out the ideas."

> "Eschewing the metalanguage of literacy locks you in to initiating a staggered, less deferential and bloated, scene."

> "In the space between wanting and happening there is prayer, and 'hoping' is a pathetically weak substitute for prayer."

## Questions Raised

1. How does the ergodic sealing of idiom differ from mere mannerism or stylistic affectation, and what prevents it from collapsing into solipsism?

2. If the metalanguage of literacy is unavoidable (as the author admits), what constitutes success in this battle—strategic deployment rather than elimination?

3. Does prayer (proposed as replacement for hope) require a particular [[declarative]] or [[sovereign]] position, or can idiom-making itself become a form of prayer?
