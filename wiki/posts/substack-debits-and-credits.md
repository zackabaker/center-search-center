## Summary

This essay reconsiders Eric Gans's foundational separation between institutional and linguistic representation in generative anthropology, arguing it creates a false autonomy. The author proposes that language is always pedagogical and institutional—never existing outside concrete scenes of transmission. Drawing on the concept of [[originary debt]], the text suggests that human culture fundamentally consists of recording, enforcing, and paying debts to the center, and that technology ultimately functions as [[scenic design]] enabling the continuous breakdown and reassembly of social practices through pedagogical platforms.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Originary debt]]**: The primal obligation owed to the center (object or institution) that sustains the community
- **[[Institutional representation]]**: The heavy, invested ritual dimension of social organization (wrongly separated from language by early GA)
- **[[Linguistic representation]]**: The seemingly autonomous dimension of language use, mistakenly imagined as free from institutional constraints
- **[[Pedagogical anthropology]]**: The view that human culture is fundamentally about the ongoing explication of the tacit through teaching
- **[[Idiom mining]]**: The creation of idioms from commonplaces; language learning as institutional circulation
- **[[Scenic design]]**: Technology understood as creating platforms for reassembling human practices and revealing latent capacities
- **[[Commonplaces]]**: The rhetorical tradition's shared formulae that constitute the material basis of language

## Notable Passages

> "If there is never any linguistic representation, i.e., no use of language, outside of institutions, then the entire separation collapses."

> "Language is always pedagogical, and pedagogy always implies an institution, some scene to be preserved, extended and restaffed."

> "Social life is the recording, enforcement and payment of debts to the center."

## Questions Raised

1. If all language is necessarily institutional, how do we account for genuine linguistic innovation or idiom creation that seems to exceed existing pedagogical structures?

2. Does the model of "debits and credits" to the center risk reinstating a metaphysical center that generative anthropology was meant to dissolve rather than preserve?

3. How would a society organized around "tokens representing future pedagogical practices" differ materially from present debt-based economies, beyond conceptual reframing?
