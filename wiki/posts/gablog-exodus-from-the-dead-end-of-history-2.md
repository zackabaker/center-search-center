## Summary

The author proposes a minimal ethical principle: do not feed your fantasies and addictions. [[Fantasy]] represents a deformation of the [[imagination]] that imposes unseen desires onto visible reality, while [[addiction]] is the deformation of [[habit]], consuming reality in the service of repetition. Both stem from the originary delusion of possessing the object alone. The solution is to bring feelings into the [[field of semblances]]—the shared world—where no single sign dominates, maintaining [[transparency]] about one's agency. This principle grounds both personal morality and cultural critique.

## Key Concepts

- [[Fantasy]]: Deformation of imagination; the unseen imposed on the seen, forcing reality to conform to desire
- [[Addiction]]: Deformation of habit; the swallowing of reality into compulsive repetition seeking impossible feeling-preservation
- [[Imagination]]: Properly constitutes reality by providing unseen background to visible foreground
- [[Habit]]: Provides continuity, fabric, and rhythm of reality (distinct from addictive distortion)
- [[Field of semblances]]: The shared world where feelings become signs; no single sign dominates
- [[Transparency]]: Acting in the world such that it is visibly *you* doing it; resistance to immersion in fantasy
- [[Originary scene]]: The primordial moment referenced as foundation for the fantasy of solitary possession

## Notable Passages

> "Fantasy is someone using it for you, even if that someone is yourself. The imagination constitutes reality by providing the unseen background to some visible foreground while fantasy imposes the unseen on the seen, forcing the latter to conform."

> "The way to resist submersion in fantasy and addiction is to keep bringing one's feelings out in the world where they can enter the field of semblances, which no one sign can dominate."

> "This implies a kind of transparency, through which whatever you do you show that it is yourself doing it. This is what keeps you in the world."

## Questions Raised

1. How does the [[originary scene]] specifically authorize the fantasy of solitary possession, and does recognizing this origin break its spell?
