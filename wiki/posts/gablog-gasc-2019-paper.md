## Summary

This paper reconciles the linguistic turn with [[generative anthropology]] by arguing that language is not primarily representational but *generative*—meanings arise from relations between signs, not from external reality. The author draws on the [[originary scene]] to reframe post-structuralist pluralism: while post-structuralists see irreducible difference in signs, GA reveals that the originary sign achieves its force through absolute unanimity. The central human problem thus becomes ensuring all participants on a scene issue and recognize the same sign, despite the impossibility of identical signs across scenes.

## Key Concepts

- [[Linguistic Turn]]: Rejection of extra-linguistic reality as legitimation for discourse; meaning produced within sign systems
- [[Generativity]]: Language creates truth, reality, and meaning through intrinsic relational structures rather than representation
- [[Originary Scene]]: The scene of human emergence where a single sign simultaneously creates the human, society, and meaning
- [[Sign Identity]]: The perpetual tension between the need for identical signs and their essential non-identity across scenes
- [[Unanimity]]: The originary requirement that all participants recognize and issue the same sign
- [[Metalanguage]]: A second-order language appealing to external reality to ground sign identity—necessarily metaphysical
- [[Self-Referential Constitution]]: Alternative means of establishing sign identity through deliberate scene-constitution

## Notable Passages

"Language, rather than being primarily representational...is generative—that is, it is relationships intrinsic to representational structures that create what we call truth, reality, nature, good and so on."

"The problem...is ensuring that all participants on a scene issue, and know themselves to be issuing, the same sign. This is a problem because it never really is the same sign."

"Signs only take on meaning within a scene, a sign on one scene cannot be identical to a sign on another scene, no matter what measures we take."

## Questions Raised

1. If the identity of signs depends either on metaphysical grounding or deliberate self-referential scene-constitution, which mechanism actually operates in ordinary language use, and can both coexist?

2. How does the requirement for unanimity on the originary scene relate to modern [[pluralism]] and the irreducible difference poststructuralists identify—is there a genealogy of dedifferentiation from originary unanimity to contemporary pluralism?

3. What practical methods can "generative literacy" employ to transform discourse so it becomes constitutive of new scenes, rather than merely appealing to external legitimation?
