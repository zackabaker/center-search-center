## Summary

This post argues that human language is fundamentally constituted by the capacity to assert "this is the same"—a distinctly human world-building act. The author traces how [[linguistic presence]] emerges through nomination of a central object, and how maintaining sameness across differences structures both language and obedience to [[center|the center]]. The [[imperative gap]]—the space between command and compliance—is where the [[center]] differentiates itself and where scribal/programming discipline inscribes the infrastructure of successive imperatives. Obedience is treated as the originary human condition, making disobedience not justified resistance but an "ungrounded" shift in allegiance to an alternate center.

## Key Concepts

- [[Linguistic presence]] — The maintenance of sameness across time and utterance, foundational to language itself
- [[Imperative gap]] — The irreducible distance between an issued command and its performance; the space where obedience must supplement the explicit
- [[Center]] — The originary locus of nomination and imperative; differentiated into occupied and signifying poles
- [[Sameness]] — The distinctly human act of asserting equivalence where none "really" exists; world-building through language
- [[Othering]] — The practice of establishing difference through same/other operations across scales
- [[Scribal discipline]] — The inscriptive infrastructure enabling imperatives to chain across time and obedience
- [[Problem of obedience]] — Why people obey; reframed here as presumption rather than puzzle

## Notable Passages

> "What makes language language is that we can say 'this is the same.' No non-human form of communication allows for this."

> "Whatever is involved in fulfilling the command but is not explicitly detailed in the plan is supplied by the imperatee, and this 'supplement' is an inscription of the imperative infrastructure."

> "If we disobey one occupant of the center, it is only because we have shifted our obedience to another."
