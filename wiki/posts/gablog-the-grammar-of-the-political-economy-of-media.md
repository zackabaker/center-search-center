## Summary

This essay critiques McLuhan's media theory as prophetic but imprecise, arguing that [[desire and resentment]] run deeper than media themselves. The author proposes grounding media theory in [[generative anthropology]] by reconceiving media not as extensions of sensory organs but as extensions of the [[originary gesture]]. Media are understood as producing new [[fields of semblances]] that emerge when mimetic tensions can no longer be deferred within existing forms, creating perpetually new arenas for desire and resentment rather than determining human consciousness.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary gesture]] — the founding human act of deferral that creates meaning and culture
- [[Desire and resentment]] — anthropologically fundamental forces that predate and supersede media effects
- [[Fields of semblances]] — objects and domains produced in tandem with media technologies
- [[Mimetic tension]] — rivalry and competition that media help defer or channel
- [[Hot and cool media]] — McLuhan's distinction reconsidered through the lens of participatory demand
- [[Institutional forms]] — the hierarchical orders and legitimacy structures that media enable
- [[Deferral]] — the central mechanism by which media manage potentially violent mimetic conflict

## Notable Passages

> "Desire and resentment lie deeper than any media, and so does the difficult historical work of channeling and shaping them through institutions."

> "Once it had been invented in some form, for whatever purposes, it could be put to work serving the new hierarchical orders that needed not only bureaucracies but forms of legitimacy."

> "In originary terms, [media] means they create new arenas of desire and resentment."

## Questions Raised

1. How precisely do we distinguish between a medium's inherent structural properties (hot vs. cool) and the contingent historical uses to which it's put? Does the distinction collapse under scrutiny?

2. If all media ultimately defer mimetic tensions, what happens when a medium's capacity for deferral becomes exhausted—does it necessitate a new medium, or a new institutional framework?

3. How would an originary theory of media account for media that seem designed to *intensify* rather than defer desire and resentment (propaganda, advertising, social media)?
