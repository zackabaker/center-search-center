## Summary

This brief chronicle announcement points toward an examination of how [[faith]] operates across different registers—minimal and maximal—in relation to [[tragedy]] and Christian thought. The piece gestures toward understanding "varieties of firstness," suggesting that faith itself comes in degrees or intensities tied to originary moments and their recurrence. By connecting tragedy (a secular-aesthetic form) to Christianity (a salvational narrative), Gans appears to be exploring how different cultures and epochs organize their relationship to the [[sacred]] through variant experiences of primordial [[firstness]].

## Key Concepts

- **[[Firstness]]** – The originary moment or its structural recurrence; the inaugural presence before differentiation
- **[[Faith]]** – Commitment to the transcendent or sacred; varies between minimal (structural necessity) and maximal (transformative devotion)
- **[[Tragedy]]** – A scene organizing human meaning around inevitable suffering and limitation; a form of secular or pagan sacred
- **[[Christianity]]** – A religion centered on salvation and redemption; offers an alternative structure to pagan tragedy
- **[[Sacred difference]]** – The originary gap between human and divine; how cultures locate the sacred
- **[[Originary thinking]]** – Generative anthropology's method of tracing human culture to its sacred origin

## Notable Passages

> "the relationship between the varieties of firstness"

This phrase encapsulates the core inquiry: faith isn't univocal but manifests in multiple versions depending on how one accesses or experiences the originary.

## Questions Raised

1. How precisely does [[firstness]] structure differently in tragic pagan consciousness versus Christian redemptive consciousness? What makes one "minimal" and the other "maximal"?

2. Does the move from tragedy to Christianity represent a fundamental shift in how humans experience [[originary]] presence, or merely a rhetorical reframing of a constant anthropological structure?

3. If [[faith]] itself is tied to varieties of firstness, can we map other non-tragic, non-Christian forms of faith to their originary moments?
