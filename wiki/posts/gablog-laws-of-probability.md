## Summary

This essay confronts the paradox of statistical disparities in post-victimocratic society: we can measure group differences with increasing precision, yet the [[victimocracy]] demands we interpret all disparities as proof of systemic oppression. The author argues there is no middle ground—either accept disparities as reflecting real differences in capabilities/propensities, or treat them as evidence of oppression. Both paths threaten liberal equality. The only escape lies in cultivating [[discipline]]—a trained attentiveness to counter-statistical improbabilities that preserves hope without denying reality.

## Key Concepts

- [[Victimocracy]] — A system that justifies its existence through perpetual exposure of oppression, collapsing statistical facts into moral indictments
- [[Statistical disparities]] — Measurable group differences in achievement, aptitude, and outcomes that resist ideological neutralization
- [[Blank slate equality]] — The now-shattered pretense that legal/political equality requires believing in natural human equivalence
- [[Discipline]] — A trained capacity to acknowledge statistical trends while remaining alert to counter-trends and improbabilities
- [[Ethico-epistemological cartel]] — The victimocracy's monopoly on how we are permitted to speak about and interpret social facts
- [[Counter-trends]] — Minor deviations from statistical patterns that preserve space for merit, individuality, and hope

## Notable Passages

> "There is no third way... because the precise proportions are then, unbearably, open for discussion."

> "The only effective counter-statistical measure, though, is discipline—the interest in an enterprise that depends on being open to improbabilities, to the idea or individual no one else expects much from."

> "There will always be sufficient limits to our understanding of human capabilities and their distribution to justify the energy expended in the detection of such counter-trends, which can perhaps serve as a proxy for a kind of hope."

## Questions Raised

1. Can [[discipline]] as described here—a trained openness to counter-statistical improbabilities—actually function as a social practice, or does it remain an individual virtue insufficient to resist structural incentives?

2. If acknowledging statistical disparities inevitably "entrenches and intensifies" them (through stereotype and self-fulfilling prophecy), what grounds our hope that disciplined attention to counter-trends can overcome this dynamic?

3. How might we speak about group differences in a post-victimocratic idiom that neither collapses into oppression-narratives nor hardens into naturalistic determinism?
