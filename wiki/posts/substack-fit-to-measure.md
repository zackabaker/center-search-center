## Summary

This essay interrogates the relationship between [[syntax]], [[semantics]], and the [[originary hypothesis]] in an era of total computational surveillance. Drawing on Noah Horwitz's argument that we are fundamentally data-beings whose gestures can be recorded as syntactic information, the author asks whether a theocratic constraint—rooted in the [[incomputable]]—offers the only viable check on computational power. The piece proposes that an [[originary syntax]] precedes the subject-predicate structure of language, where the peripheral gesture predicates the center, and that this originary relation may be what constrains tyranny where liberal checks and balances fail.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Originary syntax]]**: A pre-linguistic formal relation where peripheral gestures predicate the center as their referent
- **[[Declarative sentence]]**: The emergence of explicit subject-predicate articulation in human language
- **[[Computational theocracy]]**: A regime in which administrative rules are imposed from a transcendent source and enforced through data capture
- **[[The incomputable]]**: That which cannot be fully computed by any system, ensuring surplus meaning and undetermined futures
- **[[Center and periphery]]**: The foundational spatial relation of the originary event, where the center emerges through peripheral deferral
- **[[Measurement]]**: The fundamental relation by which one thing effects the composition of another, a minimal assumption of shared reality
- **[[Semantics/syntax distinction]]**: Traditional linguistic categories that may be transcended by originary thinking

## Notable Passages

> "Any movement or gesture that we perform can be recorded as syntactic information... To be invisible here is not to exist."

> "If computation implies the incomputable, since the data analyses carried out computationally always generates more data that it could not have already computed, then something like an originary syntax is the word of God."

> "No occupant of the center can deny the incomputable, nor need he in order to see to his own succession."

## Questions Raised

1. If an [[originary syntax]] precedes semantic meaning, does this require us to make explicit ontological commitments about the animal and natural worlds from which humans emerged—or can we remain agnostic on such questions while still deploying the concept?

2. How does the [[incomputable]]—that excess data generated by computation itself—function as a genuine constraint on those wielding power over information systems, versus merely a theoretical escape hatch?

3. Can measurement as a minimal relational fact between things in a shared universe be harmonized with the originary hypothesis, and if so, what does this imply for understanding how [[center]] and [[periphery]] are originally constituted?
