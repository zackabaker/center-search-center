## Summary

This essay contests the anti-absolutist claim that sovereignty is limited by pre-existing social material (customs, institutions, nature). Rather than treating resistance to sovereign command as evidence of prior spontaneous social formation, the author argues that what appears to constrain the sovereign is actually the sedimented residue of **prior acts of sovereignty** itself. Sovereignty is temporally constitutive—it doesn't merely exercise power within constraints, but creates the very "denseness" that seems to resist it. The challenge for absolutist theory is showing how the strongest objection—that sovereigns cannot command the impossible—actually supports rather than undermines the absolutist position.

## Key Concepts

- [[Sovereignty]] — The constitutive power to command and establish social order, not merely constrained by external conditions
- [[Center]] — The originary locus of meaning-making and social organization that commands before it is commanded
- [[Social Material]] — The inherited customs, rituals, and institutions that appear to resist sovereign command but are themselves sedimented sovereignty
- [[Absolutism]] — The ontological claim that the sovereign center is constitutive of rather than derivative from social relations
- [[Command]] — The directive force through which sovereignty operates and creates constraints for future sovereigns
- [[Temporality]] — The temporal layering of successive sovereign acts that creates apparent "resistance" to current authority
- [[Legitimacy]] — The contested ground between theories that ground authority in pre-sovereign conditions vs. constitutive power

## Notable Passages

> "Any sovereign command comes up against some resistance, some 'denseness' in the social material...that resistance is prior to the sovereign, in which case sovereignty is raised up upon the basis of those relationships which formed external to sovereign power."

> "The best way of arguing in the social and human sciences is to show how your opponent's strongest claims can be reframed in terms of your own assumptions."

> "What limits sovereignty...is prior acts of sovereignty—what the sovereign is dependent upon..."

## Questions Raised

1. If all apparent constraints on sovereignty are themselves sedimented prior sovereignties, what prevents infinite regress? Where does the chain of prior commands terminate?

2. How does this reframing account for genuinely novel constraints (technological impossibility, biological limits) that seem independent of human sovereign acts?

3. If the strongest anti-absolutist argument can be reframed to support absolutism, doesn't this methodology risk making the theory unfalsifiable?
