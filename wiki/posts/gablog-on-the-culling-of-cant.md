## Summary

This essay distinguishes cant as both hypocritical sanctimony and fashionable argot, identifying it as a specifically liberal linguistic phenomenon where fashionable speech becomes the marker of morality. The author argues that cant is fundamentally a misuse of [[metalanguage]]—assessment of language use without issuing [[operable imperatives]]. Drawing on GA's understanding of the [[declarative sentence]], the piece traces how philosophy itself becomes metalanguage-on-metalanguage, and how concepts mask direct reference to reality by embedding users in linguistic systems of which they remain largely unconscious.

## Key Concepts

- [[Metalanguage]] — Language turned into an object of inquiry; the pedagogical dimension within language itself
- [[Operable imperative]] — A command that actually directs behavior; distinguished from masked declaratives masquerading as imperatives
- [[Cant]] — Assessment of language use without issuing operable imperatives; fashionable sanctimony
- [[Declarative sentence]] — The prototypical form of language according to metaphysics; foundation of philosophical metalanguage
- [[Declarative masquerading]] — An imperative (like "be clear!") that secretly asserts the speaker's authority rather than directing action
- [[Linguistic system]] — The web of interdependent concepts within which every word-use is embedded, largely invisible to the speaker

## Notable Passages

> "Fluently employing the latest argot, imposed and enforced by an elite, becomes the marker of morality."

> "Any use of the word 'deep' will fall into this category ('go deeper!'). Another excellent example is 'critical,' which is very popular these days, especially in the form of 'critical thinking.'"

> "When you use a concept, all this is not present in your mind, so it's easy to fall into the illusion that in using the word you are simply referring directly to something out there in the world."

## Questions Raised

1. If philosophy is metalanguage on metalanguage, at what point does self-awareness of the system break the illusion of direct reference, and does this awareness itself become another layer of cant?

2. How do we issue [[operable imperatives]] about language use without ourselves falling into the trap of masked declaratives that only assert authority rather than direct action?

3. What distinguishes genuinely critical thought from the fashionable application of the term "critical" within elite linguistic systems?
