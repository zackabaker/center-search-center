# The Day of the Locust

## Summary

Drawing on Nathanael West's 1939 novel, this post argues that modern mass media provokes an impossible resentment in audiences by raising expectations that can never be satisfied within consumer society's own terms. West's insight—that mob violence is provoked specifically by *representation* rather than material deprivation—illuminates contemporary phenomena like Occupy Wall Street and the competitive dynamics of political correctness. The post suggests that politics has become fundamentally a battle for moral superiority rather than substantive reform.

## Key Concepts

- [[Mimetic desire]]: Competitive, imitative wanting; desire shaped by what others desire
- [[Mass media]]: The representational apparatus that generates resentment through endless sensationalism
- [[Resentment]]: The affective engine of modern politics, born from the gap between inflated expectations and achievable satisfaction
- [[Representation]]: The symbolic realm where violence is provoked, distinct from material facts or events
- [[Political correctness]]: A competitive moral hierarchy vulnerable to recursive radicalization
- [[Mob dynamics]]: Group psychology driven by mimetic escalation rather than coherent demands

## Notable Passages
