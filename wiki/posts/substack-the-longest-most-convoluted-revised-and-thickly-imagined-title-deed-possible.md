## Summary

This essay reinterprets the Hebrew Bible's central covenant between God and the Jewish people through a radically materialist lens: as a [[title deed]] to land granted by an emperor to Abraham as part of administrative allocation. Rather than a purely theological agreement, this reading emphasizes the ongoing necessity to re-assert claims and transmit inheritance through verifiable lineages. The patriarchal narratives—with their "dubious proceedings" and unscrupulous maneuvering—suddenly cohere as documentary evidence of legitimate transmission, where moral judgment is irrelevant compared to establishing unbroken chains of possession and responsibility.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Covenant]]** — Not sworn to a god as judge, but entered into *with* God as party; shifts from theological to contractual framing
- **[[Title deed]]** — The material-legal foundation of Jewish claim, requiring constant re-assertion across generations
- **[[Inheritance transmission]]** — The organizing principle of patriarchal narratives; every story proves a link in an unbroken chain
- **[[Political theology]]** — Makes the sacred fundamentally about land, power, and imperial delegation rather than metaphysics
- **[[Originary thinking]]** — Reading foundational texts for what they *actually* encode rather than what we project onto them

## Notable Passages

> "If God gave your people the land in perpetuity that would be an end to it... if, however, the memory of a more mundane exchange is never completely lost, the claim to the land must constantly be re-asserted."

> "The authors could not care less whether we see the protagonists as heroic or not... Providing evidence that the title deed has, indeed, been transmitted from its original lessee through a verifiable chain of inheritances."

> "The attempt to 'cover up' a murder so as to preserve a viable line of inheritance resulted in one of the most generative foundation stories of Western civilization."
