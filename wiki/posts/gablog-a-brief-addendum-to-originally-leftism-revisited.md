## Summary

This piece argues that leftism's fundamental appeal lies in its capacity to deconstruct the link between [[discipline]] and benefit, thereby opening infinite fields for social-scientific investigation. The social sciences emerged precisely to sever this connection—making them constitutively leftist. While leftism offers inexhaustible intellectual resentment through perpetual reanalysis of social "structures," a [[civilizational]] politics grounded in irreducible faith cannot compete institutionally. The author claims GA's approach yields greater intellectual rewards but necessarily remains marginal because it refuses the impersonal abstraction required by social-scientific legitimacy.

## Key Concepts

- [[Discipline]] – systematic practice whose benefits are always contestable and reinterpretable
- [[Centrality]] – the resource or recognition at stake; leftism "exposes" its theft through structural analysis
- [[Social sciences]] – constitutively anti-disciplinary discourses that deny discipline generates proportional reward
- [[Leftism]] – the imperative to sever discipline from benefit, generating perpetual intellectual resentment
- [[Civilizational politics]] – grounded in undeconstructable faith; cannot match leftism's analytical invigorating power
- [[Resentment]] – the intellectual fuel enabling endless redefinition of social laws and structures
- [[Impersonal explanation]] – the scientific demand that apparent benefit be explained away from personal/disciplinary causation

## Notable Passages

> "The origin of the social sciences lies in the imperative to sever the link between discipline and benefit (which would mean all the social sciences are constitutively leftist)."

> "You can always define and analyze the social laws and structures in more complex ways, you can always challenge some previous definition—therefore was not sufficiently scientific."

> "My approach would also be too 'personalizing,' insofar as it must reject other approaches as dyscivilizational."

## Questions Raised

1. If the social sciences are constitutively leftist, can a non-leftist social science exist, or must any empirical-structural approach necessarily decouple discipline from reward?

2. Does GA's marginal institutional status reflect a genuine intellectual superiority that cannot survive professionalization, or does the author's "personalizing" requirement itself limit GA's reach?

3. How does the "faith" required by civilizational politics differ from the unprovable assumptions embedded in social-scientific "impersonal explanation"?
