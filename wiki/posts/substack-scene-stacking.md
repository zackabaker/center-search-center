## Summary

This post develops a GA critique of political ideological positions by introducing "scene stacking" as a framework for understanding how systems transform through anomalies rather than intentions. Rather than adopting declarative professions of faith, the author proposes inhabiting the [[originary hypothesis]] as a practice of gathering data on scenes and their non-closure. Drawing on Bateson's double bind and the historical example of exilic Judaism, the text argues that new forms emerge when attempts to comply with imperatives from an external scene repeatedly fail, forcing recursive self-correction that ultimately transforms both the subject and the imperative itself.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary hypothesis]]: The foundational GA insight that human meaning-making derives from a primordial scene of simultaneous self-awareness and deferral
- [[Declarative order]]: The linguistic regime that presupposes already-constituted meanings, making "belief" and "faith" inherently problematic categories
- [[Scene stacking]]: The accidental emergence of megastructures through attempts to resolve anomalies within existing systems using available means
- [[Internal scene]]: The psychic space formed when external imperatives become impossible to satisfy, forcing internalized response-formation
- [[Speech forms]]: The successive linguistic emergences (ostensive, imperative, declarative, interrogative) derived with rigorous precision in GA theory
- [[Double bind]]: The impossible situation where compliance itself becomes indeterminate, forcing recursive reinterpretation (Bateson)
- [[Anomaly resolution]]: System transformation that occurs not through intention but through failed compliance attempts that recursively alter both subject and authority

## Notable Passages

> "To inhabit the hypothesis is to have a different relation to inherited vocabularies, a zone between literalness and allegory, demystification and unreserved indebtedness to the Other."

> "Whenever anything new emerges, it does not result from an intention to create that thing. It results from something more 'profound'—an attempt to resolve an anomaly within another system, using the means available within that system."

> "One may have mistaken the purpose of the imperative and therefore the character of the authority who issued it: maybe... rather than wanting me to fulfill the imperative successfully, he wanted me to fail, so that I would be forced to inspect my thoughts and practices."
