## Summary

This essay diagnoses the collapse of classical liberal distinction-making—the ability to defend someone's right to speak without endorsing their views. Rather than explicit anti-liberal argument, the younger generations find this distinction increasingly *unintelligible*, confusing free speech protection with agreement. The author argues that both the victimary left and libertine right represent pathological responses to market-driven wealth polarization that has erased the respectable middle class. Without functional means of adjudication between competing claims on recognition and freedom, we face a standoff between two equally implausible children of modernity.

## Key Concepts

- [[Liberalism]] — the Enlightenment principle distinguishing a right to express views from agreement with those views; now collapsing into unintelligibility
- [[Victimary]] — groups demanding recognition through symbolic rebellion against systemic inequality, functioning as sacrificial substitutes for deeper economic dysfunction
- [[Recognition]] — the distribution of respectability and social standing; increasingly polarized as the middle class erodes
- [[Market system]] — the economic structure generating wealth polarization and the erasure of normal forms of legitimacy
- [[Declarative culture]] — the collapse of shared language for adjudicating competing claims and grounds for action
- [[Rights discourse]] — the fictional but functional equilibrium mechanism now failing to mediate between competing interests
- [[Libertine]] — those confusing what is permitted with what is good, defending pleasures through rights-talk rather than substantive argument

## Notable Passages

> "If you say that those opposed to same sex marriage should have the right to voice their opinion...the increasingly likely response is: why do you hate gays?"

> "With the respectable middle cut out, what are left are pathological forms of 'theater'—mass killings and other forms of cheaply and/or viciously acquired celebrity."

> "Both sides live in fantasy worlds"—the victimary symbolically rebelling against polarization, libertines vicariously identifying with it.

## Questions Raised

1. If the means of liberal adjudication have genuinely collapsed rather than merely degraded, what institutional or linguistic innovations might restore some functional equilibrium between competing claims?

2. Can the distinction between recognition-distribution and wealth-distribution be recovered, or are they now so entangled that addressing one requires transforming the other?

3. Does the pathological "theater" of celebrity violence represent a genuinely new form of claiming presence in a desiccated middle, or a symptom we should expect to intensify?
