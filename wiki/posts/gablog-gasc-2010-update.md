## Summary

This brief announcement promotes the 4th annual Generative Anthropology Summer Conference (GASC 2010), held at Westminster College and Brigham Young University in Utah. The conference theme, "The Anthropology of Modernity: The Sacred, Science, and Aesthetics," frames inquiry into how humans have always inhabited modernity. Eric Gans's keynote engages Bruno Latour's epistemological challenge to modernism itself, while Vince Pecora addresses the conceptual distinction between secularism and secularization. The announcement invites all interested parties to participate in what promises to be a significant gathering of GA scholars.

## Key Concepts

- [[Generative Anthropology]] – The originary thinking of human culture and representation
- [[Modernity]] – The historical and conceptual condition the conference interrogates
- [[The Sacred]] – One of three domains (sacred, science, aesthetics) structuring anthropological analysis
- [[Anthropoetica]] – The aesthetic and poetic dimensions of anthropological thought
- [[Secularization]] – Historical process distinct from the ideological stance of secularism

## Notable Passages

> "Haven't We Always Been Modern" – Eric Gans's plenary in dialogue with Bruno Latour's *We Have Never Been Modern*.

> "Secularism, Secularization, and Why the Difference Matters" – Vince Pecora's plenary on conceptual distinctions in religious modernity.

> "The Anthropology of Modernity: The Sacred, Science, and Aesthetics" – The conference theme integrating three fundamental human domains.

## Questions Raised

1. How does the claim "we have always been modern" reshape our understanding of pre-modern culture and representation?
2. What precisely distinguishes secularism (ideological) from secularization (historical process), and why does this difference matter for anthropological analysis?
3. How do the sacred, science, and aesthetics function together as domains for understanding human modernity rather than as competing explanatory systems?
