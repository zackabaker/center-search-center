## Summary

Bouvard explores God-language as an [[idiom]] that operates across multiple [[scene]]s simultaneously—an irruptive voice that interrupts present conversation and reveals parallel meanings elsewhere. He threads this framework through revisionist biblical scholarship, particularly Bernard Lamborelle's argument that Abraham's covenant was originally with an earthly king (possibly Hammurabi), later transformed into a meta-personal divine relationship. This reading clarifies how the Jewish God emerged from a [[mimetic]] and [[resentful]] relation to divine kingship, with the original earthly contract preserved tenaciously in textual memory.
