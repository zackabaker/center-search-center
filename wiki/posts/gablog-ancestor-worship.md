## Summary

This essay critiques the accelerating pace of moral condemnation applied retrospectively to past cultural artifacts and historical actors. The author observes that the interval required to declare something "sexist" or "bigoted" has collapsed from decades to years or months, driven by a perpetually shifting [[Overton Window]]. Rather than understanding historical thought in context, contemporary progressive culture treats engagement with "discredited" perspectives as morally contaminating, producing a generation with dangerously thin historical literacy. The essay suggests this represents a pathological inversion of traditional [[ancestor worship]]—not honoring but erasing the past.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Overton Window]]** – The shifting range of socially acceptable opinions that determines which views are permissible to express at any given moment
- **[[Victimary progressivism]]** – The moral framework that perpetually expands categories of victimhood and requires constant purification from past errors
- **[[Declarative culture]]** – Culture defined by explicit, performative moral declarations rather than sustained understanding or practice
- **[[Moral acceleration]]** – The exponential speed-up in the rate at which new ethical categories are discovered and retroactively applied to history
- **[[Center and periphery]]** – The distinction between what is allowed at the moral center versus what remains marginal or banned
- **[[Historical consciousness]]** – The capacity to understand past actors and ideas within their own contexts rather than through contemporary moral standards

## Notable Passages

> "Completely new forms of racism, sexism, and other isms and phobias are discovered daily—no claim to not be 'bigoted' made prior to the latest shift in the Overton Window can expect to be greeted with anything other than mocking contempt."

> "The Stalinists at least respected the basic narrative structure of the past, even as they erased individuals... but why and how people thought as they thought now seems to be of no interest at all."

> "Articulating 'discredited' perspectives has become indistinguishable from defending them, as if to understand were to be contaminated."

## Questions Raised

1. What is lost when historical understanding becomes impossible without moral contamination—can [[generative anthropology]] recover a mode of studying the past that maintains both rigor and charitable interpretation?

2. Does the exponential acceleration of moral categories suggest a structural limit, or can this process continue indefinitely without producing generational ignorance that destabilizes society itself?

3. If "ancestor worship" once meant honoring the dead as repositories of transmitted wisdom, what replaces that function when all ancestors are declared morally obsolete?
