## Summary

This post argues that moving beyond metaphysics requires understanding thinking as semiotic operations within language rather than accessing transcendent Ideas. The author traces metaphysics' power to its elegant description of invisible mental operations through permanent, unchanging forms, yet proposes that [[Generative Anthropology|GA]] offers a superior account: signs become meaningful through iterative operations on a "field of semblances" where subjects and objects reciprocally index one another. The three linguistic modes—[[ostensive]], [[imperative]], and [[declarative]]—model actual cognitive processes, ultimately pointing toward how new declaratives emerge mysteriously from both conscious deliberation and external sources.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Semiosis]]**: Meaningful operation through iterability; the basic process by which signs function and sustain themselves collaboratively
- **[[Declarative]]**: The linguistic form that produces meaning by associating permanent ideas with progressively adequate predicates
- **[[Field of semblances]]**: The space where signs index objects and vice versa, characterized by fluctuating proportions of "signness" and "objectness"
- **[[Originary scene]]**: The constitutive moment where the sign returns to its first signifier, simultaneously acknowledging and forgetting its origin
- **[[Ostensive/Imperative/Declarative articulation]]**: Three reciprocally converting linguistic modes that enact actual cognitive processes
- **[[Originary nihilism]]**: The crisis created by an impossible imperative that demands a new declarative articulation to resolve it

## Notable Passages

"The sign becomes sign through iteration; and yet it must have already been sign if it was to be iterated. This paradox leads one to posit a kind of internal, constitutive iteration."

"Signs, then, represent lines of force and constellations within the field characterized by fluctuating proportions of 'signness' and 'objectness'."

"We must admit appears to us from God knows where at least as much as we produce it ourselves"—on how new declaratives emerge both consciously and mysteriously.

## Questions Raised

1. How precisely does the "internal, constitutive iteration" on the originary scene differ from metaphysical self-presence, and does GA thereby escape or merely relocate the metaphysical problem?

2. If new declaratives emerge "from God knows where," what role does human agency actually play in the production of meaning, and how can this be reconciled with GA's emphasis on conscious articulation?

3. What would constitute adequate proof that semiotic iteration provides a more explanatory account of thinking than metaphysics' permanent Ideas, especially given metaphysics' historical staying power?
