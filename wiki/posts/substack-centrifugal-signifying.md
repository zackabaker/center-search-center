## Summary

This post explores how humans navigate between the originary ritual center and other centers of meaning-making through different modes of signification. Drawing on Gans's distinction between moral freedom (communal reciprocity) and ethical freedom (individual autonomy), the author proposes that we can better account for human turning-away from ritual by thinking in terms of "centrifugal" modes of signifying—particularly through deferred attention and inscription. The text sketches a Derridean hypothesis about language origin grounded in hunting practices and deliberate trace-leaving, suggesting that shared signs emerge not from joint attention to a central object, but from delayed attention to marks left behind.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary scene]]: The anthropological moment of sign-production and equal distribution of the central object, constitutive of human culture
- [[Moral freedom]]: Freedom defined by reciprocal exchange and communal constraint; deferral of appropriative acts
- [[Ethical freedom]]: Individual autonomy to act outside the communal sphere; potential for inequality and economic differentiation
- [[Deferred attention]]: Attention distributed across time rather than co-present; fundamental to tracking and inscription-based signification
- [[Tertiary memory]]: External inscription preserved and shared among group members; technics as human-specific
- [[Inscription]]: Any iterable mark capable of directing attention; Derrida's broader concept extending beyond written language
- [[Threshold of significance]]: The gradual lowering that permits language to spread beyond purely ritual contexts

## Notable Passages

> "Although they are the first acts of creatures freed from the dominance of instinct, they are constituted as free actions only on the participants' internal scene of representation."

> "Rather than joint attention we have delayed attention, rather than mimetic desire and crisis we have a kind of mimetic projection of a possible shared risk."

> "We might be able to tease out of those remarkable pages of Derrida an interesting hypothesis of the origin of language" by understanding inscription as the human-specific tertiary memory.

## Questions Raised

- How precisely does deferred, trace-based signification relate to the [[lowering of the threshold of significance]] that allows language to escape ritual constraint?
- Can we distinguish sharply between mimetic desire (originary crisis) and mimetic projection (shared risk in hunting), or do they exist on a continuum of human signification?
- What makes a deliberately-left trace *interpersonally* binding in a way that simple trail-following might not be—what creates obligation from inscription?
