## Summary

The modern state functions fundamentally as a [[debt]] enforcement and forgiveness agency, originating in England's dual innovations: central bank financing and rotational two-party governance. All citizens exist in originary [[debt]] to the [[center]], but modernly this debt is valued through expectations of future earnings, making inflation and asset depreciation tools of governance itself. Political rotation and policy disputes are constrained by maintaining the financial spread determined by creditor classes, while [[differential accumulation]] continuously reshuffles the [[nomos]] (originary distribution) and determines which groups face fierce debt enforcement versus forgiveness protocols.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Debt to the Center]]**: The originary human obligation to the center, modernized by valuation through future earnings expectations
- **[[Rotational Occupancy]]**: Two-party system ensuring the replacement of center occupants becomes routinized rather than revolutionary
- **[[Outside Spread]]**: The financial margin maintained by lender-of-last-resort institutions, determining political viability
- **[[Nomos]]**: The originary distribution of goods and positions, perpetually rearranged by capital's differential accumulation
- **[[Differential Accumulation]]**: Competitive capital reshuffling property forms and risk distribution across social groups
- **[[Enforcement/Forgiveness Protocols]]**: Court and policy frameworks determining which groups' debts are collected versus pardoned
- **[[The Stack]]**: The surveillance, mapping, recording, and simulating infrastructure through which political attention operates

## Notable Passages

> "Which side is up in the rotation in central occupancy, then, is determined by the leading sector of capital's competitive needs."
