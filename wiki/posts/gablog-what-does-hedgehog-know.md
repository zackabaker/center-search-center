## Summary

This brief reflection on the ancient aphorism "the fox knows many things, the hedgehog one big thing" positions [[generative anthropology]] as fundamentally hedgehog-like: focused on a single originary question (the origin of language and representation) rather than dispersed factual collection. The hedgehog's knowledge isn't ignorance of detail but rather a foundational principle that organizes all other knowledge into coherent meaning. GA's "one big thing"—the [[deferral of violence through representation]]—provides the cornerstone upon which all human cultural phenomena can be understood.

## Key Concepts

- [[Deferral of violence through representation]] — GA's central insight: language and symbolic culture emerge from the need to prevent mimetic conflict through mediation
- [[Originary thinking]] — the hedgehog's focus on the origin question, which grounds all subsequent understanding
- [[Foundational principle]] — a cornerstone of meaning that organizes dispersed facts into coherence
- [[Mimetic crisis]] — the implicit threat that representation defers (referenced obliquely)
- [[Anthropoetics]] — the study of human origin through language and culture
- [[Fox versus Hedgehog distinction]] — epistemological strategy: detail-gathering versus principled coherence

## Notable Passages

> "The hedgehog is not necessarily a 'big picture' thinker, but he knows one thing for sure, he stays with that one thing, and he builds on it slowly to construct something more lasting."

> "The hedgehog knows everything the fox knows, but he puts a foundation or cornerstone under it, so that it all coheres into a meaningful whole."

> "The importance of asking that question is, in one sense, what hedgehog knows."
