## Summary

This essay critiques the role of "principles" in conservative and progressive politics, arguing that principles function not as genuine guides to action but as ideological cover for elite competition over sovereignty. The author traces how principles—whether constitutional, free-market, or democratic—operate at several removes from actual power dynamics, yet remain crucial as indices of shifting modes of sovereignty. Rather than blueprint policy, principles reveal what kind of sovereign imaginary a given elite is attempting to impose, rendering them both essential and fundamentally misleading.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Sovereignty]]**: The imagined mode of governance a principle presupposes; what elites truly compete over
- **[[Principles]]**: Ideological formulations that index shifts in power while obscuring the real mechanisms of elite competition
- **[[Elite action]]**: The actual ground-level operations of power, distinct from the principled rhetoric that justifies them
- **[[Declarative culture]]**: The ideological articulations through which sovereignty gets imagined and communicated
- **[[Exception]]**: The gap between principle and practice; what reveals principles are post-hoc rationalizations
- **[[Anarchy]]**: The implicit fantasy underlying liberal sovereignty—unrestrained discourse producing truth and consensus

## Notable Passages

> "Country trumps Constitution. We all know by now that 'principles,' left or right, need to go through the grinder of lobbyists, donors, trade-offs with specific representatives... before producing a result—a result which will look little like the 'principle' it started with."

> "The elites don't work according to principles, even if they may believe in them—the elites, we can assume, want some freedom of action not afforded them under current conditions."

> "Principles are located at several removes from where the real action is, but they are still not quite merely 'superstructural'—they are readings and indices of shifts in sovereignty."

## Questions Raised

1. If principles merely index shifts in sovereignty rather than guide it, how do we distinguish between sincere ideological commitment and cynical manipulation by elites—or is that distinction itself misleading?

2. Can a political movement or society function without appeal to principles, or does the [[declarative]] nature of human culture make some ideological articulation structurally necessary?

3. How might we analytically access the "real action" beneath principles without simply inverting to crude materialist determinism?
