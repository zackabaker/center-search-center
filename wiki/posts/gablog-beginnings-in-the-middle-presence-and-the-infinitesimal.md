## Summary

This post explores how [[presence]] differs from transcendence by grounding attention in mutual, continuous attending rather than external sustenance. Gans argues that scenes require felt deficiency to persist, and that scenes are constituted through iterating the [[sign]] that frames a [[mimetic crisis]]. The central puzzle is how we transition from one scene ending to another beginning—requiring a [[disciplinary scene]] that identifies the boundary between them. The text suggests that understanding historical events (like the Holocaust) demands clarity about when scenes begin and end, and whether their constitutive signs remain active or have been superseded.

## Key Concepts

- [[Presence]]: Mutual attending to a shared object, where each participant's attention reshapes the others' in continuous circulation
- [[Scenicity]]: The absolute human need to occupy a scene jointly; absence from scene means existential non-being
- [[Sign iteration]]: The repetitive articulation of the sign that constitutes a scene—fulfilled orders, defied orders, redirected attention, irony all iterate the same sign
- [[Mimetic crisis]]: The actual or anticipated rivalry that a scene's sign frames and defers
- [[Disciplinary scene]]: A meta-scene that identifies the boundary between two scenes, thereby constituting the transition itself
- [[Scene texture]]: The articulation between sign and object achieved through cross-referencing all scene elements to one another
- [[Shared sign]]: The moment when a scene closes, signaling the restart of mimetic desire and rivalry in a new scene

## Notable Passages

> "If we are not on a scene we are nowhere. This absolute need for scenicity accounts for the ecstasy of the mystic and the teenager driven by boredom to do just about anything."

> "We must posit a third scene, a disciplinary scene constituted so as to identify the boundary between the two scenes; to identify the boundary is also to identify the transition from one to the other."

> "Or, we can say that in a sense it hasn't ended, and that the sign that emerged in its wake is still active, still tenuous, and has not given way to a new one."

## Questions Raised

1. If scene boundaries are constituted *retroactively* from within a new disciplinary scene, how do we avoid infinite regress—doesn't identifying a boundary require yet another scene to stand outside the previous two?

2. How does the framework distinguish between a sign that remains "tenuous and active" (unfinished scene) versus one that has genuinely been superseded? What empirical criteria apply?

3. If [[mimetic crisis]] is what all scenes frame, does the framework risk circularity—are we not always already inside some scene, making the originary crisis permanently inaccessible?
