## Summary

This essay applies Eric Gans's [[originary hypothesis]] to explain contemporary anti-Semitism as rooted in the paradox of Jewish monotheism and its relationship to the [[market system]]. The Jew, according to Gans, serves as a model of the market's unfigurable center—a paradoxical figure who both regulates and embodies mediation itself. Katz argues that anti-Semitism and Islamophobia are competing victimary narratives, and that the survival of anti-Semitism as a concept depends on its capacity to name something real about civilization's structural tensions rather than merely serve as a vehicle for resentment.

## Key Concepts

- [[originary hypothesis]] — The founding event in which a mimetic crisis is arrested by an aborted gesture, inaugurating representation and deferral of violence
- [[market system]] — The social structure best realizing reciprocity from the originary scene; history as liberation toward universal exchange
- [[scapegoating]] — Elimination of a figure embodying structural paradox to gain illusory control over an inhuman mechanism
- [[mimetic desire]] — Rivalry and convergence upon a common object that threatens social cohesion
- [[the center]] — The unfigurable locus of market mediation that cannot be directly incarnate or controlled
- [[monotheism]] — The Judaic revelation presenting a single God and single humanity, yet granted to a chosen people
- [[victimary era]] — The contemporary moment in which competing narratives of victimhood organize political identity

## Notable Passages

> "The Jew is not in some undefined sense a scapegoat for the larger society's frustrations. He serves as a model of the inexistent and unfigurable center of the market system."

> "In the postritual world of market exchange, the Jew is a paradoxical construction who regulates the self-regulating market, who fixes the prices determined by the interaction of supply and demand."

> "There are now two terms in play, each seeking to name the source of global violence: 'anti-semitism' and 'Islamophobia.' One will disappear or be significantly marginalized."
