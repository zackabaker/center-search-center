## Summary

Bio-politics, following Foucault, describes governance that intervenes in population production and health as sources of wealth rather than merely normalizing subjects through institutions. The post traces Foucault's sympathetic examination of liberal and neoliberal thought, where government increasingly operates through indirect "nudges" on choice architecture rather than explicit command. The author extends the concept beyond health management to include electoral stability and demographic sorting, suggesting bio-politics encompasses all attempts to manage populations as productive and predictable units.

## Key Concepts

- [[Bio-politics]]: Governance focused on intervening in population health and production from birth onward as sources of state wealth
- [[Disciplinary society]]: Earlier mode of social control through institutional normalization, now superseded by bio-political management
- [[Market self]]: The conception of the individual as infinitely complicated market actor whose choices must be optimized
- [[Choice architecture]]: Indirect governmental intervention through structuring options rather than explicit prohibition or command
- [[Nudges]]: Foucauldian interventions that influence decisions without appearing coercive, operating on incentives
- [[Security as health]]: Inversion where traditional state security concerns (defense) become subordinate to population health management
- [[Population stabilization]]: Electoral and demographic sorting that renders populations predictable and governable
