## Summary

This post argues that all human action and meaning-making operates through [[utterance|utterances]] and [[signs]], which gain power through their capacity to make other utterances maximally addressable. The author contends that ethical decision-making involves recognizing oneself as an addressee of everything one perceives, then choosing what kind of addressee to become. The [[imperative of the center]] emerges as a discovery procedure: to act in ways that spread meaning across the semiotic field and enable others to assume similar roles. Moral action scales from immediate situations (intervening in a mugging) to broader civilizational concerns (which utterances to preserve for posterity).

## Key Concepts

- **[[Addressability]]** — The capacity of an utterance to create and implicate addressees; the measure of an utterance's power to generate further meaningful utterances
- **[[Utterance]]** — The fundamental unit of meaning-making; all human action only achieves significance insofar as it constitutes or generates signs
- **[[Addressee]]** — One who is implicated by an utterance; addresseehood is not passive but involves choosing what kind of respondent to be
- **[[Imperative of the center]]** — The ethical obligation to act in ways that maximize addressability and spread transformative meaning through the semiotic field
- **[[Declarative space]]** — The disciplinary terrain constituted by utterances that address potential rather than immediate presence
- **[[Semiotic field]]** — The totality of signs, utterances, and their possibilities; the space within which meaning circulates and transforms
- **[[Maximal addressability]]** — The ideal utterance or action that simultaneously addresses and enables multiple addressees across time and institutional contexts

## Notable Passages

"Everything is samples, signs, or utterances—even the most vigorous action in the sense of physical action in the real world involving lots of people only makes sense, has effects, is recorded, remembered and institutionalized insofar as it takes the form of signs and generates other signs."

"You're addressed by everything you see and hear—it's just a question of what kind of addressee you're going to be."

"The search for maximal addressability is the discovery procedure for eliciting the imperative of the center."

## Questions Raised

1. How do we distinguish between actions that genuinely maximize addressability versus those that merely *appear* to do so—especially when the full semiotic effects only manifest across generations?

2. If meaning is "always the articulation of a hypothesis," can we develop heuristics for choosing among competing hypotheses about how an utterance will propagate through the field?

3. Does the imperative toward maximal addressability ever conflict with other ethical demands (loyalty, privacy, immediacy)? How does [[Center Study]] adjudicate such tensions?
