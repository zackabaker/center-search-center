## Summary

This post examines the paradoxical alliance between the State and Big Business that both the far Left and Libertarian Right oppose, though for opposite reasons. The author argues that Marx's explanation—the state as "general committee of the ruling class"—is more coherent than libertarian conspiracy theories. By conceptualizing entrepreneurs as a "peaceful predatory class," the author suggests that the state-business alliance is structurally inevitable under civilization: the [[Big Man]] function, necessary for moving beyond egalitarian tribes, perpetually regenerates centers of power that cannot be abolished without abandoning civilization itself.

## Key Concepts

- [[Big Man]]: The centralized figure whose predatory capacity, redirected toward external defense, enables civilization and resource concentration
- [[Predation]]: Not critique but descriptive term for the power dynamics inherent in all hierarchical organizing
- [[State]]: The mechanism that stabilizes the space within which peaceful trade and market activity occur
- [[Division of labor]]: The economic foundation that creates mutual dependence and generates marketplace activity
- [[Market system]]: Distinguished from simple marketplace exchange; involves capital movement without "instinct of workmanship"
- [[Center of gravity]]: The inevitable emergence of dominance in any group, preventing true egalitarianism despite egalitarian ideals
- [[Sovereignty]]: The power to create and maintain the stable order within which economic activity functions

## Notable Passages

"Without the Big Man, there would have been no way of centralizing resources needed to move humanity beyond the level of egalitarian hunter-gatherer tribes; civilization itself is predicated upon turning this predatory figure away from preying upon the weak."

"For all our egalitarianism...there is almost never a situation involving a group of people of any size that doesn't generate a center of gravity—someone dominates the conversation."

"The difference is between a stable division of labor and one that is in continual upheaval."

## Questions Raised

1. If the [[Big Man]] function is permanently necessary to civilization, what distinguishes legitimate [[sovereignty]] from mere predatory domination?

2. Can the "peaceful" character of market predation be sustained indefinitely, or does continual upheaval in the [[division of labor]] eventually destabilize the state's legitimacy?

3. How does the inevitability of [[centers of gravity]] in human groups challenge both libertarian and progressive utopias differently?
