## Summary

This essay challenges the liberal narrative that separate spheres of culture, religion, and politics naturally emerge from progressive ideas. Instead, it argues that liberalism deliberately fragments these domains as a power strategy—dissolving mediating institutions to expose individuals directly to state control. Drawing on [[generative anthropology]], the author proposes that human society originates around a [[sacred center]] where politics, art, religion, and ethics are fundamentally unified. Modern reactionary thought must recover this primordial integration, recognizing that [[central power]] naturally concentrates as a community's "Big Man" replaces the sacred animal at the center.

## Key Concepts

- [[Sacred center]] – The originary focal point around which human community organizes, initially an animal object, later a human authority figure
- [[Generative anthropology]] – The theoretical framework treating humanity's founding in the deferral of violence through the sign
- [[Central power]] – Authority that transcends egalitarian reciprocity by controlling distribution for the whole community
- [[Dedifferentiation]] – The false modern separation of culture, religion, art, and politics into autonomous domains
- [[State power]] – The liberal project to eliminate mediating institutions and expose bare individuals to centralized control
- [[Big Man]] – The figure who accumulates wealth, trust, and obligation, occupying the sacred center
- [[Ritual mediation]] – The thick articulation of worship, doctrine, and ecclesiastical authority before liberal "thinning"

## Notable Passages

> "The very ideas of freedom of religion and free speech, while apparently protecting individuals from the state, in fact ensure the domestication of what gets marked as 'religion' and 'speech.'"

> "The sacred, the invisible, the ethical, the moral, and the political are all bound up together in more primordial categories. For the originary hypothesis, this more primordial category is the sacred center."

> "Reactionary absolutism must rearticulate [the bundled forms of human life] in terms of the sacred center."

## Questions Raised

1. If the liberal separation of spheres is fundamentally a power strategy rather than an enlightenment, how do we distinguish between legitimate [[central authority]] and the totalizing state apparatus it describes?

2. Can the [[sacred center]] be consciously recovered or reinstated, or does it require organic emergence from community practices—and what role does intellectual articulation play in either case?

3. How does the transition from animal to human [[Big Man]] at the sacred center affect the mechanisms of deferral and sign-mediation that prevent social self-immolation?
