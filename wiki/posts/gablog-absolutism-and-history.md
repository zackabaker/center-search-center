## Summary

The text traces how modern history begins when elites weaponize the absolutist monarch's center-power logic against monarchy itself, replacing divinely-bestowed authority with claimed equidistance of all subjects. This transition introduces the public-private distinction, which fundamentally conceals power by declaring the private sphere "powerless" while actual influence operates invisibly through unnamed delegations. Restarting the absolutist project requires properly naming these dissimulated powers and identifying historical actors who resisted the corruption of names, establishing a political ontology where formality and informality are rigorously distinguished and traced.

## Key Concepts

- [[Absolutism]]: Consolidation of power by reducing all subjects to equidistance from a central authority
- [[Center]] / [[Centering]]: The organizing principle from which power radiates; the question of who occupies and legitimizes it
- [[Power and dissimulation]]: The modern concealment of actual power through formal distinctions (public/private, state/citizen)
- [[Naming]]: Attaching proper names to powers; the corruption of names enables informal power to function invisibly
- [[Equidistance]]: The claimed equality of all subjects relative to a center—either granted by monarch or self-determined
- [[Delegated power]]: Informal authority exercised through institutions (press, foundations) that cannot function if openly acknowledged
- [[High-low vs. middle logic]]: The structural relationship that allows elites to destabilize hierarchy by appealing to lower against middle

## Notable Passages

> "To be a private entity is to be officially bereft of any formal power, and hence free of responsibility for the power one exercises."

> "An absolutist history identifies the dilution and then dissimulation of names for power, along with seeking out the actions and accounts of those who, in the midst of the corruption of names, sought to reattach them to their proper objects."

> "If theNew York Times admitted that [it was the press agency of the Obama Administration], much less if the Obama Administration had officially delegated such duties to them, they would have been completely unable to fulfill them, and hence disempowered."

## Questions Raised

1. If all informal power depends on remaining unnamed, is a fully transparent political order theoretically possible, or does sovereignty require some degree of obscurity to function?

2. How do we distinguish between legitimate historical actors who "reattach names to proper objects" and those who merely deploy naming strategically for their own power consolidation?

3. Does anti-discrimination law's erosion of the public-private distinction represent an unconscious move toward re-absolutization, or does it reveal something else about how modern power actually operates?
