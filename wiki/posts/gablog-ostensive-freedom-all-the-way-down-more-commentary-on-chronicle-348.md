## Summary

This essay traces the philosophical and social stakes of Western metaphysics' occlusion of the [[ostensive]] in favor of the [[declarative]]. The author argues that while metaphysics may have been necessary to distance post-ritual order from the charged nature of the ostensive, we must now ask whether dismantling this metaphysical order risks social danger—or whether [[liberal democracy]] itself represents the apotheosis of declarative freedom, making such critique moot. The piece reconstructs Eric Gans's account of how the ostensive, imperative, and declarative forms emerged sequentially through the deferral of [[mimetic crisis]], showing how naming practices evolved from sacred ritual to routine coordination.

## Key Concepts

- [[Ostensive]] – The mode of signification that names and thereby constitutes a central object as sacred Being; the primordial linguistic gesture
- [[Declarative]] – The sentence form that refers to absence and makes predications; the basis of metaphysics and metaphysical thought
- [[Imperative]] – The command form; emerges as inappropriate ostensives (names used in absence) come to demand production of objects
- [[Mimetic crisis]] – Moments of potential violent conflict over the same object; the motive for all three linguistic forms
- [[Metaphysics]] – The post-ritual mode of thought that privileges the declarative and occludes the ostensive origin
- [[Negative ostensive]] – The deferral gesture of repeating an object's name when the imperative cannot be fulfilled; germ of the declarative
- [[Sacred Being]] – The ontological status conferred upon objects through ostensive naming in communal ritual
- [[Liberal democracy]] – A system that constitutes ruling force through declarative freedom; the apotheosis of metaphysics

## Notable Passages

> "The ostensive names the central object and thereby constitutes it as sacred Being. Once one object has been thus designated and thereby preserved as the ritual center of the emergent community, the same mode of signification can be applied to the naming of other objects."

> "If declarative freedom, our freedom to determine together the nature of the imperatives by which we will be bound, is constitutive of liberal democracy, then isn't liberal democracy the apotheosis of metaphysics?"

> "The negative ostensive becomes the germ of the declarative, since it 'refers' to the absence of the object."

## Questions Raised

1. If metaphysics was necessary to contain the charge of the ostensive after the crisis of arbitration emerged, what social mechanisms would replace this containment if we successfully deconstructed metaphysical order?

2. Can liberal democracy genuinely be critiqued from within the declarative order it privileges, or does such critique remain "esoteric and ineffectual"?

3. What would a politics that consciously honors the ostensive's primacy—rather than occluding it—actually look like in practice?
