## Summary

This essay attempts to synthesize discourse on [[media]] and [[technics]] by grounding both in the [[originary scene]] and the [[imperative]]. The author argues that media constitute the scenic arrangements that enable signs to be iterable and readable, while technics serve the perfection of [[practice]] through successive generations. By abolishing the distinction between media and technology within [[scenic design practices]], the text proposes that all cultural transmission aims at ensuring practices continue in ways consistent with their originary constitution—a process extending indefinitely toward perfecting the [[center]]'s intelligence.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary scene]]: The primordial moment of human signification where aborted appropriation becomes gesture
- [[Media]]: The scenic arrangement of positioning, posture, props, and spatial relationships that enables signs to be received and iterated
- [[Technics]]: The imperative-driven perfection of practice across generations, originally directed at conforming ritual scenes to central demands
- [[Imperative]]: The relay of demands from the center that drives both media construction and technical innovation
- [[Scenic design practices]]: The unified framework encompassing media and technics as arrangements generating distributable scenes
- [[Practice]]: The singularized, successive actions constituted by originary principles and transmitted to successor practitioners
- [[Center]]: The sacred locus whose relation structures all scenic meaning; once "broken," enables unlimited scene distribution

## Notable Passages

> "The perfection of the practice of the user is manifested in the provision of the successor practices to your own... making that constitutive of your practices."

> "The media is nothing but the means of generating scenes, and once the relation to the sacred center is broken, the possibility of generating... an unlimited succession and distribution of scenes coincides with the turning of technics."

> "Scenic design practices" unifies the discourse once we recognize that technics are first directed towards media—toward "bringing the entire ritual scene into greater conformity with the increasing complexity of demands taken from the center."

## Questions Raised
