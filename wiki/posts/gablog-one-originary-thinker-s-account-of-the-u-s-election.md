## Summary

This essay applies generative anthropology to the 2008 U.S. election, analyzing Obama's victory as the successful scapegoating of Bush—a symbolic sacrifice that deferred rather than resolved underlying [[mimetic crisis]]. The author argues that Obama's vapid oratory gains power precisely as a photographic negative of Bush stereotypes, allowing the Left to project all goodness onto him while depositing White Guilt onto his predecessor. The critical question becomes whether symbolic scapegoating through democratic procedures can actually resolve mimetic tensions, or whether it merely stages catharsis while the underlying resentments remain intact.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Scapegoating]]**: The mechanism by which a community singles out a figure to bear collective blame, achieving temporary mimetic resolution through sacrifice.
- **[[Mimetic crisis]]**: A state of escalating rivalry and undifferentiation where competing claims cannot be adjudicated; scapegoating traditionally resolves it.
- **[[Victimary]]**: The ideological structure in which moral authority derives from victimhood claims; the "victimary gods" demand perpetual acknowledgment of suffering.
- **[[White Guilt]]**: A deposited resentment transferred onto Bush's figure, which Obama's persona allows the Left to symbolically cleanse.
- **[[Center]]**: The structural position from which genuine authority operates—distinct from being the idol of resentful parasites.
- **[[Liberal democracy]]**: A system that provides "simulations of scapegoating events" to prevent actual violence, yet risks becoming empty ritual.

## Notable Passages

> "The power of Obama's otherwise vapid oratory might be explicable on these terms: from a time of division to a time of unity, from antagonizing the world to inspiring it, from the old (white) guard to the new inclusive order."

> "Scapegoating has no epistemological value–it offers no help in providing accounts of reality and preparing for future events."

> "It will perhaps be up to Obama whether those terms remain unchanged...he would have to stand in between the Democratic majority and one or more of their interest groups as they grab for more than their 'fair' share."
