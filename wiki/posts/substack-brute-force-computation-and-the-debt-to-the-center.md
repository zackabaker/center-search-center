## Summary

The author argues that sufficient computational power will collapse the distinction between correlation and causation, enabling algorithmic governance that operates not through centralized planning but through continuous data aggregation and simulation. Drawing on Hayek's critique of communist planning, he reframes "the center" not as a traditional command structure but as whatever informational space sits most upstream in data flows. The piece then traces how neoliberalism transformed assets into data through financialization, framing this shift as a counter-revolution against post-war institutional socialism and colonial liberation movements.

## Key Concepts

- [[Center]] — the informational space furthest upstream of data flows; determined retroactively by which analytical site performs best
- [[Data conversion]] — the neoliberal reduction of assets, labor, and value to quantifiable, vendible information
- [[Brute force computation]] — overwhelming correlational analysis replacing hypothesis-driven experimentation and causal reasoning
- [[Tacit knowledge]] — Hayekian insight that on-the-ground decision-making produces irreducible authority that cannot be "uploaded"
- [[Neoliberalism]] — a counter-revolutionary political-economic regime against institutional communism, union power, and decolonization
- [[Algorithmic governance]] — authority exercised through continuous filtering, mapping, and simulating of aggregated data rather than centralized command
- [[Debt to the center]] — the structural obligation to the informational infrastructure that captures and processes one's activities

## Notable Passages

> "Every point of decision making is irreducible and the authority needed to make each decision is to be respected. But the center need not be thought of as a planning center; rather, it is the result of continual sifting, filtering, testing, mapping, simulating performed on data."

> "The very possibility of thinking economics, or the debt to the center, in terms of continually reducible and vendible assets is a result of the neoliberal political economic transformation."

> "Every act every one of us carries out produces data that goes into the system along with taking data out—the center is whatever will turn out to have been furthest upstream of that."

## Questions Raised

1. If the center is determined retroactively by whatever informational apparatus performs best, how can we distinguish genuine efficiency from captured or corrupted metrics that serve narrow interests?

2. Does the author's account leave room for resistance or alternative centers, or does the logic of data aggregation inevitably consolidate power upstream?

3. How does the conversion of tacit, embodied knowledge into data fundamentally alter the nature of authority and judgment itself—and is this transformation reversible?
