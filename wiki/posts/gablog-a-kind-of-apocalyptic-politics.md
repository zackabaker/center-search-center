## Summary

This piece proposes "apocalyptic politics"—an ethics of continuous revelation aimed at exposing the [[center]] that all acts of naming both conceal and draw power from. Rather than dismissing lies as mere falsehood, the author argues that noble lies defer conflicts by pointing to absent centers that must eventually be embodied truthfully or they generate long-term problems. Language itself is fundamentally performative, not propositional: meaningful disclosure requires vulnerability and exposure to the other's potential violence. The text suggests that [[deferral and discipline]] beyond what observers can achieve generates [[charisma]], and that recognizing such ascension allows one to model higher self-discipline rather than follow shortcuts.

## Key Concepts

- [[Apocalyptic politics]]: Continuous revelation of the center concealed within every act of naming and power.
- [[Center]]: The concealed object or deferral that any utterance both discloses and draws authority from.
- [[Deferral and discipline]]: The restraint and self-mastery that creates meaningful gesture and generates charisma.
- [[Charisma]]: The magnetic power of displaying deferral beyond the observer's capacity, oscillating between exploitation and modeling.
- [[Disclosure and concealment]]: The simultaneous revealing and hiding inherent in all language and practice.
- [[Victimary politics]]: A politics based on grievance that apocalyptic politics proposes to replace.
- [[Originary scene]]: The first act of language where the gesture's meaning depends on the speaker's vulnerability and deferral.

## Notable Passages

> "Reveal what? The only thing worth revealing: the center. Not some object, but whatever some act of naming conceals while disclosing and drawing power from."

> "The odds are not so different for each new act of disclosure, and correct and accurate uses of the sign after the danger has passed will always be suspect."

> "As soon as I experience an ascension to a level of self-discipline...I want to propose the mode of self-discipline as a model by pointing out that the short-cut is really...a dead end."

## Questions Raised

1. How does one distinguish between noble lies that eventually become true through embodiment and those that merely calcify into self-serving orthodoxy?

2. If all disclosure simultaneously conceals, what practical criteria could guide an apocalyptic politics in choosing what to reveal at what moment?

3. Can the mimetic modeling of charisma occur at scale in mass democracy, or does it require face-to-face proximity to the displayed deferral?
