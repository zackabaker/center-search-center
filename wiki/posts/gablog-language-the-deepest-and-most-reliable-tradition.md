## Summary

Language exemplifies [[tacit knowledge]]—we know far more than we can articulate. While each language frames reality distinctly through culturally-embedded semantic domains (Wierzbicka's analysis of English empiricism), all human languages share a minimalist core of ~200 "semantic primes" that may represent linguistic sediment rather than a pre-linguistic universal. Language could only have originated through the [[ostensive sign]]—a public renunciation of unilateral appropriation—making translation and cross-cultural understanding perpetually complex yet possible through the [[natural semantic metalanguage]].

## Key Concepts

- [[Ostensive sign]]: The originary linguistic gesture pointing to a desired object while renouncing direct seizure; untranslatable yet iterable across all participants
- [[Natural semantic metalanguage]]: ~200 minimal words (say, think, see, happen) common to all languages, likely worn down through historical usage rather than pre-linguistic universals
- [[Tacit knowledge]]: Polanyi's insight that linguistic competence vastly exceeds explicit articulable knowledge
- [[Semantic primes]]: Core universal concepts underlying linguistic diversity, stripped of ritual residue
- [[Linguistic frames]]: How each language distinctly constructs reality through culturally-specific semantic domains
- [[Empiricist revolution]]: Locke-derived Anglo concepts (experience, fair, sense) exported globally through English hegemony

## Notable Passages

"We know more than we can say"—Polanyi's formulation of how language contains ages of thought compressed into tacit competence.

"Language is inconceivable without" a word for God/sacred; their absence from the NSM proves they remain embedded in ritual, never bleached into universal equivalence.

Language "could only have originated as an ostensive sign, pointing to a desired object in order to renounce direct, unilateral appropriation."
