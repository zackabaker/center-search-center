## Summary

This essay traces the genealogy of "ideology" from Enlightenment reason-liberation through Marxist alienation theory to contemporary identity-based critiques, arguing that the entire problem has been misdirected. The author contends that ideological "mystification" does not reflect confused minds but rather genuinely **divided and unsecure [[power]]** at the [[center]]. When sovereignty is ambiguous, people rationally attempt to identify the true ruler by mapping their [[originary scene|originary moral model]] onto visible configurations of authority—a process that inevitably produces competing interpretations, not false consciousness.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Center]]** – the locus of [[sovereignty]] and legitimate [[power]]; when its occupancy is unclear, subjects necessarily disagree about who truly rules
- **[[Originary scene]]** – the moral model underlying social organization; individuals map this onto perceived power configurations differently
- **[[Ideology]]** – traditionally understood as false consciousness, but reconceived here as rational navigation of genuinely obscure [[power]] relations
- **[[Sovereignty]]** – the ultimate locus of authority; [[absolutist theory of sovereignty|absolutist theory]] enables diagnosis of why ideology-critique has failed
- **[[Power]] divisions** – the fragmentation of authority that makes identifying responsible parties genuinely difficult, not merely a matter of mystification
- **False consciousness** – a misnomer; what appears as confusion actually reflects real uncertainty about where [[power]] lies

## Notable Passages

> "If it is in reality unclear who rules, who occupies the center, all members of society have no choice but to do their best to identify the real ruler."

> "The confusions theories of ideology denounce are real, but they are confusions over who rules; moreover, these confusions do not bespeak confused minds but, rather, divided and unsecure power."

> "We are way past arguments over reasonable differences regarding the fitness of candidates and the justice or effectiveness of public policies—there is no one to persuade, only enemies to destroy and potential allies to be liberated from their false consciousness."

## Questions Raised

1. If ideology-critique universally fails because people are rationally mapping moral models onto genuinely ambiguous [[power]] configurations, what would count as successful political communication—and is it even possible under conditions of [[sovereignty|sovereign]] fragmentation?

2. How might an [[absolutist theory of sovereignty|absolutist framework]] actually resolve the "problem that has never been adequately formulated"—what does it reveal about [[power]] that previous theories miss?

3. Does the shift from "false consciousness" to "rational navigation of obscure authority" dissolve the activist's mandate for liberation, or does it simply redirect it toward clarifying the [[center]]?
