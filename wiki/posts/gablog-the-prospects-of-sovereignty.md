## Summary

This essay argues that the 2016 election crystallized a fundamental question of [[sovereignty]]: whether America will remain a sovereign nation or be deliberately undermined by hostile elites through mass immigration, global economic integration, and anti-national cultural politics. The author contends that Trump's campaign revealed the depth of elite hostility to national sovereignty and established a preliminary template for what genuine restoration would require—including heroes willing to sacrifice everything, border control, and comprehensive auditing of immigration and naturalization. The restoration of sovereignty demands rigorous institutional and political measures to reclaim decision-making authority from a [[victimocracy|victimocratic]] establishment.

## Key Concepts

- [[Sovereignty]]: The legitimate power of a nation to govern itself and control its borders
- [[Victimocracy]]: A system where elite power masquerades as moral progress by victimizing the majority
- [[Declarative culture]]: Culture imposed through elite declaration rather than emerging organically from the people
- [[Absolutist restoration]]: The replacement of liberal democratic structures with direct executive authority
- [[Center]]: The originary scene of collective decision-making, now captured by hostile elites
- [[Hero]]: One who risks personal destruction to defend the common good and sovereignty

## Notable Passages

> "Trump's campaign should provide us with a preliminary template of the rigors, dispositions, and, of course, political decisions, that would be required in restoring sovereignty."

> "We have a man risking everything to save his country. When was the last time we could say that about a candidate for high public office in the US?"

> "The post-1965 mass immigration has been one of the most hostile acts by a ruling elite against the people it rules in recent history."

## Questions Raised

1. What institutional forms would genuine sovereignty require, beyond electoral politics, to resist capture by hostile elites?
2. How can the [[center]] be restored as a site of authentic collective decision-making rather than elite declarative imposition?
3. If sovereignty demands "heroes" willing to sacrifice everything, what social conditions make such figures possible or impossible?
