## Summary

This post argues that left-wing rule has become the default state in advanced market societies, while conservative rule emerges only during genuine crises requiring decisive gestures. Using the [[originary scene]] as a model, the author suggests that the critical moment of intervention occupies perhaps 1% of political time, yet conservatives must be perpetually positioned to supply the necessary gesture when crisis arrives. Rather than seeking permanent power, responsible conservative politics should focus on preserving and clarifying the normative materials that enable crisis response, while recognizing that left-wing governance offers a comprehensive sparagmos of benefits that naturally dominates ordinary political life.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Originary scene]]**: The founding moment of human culture; here used as a model for understanding political crisis and the emergence of binding gestures
- **[[Victimary logic]]**: The left's strategy of identifying marginalized groups and promising comprehensive remedies through redistribution
- **[[The gesture]]**: The decisive normative act that resolves crisis; conservatives' responsibility to emit this when required
- **[[Sparagmos]]**: Collective consumption/satisfaction; the left's promise of universal benefits (healthcare, education, employment, etc.)
- **[[Norms and exceptions]]**: Norms crystallize from exceptional circumstances and fade into ritualized routine; preserving their originary logic requires constant clarification
- **[[Declarative culture]]**: The power to establish binding norms through authoritative speech

## Notable Passages

"A genuinely responsible political party wants to be the party that supplies the gesture when needed." 

"Norms are established in exceptional circumstances...and become normal as the memory of that circumstance becomes ritualized and largely absent from daily life."

"The conservative path back to power will be the same one pursued by Nixon, Reagan, Giuliani and Gingrich: restoring the norms that have been trampled on by leftist experimentation."

## Questions Raised

1. If conservative rule is structurally limited to crisis moments, what prevents conservatives from manufacturing or exaggerating crises to extend their power beyond the 1%?

2. Does the collapse of tax-cut ideology reveal a deeper problem with conservative political imagination, or merely the need for new declarative gestures adapted to post-tax-cut conditions?

3. How do we distinguish between "preserving norms" and reactionary nostalgia—between genuine clarification of originary principles and mere resistance to change?
