## Summary

This essay grapples with the conceptual hygiene of GA itself, arguing that even minimal theoretical frameworks can harbor indigestible concepts. The author questions whether [[resentment]]—central to GA's understanding of the [[originary scene]]—can be reliably identified in practice or remains locked in the subject's interiority. Drawing on Wierzbicka's demand for universal intelligibility through semantic primes, the piece asks whether [[resentment]] as an operative concept can withstand rigorous scrutiny, or whether its identification always depends on the interpreter's own hermeneutic position and emotional stance.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Resentment]]**: The emotion or attitude toward whoever denies one's desire; originarily directed at the [[center]], which withholds itself and becomes more desirable through that withholding.
- **[[Originary Scene]]**: The founding moment where the [[center]] saves and creates the [[community]] through sacred mediation, establishing the primordial structure of desire.
- **[[Center]]**: The sacred locus toward which all desire is ultimately directed; the source of all authority and denial, real or perceived.
- **[[Desire]]**: The fundamental drive to be recognized by or possess the center, later structured into proper "allotments" within the community.
- **[[Love]]**: The transcendent counterforce to resentment, though its definition and boundaries remain as elusive as resentment's markers.
- **[[Semantic Primes]]**: Wierzbicka's minimal vocabulary of universally intelligible concepts through which any theory should be articulable.

## Notable Passages

> "Resentment is the emotion (?) or attitude (?) one has towards whomever denies you your desire. On the originary scene, this is the sacred center, which 'withholds' itself from the desiring community, and becomes even more desirable as a result."

> "If we zoom in close, we may see resentment, while if we pull back we see love—in that case, the question is, how do we decide to focus? Presumably out of resentment or love ourselves, which means someone must be reading our reading in turn."

> "None of this necessarily invalidates or vitiates the concept, but it does make its use contingent on what kind of scene that application of the concept helps maintain."

## Questions Raised

1. Can [[resentment]] be identified as a marker in observable action itself, or does its detection always remain parasitic on the interpreter's own emotional and hermeneutic position?

2. If the identification of resentment versus love depends on interpretive distance and framing, does this undermine GA's claim to scientific rigor, or does it simply expose the necessarily scenic nature of all theoretical practice?

3. What would it mean to articulate [[resentment]] and [[desire]] through Wierzbicka's semantic primes, and would such a reduction preserve the concept's explanatory power?
