## Summary

This post argues that all thinking and morality originate in the deferral of violence through [[sign]] production. Following the [[originary scene]] model, every thought emerges as a response to self-threatening violence—whether existential panic, complacency, or intellectual shutdown. Thinking has a "firstness" (originary departure) and "secondness" (mediation through inherited formations), and genuine innovation requires rerouting inherited signs back through their originary structure. The text critically engages contemporary debates about stereotyping and group differences, exposing how anti-stereotyping discourse itself has become predictable and sacrificial.

## Key Concepts

- [[Centered ordinality]]: The graded, staggered model of action where meaning-making defers immanent violence
- [[Originary scene]]: The founding moment where the first sign appears as deferral of collective violence
- [[Sign]]: Anything taken to produce meaning; operates through the threat of what would be lost without it
- [[Firstness of thinking]]: The original, unprecedented moment of thought emerging against self-threat
- [[Secondness of thought]]: The channeling of thinking through inherited linguistic and cultural formations
- [[Sacrificial thinking]]: The mechanism by which scapegoats are identified and excluded to resolve social calamity
- [[Semiotic ecology]]: The external environment of signs that can impose violence through shutdown of thought

## Notable Passages

"If the first sign appeared as a deferral of violence, then every sign appears likewise: not imminent collectively destructive violence... but whatever would count as self-threatening violence for the thinker."

"A sign can only be meaningful insofar as it has previously generated meaning, but it can also only be meaningful if it represents a new beginning."

"Stereotyping is the highest form of sacrificial thinking: if someone needs to be blamed for some social calamity and excluded or made an example of, the stereotype tells you where to look and allows for no appeal."

## Questions Raised

1. If all thinking defers violence, does this make [[sacrificial thinking]] inescapable rather than merely common? Can we ever fully transcend the scapegoating mechanism?

2. How do we distinguish genuine [[firstness]] (original innovation) from merely novel packaging of inherited forms? What markers identify true rerouting to originary structure?

3. If anti-stereotyping discourse has become predictable and sacrificial itself, what would non-sacrificial thinking about group difference look like?
