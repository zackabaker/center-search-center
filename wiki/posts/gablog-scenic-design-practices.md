## Summary

Gans proposes "scenic design practices" as a unified framework encompassing all media and technology—from speech to computing—as means of constituting and redesigning scenes. Drawing on [[anthropomorphics]], he traces technology from the [[originary scene]] through ritual enhancement devices to modern algorithmic systems, arguing that all human practices reshape spatial and social arrangements within constraints. The key insight is that media and technology are inseparable, both participating in "planetary scale computation" while all practices, whether by powerful or powerless actors, redesign scenes with available techno-media.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary scene]]: The first scene, constructed and embodied as humans conform their bodies to an emergent community, foundational to sign emergence
- [[Scenic design]]: All means of constituting and redesigning scenes through available practices and technologies
- [[Anthropomorphics]]: The understanding that all media constitute scenes and technology articulates desacralized human practices
- [[Ritual-mythic nexus]]: The tightly bound system of imperative exchange binding communities until empire disrupted local ritual systems
- [[Ritual enhancing device]]: First tools that materialized the originary scene's memory by marking center/periphery boundaries
- [[Techno-media]]: The unified term for all media and technology as interdependent means of scene constitution

## Notable Passages

> "Every practice is designing a scene; or, really, redesigning a scene, or some portion of a scene, with the techno-media available."

> "The first 'medium,' of course, was the scene constructed and embodied as the originary scene, part and parcel of the emergence of the sign."

> "Nothing can be outside of the ritual-mythic nexus, a very tightly bound up system of imperative exchange, until the emergence of the ancient empires."
