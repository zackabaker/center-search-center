## Summary

This essay argues that the transition of power—succession—has been systematically neglected as the central organizing principle of political theory, despite being the most economical way to distinguish and evaluate regime forms. Drawing on [[generative anthropology]], the author proposes that the present occupant of the [[center]] must choose their successor based on character rather than rule or blood, thereby avoiding [[imperium in imperio]] (sovereignty over sovereignty). This solution generates a cascading set of cultural and pedagogical imperatives that would reshape the entire social order around the process of selecting and training the next ruler, creating a post-sacral, post-liberal political framework.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Center]]** — The position of sovereign power whose occupancy and transition defines regime legitimacy
- **[[Succession]]** — The transfer of central power, proposed as the primary distinguishing feature of political regimes
- **[[Imperium in Imperio]]** — Forbidden "sovereignty over sovereignty"; any independent rule of succession that requires interpretation becomes a competing locus of power
- **[[Post-liberal order]]** — The imagined political conditions beyond contemporary liberal democracy where first-principle thinking becomes possible
- **[[Character selection]]** — The criterion for succession: the ability to subordinate personal and familial interests to the country's welfare
- **[[Occupant of the center]]** — The present sovereign, whose education and character determine the regime's trajectory

## Notable Passages

> "Any regime that could present its form of succession as representing a form of continuity traced back with as little question as possible to the origin of the social order itself would surely be the best possible regime."

> "The question of succession being central, the entire social order would be oriented towards the process."

> "If there is some rule of succession independent of the ruler, then the interpreter of that rule is sovereign."

## Questions Raised

1. How would competitive academies actually identify and filter for the character trait of selflessness across generations without reintroducing hidden hierarchies or new forms of [[imperium in imperio]]?

2. If succession depends entirely on the present occupant's judgment and education, what prevents a corrupted center from selecting a successor in its own interest, and how is that different from dynastic monarchy?

3. What mechanisms ensure continuity with "the origin of the social order itself" in a post-sacral context where foundational myths no longer bind the community?
