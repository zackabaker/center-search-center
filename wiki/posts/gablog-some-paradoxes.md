## Summary

This post traces two interconnected historical paradoxes: desacralization both reveals the sacred as shared presence while enabling its replacement through explicit representation, and anti-tyrannical secularization generates the very tyranny it opposes. The author argues that re-traditionalizing knowledge requires not recovering ritual but uncovering the tacit [[ostensive-imperative]] foundations buried beneath [[hyper-declarative]] culture. Drawing on Jousse, Wierzbicka, and Olson, the text exposes how literacy reifies language into discrete words and grammatical rules, obscuring the formulaic, phrase-based reality of actual speech that oral cultures preserve.

## Key Concepts

- [[Desacralization]]: Historical process revealing sacred as shared presence while enabling its replacement through explicit representation
- [[Declarative culture]]: Hyper-literate order that treats language as objects with fixed definitions, obscuring oral formulaic practice
- [[Ostensive-imperative world]]: The tacit, gestural-linguistic foundation always surrounding hyper-declarative systems
- [[Transfer translation]]: Jousse's term for formulaic correspondence between languages, preserving tradition across linguistic shifts
- [[Metanguage of literacy]]: Olson's concept describing how writing imposes object-like categories (phonemes, words, sentences) onto fluid speech
- [[Originary responsibility]]: The deferred acknowledgment of charges that prevents entrenchment of tyranny
- [[Formulaic language]]: Actual speech as vast array of phrases, commonplaces, and proverbs articulated variably—not discrete words

## Notable Passages

> "Attempts to supplement the vanishing sacred through the disciplines advance secularization while revealing the means to replace the sacred with explicit representations of our sociality."

> "The remedy for desacralization must be a retrieval of traditionally grounded knowledge from the hyper-declarative order that razes traditions to the ground."

> "Language is a vast array of formulas, phrases, commonplaces, and proverbs that can be articulated in various ways with each other."

## Questions Raised

1. If literacy's reification of language into discrete units is irreversible, how can we practically "uncover" the obscured ostensive-imperative foundation without collapsing back into pre-literate forms?

2. How do [[transfer translations]] between languages preserve meaning when the formulaic structures themselves—not just semantic content—carry cultural and ritual weight? What is actually "transferred"?

3. Can contemporary declarative systems acknowledge their own tyrannical tendencies without simultaneously generating new charges of tyranny, or does the paradox require perpetual deferral rather than resolution?
