## Summary

This post articulates the fundamental theoretical divide within Generative Anthropology between liberal and reactionary orientations: whether the modern marketplace is genuinely decentered or necessarily subordinate to centers of power. The author argues that empirical evidence—state expansion rather than withering, selective resentment registration, and the historical inability of markets to exist without coercive territorial protection—favors the reactionary view. Even anarchist libertarian thought, when logically extended, leads back to absolute sovereignty exercised by dominant property holders.

## Key Concepts

- [[Marketplace]] – The economic domain claimed by liberals to be self-governing; reactionaries argue it remains subordinate to power centers
- [[Center]] – The locus of sovereign power; whether it can be absent from a functioning social order
- [[Sovereignty]] – Ultimate control over territory and force; logically required to protect and sustain any market system
- [[Declarative culture]] – An order where imperatives are deferred through speech and law rather than direct command
- [[Resentment]] – Social grievances; the liberal state claims to register all equally, but selectively promotes or criminalizes them
- [[Imperative]] – Direct command; characterizes gift and command economies, deferred in modern declarative orders
- [[Producer's desire vs. consumer satisfaction]] – Competing logics: whether social order serves those who command or those who consume

## Notable Passages

> "If the marketplace were genuinely decentered, we would have expected to see a gradual 'withering away' of the state since the modern market emerged in the 19th century while exactly the opposite has occurred."

> "Has there ever been, could there ever be, a sustained market unprotected by force of arms that makes a particular territory safe for the transport and buying and selling of products?"

> "Absolute sovereignty can be seen as the logical conclusion of anarchist libertarianism, its perfect inversion: just assume all property owned by a single property owner, whoever is actually capable of controlling and defending it, and there you are."
