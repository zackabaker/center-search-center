## Summary

Rather than grounding politics in abstract first principles, this text proposes an anthropological approach centered on the [[sacred center]] as the fundamental political reality. The author traces how human communities have managed the center—from originary scenes of collective deferral, through the [[Big Man]] economy, to [[sacral kingship]], where a human occupies the sacred position and becomes responsible for distribution and cosmic mediation. The text suggests that understanding political evolution requires tracking how power relations reshape sacrificial obligations rather than applying deductive philosophies.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Sacred center]]** — an object of devotion, source of life, fear, and guidance that communities donate their desires and resentments toward
- **[[Sacral kingship]]** — the sacralization of a human ruler who takes on attributes of the sacred center, including potential ritual sacrifice
- **[[Big Man]]** — the first figure to usurp central distribution through gift economy, establishing merit-based leadership prior to kingship
- **[[Mimetic emotions]]** — collective emotional states (outrage, hope, despair, triumph) that bind the community to the center
- **[[Imperio in imperio]]** — hidden power structures that emerge when justification becomes institutionalized, requiring endless contestation
- **[[Deferral]]** — the primordial restraint from violence established at the originary scene, continually re-donated to the center

## Notable Passages

> "Our only question is what the center wants from and for us. We turn to the center in times of despair, doubt, hope and triumph: all mimetic emotions."

> "It would still be unimaginable that there might be no one at the center."

> "Such kingship is still sacred...elaborate ceremonies and exalted offices are created and given the sanction of tradition and divine origin so as to sanctify."

## Questions Raised

1. How does the deferral of sacrificial obligation relate to the emergence of rationalized legal systems—is modern law simply a secularization of sacral restraint, or a genuine break from it?

2. What mechanisms allow modern polities to function without the explicit acknowledgment of a sacred center that sacral kingship provided?

3. Can the transition from sacral to secular power be understood as communities learning to "donate" their mimetic investments to abstract institutions rather than human figures?
