## Summary

This brief piece defends libertarianism against the charge of utopianism by arguing that libertarianism is the most *realistic* political framework—it seeks only to maximize individual freedom and protect property, not to engineer an ideal society. The author distinguishes between libertarianism proper (which is pragmatic) and libertarian foreign policy isolationism (which is genuinely utopian). The critique reveals how [[utopia|utopianism]] is often misattributed to positions that refuse grandiose social engineering.

## Key Concepts

- [[Libertarianism]] – political philosophy prioritizing individual freedom and property rights over utopian social designs
- [[Utopia|Utopianism]] – the project of creating an ideal society through systemic transformation; inherent to modern Western democracies
- [[Sovereignty]] – the capacity to defend against foreign aggression; a minimal but necessary state function
- [[Private property]] – the foundational protection that libertarianism centers, distinct from utopian redistribution schemes
- [[Political order]] – the institutional arrangement that either maximizes freedom or imposes ideological perfection
- [[Realism]] – the refusal to mistake wishes for political reality, particularly regarding international relations

## Notable Passages

> "Libertarianism is the only form of government that isn't utopian. The modern Western democracies, including America's, try to create an ideal society."

> "What's utopian about Ron Paul's proposals is not his libertarianism but his foreign policy isolationism, which is naïve regarding our enemies in the world."

## Questions Raised

1. Does libertarianism's claim to non-utopianism depend on a low enough bar for state function, or does it genuinely avoid the utopian impulse?
2. Can foreign policy realism be reconciled with the libertarian commitment to minimal state power, or is isolationism endemic to the philosophy?
3. What distinguishes realistic minimalism from utopian abdication in the context of genuine security threats?
