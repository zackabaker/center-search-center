## Summary

This post analyzes the alt-right not as an ideology but as a structural response to [[victimary]] politics. The author maps three conservative positions: accepting victimary premises while claiming gradual inclusion, blaming victimary movements for disabling their constituents, and the alt-right's novel move—rejecting substantive equality claims and positioning itself as defending truth against "civilization parasites." The piece suggests the alt-right emerges from the realization that the victimary has monopolized claims to fairness, forcing conservatives to abandon the modern equality framework entirely.

## Key Concepts

- [[Victimary]]: A political movement that leverages formal equality to endlessly denounce existing differences as violations of fundamental principles
- [[Political field]]: The structured space of possible positions generated by foundational political commitments
- [[Substantive equality]]: Claims about actual equal outcomes, contrasted with formal legal equality
- [[Resentment]]: The underlying emotional grammar driving victimary denunciations and claims
- [[Truth]]: The alt-right's counter-claim to civilization and knowledge against victimary ideology
- [[Formal equality]]: The procedural principle required for modern institutions, independent of outcomes
- [[Parasitization]]: The alt-right's characterization of victimary subjects as exploiting civilization rather than competing within it

## Notable Passages

> "Since reality will always generate these and other differences, these denunciations can go on forever (that's what makes it such a neat trick)."

> "Their complaints are nothing more than pleadings on behalf of those who find more advantage in parasitizing upon civilization than competing within and contributing to it."

> "The left can be treated, not as overly idealistic, or even as a racket, but as at war with knowledge, reason, truth and the civilizational discipline required to promote all of the above."

## Questions Raised

1. If the victimary's "neat trick" is inexhaustible (reality always generates differences), what exhausts it—or can it only be transcended rather than refuted?

2. Does positioning oneself as defending "truth" against ideology risk replicating the same claim structure the victimary uses, just inverted?

3. How does one theorize political legitimacy once both the victimary and its alt-right critics have abandoned consensus on what equality means?
