## Summary

This post critiques the secularization narrative by arguing that modernity doesn't escape the sacred but rather replaces one [[sacred]] figure with another. Gans's claim that secular societies fail to rationally assess [[mimetic desire|mimetic dangers]] is "irrefutable," yet the author probes deeper: concrete historical contexts are themselves products of secularization, not alternatives to it. The real process of secularization emerges specifically from Christianity's internal critique of scapegoating, generating successive humanisms (scientific heroes, liberators, oppressed classes) until language itself becomes the [[constitutive figure]], enabling the [[linguistic turn]] and ultimately [[victimary progressivism]].

## Key Concepts

- **[[Sacred and secular]]** — Not a binary but a process; the sacred measures communal danger from [[mimetic desire]], while secularization contextualizes it historically
- **[[Mimetic desire]]** — The dangerous social force that sacred mechanisms and modern systems of signs attempt to contain
- **[[Originary history]]** — The record of humanity's originary gesture, preserved in religious traditions and inaccessible to pure rationality
- **[[Constitutive figure]]** — The human subject who stands as origin and deferral of sacred violence (Christ, then heroes, then victims)
- **[[Linguistic turn]]** — The ideological replacement of humanism by presenting the human as constituted by language rather than agency
- **[[Victimary progressivism]]** — The contemporary phase where degraded others become the measure of moral authority

## Notable Passages

> "The sacred is not a mysterious, otherworldly quality; it measures the human community's sense of the danger posed to it by the [[mimetic desire]] aroused by different phenomena."

> "Modernity is then driven by the replacement of one constitutive human figure...after another—from the elevated (heroic scientists, artists, liberators and philosophers) to degraded (the various class, racial, sexual, and other others of the victimary period)."

> "The only way of doing that is by presenting the human as constituted by something else; language, a self-contained system that couldn't have come before but doesn't in any clear way seem to have come from humans."
