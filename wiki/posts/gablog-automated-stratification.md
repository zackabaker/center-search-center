## Summary

Modern automation is creating an unprecedented form of human stratification based not on traditional privilege but on the ability to manipulate abstract symbols—particularly mathematics and STEM competencies. This symbolic bifurcation severs the "common symbolic link" that once unified social classes through ritual and shared language, enabling a cognitive elite to detach from the broader population in functionally new ways. The text argues that victimary resentment, while misdirected at "white privilege," obscures this deeper inequality rooted in native ability and differential access to nurturing childhoods. Yet moral reciprocity demands we deny such hierarchies even as we recognize them, creating an urgent tension between descriptive reality and prescriptive ethics that GA must address directly.

## Key Concepts

- [[Victimocracy]]: The political deployment of victim narratives that obscures actual structural inequalities
- [[Symbolic function]]: The distinctly human capacity to use empty linguistic tokens; its stratification marks a new class divide
- [[Common symbolic link]]: Shared ritual and language that historically unified classes; now severed by automation
- [[Moral reciprocity]]: The foundational GA principle of human equality that forbids condoning hierarchy
- [[Administrative state]]: The bureaucratic machinery that reflects and reinforces these new symbolic stratifications
- [[Assortative mating]]: The tendency of cognitive/professional elites to marry within their class, accelerating stratification

## Notable Passages

> "For the first time, a whole segment of the population...has been detached from the 'common folk,' not simply by privilege as in the past, but by the ability to use symbols."

> "There is, in fact, a dramatically new and potentially uniquely intractable form of inequality, even if it's not the inequality the victimary activists complain about."

## Questions Raised

1. If symbolic ability becomes the primary stratifier, what forms of "human engineering" might restore symbolic access without abandoning the moral commitment to equality?
