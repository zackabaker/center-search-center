## Summary

This brief post announces the author's intention to develop an [[originary]] account of healthcare policy debates, particularly around "pre-existing conditions." Rather than treating healthcare as a technical or economic problem, the author suggests it should be analyzed through GA's framework of [[declarative culture]] and the dynamics of [[victimocracy]]. The post defers the full analysis but references a related piece exploring how victim-narratives structure contemporary political discourse around access and entitlement.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary analysis]] – interpreting contemporary institutions by tracing them back to their founding moral and linguistic moments
- [[Declarative culture]] – societies organized around public declarations of value and entitlement rather than market mechanisms alone
- [[Victimocracy]] – political systems where victimhood claims function as primary sources of moral authority and policy legitimacy
- [[Resentment]] – the emotional-linguistic substrate underlying demands for redress and redistribution
- [[Healthcare debate]] – site where competing claims about need, desert, and collective obligation crystallize
