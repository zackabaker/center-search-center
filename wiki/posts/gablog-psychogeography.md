# Psychogeography

## Summary

This post argues that victimary discourse operates at a deeper level than political strategy—it functions as a sacred, psychogeographic structure rooted in fundamental [[scenicity]]. The author rejects the illusion that counter-victimary arguments (defending "victims of victims") can break victimary logic's grip, since the center can never register as victim in this framework. Instead, victimary thinking must be understood as a cultural and psychological phenomenon connected to modernity's "Gnostic" orientation, requiring analysis of how [[constitutive fantasy]] shapes collective imagination around victim identity.

## Key Concepts

- [[Victimary discourse]] — the modern sacred structure that recognizes only certain victims as legitimate
- [[Psychogeography]] — analysis attending to how psychological and spatial/cultural structures interweave
- [[Scenicity]] — the fundamentally scenic nature of human meaning-making; what makes something register as sacred
- [[Constitutive fantasy]] — an imaginary structure that constitutes identity and shapes collective belief
- [[Center]] — the locus of power and order against which victimary logic defines itself
- [[Gnostic modernity]] — Voegelin's diagnosis of modern consciousness as driven by heterodox transcendence
- [[Sacred]] — what registers as meaningful and binding in a culture's collective imagination

## Notable Passages

> "Only the victims of the center register for victimary discourse, while victimizations carried out by the victims of the center only further indict the center."

> "The victimary goes well beyond politics, striking deep roots in the culture and the psyche. Ultimately, it raises questions of fundamental scenicity, which is to say of the sacred."

> "Why are so many in the modern world vulnerable to gnostic faiths? How to make the needed upward moral innovations possible?"

## Questions Raised

1. If victimary logic is rooted in [[scenicity]] itself—in what registers as sacred—can it be countered through rational argument, or only through a resacralization of alternative center-maintaining forms?

2. What would constitute the "upward moral innovations" needed to make Christian differentiations sustainable for modern populations without Gnostic regression?

3. How does [[constitutive fantasy]] differ from ideology, and what would it mean to make audiences conscious of their own constitutive fantasies without dissolving the sacred structures necessary for social order?
