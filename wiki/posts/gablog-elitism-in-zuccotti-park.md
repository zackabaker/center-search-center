## Summary

This piece critiques the Occupy Wall Street movement as fundamentally elitist despite its socialist rhetoric, exposing the contradiction between its anti-private-property ideology and the protesters' own class privileges and material possessions. The author argues that modern progressive movements (from multiculturalism to environmentalism) operate as closed systems of perpetual grievance that cannot be satisfied by reform, creating an "existential condition" of permanent discontent. The real path to prosperity lies not in redistributive socialism but in [[sovereign selection]] and individual capital accumulation—what Bush called the "Ownership Society."

## Key Concepts

- **[[Victimocracy]]**: A system where grievance becomes the organizing principle of politics, immune to remedial solutions
- **[[Declarative culture]]**: The gap between stated ideals (anti-property, egalitarianism) and actual behavior (hoarding iPads, secret meetings)
- **[[Resentment]]**: The driving emotional engine of progressive movements, structurally incapable of satisfaction
- **[[Center and periphery]]**: The spatial division of Zuccotti Park reflecting hidden class hierarchies within supposedly egalitarian spaces
- **[[Moral self-righteousness]]**: The aesthetic cover for what is actually appropriation and power-seeking
- **[[The market]]**: The mechanism through which ordinary people can build wealth without waiting for government distribution

## Notable Passages

> "The socialist opposition to private property, apart from its moral self-righteousness, is really only the desire to appropriate the private property of others."

> "There's no conceivable reform that could satisfy the OWS protesters...Modern liberalism is an existential condition that will suffer no remedy."

> "For every symptom that is eased, another is made worse. The horse leech's daughter is a closed system."
