## Summary

This brief post critiques a French intellectual's romanticization of Aztec civilization in an interview with *Le Nouvel Observateur*. While Le Clézio celebrates Aztec achievements in agriculture, astronomy, and philosophy, he conspicuously omits the central fact of Aztec practice: systematic human sacrifice. Gans identifies this rhetorical evasion as symptomatic of European [[victimary progressivism]]—a self-hating inversion that celebrates non-Western cultures while occluding their actual practices to maintain a narrative of Western moral failing.

## Key Concepts

- [[Victimary progressivism]]: The ideological impulse to invert historical narratives, celebrating victimized peoples while denigrating Western civilization
- [[Resentment]]: The underlying emotional structure driving selective historical comparison and moral displacement
- [[Moral imagination]]: The capacity (or failure) to hold complex truths simultaneously about civilizations
- [[Declarative culture]]: The power of narrative framing to establish what "counts" as historically significant
- [[Historical self-hatred]]: Europe's pathological relationship to its own achievements and moral legacy

## Notable Passages

> "Except for the practice of total war (firearms, cavalry, but above all the practice of terror: burned villages, women and children sold into slavery or massacred...)" — Le Clézio, *Le nouvel observateur*

> "It is difficult to evaluate the domain of Aztec and Inca philosophy, but the first Spanish chroniclers...speak with admiration" — Evasion through vagueness masking human sacrifice

> "the final battle of Mexico-Tenochtitlan left 260,000 dead, most of whom belonged to the city's elite" — Historical scale distorted by omission
