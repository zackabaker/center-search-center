## Summary

This post critiques "Big Scene" thinking—the tendency to interpret social and political reality through mythologized narratives organized around a central power that can be propitiated or leveraged. Gans argues that such thinking is fundamentally magical, projecting the structure of the [[originary scene]] onto complex contemporary systems. Against this, he proposes a more austere practice of hypothesizing: acknowledging that all knowledge is hypothesis-based, but stripping away melodramatic narrative flourishes and instead maintaining a horizontal oscillation between competing explanations that generates further inquiry.

## Key Concepts

- [[Big Scene thinking]]: The fantasy that social order mirrors the originary scene with a divisible center that can be propitiatedthrough redistribution
- [[Originary scene]]: The founding moment of human culture where a shared center organizes imperative exchanges
- [[Imperative exchange]]: Ritualized communication directed toward a center, presumed to reduce resentment through propitiation
- [[Sparagmos]]: The divided body; the location where Big Scenic thinking actually dwells despite claiming to address the center
- [[Hypothesis (austere)]]: Tentative explanation stripped of melodramatic narrative props, held in productive oscillation
- [[Deferral]]: The ongoing suspension of definitive interpretation, generating new inquiry through "if...then" chains
- [[Resentment management]]: The magical belief that inequity reduction will lessen social tension

## Notable Passages

> "An absolute precondition for any serious project of social renewal is the unqualified rejection of [magical thinking and cargo-culting]."

> "We have nothing but samples and are nothing but samples ourselves. We have to hypothesize the present out of a single sample, which in turn produces a new sample out of which one again hypothesizes the present."

> "If you withhold the narrative props, though, you can freely oscillate between these hypotheses and use them to generate further 'if…then' hypotheses predicated on each of them."

## Questions Raised

1. How can we maintain genuine intellectual honesty about political and historical causation without collapsing into either radical skepticism or hidden narrative mythmaking?

2. What distinguishes productive oscillation between hypotheses from mere paralysis or irresponsibility in decision-making contexts where action is demanded?

3. Does the austere practice of hypothesis-deferral have ethical or political implications—does it enable or disable forms of collective responsibility?
