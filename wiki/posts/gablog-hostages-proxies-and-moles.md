## Summary

This post argues that alt-right and neo-reactionary thought has recovered a richer vocabulary for describing social differentiation—particularly [[caste]] (soldiers, priests, merchants) and [[socio-sexual hierarchies]]—that liberalism flattens into undifferentiated "citizens." The author contends that while liberal categories are post-hoc statistical observations with no binding obligations, genuine social differentiation flows from [[team]] structure and [[vocation]]. A grammatical approach identifying individuals through their relation to [[ostensives]], [[imperatives]], [[interrogatives]], and [[declaratives]] offers a foundational framework for understanding how social hierarchies actually function across professions and disciplines.

## Key Concepts

- [[Caste]]: Foundational social differentiation based on functional roles (soldiers, priests, merchants) necessary for assembling effective teams
- [[Socio-sexual hierarchies]]: Status orderings (alpha, beta, gamma, etc.) that show clear patterns in youth but become mediated by professional discipline in adulthood
- [[Team structure]]: The organizing principle where belonging creates qualitative identity and role clarity that liberalism cannot accommodate
- [[Vocation]]: One's social position identified through grammatical relation to speech acts and their authority structure
- [[Grammatical differentiation]]: Identifying social position via mastery of ostensives, imperatives, interrogatives, and declaratives
- [[Liberal equalization]]: The flattening of all subjects into undifferentiated citizens, eliminating binding obligations or qualitative distinctions
- [[Center turnover]]: The mechanism by which liberal equality resists hierarchical stability through constant circulation of power

## Notable Passages

> "Liberalism flattens everyone out into 'citizens'... But the liberal designations just 'happen'—we notice them as statistical distributions after the fact, and they have nothing to do with decisions made or founding events."

> "It is belonging to a team that makes sense of qualitative 'identities.' Teams have captains... Liberalism can't do much with such an approach, because a team needs to be very clear about qualifications and roles."

> "What we are looking for in such group differentiations is resistance to the equalization pushed by turnover at the center."
