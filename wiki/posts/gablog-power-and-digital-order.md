## Summary

The text argues that the digital economy's concentration of power among symbolic manipulators creates an unprecedented economic gulf, collapsing the need for broad [[cultural solidarity]]. This fragmentation drives intensified [[victimary politics]] as the only remaining mechanism for moral reciprocity. The author traces how algorithmic order requires fewer "bytes" for cultural cohesion even as it demands terabytes for technical infrastructure, leaving those outside elite networks economically obsolete and forcing them toward victimary grievance narratives.

## Key Concepts

- [[Victimary politics]]: Cultural demand for moral reciprocity expressed through victim-binary framing (Nazi-Jew model applied universally)
- [[Symbolic manipulation]]: The core skill of digital economy elites; reduction of all production to abstract manipulation
- [[Cultural solidarity]]: Shared norms and intuitions necessary for cross-generational transmission of behavioral models
- [[Disparate impact]]: Metric-based exclusion replacing older aristocratic systems of authority and reassurance
- [[Originary hypothesis]]: GA's imagination-based truth-seeking, which cannot function without shared symbolic world
- [[Algorithmic order]]: Data-driven systems that eliminate need for humanistic understanding or consensus

## Notable Passages

"The more bytes required to organize the material economy...the fewer bytes needed to maintain cultural solidarity."

"GA has only imagination, and there is no longer enough of a common symbolic world to allow sharing imaginary constructs as a mode of truth-seeking."

"The only cultural solidarity needed there is that of the graduates of the top dozen or so universities...Soon enough, people will forget what 'wives' were."
