## Summary

Money is not a gradual evolution from barter but rather a state-imposed technology that displaces the sacrificial scene while preserving center-margin configurations. Following Seaford and Graeber, the author argues that money functions as a medium—like language or writing—that generates new metaphysical categories and ways of thinking about power, debt, and individual agency. Crucially, money and power are competing media: money obscures structural power relations by commodifying them, while power exposes money as mere access to the center rather than intrinsic value. Understanding capital requires recognizing how it differs from money as a generative force tied to productive capacity.

## Key Concepts

- [[Sacrifice]] — The primordial communal scene that money displaces while maintaining its structural logic
- [[Center-margin]] — The fundamental scenic configuration preserved across transformations from sacrifice to monetized markets
- [[Media]] — Sign systems (money, writing, power) that shape thought and perception rather than distort neutral reality
- [[Sacral kingship]] — Authority undermined by circulating money, which creates rival power centers the king cannot control
- [[Debt]] — The theological and practical relation generated alongside money that structures human-divine and human-human bonds
- [[Power as medium]] — The competing framework to money that reveals commodification as illusory access to centrality
- [[Capital]] — Distinguished from money as a productive force whose meaning crystallizes in later economic systems

## Notable Passages

> "Money displaces the sacrificial scene, but without abolishing the center-margin configuration constitutive of that or any scene."

> "Money is certainly a significant factor in undermining sacral kingship, because it creates centers of power and competencies that a sacral king cannot control."

> "Money clouds our view of the medium of power, because it represents power as something to be bought and sold and as a way of utilizing others and oneself for some immediate advantage."
