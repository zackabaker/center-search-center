## Summary

This ultra-concise post frames contemporary conflict not as civilizational or religious but as a question of *alignment* with respect to an originary center. The author proposes that the real war is against those Muslims who refuse to take sides against jihadist antagonists—a formulation that is simultaneously descriptive (this is the actual state) and prescriptive (this must become the norm). The statement collapses the distinction between passive tolerance and active enmity, arguing that neutrality in the face of sacred violence is itself a form of war.

## Key Concepts

- [[Declarative Culture]]: Culture constituted through explicit commitments and declarations of alignment; here, one's position relative to sacred violence
- [[Center]]: The locus of sovereignty and order whose defense defines the terms of conflict
- [[Sacred Violence]]: Mimetic killing justified through religious or victimary claims, which forces all within its orbit to choose sides
- [[Victimary Logic]]: The logic by which victimhood excuses or absolves complicity; the post rejects this exemption
- [[Mimetic Desire]]: The contagious nature of conflict; neutrality becomes impossible once mimetic antagonism spreads

## Notable Passages

> "We are at war with all those Muslims who are not also at war with the Muslims at war with us."

This formulation is notably *structural* rather than essentialist—it defines enemies by their failure to declare opposition, not by their faith.

> "will not become less true until we have won the war"

The statement gestures toward the prescriptive function: this enemy definition holds validity only until the underlying conflict is resolved.
