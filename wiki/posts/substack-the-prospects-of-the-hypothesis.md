## Summary

The originary hypothesis offers more than abstract theory—it provides a research program for diagnosing and intervening in global crisis. Gans argues that GA requires defensible anthropological foundations (understanding humans as self-endangering through mimetic rivalry), a transdisciplinary scaling capacity, and methods for analyzing historical transformation as actionable intervention. Rather than remaining neutral, originary thinking can guide practical remedies while avoiding both naive pacifism and ideological capture.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary Hypothesis]] – The founding GA framework explaining human emergence through gesture and deferral
- [[Mimetic Constitution]] – The human subject constituted through imitation and rivalry with others
- [[Center]] – The shared, compelling focus that defers mimetic violence through sacred concentration
- [[Deferral]] – The core mechanism by which human communities establish order and forestall rivalry
- [[Transdisciplinary Approach]] – Method that scales analysis from individual desire to geopolitical fracture without disciplinary silos
- [[Sacred]] – The ultimate reference point toward which all deferrals gesture, knowable only through its various cultural forms
- [[Historical Transformation]] – Change analyzable through originary logic and thus available for intervention

## Notable Passages

> "The human is that being who is a greater danger to himself than is posed by any external danger. This follows from the mimetic constitution of the human."

> "Our relation to the center is both the source of rivalry and the means of controlling it."

> "Originary thinking can also lead to the conclusion that only certain changes on the ground can make a new mode of deferral possible."

## Questions Raised

1. How can originary thinkers move from diagnosis of a crisis to *specific* ground-level interventions without abandoning the hypothesis's transdisciplinary scope?

2. If disagreement among originary thinkers is possible and expected, what criteria distinguish productive from destructive dispute within the research program?

3. What distinguishes the "courage and devotion" required to found a new deferral from mere conflict-seeking or self-sacrifice?
