## Summary

This post argues that [[declarative]] sentences are not primarily representations of independent reality, but rather inquiries into the [[imperative]] conditions that make action possible. The [[interrogative]] sits between imperative and declarative as a "softened" command—a command recognizing it may not be obeyed, thus shifting from demand to information-seeking. Understanding this chain reveals that all social theory must trace declaratives back through interrogatives to the imperatives that actually structure human life and coordination.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Declarative]]**: A sentence appearing to represent independent reality, but functionally an inquiry into the conditions under which imperatives can be fulfilled
- **[[Imperative]]**: A direct command creating new reality through compulsion; the foundational form underlying all discourse
- **[[Interrogative]]**: A softened or hedged imperative; a command transitioning into a request for information about fulfillment conditions
- **[[Linguistic hierarchy]]**: The structural ordering of imperative→interrogative→declarative as layers of human coordination
- **[[Social ontology]]**: The study of how imperatives actually structure human life rather than how declaratives represent it
- **[[Hypothetical]]**: The implicit or explicit condition under which an imperative might be obeyed

## Notable Passages

> "Declaratives are attempts to determine the conditions under which imperatives can be obeyed and fulfilled."

> "Imperatives involve some direct connection between at least two people; imperatives create a new reality, or fail to."

> "A social science that focused solely on how imperatives work in all the varied social situations in which they are used would have an inexhaustible topic that leaves no area of human life unexamined."

## Questions Raised

1. If all declaratives are ultimately inquiries into imperatives, does a truly "free" society require the ability to refuse imperatives, or merely the ability to negotiate their terms interrogatively?

2. How does the imperative→interrogative→declarative chain differ across cultures or historical periods—are some societies more willing to keep imperatives explicit rather than concealed in declarative form?

3. If understanding social theory requires tracing each declarative back to its originary imperative, what happens when the original imperator is dead, absent, or collective—does the chain become untraceable?
