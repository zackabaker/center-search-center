## Summary

This essay proposes a Machiavellian manual for restoring sovereignty in the contemporary world, modeled on Lenin's *What is to be Done?* but inverted reactionary. Rather than attempting to restore literal pre-revolutionary forms (aristocracy, demographics, established churches), the author argues for identifying and undoing the historical sources of disorder that undermined an abstracted model of [[sovereignty]]—one concerned with internal order, civilization, and external differentiation. The project treats political action as fundamentally a form of [[deferral]], and grounds itself in the [[originary hypothesis]] as a means of aligning speech with truth and action with sovereign authority.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Sovereignty]]**: The power to decide on the exception; whoever determines what constitutes compelling state interest holds sovereign authority
- **[[Reaction]]**: Not restoration of literal past forms, but stripping excrescences from an abstract model of order that preceded revolutionary modernity
- **[[Deferral]]**: All action is fundamentally a form of undoing; the originary insight that doing is a kind of deferral
- **[[Democratic Centralism]]**: Lenin's model inverted: rather than top-down obedience after collective deliberation, a reactionary approach to preserving lines of authority traceable to sovereign decisions
- **[[Originary Hypothesis]]**: The framework through which to identify sources of disorder and understand action as inherently deferent
- **[[Producerism]]**: Replacing consumerism as a model for intellectual activity; writing performatively within the originary scene rather than about it

## Notable Passages

> "If we think about reaction as a stripping of excrescences from a model of sovereignty that has certainly existed almost everywhere and that attained a kind of perfection before revolutionary modernity demolished it once and for all, then setting ourselves the task of identifying and undoing all the sources of disorder...we have a perfectly reasonable project."

> "Sovereign is he who decides on the exception...Whoever decides what counts as a 'compelling state interest' is sovereign."

> "The sovereign, then, wants everyone else to be sovereign in their own sphere."

## Questions Raised

1. How does one distinguish between reactionary "stripping of excrescences" and merely nostalgic or regressive restoration? What criterion separates reasonable historical undoing from fantasy?

2. If sovereignty requires that "everyone else be sovereign in their own sphere," does this distribute sovereignty so widely that the concept loses coherence, or does it represent a genuinely different model of political order?

3. How would writing "performatively, pointing to its own participation in the concepts it constructs" actually operate as political intervention rather than aesthetic self-awareness?
