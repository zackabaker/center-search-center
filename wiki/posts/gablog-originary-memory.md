## Summary

Ethics is fundamentally grounded not in future consequences or abstract principles, but in remembering the [[originary scene]] through attentive sign use. Every sign carries traces of prior scenes where it was performed, and proper care for language means honoring those genealogies back to the first utterance. True ethical action emerges from sharpening our intuitions as sign users—intuitions ultimately derived from the originary event—rather than from arrogant calculations about improving the future or adherence to predetermined principles.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary scene]]: The theoretical construct of the first human sign use, from which all subsequent meaning-making derives
- [[Sign use]]: The proper deployment of language that defers violence and honors earlier scenes of utterance
- [[Joint attention]]: The shared focus that grounds both language learning and ethical attunement
- [[Upclining]]: The retrieval of signs from more originary events through present signs
- [[Originary intuition]]: Our capacity to sense proper sign use, rooted in the originary event itself
- [[Auto-probatory signs]]: Signs that prove themselves through their genealogical coherence with earlier utterances

## Notable Passages

> "A sign well used is a sign that defers violence, even violence several or many degrees removed from the scene upon which the sign is used."

> "Any future-oriented ethics will be shallow, self-serving, and even fraudulent—none of us knows the slightest thing about the future."

> "By thus heading back into the past, enriching the originary scene with everything that has happened since...we open up possible futures."

## Questions Raised

1. If the [[originary scene]] is a theoretical construct rather than an experienced reality (unlike religious revelation), how do we distinguish genuine "originary intuition" from mere aesthetic preference or individual whim?

2. How does "proper care of the sign" differ practically from principle-based ethics? What prevents this framework from becoming equally abstract and evasive?

3. If all ethics is retrospective (turned toward the originary past), what resources does this provide for navigating genuinely novel situations where no prior scene offers clear guidance?
