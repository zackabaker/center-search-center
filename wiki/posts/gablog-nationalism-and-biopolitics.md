## Summary

This post argues that liberalism's founding horror of biology—its aspiration to create a rational, exchange-based system independent of tribal and biological realities—has metastasized into an active assault on biological difference itself. The acceleration of this "biophobia" after Nazi biopolitics produced an autoimmune response in which liberalism became a self-enclosed viral system automatically excluding any politically-tainted biological claims. The author suggests that nationalism and populism, which allow biological and mimetic dimensions into politics, threaten liberalism's categorical order precisely because biology proves stronger than constitutional law.

## Key Concepts

- [[Liberalism]] — A system of rational exchange that seeks to transcend tribal and biological relations by creating a neutral marketplace of abstract subjects
- [[Biopolitics]] — The management and regulation of biological life by state power, typically routed through rights discourse
- [[Autoimmune response]] — Liberalism's violent rejection of biological difference after Nazism, attacking those who introduce such claims into politics
- [[Victimary]] — The post-liberal system that perpetuates liberalism's logic through rights accumulation rather than offering genuine limitation
- [[Nationalism]] — Political assertion of collective identity grounded in territory, kinship, and shared mimetic life; inherently resistant to liberal abstraction
- [[Populism]] — Political eruption of unmanaged emotional and mimetic forces that resist the approved discourse of policy and principles
- [[Mimetic contagion]] — The spread of desires, affects, and behavioral patterns through imitation rather than rational deliberation

## Notable Passages

> "Liberalism didn't address such issues because there was no need to, as such essentially tribal relations were still visible and, in fact, constituted the tacit background out of which liberalism sought to carve a new space."

> "The anathematization of nationalism and populism in liberal thinking is an expression of this autoimmune response: nationalism is not quite as deeply steeped in biology as race and sex, but there is always a racial component and sexual politics to nationalisms."

> "What's good for the American people?" will almost certainly prove stronger than the categorical imperative embedded in Constitutional provision."
