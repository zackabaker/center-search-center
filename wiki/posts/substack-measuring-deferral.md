## Summary

This piece pursues a minimal ontology grounded in [[generative anthropology]] and the [[originary hypothesis]], arguing that language itself is fundamentally an ongoing measure of [[deferral]]. Rather than accepting the premise that life or the universe is computation, the author proposes four foundational categories—[[mark]], [[measure]], [[boundary]], and [[fit]]—that are simultaneously part of reality and necessary for representing it. Language measures while effecting deferral, making it impossible to derive true human measures from non-human sources; we always measure one part of humanity against another.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary hypothesis]] — The founding GA insight that language emerged from a collective scene of deferred violence
- [[Deferral]] — The postponement of violence through linguistic representation; language's primary function
- [[Mark]] — The fundamental act of separating one piece of reality from others
- [[Measure]] — The articulation between marks; the distance that creates meaningful distinction
- [[Boundary]] — An articulation of marks and measures that creates something new on both sides
- [[Fit]] — The way things occupy space together; how marks and measures relate to one another
- [[Arche-writing]] — Derrida's concept of inscription as originary; the trace before speech
- [[Originary grammar]] — Grammar derived entirely from the originary event and its logic

## Notable Passages

> "Language, or sampling, as the ongoing measure of deferral is the path forward here—we are always measuring our respective measures of deferral, in the process building new infrastructures of deferral."

> "A measurement is really at least two marks, with the distance between the marks [being] the measure."

> "No true measure of anything human can come from something non-human—we are always speaking in terms of one part of what is human measuring another part, for the purpose of fitting and bounding."

## Questions Raised

1. Can these four categories (mark, measure, boundary, fit) truly generate a complete ontology without collapse into either dualism or infinite regress?

2. If language always measures "one part of humanity against another," what happens to the possibility of measuring human against non-human or language against reality itself?

3. How does this minimal ontology avoid the very computational problem it defers—namely, who or what computes the emergence of the marking/measuring system in the first place?
