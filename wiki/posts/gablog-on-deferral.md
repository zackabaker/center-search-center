## Summary

This post argues that [[deferral]] is the minimal and most philosophically rigorous term for understanding the [[originary sign]]'s function in preventing [[mimetic crisis]]. Terms like "resolution" or "prevention" impose external, metaphysical frameworks that obscure the contingency and immanence of the originary act itself. [[Deferral]] captures the precarious position *within* the act—we can only delay catastrophe, never finally solve it, and this very uncertainty is what gives us space to think and act. The text explores how acts of deferral necessarily require absolute commitment while remaining fundamentally fallible, and how the repeatable sign extends our [[deferral]] horizons across time.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Deferral]]**: The minimal term for how the [[originary sign]] postpones rather than resolves [[mimetic rivalry]], preserving contingency and immanence.
- **[[Originary sign]]**: The first human gesture that defers convergence on the central object, creating the space for culture.
- **[[Mimetic crisis]]**: The escalating rivalry and potential violence arising from undifferentiated desire for the same object.
- **[[Originary scene]]**: The hypothetical moment of human emergence structured around the deferral of violent convergence.
- **[[Metaphysical thinking]]**: External frameworks that seek causes, rules, and final solutions—what GA rejects in favor of originary analysis.
- **[[Imminence of imminence]]**: The paradox that deferral makes destruction imminent rather than eliminating it, creating minimal working space.
- **[[The sign]]**: The repeatable gesture that can be defended and transmitted across time, extending the horizon of deferral.

## Notable Passages

> "One can't know any more than that whatever gesture one puts forth is minimally more likely to subtract from rather than accelerate the momentum dragging us along toward the catastrophe."

> "I have to completely believe my act of deferral will succeed, at least for that period in which I am enacting it; which would further imply that I must exclude from consideration all the indications which suggest that it might not, in fact succeed."

> "Our horizons, though, can be progressively extended insofar as any act of deferral leaves behind it a sign, which can be repeated by someone other than me, and provides a starting point for the next act of deferral."

## Questions Raised

1. How do we distinguish between acts of deferral that genuinely extend our horizon versus those that merely intensify the underlying crisis for future generations?

2. Can the framework of deferral account for moments of genuine [[originary]] creation versus mere repetition of existing signs, or does this distinction itself belong to "metaphysical accretions"?

3. What does it mean to "defend the sign" when the text breaks off—and how does sign-defense differ from the original act of deferral that created it?
