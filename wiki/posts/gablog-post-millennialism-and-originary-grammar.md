## Summary

This essay traces the genealogy of market competition from the [[originary scene]] itself, arguing that differentiation and rivalry emerge whenever proficiency is publicly recognized. Gans maps a historical progression from egalitarian gift economies through the Big Man stage to imperial order and finally to modern market economies—each phase negotiating the tension between [[center|centripetal pull of the center]] (homogenizing, sacred) and competitive differentiation on a public scene. The ancient Greek city-state uniquely attempted to combine open marketplace with patriarchal sacrifice but failed. Only monotheism and metaphysics could sustain centralized order by embedding hierarchy into cosmological structure, though this order now faces erosion as markets expand beyond the margins.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Originary scene]]**: The inaugural human gathering where shared consumption and deferred violence establish property and [[resentment]] simultaneously.
- **[[Public competition]]**: Differentiation achieved before witnesses; value established through spectatorial judgment rather than mere fact.
- **[[Center]]**: The sacred focal point organizing society; sustains order through awe but generates [[resentment]] when rivals threaten occupancy.
- **[[Gift economy]]**: Asymmetrical tribute system wherein peripheral members pay the center (king/god) who reciprocates with life and order.
- **[[Resentment]]**: The dual pressure—against excessive differentiators and against centripetal homogenization—that destabilizes social orders.
- **[[Big Man]]**: Transitional social form extending the gift economy into stable hierarchy without formal state apparatus.
- **[[Onto-theo-logical order]]**: Metaphysical-religious embedding of social hierarchy into cosmic structure (Heidegger); resists only through existential rebellion.

## Questions Raised

1. If [[resentment]] perpetually oscillates between the center's homogenizing pull and competitive differentiation, what historical moment (if any) can genuinely *escape* this dynamic rather than merely relocate it?

2. Does the modern market economy represent a fundamental break from onto-theo-logical order, or merely its latest (potentially final) disguise—decentralizing the [[center]] while maintaining its structural necessity?

3. What role does the "disappearance" of aristocratic values play in the transition to post-millennialism, and can those values be fully "extinct" or merely sublimated into new competitive forms?
