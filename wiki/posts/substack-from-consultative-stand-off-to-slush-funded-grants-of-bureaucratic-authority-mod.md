## Summary

This essay argues that the modern legislature is anomalous within the three-branch system—unlike the executive and judiciary, which descend recognizably from pre-modern institutions, the legislature bears no resemblance to its medieval precedent (parliaments called by kings for funding). Rather than creating genuine law, modern legislatures create grants of authority to bureaucratic agencies, funding them without meaningful oversight. This mechanism enables a self-perpetuating cycle of campaign management, interest-group penetration, and media-intelligence agency coordination that prioritizes its own survival over actual governance. The author suggests the legislature itself—not its dysfunction—is the central locus of modern political transformation and disorder.

## Key Concepts

- [[Center and Periphery]]: The need for a responsible occupant of the center (executive) reflects anthropological necessity; legislatures lack this grounding
- [[Declarative Authority]]: Modern legislatures don't create law but issue bureaucratic grants of authority that bypass traditional oversight
- [[Imperium in Imperio]]: The legislature operates as a parasitical self-perpetuating system within the state structure
- [[Sacred Form]]: Judges maintain quasi-ritualized settings with formalized procedure; legislatures lack any comparable legitimating form
- [[Originary Responsibility]]: Only those responsible for seeing decisions through can authentically "make law"
- [[Popular Rule]]: Democratic legitimacy paradoxically shields the legislature's circular self-dealing from scrutiny

## Notable Passages

"Only someone with a responsibility to see through the decision-making process to its conclusion can 'make law.' What modern legislatures do is create grants of authority to bureaucratic agencies."

"This is really the only mode of governance consistent with 'popular rule'... mostly ensures that the control of and investment in election process will be a central concern of all institutions."

"Like all parasitical entities, this liberal democratic model will presumably not survive its host, but there may be sufficient countertendencies... able to stave off collapse indefinitely."
