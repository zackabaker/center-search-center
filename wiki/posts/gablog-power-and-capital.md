## Summary

Nitzan and Bichler's *Capital as Power* offers a radical reframing of economics by collapsing the politics/economy distinction and defining capital as a mode of [[power]] itself. Power is fundamentally "confidence in obedience"—a circular, self-perpetuating force that reshapes social order according to its own logic. Against capital stands [[industry]], a hologrammic system of productive activities governed by the "instinct of workmanship" and irreducible to price. The author argues that capitalization—the discounting of expected future earnings—is the algorithm through which capitalist power constantly structures reality, making Nitzan and Bichler marginal but essential thinkers aligned with the originary hypothesis.

## Key Concepts

- [[Power]]: Confidence in obedience; circular and self-perpetuating reshaping of social order
- [[Capital as power]]: The capitalist mode counted in prices; differential earnings denote differential power
- [[Mega-machine]]: Mumford's concept; power impressed through vast infrastructural projects
- [[Industry]]: Hologrammic productive system governed by workmanship, irreducible to quantification
- [[Capitalization]]: The algorithm discounting expected future earnings to restructure present order
- [[Center]]: Iterative, concerned with reshaping order through conscious intentionality
- [[Originary hypothesis]]: The foundational GA thinking indigestible to existing disciplines

## Notable Passages

> "Power is 'confidence in obedience.' With such confidence those with power reshape the social order in accord with the principle of its own power."

> "Capitalization discounts a particular trajectory of expected future earnings...the higher and more predictable these earnings are relative to other groups of companies, the greater the differential power."

> "Nitzan and Bichler have a very simple hypothesis with ramifications that cut down forests of disciplinary obfuscation."
