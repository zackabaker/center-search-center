## Summary

This essay argues that Trump's animating concern is restoring [[sovereignty]] by achieving [[commensurability]] between power and responsibility—a goal blocked by three interlocking obstacles: transnational corporate interests (globalism), the political left's use of liberal destabilization tools, and the permanent bureaucratic state (the "deep state" or "Cathedral"). The author credits Trump with awareness of this configuration and explores how addressing economic sovereignty through tariffs and immigration control might provide a path toward genuine order within and potentially beyond liberal democracy.

## Key Concepts

- [[Sovereignty]] — the capacity of a government to issue binding commands over a defined territory and people
- [[Commensurability]] — alignment between those holding power and those bearing responsibility for outcomes
- [[Globalism]] — transnational corporate power that uses economic leverage to override national state interests
- [[The Deep State]] — permanent bureaucratic institutions whose members outlast elected officials and resist executive command
- [[The Cathedral]] — Moldbug's concept of the interlocking media, academy, and government forming a permanent inquisition
- [[Liberal destabilization]] — institutional tools (rights discourse, judicial delay, media) that short-circuit executive command implementation
- [[The Revolving Door]] — the circulation of officials between government and lobbying that corrupts political incentives

## Notable Passages

> "Governments should govern, i.e., oversee the interactions of a particular people located on a particular territory, which means those with responsibility should issue commands that should in turn be fulfilled."

> "Immigration, especially illegal immigration, is such a central concern because it brings together all three of these obstacles to sovereignty."

> "Imagining the success of his plan and method provides us with a way of plotting out a particular path, within the liberal democratic order, towards the end of that order—towards, well, order."

## Questions Raised

1. How can executive sovereignty be restored when the bureaucratic apparatus itself has become a self-perpetuating interest? What leverage remains outside the system?

2. Does the path toward genuine order necessarily run through the dissolution of liberal democracy, or can sovereignty be reclaimed within its institutional forms?

3. If the revolving door and regulatory capture are systemic features rather than contingent corruptions, what structural reforms (beyond term limits or campaign finance) could actually interrupt them?
