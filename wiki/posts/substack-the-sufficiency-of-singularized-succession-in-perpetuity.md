## Summary

The author develops [[singularized succession in perpetuity]] as the fundamental logic of actual power: a leader's true authority manifests only in choosing their successor. Rather than relying on hereditary monarchy or chaotic succession scrambles, this concept abstracts to a principle where all social organization becomes explicitly oriented toward ensuring continuous, thoughtful transmission of power across infinite time. This framework directly addresses the [[imperative gap]] between nominal and occupied centers, offering a counterargument to capital's short-term discounting of future value through a vision of perpetual institutional care.

## Key Concepts

- [[Singularized succession in perpetuity]] – The principle that actual power is demonstrated through carefully selecting one's successor with perpetual continuity in view
- [[Center]] – The occupied and signifying locus of authority whose legitimacy depends on closure of the imperative gap
- [[Imperative gap]] – The distance between what the center nominally commands and what actually gets done, closed through succession planning
- [[Open source Messianism]] – The infinity of perpetual succession embodied as a cultural infrastructure for continuously generating worthy successors
- [[Sign user]] – The human being whose constitutive desire for immortality drives succession logic
- [[Nominal vs. occupied power]] – The distinction between delegated authority and actual control; actual power requires successor selection

## Notable Passages

> "If you are not choosing your successor you are not actually in power, because everyone around you can hedge on various degrees of obedience and support by transferring promises to more likely successors."

> "Viewing everyone, in every field of endeavor, as concentrating their efforts on selecting their successor is the most powerful way of reading human practices in terms of the desire for immortality constitutive of the sign user."

> "The entirety of culture is therefore organized around producing candidates for succession, creating spaces of performance for them to test and prove themselves publicly."

## Questions Raised

1. How does "singularized succession in perpetuity" differ practically from hereditary monarchy, and what specific institutional structures would enable it without aristocratic entrenchment?

2. If all human endeavor becomes organized around successor-selection, does this not create the same rigidity Gans wished to avoid by abstracting away from monarchy—merely displacing the problem onto an infinite recursion?

3. How does one distinguish between genuine successors selected through this logic and those merely appearing competent within corrupted selection infrastructure?
