## Summary

Drawing on Bertrand de Jouvenel's account of power's metastasis, this essay traces absolutism to the king's rational need to bypass recalcitrant aristocratic intermediaries through a high-low alliance. However, this creates a permanent instability: the moment of successful sovereignty—when shared threat compels unanimous obedience—becomes pathologized into a perpetual machinery of power-seeking. The text argues that absolutist theory cannot explain its own origin without admitting it models itself on rare moments of genuine collective will, yet seeks to manufacture those moments artificially.

## Key Concepts

- [[Sovereignty]]: The reducibility of collective will to a single binding decision-maker, dependent on legitimating moments of actual unanimity
- [[High-low alliance]]: The ruler's strategy of elevating subordinates against intermediate power-holders (aristocrats, middle classes), repeated in recursive cycles
- [[Power dynamics]]: The pathological expansion of authority that follows from securing power through elimination of rival centers
- [[Absolutism]]: Political theory and method attempting to permanently lock in debts and enforce perpetual mobilization capacity
- [[Shared threat]]: The originary condition enabling genuine collective will—the only non-coercive basis for unified action
- [[Debts]]: The relational bonds between ruler and subordinates that must be "called in" at moments of genuine necessity
- [[Center and periphery]]: The structural oscillation between unified command and the emergence of new intermediate powers

## Notable Passages

> "The king, then, elevates the subordinates of those aristocrats, makes them subject to his authority alone, and thereby marginalizes the aristocrats. This is the high-low alliance against the middle, repeated over and over again in the career of state power."

> "Aristocratic resistance to, say, 15% of the king's projects might have been frustrating enough...but that would still mean he attained 85% compliance. Even if the numbers are lower, the point would stand: unity of will was obtained, repeatedly, so we can figure out how."

> "What we are looking for is the king wisely 'calling in his debts' when the future of his realm most depends upon it. Absolutism, then, as political theory and method, aims at having and keeping those debts in place permanently."

## Questions Raised

1. If genuine sovereignty requires moments of real shared threat to achieve non-coercive unanimity, can any political system manufacture legitimacy once those moments become rare or artificially staged?

2. Does the recursive cycle of high-low alliance suggest that all political orders contain an internal timer toward their own destabilization—or can a sufficiently sophisticated center anticipate and reset the cycle?

3. How would a GA originary perspective distinguish between the momentary gathering around a [[center]] and the institutionalized violence required to hold that moment permanently?
