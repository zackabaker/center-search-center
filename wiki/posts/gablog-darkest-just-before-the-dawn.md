## Summary

This post critiques how mainstream media coverage of the Israel-Hezbollah conflict reveals the deep entrenchment of [[victimary thinking]] in contemporary journalism. Gans observes that coverage systematically frames the winning side (Israel) as inherently more brutal while sympathizing with the losing side, a bias rooted in postwar European guilt culture. He argues this mindset becomes dangerous when applied to adversaries like Ahmadinejad who cannot be deterred by moral relativism—sometimes winning morally requires the will to actually win militarily.

## Key Concepts

- [[Victimary thinking]]: The ideological frame that privileges victims and interprets power as inherent cruelty
- [[Media bias]]: Journalism structured to blame Western/Israeli power while obscuring enemy intentions
- [[Ressentiment]]: The resentful logic that transforms military victory into moral condemnation
- [[Deterrence]]: Security systems that work only when opponents accept the cost-benefit calculus
- [[Sovereignty]]: The capacity to act decisively in one's own interest despite moral ambiguity
- [[Postwar guilt]]: European cultural psychology that inverts WWII outcomes into victim narratives
