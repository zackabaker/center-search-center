## Summary

This brief announcement directs readers to Chronicle 340, which examines Jacques Derrida's recent work through a generative anthropological lens. The piece explores Derrida's engagement with questions of "minimal faith"—a concept that touches on the sacred and foundational assumptions underlying human culture. By connecting Derrida's thought to GA's originary framework, the post suggests that deconstruction and generative anthropology can illuminate each other's insights about culture's anthropological foundations.

## Key Concepts

- [[Minimal faith]] — the irreducible trust or belief that precedes and enables all cultural systems
- [[Originary anthropology]] — the study of culture's origins in the primordial scenic event
- [[Sacred]] — the center around which human attention and meaning crystallize
- [[Deconstruction]] — the philosophical method of revealing hidden assumptions and binary structures
- [[Derrida]] — poststructuralist philosopher whose later work approached religious and anthropological themes
