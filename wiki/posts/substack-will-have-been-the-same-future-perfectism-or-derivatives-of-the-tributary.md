## Summary

Bouvard argues that what we call "economy" is fundamentally [[ritual distribution]] from the center, not free-market exchange. By studying derivatives through anthropological and GA frameworks, he reveals that all economic activity aims at establishing [[presence]]—making something appear the same across different scenes. The derivative, typically obscured by economistic models, actually embodies a vast social cooperation that becomes explicit in crisis moments invoking hope and prayer. Knowledge of risk thus becomes a totalizing study of institutional power itself.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Ritual distribution]]**: The anthropological logic underlying all exchange; the center allocates value through ceremonial rather than mechanical means
- **[[Presence]]**: Gans's concept adapted from linguistic theory; all sign use aims at establishing presence across scenes
- **[[Derivative]]**: A financial instrument that reveals the social logic of exchange by hedging against the impossibility of simultaneous arbitrage
- **[[Arbitrage]]**: Risk-free profit from price differentials; the theoretical ideal that makes derivative logic intelligible
- **[[Sameness]]**: The fundamental human operation of showing things to be the same across different markets or scenes
- **[[Center]]**: The originary locus from which ritual distribution occurs, replacing both liberal and democratic vocabularies
- **[[Hedging]]**: The disciplinary study and institutional management of all interference with presence

## Notable Passages

## Questions Raised

1. If all economy is ritual distribution from a center, what is the mechanism by which we collectively deny this knowledge—and what would change if that denial collapsed?

2. How precisely does the moment of financial crisis make the language of prayer and miracle transparent, and does this reveal something about non-crisis moments too?

3. Can the distinction between studying risk and intervening in conditions ever be truly maintained, or is knowledge always already power over the order?
