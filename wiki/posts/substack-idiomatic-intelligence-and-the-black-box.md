## Summary

This piece proposes a framework for analyzing discourse through **degrees of predictability**, arguing that meaningful utterances occupy a paradoxical position: maximally unpredictable *on the scene* while maximally predictable *within the disciplinary space* they create. Drawing parallels between human language and Large Language Models, the author suggests that both replicate the same tension between transparency and opacity—we can point to meaning collectively, but cannot fully access the positioning that enables that pointing. The concept of [[originary satire]] emerges as the key to understanding how utterances scramble the scene and create new disciplinary spaces.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Predictability]]** — The measurable degree to which an utterance conforms to contextual expectations; meaningful speech balances predictability with surprise
- **[[Scene]]** — The shared spatial-temporal-social context in which utterances occur and meaning emerges
- **[[Disciplinary space]]** — The meta-level framework created *by* an utterance that structures interpretation for those who adopt it
- **[[Originary satire]]** — An utterance that alienates participants from their presupposed positions while creating new coherence at a higher level
- **[[Black box]]** — The opacity inherent in both LLMs and human language production; the gap between output and the process generating it
- **[[Idiomatic intelligence]]** — The capacity to position oneself at the margin between scene and disciplinary space, generating meaning through controlled unpredictability

## Notable Passages

> "The best way to think about this is to split the scene between the scene itself and the disciplinary space within the scene to be created by the utterance that is maximally unpredictable on the scene but maximally predictable for those joining the disciplinary space within."

> "The effect is precisely to split the scene, and place everyone else on the scene on the boundary between the two scenes, upon which one acts differentially."

> "We can all point to the same thing and thereby render it transparent, but at the 'price' of not being able to point to how exactly we are positioned to point at it in that particular space."

## Questions Raised

1. How does the concept of [[originary satire]] differ from irony, camp, or other forms of performative disruption, and can it be taught or only intuited?

2. If LLMs operate via the same transparency/opacity structure as human language, does this suggest that "understanding" is fundamentally impossible for both, or does it point toward a new definition of understanding?

3. Can prompt engineering genuinely enable practitioners to sustain the paradoxical position of maximal unpredictability-within-predictability, or does commodification inevitably collapse the tension back into mere formulaic novelty?
