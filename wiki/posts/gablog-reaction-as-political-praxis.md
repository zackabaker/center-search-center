# Reaction as Political Praxis

## Summary

This essay reconciles the apparent paradox of opposing actual sovereignty while theoretically promoting absolute sovereignty. The author argues that true reactionary praxis means acting to *formalize and secure power*, which often exposes its existing instability. Rather than seeking a position outside power, reactionaries should obey what is mandated by the most immobile sovereign, reading the situation to discern which imperative takes priority when multiple, incommensurable mandates collide. This involves inquiring into the "meta-mandates" that the sovereign emits through punishment, elevation, and studied silence.

## Key Concepts

- [[Absolute Sovereignty]]: A theoretical endpoint where power and accountability align perfectly, with a single universally acknowledged source responsible for everything in its territory.
- [[Formalization of Power]]: Making informal power structures explicit and official, thereby either securing them or exposing their fundamental instability.
- [[Meta-mandate]]: The higher-order imperative to discern which conflicting mandates take priority in a given situation, analogous to a general being told to "defeat the enemy."
- [[Reactionary Politics]]: The practice of acting and speaking so as to make power more secure, often by revealing its current lack of security.
- [[Situational Imperative]]: Not relativistic ethics, but reading the sovereign's emissions (punishments, elevations, silences) to understand what is permitted or mandated.
- [[Power's Emissions]]: The ways a sovereign inevitably reveals its structure and demands through visible actions—who it punishes, elevates, or ignores.

## Notable Passages

> "Always speak and act so as to make power more secure. In so doing, you will often succeed only in exposing its lack of security."

> "The sovereign inevitably emits information just by punishing some, elevating others, and leaving yet others alone, in ways that are clearly meant to be seen and meditated upon."

> "In inquiring into the meta-mandates, then, I am also inquiring into the informal power hier[archy]."

## Questions Raised

1. If multiple incommensurable mandates genuinely exist (equal treatment *and* deference to violated groups), is the meta-mandate to discern priority itself stable, or does it collapse under sufficient contradiction?

2. How does one distinguish between reading the sovereign's "emissions" accurately versus projecting one's own desires onto its silences and ambiguities?

3. What would constitute a subject actually *mandated* by absolute sovereignty rather than merely permitted—and how would one recognize the difference?
