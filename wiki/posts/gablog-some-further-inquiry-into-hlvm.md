## Summary

This essay analyzes the mechanics of leftist political mobilization through the lens of [[resentment]] and state centralization. The author argues that the left functions as cadres mobilizing masses on behalf of elites seeking to concentrate state power by dismantling intermediate institutions. Rather than simple economic inequality, the essay proposes that [[resentment]] operates at vulnerable "choke points" in the social field—sites where previous efforts to minimize resentment have created levers for exacerbating it. The text probes the originary form of resentment that animates this machinery, questioning whether it stems from inequality or from perceived violations of trust.

## Key Concepts

- [[State Power]]: Direct centralized authority over individuals, enabled through undermining intermediate power centers
- [[Resentment]]: The fundamental emotional motor of leftist mobilization; operates at specific vulnerable points in social fields
- [[Intermediate Institutions]]: Local monopolies, regulatory regimes, and diverse prerogatives that resist state centralization
- [[Elite Mobilization]]: How elites interested in centralization "seed" resentment-based movements rather than directly control them
- [[Choke Points]]: Vulnerable sites in social relations where resentment has been minimized and can be re-exacerbated
- [[Victimocracy]]: The implicit system where resentment is weaponized as a form of power and extortion

## Notable Passages

> "Power over individuals can always be further centralized: the state can arrest you for all manner of crimes, including crimes generated by your interactions with the state."

> "The left establishes rackets at vulnerable 'choke points' in the social field of resentments—sites where attempts have been made to minimize resentments, and where the levers for exacerbating them also therefore exist."

> "Elite funding is more like seeding than recruiting—it is widespread and long-term, and no foundation thinks in terms of generating a protest of this size with this specific legislative impact on a regular basis."

## Questions Raised

1. If elite funding operates as "seeding" rather than direct manipulation, how do we distinguish between authentic grassroots resentment and systematically cultivated outrage—and does the distinction matter?

2. What constitutes the "originary form of resentment" that avoids infinite regress? Is it rooted in inequality, violated trust, or something more fundamental to [[scene-ness]] itself?

3. How can we map the specific "choke points" where resentment has been minimized in our current moment, and what does that reveal about which social domains are being targeted for re-centralization?
