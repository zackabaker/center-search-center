## Summary

This essay argues that early modern absolutism, particularly as theorized by Robert Filmer, represents a genuine clarification of sovereignty by making explicit what medieval kingship left confused: that ultimate power must be personal and decisional, not structural or procedural. The author critiques modern social sciences for evading decision through language of "process" and "structure," seeing this as moral immaturity. However, he also notes that historical absolutist monarchs centralized power destructively by using "the people" against intermediate institutions, destroying traditional moral orders. The essay poses a central question: can there be a form of absolutism today that clarifies power while preserving differentiation and embeddedness rather than collapsing all into equality-seeking conformity?

## Key Concepts

- **[[Sovereignty]]** – Ultimate, personal authority that cannot be diffused into structural processes; requires explicit clarification rather than medieval ambiguity
- **[[Decision]]** – The irreducibly human act of choice; obscured by social science language of process and structure
- **[[Moral maturity]]** – Examining how we make decisions to preserve or reverse past decisions, rather than deferring to impersonal forces
- **[[Absolutism]]** – A system of clarified, centralized power; distinct from the corrupted historical forms that demolished intermediate institutions
- **[[Representation]]** – The originary deferral of violence through common focus on an object; foundational to human moral development
- **[[Differentiation]]** – Preservation of distinct roles and embeddedness within institutions; threatened by modern equality-driven centralization

## Notable Passages

> "Rule, at any rate, must be personal. A decision can be disguised as a corporate affair, but ultimately someone has made it, and all human activities and institutions are the results of decisions."

> "We could see something moral in this evasion, insofar as it is a mode of deferral; but it is a marker of moral immaturity, like telling children the tooth fairy will come to make them stop crying."

> "What kind of absolutism would preserve and even enhance differentiation and embeddedness?"

## Questions Raised

1. **Can absolutism avoid the leveling trap?** If historical absolutists used the people against nobility to centralize power, what structural or institutional safeguards would prevent a contemporary absolutism from collapsing all differentiation into egalitarian conformity?

2. **Is decision-making separable from structure?** The author insists decision is personal and irreducible, yet doesn't all decision-making occur within some institutional or cultural framework—can one truly escape "structure" entirely?

3. **What would originary clarification look like today?** If Filmer clarified medieval confusion about sovereignty, what remains confused in our present moment that needs equivalent philosophical clarification?
