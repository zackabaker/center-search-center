## Summary

This essay uses two scenes—a roadside accident and the originary human gathering—to argue that all social organization centers on objects of desire and potential conflict resolution through [[the sign]]. The author traces how human hierarchy emerges not from natural pecking orders but from the collective deferral of violence through gesture. Even seemingly cooperative scenes like rescue efforts contain latent resentment that requires someone in a primary position to command gestures of deference and prevent status conflict from erupting.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary hypothesis]]: Gans's model of the first human scene where mimetic desire for a central object creates collective consciousness through the birth of the sign
- [[Mimetic desire]]: Appetite intensified and shaped by awareness of others' desire for the same object
- [[The sign]]: The aborted gesture of appropriation; a conversion of violent intention into peaceful deferral
- [[Social differentiation]]: Organization of people according to their capacity to meet specific needs and dangers
- [[Resentment]]: The latent conflict lying beneath all human scenes, requiring active deferral through gesture
- [[The center]]: The object of desire around which all social scenes organize and potential conflict concentrates

## Notable Passages

> "The first human scene...is more precisely specified. Here we have a desirable object, presumably some food item, at the center of the not yet human group...This intensifying desire overrides the animal pecking order that normally maintains peace within the group."

> "The initial sign was the invention and discovery of this 'method' of converting violent actions into gestures of deferral. The gesture is likely to be more effective and enduring the more it actually mimics and therefore evokes the violence deferred."

> "Every human scene has an object of desire and hence contains within it potential conflict...There must be someone first on the scene in a more primary sense—someone who can command the gestures of deferral needed to prevent those resentments lying right beneath the surface."

## Questions Raised

1. If all human scenes contain latent resentment requiring active deferral, what happens to a society that loses faith in the legitimacy of its primary figures' right to command these gestures?

2. How does the emptying-out of the handshake's original violent content—where modern gesture no longer evokes the danger it deferred—relate to the breakdown of social order more broadly?

3. Can [[social differentiation]] based on "natural" sorting (the qualified person steps forward) truly resist resentment, or does the very fact of hierarchy always generate claims of unfairness?
