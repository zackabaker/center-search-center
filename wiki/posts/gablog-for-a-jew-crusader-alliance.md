## Summary

This post engages Eric Gans's argument that Western Europe can reverse demographic decline through Christian revival rather than cultural collapse. The author agrees but pushes back on Gans's optimism about emerging generations recognizing systemic bankruptcy, arguing that genuine religious revival cannot be a calculated policy—it must be an event. The piece traces how [[white guilt]] since WWII has paradoxically weakened European self-confidence, making demographic renewal contingent on recovering a viable civilizational narrative that market society alone cannot provide.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary demography]]: The connection between demographic vitality and spiritual/religious coherence in civilizational survival
- [[White guilt]]: Post-WWII inhibition against exercising power or group dominance, stemming from Auschwitz and Hiroshima
- [[Christian revival]]: Not fundamentalist literalism but renewed faith aligned with anthropological self-knowledge
- [[Market society]]: The modern secular framework that must prove viable to reconcile Muslims and Europeans with modernity
- [[Victimary culture]]: The postwar social-insurance state's spiritual bankruptcy and inability to sustain itself demographically
- [[Judeo-Christian anthropology]]: René Girard and GA-informed understanding of religious tradition as aligned with fundamental anthropology
- [[Event vs. policy]]: The distinction between engineered social reform and genuine revelation/enthusiasm

## Notable Passages

> "The only way to reconcile European Muslims with modernity is by demonstrating to them that European market society is indeed a viable human project–as a society that disdains to reproduce itself is not."

> "If we genuinely want a revival of Christianity, we can't pretend to desire it in forms we find preferable. Such a revival will have to be an event, it cannot be a calculated social policy."

> "The most salient fact of American history in the sixty years since Nagasaki is that the United States has not won a definitive military victory."
