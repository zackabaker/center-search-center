## Summary

This essay critiques the [[victimary theology]] that demands unconditional belief in all sexual abuse accusations, rendering skepticism and due process impossible. The author traces how this logic, despite historical hoaxes (Brawley, McMartin, Duke Lacrosse), has tightened its grip on public discourse and law. Rather than justice, the system produces what he calls "unctuous totalitarianism"—perpetual hounding of the accused and infinite victimhood for the accuser, both trapped within the victimary church's demand for unqualified recognition of absolute harm.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Victimary theology]]** — Faith-based demand for unreserved belief in self-proclaimed victims, regardless of evidence or procedure
- **[[Unctuous totalitarianism]]** — Perpetual, unremitting persecution of the accused combined with sacred status for the victim
- **[[Declarative culture]]** — The victim's self-definition of harm becomes binding truth, overriding juridical frames
- **[[Presumption of innocence]]** — Eroded principle that accusations require vetting and skepticism before judgment
- **[[Saint-like status]]** — Ironic trap where the victim achieves sacred standing but remains bound to perpetual "healing"
- **[[Juridical frame]]** — Law's attempt to contain victimary passion through expanding but ultimately insufficient harassment codes

## Notable Passages

> "The logic of faith in the self-proclaimed victim is that nothing but a perpetual, unremitting hounding of the presumed perpetrator, his expulsion from public life and the ruination of his private life, will suffice."

> "The law itself...just means that she is victimized again, and her deepest injury left unacknowledged."

> "Just as it is now dogma in the addiction industry that once an addict, always an addict...the victim undergoes a perpetual process of 'healing' and exemplifying the universal condition of trauma."

## Questions Raised

1. At what point does protection of vulnerable populations become indistinguishable from totalitarian control? Where is the principled boundary?

2. If the [[victimary theology]] demands the victim be eternally wounded and healing, does it actually serve victims' genuine flourishing, or trap them in perpetual trauma identity?

3. How can we simultaneously maintain genuine accountability for abuse *and* preserve the [[presumption of innocence]] when the system itself prohibits skepticism?
