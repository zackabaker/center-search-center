## Summary

Rather than debate God's existence, we should ask whether the word "God" means anything. Following Gans, "God" is unique—its meaning is identical to its referent, functioning as a verbal ostensive gesture that grounds all other language. Every utterance is ultimately a naming of God, attempting to defer crisis through a lineage of signs reaching back to the originary scene. Disciplinary spaces and technomedia are organized continuations of this naming, creating forms of shared attention that extend central authority through practice and command.

## Key Concepts

- [[ostensive gesture]] – A pointing toward reality that grounds linguistic meaning itself; "God" is the only word whose meaning equals its referent.
- [[central authority]] – The organizing principle descending from the originary Big Man; all political wants presuppose this authority.
- [[disciplinary space]] – Organizations of shared attention that study central commands and extend them through practices and media.
- [[originary scene]] – The first deferral crisis resolved through gesture; all language traces back to this foundational moment.
- [[naming]] – Every utterance is a naming of God's manifestations; all language attempts to anchor imminent crises within prior utterances.
- [[technomedia]] – Sign-producing systems that distance themselves from while presupposing a scene of shared attention and deferral.

## Notable Passages

> "God" is the only word whose meaning is the same as its referent... After defining all known words in terms of other known words, the question of how we know these things are called by these names can only be answered by saying "God tells us so."

> Every utterance is the name of God because every utterance tries to anchor some imminent or distantly imagined crisis in a lineage of utterances that have deferred such crises going back to the first one.

> Disciplinary spaces studying the production and dissemination of signs generate new forms of media, each of which distances itself from while presupposing a scene comprised of participants capable of attending.
