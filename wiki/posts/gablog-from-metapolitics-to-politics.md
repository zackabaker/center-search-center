## Summary

This text argues that **neoabsolutism** represents a politics organized around deference to [[central authority]] through commands rather than demands. The author distinguishes between those who seek to fulfill commands issued by central authority and those who make transactional demands upon it—the former being coherent politics, the latter delusion. Crucially, all meaningful political action flows through [[naming]], which institutes [[originary center]]s and establishes ordered, authorized access to contested objects. The text proposes that understanding sovereignty means recognizing how names both concentrate resentment and defer it through appeal to central authority's authority to confer and redeem names.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Neoabsolutism]]**: Political organization centered on seeking commands from central authority rather than making demands upon it
- **[[Central authority]]**: The locus of command-issuance; all meaningful politics occurs as fulfillment of its commands (even when disguised as demands)
- **[[Naming]]**: The declarative act that institutes centers, makes objects divisible and available, and establishes authorized access to potential sources of rivalry
- **[[Originary center]]**: What a name refers to—an object that is or might be desired, thus a potential source of resentment and violent centralization
- **[[Resentment deferred]]**: The self-centering that occurs when one respects the central authority's authority over a named object, rather than violently centralizing it oneself
- **[[Declarative culture]]**: The encompassing mode in which we name, designate centers, and speak about anything by relating it to more inclusive centers
- **[[Sovereign resources]]**: The actual forces that back a name or claim; elicited through respectful inquiry into contested names

## Notable Passages

> "If your demands are not at the end of a chain of command issued by the central authority, they are simply delusional. If they are, they are commands followed in disguise."

> "Naming the thing makes the object available or divisible in an authorized and orderly way... We refrain from violent centralization by deferring to the central authority conferring and redeeming the name."

> "The only way we can name, which is also the only way we can speak about anything, is by providing the means to 'point' to its relation to some more inclusive center."

## Questions Raised

1. If all politics necessarily flows through chains of command from central authority, how does genuine opposition or counter-authority ever emerge without collapsing into either delusion or hidden obedience?

2. Can the distinction between respecting a contested name and entering an "imaginary space" (the New Amsterdam example) hold up when the name itself is the object of mortal struggle—i.e., when respecting the enemy's naming is itself a strategic move?

3. How does one determine whether one is actually deferring to genuine [[central authority]] versus a false or captured version of it, especially when competing factions each claim to represent the true center?
