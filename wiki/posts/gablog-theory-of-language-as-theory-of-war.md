## Summary

This essay argues that anti-SJW critiques, while tactically astute about progressive tactics, fail to explain *why* victimary politics succeeds. The author contends that the real source of this power is the tacit adoption of [[post-structuralist]] theory of language—particularly Butler's synthesis of Derrida, Foucault, and speech act theory—which treats all language as inherently performative and constitutive of reality. Those operating under this assumption gain decisive advantage over those still believing in objective, detached analysis. Understanding language itself as a form of war is essential to mounting an effective counter-response.

## Key Concepts

- [[Victimary politics]] – The political weaponization of claims to victimhood and marginalization used to delegitimize opponents and extract concessions
- [[Performative language]] – The post-structuralist doctrine that all utterances are acts that constitute reality rather than merely describe it
- [[Speech act theory]] – Austin and Searle's framework showing how language performs social action; weaponized by Butler for identity politics
- [[White Guilt]] – The internalized sense of complicity and moral debt that paralyzes defensive response to victimary accusations
- [[Declarative culture]] – A society organized around contested claims and their establishment as binding truth rather than inquiry
- [[4th Generation Warfare]] – Asymmetrical conflict targeting civilian will and social cohesion rather than military defeat

## Notable Passages

> "Whoever practices language according to this assumption will have incalculable advantages over people still adhering to what are ultimately metaphysical understandings of a detached, disinterested, objective analysis."

> "They see that you are building fences, prescribing behaviors, disciplining and organizing masses of people."

> "The source of the power of victimary politics is the assimilation, by now tacitly by most participants, of a post-structuralist understanding of language."
