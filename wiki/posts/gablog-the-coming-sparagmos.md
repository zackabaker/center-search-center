## Summary

This post diagnoses a potential Democratic electoral victory not as ordinary political correction but as ritualistic dismantling—a [[sparagmos]]—driven by anti-American forces operating through the party apparatus. The author argues that standard explanations for voter discontent (Iraq War, scandals) fail to account for the depth of pathology on display, and that the real issue is a civilizational struggle over constitutional governance, economic freedom, and cultural boundaries. The text treats Democratic gains as symptomatic of deeper [[victimary progressivism]] captured by [[declarative culture]] rather than reasoned political preference.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Sparagmos]]**: Ritual tearing apart and devouring of an order; the dismantling of institutions and authority through cyclical violence.
- **[[Victimary progressivism]]**: A political movement driven by victimhood narratives and resentment rather than constructive policy.
- **[[Declarative culture]]**: Culture structured around declarations of identity and grievance rather than shared origin and practice.
- **[[Constitutional order]]**: The framework of limited government, free markets, and cultural continuity under law.
- **[[Anti-war netroots]]**: Organized digital resistance funded and directed by global forces hostile to national sovereignty.
- **[[Counter-insurgency]]**: Long-term strategic patience required to defeat dispersed opposition; antithetical to impatient victimary demands.

## Notable Passages

> "The Democrats offer nothing–indeed, claim to offer nothing–other than a ritualistic tearing apart and devouring of the Bush Administration and all of its works."

> "There is no way Democrats in power would be able to resist the forces that have placed them there–the anti-war 'netroots,' funded by George Soros and crazed by years of flailing against what they see as dark, conspiratorial forces."

> "Those who complain about the number of dead in Iraq will not consider themselves obliged to tell you how many dead they think would be 'worth' obtaining our goal there."

## Questions Raised

1. What distinguishes ordinary political opposition from the [[sparagmos]] dynamic described here—i.e., at what point does criticism of policy become ritual destruction of order itself?

2. If victimary movements cannot be "made responsible" by acquiring power (as the author argues), what structural conditions would allow democratic recovery or does the logic demand civilizational transformation?

3. How do we account for the gap between material conditions (strong economy, security) and mass political affect oriented toward dismantling rather than adjustment?
