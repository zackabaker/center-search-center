## Summary

This essay positions grammar as foundational to understanding contemporary technology, particularly [[prompt engineering]] and AI interaction. Rather than treating language as merely instrumental to technology, the author argues that grammar itself—understood as the unfolding of anticipated dialogue within sentences—is being technologized into a mode of wisdom inquiry. By recovering classical grammatical analysis (subjects, predicates, modifiers) and pivoting toward [[construction grammar]], the text suggests that mastering language construction is essential to navigating databases, social networks, and the emerging "repository of all data."

## Key Concepts

- **[[Prompt engineering]]**: The design of search terms that elicit results from textual databases (AI) and generate new search terms in return
- **[[Declarative sentence]]**: A grammatical unit structured as an anticipated dialogue between subject and predicate
- **[[Construction grammar]]**: A framework placing grammatical formulas at the center of language, displacing word-sentence dualism
- **[[Hypotactic relations]]**: Subordinating conjunctions (if, but, because) that structure logical dependence, crucial for corpus searches
- **[[Data exchange]]**: The positioning of one's language/identity within databases awaiting discovery through others' searches
- **[[Attentional dialogue]]**: The embedded question-answer structure within any declarative utterance

## Notable Passages

> "Prompt engineering or search term design goes well beyond database searches and reach into all social networks—your search results will situate you within the data, waiting there to be found via another's search."

> "Even coding is coming to be generated through verbal search terms... language back at the center of technology."

> "Grammar... can incorporate the transdisciplinary discourse [as] a mode of wisdom literature."

## Questions Raised

1. If grammar is fundamentally dialogical (embedded anticipation of questions), how does this change our understanding of what AI "learns" when trained on textual corpora? Are large language models inheriting the dialogical structure itself?

2. How do [[hypotactic]] relations ("if," "but," "because") differ functionally in prompt engineering versus classical rhetoric, and what does this reveal about changing epistemic priorities?

3. Can wisdom literature and database query design be genuinely unified under a single grammatical theory, or does the analogy risk collapsing the distinction between human interpretive depth and algorithmic pattern-matching?
