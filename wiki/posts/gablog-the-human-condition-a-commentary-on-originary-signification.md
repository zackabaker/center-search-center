## Summary

This essay grounds sign-making in three functional conditions: the possibility of deception (which presupposes an [[originary scene]] of absolute trust), the invocation of a central presence or intelligence through prayer, and hypothetical prediction of audience response. The author argues that [[grammar]] itself emerges from embedded imperatives—divine commands that have become habituated into rule-following. Language is not arbitrary convention but a sedimented prayer: conscientiously following rules is an act of faith that when rules fail, the deeper imperatives they preserve will guide us rightly.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary scene]] — The founding moment of human signification where attention converges on a central object prior to truth/falsehood
- [[Sign]] — A functional unit requiring surety (someone vouching for an object's presence), invocation of a center, and audience hypothesis
- [[Prayer]] — An imperative directed at central intelligence; the grammatical element of all semiotics
- [[Grammar]] — Habituated imperatives woven into transactional fabric; preserved divine commands made ordinary
- [[Imperative]] — The foundational speech act underlying all language; moderated through habit into rule-following
- [[Surety]] — The tacit trust that someone stands guarantee for what a sign indicates; the condition of meaningfulness
- [[Declarative]] — Propositional truth-claims possible only *after* the originary scene

## Notable Passages

> "Every sign, to be meaningful, or to work, must have its audience presuppose someone to stand in surety for some material or immaterial object of the sign."

> "Conscientiously following the rules, including the construction of ideal or model modes of rule following, is a form of prayer and faith that the right or needful thing to do when the rules fall short will be made present to me."

> "The originary scene itself is, indeed, beyond truth and falsehood—that some central object is indicated is simply constitutive of the scene; to put it another way, no object, no convergence of attention, no scene, and therefore no lie."

## Questions Raised

1. If all grammar is sedimented prayer, how do we distinguish between imperatives that *should* be internalized and those that represent oppressive constraint? Where is the ethical boundary?

2. Can the author's theory of surety explain the function of signs in purely anonymous or digital contexts where no identifiable person vouches for an object's "thereness"?

3. Does the hypothesis about audience response (the third condition) ultimately reduce to prediction, or does it carry irreducible [[Violent Imaginaries|imaginative]] weight that precedes calculability?
