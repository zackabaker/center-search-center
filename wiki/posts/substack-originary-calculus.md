## Summary

Gans argues that [[deferral]]—the prevention of mimetic crisis through coordinated restraint—must be reconceived as a form of agency rather than its negation. Drawing on calculus and infinitesimals, he shows that actions gain their meaning relative to the imminence of crisis they defer, creating fractal, nested structures of action. The crucial distinction lies between those who merely mimic already-established deferral fields (late-comers, parvenus) and those who *found* the field itself, the latter generating rich transactions between [[implicit]] and [[explicit]] meaning that anticipate and potentially reverse the exhaustion of the deferral regime.

## Key Concepts

- [[Deferral]]: Prevention of mimetic crisis through coordinated restraint; the originary human gesture made positive.
- [[Mimetic crisis]]: The generalized grasping and erosion of boundaries that deferral forestalls.
- [[Agency]]: Reconceived not as intention-driven action but as the measure of crisis-prevention relative to imminence.
- [[Implicit-explicit transactions]]: The gap between what is formally said and what is communicated; widest in founding gestures.
- [[Fractal action]]: Actions nested within actions, affecting non-linear cascades through recalibration of crisis-imminence.
- [[Founding gesture]]: The originary act that establishes a field of deferral, distinct from late-coming mimicry.
- [[Field exhaustion]]: The devolution when late-comers and bureaucrats displace founders; signals the appearance of [[victimary]] logic.

## Notable Passages

> "You're 'always already' doing things any articulation of doing and happening has its origin in a not doing so as to prevent something from happening."

> "In the case of founding the field, you create and allow for a wide range of transactions between implicit and explicit: there is much more to what everybody is saying in this case."

> "I'm writing this for those who want to anticipate, delay and reverse such developments, beginning with the founding gesture itself—in other words, those interested in working in the field of derivatives."

## Questions Raised

1. If deferral is always calculative—adjusting gesture to crisis-imminence—can we distinguish genuine [[declarative]] speech (founding) from strategic mimicry without collapsing into endless suspicion about motives?

2. How does the shift from "reading signs" (stars, entrails) to "narrative structure" (intentional action) map onto the transition from founding to late-coming deferral regimes? Is modernity the age of narrative exhaustion?

3. Can the "field of derivatives" be taught, or is anticipating field-exhaustion and reversing it an irreducibly singular, originary capacity?
