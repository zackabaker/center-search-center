## Summary

The Left's defining characteristic is its endless pursuit of equality, which necessarily generates perpetual activism because sameness in one dimension inevitably foregrounds differences in another. Rather than treating equality as a measure within specific cooperative spaces, the Left projects equality onto society as a whole through the state apparatus, creating a bureaucratic hunt for unclassifiable differences. This drive is ultimately rooted in [[resentment]] of the sovereign center and produces what the author calls "violence against reality"—the violent erasure of permanent human differences, particularly sex, that resurface in new forms.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Equality]]** — The Left's foundational obsession: sameness across all dimensions, impossible to achieve without generating new inequalities
- **[[Resentment]]** — The emotional engine driving citizens to erect states that encode their own grievance against centralized power
- **[[Victimary thinking]]** — An intensification of rights-based equality logic, treating all differences as potential oppressions
- **[[Bureaucracy]]** — The state apparatus that catalogs and accounts for the population, necessary precondition for leftism
- **[[Center]]** — The sovereign point from which equality is measured; the Left's implicit target
- **[[Difference]]** — Permanent human variations (sex, knowledge, ability) that equality rhetoric treats as suspect anomalies
- **[[Pluralism]]** — Non-hierarchical proliferation of specific-purpose communities with limited authority—antidote to leftism

## Notable Passages

> "It is impossible to treat people equally in one way without treating them unequally in other ways."

> "The violent attempt to make one of the more obvious and permanent differences among individuals, that of sex, irrelevant, has no end in sight."

> "The only way to remain immune to leftism is to bind equality of treatment up with shared obligations within forms of cooperation serving specific purposes...within a pluralist frame in which no form of cooperation has authority over any other."

## Questions Raised

2. Does the author's critique apply equally to all forms of egalitarianism, or only to state-sponsored redistributive equality divorced from specific cooperative purposes?
