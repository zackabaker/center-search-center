## Summary

This post establishes a methodological framework for understanding history through GA's core insight: reality consists fundamentally of [[event]]s, not inevitable processes. Each [[event]] generates a [[sign]] that closes a crisis born from [[mimetic rivalry]], and subsequent events either iterate within that sign's dispensation or transcend it by producing a new sign. The author argues that in our current period of "compressed history," this event-centered analysis is essential for understanding how contingent human action—not historical necessity—shapes crises and their resolutions, particularly regarding the [[victimary]] axis.

## Key Concepts

- [[Event]]: An occurrence that cannot be explained by what precedes it, yet defines everything that follows; generates a [[sign]]
- [[Sign]]: The closure of an event-crisis; iterated through subsequent events to establish new centers of sacrality
- [[Originary hypothesis]]: The foundational GA claim that human culture emerges from a single generative scene of [[sign]]-making
- [[Mimetic rivalry]]: Competing desires and sacred claims that generate crises requiring new signs to defer violence
- [[Compressed history]]: A period where institutions no longer predictably channel events; agents lack clear advantage
- [[Victimary axis]]: The post-War sign structure organizing contemporary crises around victim-testimony and guilt
- [[Center]]: The sacred locus established by a founding sign, not to be touched or approached

## Notable Passages

> "Nothing is happening now because it has to happen, because the next stage of history demands it; everything is happening because of those who are very determined to make it happen, against others determined to make something else happen."

> "An event is over, is 'closed,' once it issues a sign which is then iterated within other events, which either remain within the dispensation provided by that sign, or produce a new sign and therefore transcend that previous, epoch-making, event."

> "The market system hasn't just flowed naturally from more rudimentary forms of reciprocity and sociability… it had to be discovered, and this could have only happened through a series of events which could just as easily have not taken place."

## Questions Raised

1. How do we distinguish genuinely epoch-making events (that generate new signs) from merely significant events that iterate existing signs—especially in real time, before iterative events disclose the sign's efficacy?

2. If "compressed history" means institutions no longer channel events predictably, what new institutional forms (if any) might restore such channeling without recreating the rigidity that made the previous institutions obsolete?

3. What would constitute a sign transcending the [[victimary]] axis, and how would we recognize it iteratively before it becomes apparent?
