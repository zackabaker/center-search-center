## Summary

This essay traces the etymology of "sample" back through "example" to its Latin root *exemplum* ("that which is taken out"), revealing how the word encodes the entire history of sacred centrality in human communities. The author argues that "sample" better captures how language functions as a [[sign]] within broader inquiry, while its genealogy—from ritual distribution around a sacred object, through mimetic crisis and scapegoating, to modern scientific sampling—allegorizes humanity's progressive distancing from the [[originary scene]]. The sacred object's history mirrors the concept's evolution: from shared presence confirming equality, to the Big Man's monopoly of distribution, to the neutral, representational status of the sample in contemporary epistemology.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary scene]]: The primal moment of human [[deferral]] around a sacred central object, where community members collectively affirm its significance through ritual.
- [[Sample]]: A part taken from a whole that reveals the constitution of that whole; functionally replaces "sign" or "utterance" while situating language within broader fields of inquiry.
- [[Sacred object]]: The center around which early communities gathered; its sacrality affirmed through equal distribution and eventual exception-making.
- [[Mimesis]]: The provision of models to follow or avoid, implicating both pedagogy and the risk of [[mimetic crisis]].
- [[Big Man]]: The figure who usurps the center and monopolizes distribution, introducing layers of mediation and distance from the originary equality.
- [[Deferral]]: The progressive distancing of communities from direct access to the center, mediated through increasingly complex systems of representation.
- [[Mimetic crisis]]: The danger inherent when the central exception becomes a potential victim, requiring scapegoating resolution.

## Notable Passages

> "The Big Man's 'usurpation' of the center introduced the first distancing of the community from the object—now, the object is only available through the Big Man's distribution."
