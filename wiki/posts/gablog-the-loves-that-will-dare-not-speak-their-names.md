## Summary

This essay critiques the disproportionate political attention lavished on same-sex marriage while genuine crises—budgetary, border control, foreign policy—go unaddressed. The author examines a libertarian counter-proposal to remove government from marriage entirely, replacing state oversight with private contracts and arbitration. However, this radical solution would create dangerous social bifurcation: those opting into disciplined private court systems versus those defaulting to informal arrangements, informal clan structures, and nanny-state intervention. The text exposes how victimocratic obsessions with pseudo-inequalities mask structural failures in governance.

## Key Concepts

- [[Victimocracy]]: A system organized around endless prosecution of fabricated victimizations and stereotyped victimizers
- [[Declarative culture]]: Power exercised through public declaration and consensus manufacture rather than genuine deliberation
- [[Center]]: The originary locus of social meaning-making now captured by victimary progressivism
- [[Contract]]: The voluntary mutual obligation that replaces sacred/traditional bonds in rational systems
- [[Sovereignty]]: The capacity to enforce rules; here, the question of who holds enforceable power
- [[Deferential order]]: Social stability based on mutual recognition rather than state coercion

## Notable Passages

"Only those issues of concern to the victimocracy get addressed expeditiously, but the only issues of concern to the victimocracy are those pseudo-inequalities that allow them to conduct unending simulated Nuremberg-style show trials."

"Once marriage can be same-sex, it can be anything—perhaps anything the government says but, ultimately, anything anyone says."

"The old system, especially once abandoned by the responsible and self-sufficient, will devolve into some combination of quasi-totalitarian nanny state rule... and renewed clan or gang systems."
