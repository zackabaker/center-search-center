## Summary

This brief notice announces reflections on the GATE (Generative Anthropology Thinking Event) conference held in Vancouver in late July. Gans frames the gathering as a significant moment for GA practitioners to gather and think together. The piece gestures toward a fuller Chronicle discussing the event's implications, available at the UCLA Anthropoetics site. It signals the importance of communal intellectual gathering for GA scholarship.

## Key Concepts

- [[GATE conference]] – A dedicated gathering space for GA scholars and practitioners to convene and think collectively
- [[thinking event]] – A moment of shared intellectual practice grounded in GA's originary insights
- [[center]] – The organizing locus around which community assembles and meaning is generated
- [[dialogue]] – The reciprocal exchange necessary for developing anthropological thought
- [[community]] – The social body that sustains and renews GA inquiry

## Questions Raised

1. What makes a "thinking event" distinct from standard academic conferences—and how does GA's originary framework reshape what intellectual gathering can accomplish?

2. How does the physical centering of GA practitioners in Vancouver create conditions for insights that distributed, online scholarship might miss or differently produce?

3. What is the relationship between GATE's existence as an institutional gathering and the sustainability of GA as a living practice rather than a historical archive?
