# Basics

## Summary

This post examines the 2008-2009 financial crisis through a GA lens, arguing that mainstream accounts obscure what actually occurred: not a genuine market failure but the collapse of a speculative system built on lending money that didn't exist to people who couldn't repay it. The author suggests the real "crisis" is that such unsustainable practices can no longer continue—a blessing, not a disaster. He further compares the Left's opportunistic use of economic emergency to Bush's post-9/11 expansion of power, questioning whether either genuinely prioritizes stated goals or merely exploits crisis for political gain. The post ultimately argues that a sustainable [[free market]] requires ethical [[deferral]] and shared responsibility; without it, accumulated [[resentment]] will destabilize the system.

## Key Concepts

- [[Free market]]: The most effective mechanism yet invented for deferring and recirculating resentments through voluntary exchange
- [[Deferral]]: The postponement of immediate gratification; foundational to market stability and ethical economic behavior
- [[Resentment]]: The accumulated social friction that threatens system stability when not adequately deferred or circulated
- [[Crisis]]: Often used as pretext for power expansion rather than genuine response to structural problems
- [[Declarative culture]]: The use of official narratives (color-coded alerts, emergency declarations) to maintain appearances of normality while expanding state power
- [[Protestant ethic]]: The cultural-ethical substrate that enables market economies to function without generating unsustainable resentment
- [[Speculative economy]]: Lending/purchasing based on future possibilities rather than present value—inherently unstable

## Notable Passages

> "People will no longer be able to lend money they don't have to people who won't be able to pay it back and people won't be able to buy, again with money they don't have, 'assets' that represent only possibilities based on speculative accounts of future economic developments. As far as I'm concerned, that's a blessing, not a crisis."

> "The market GA supports is one in which everyone takes on their share of deferral, participating in the Weberian 'Protestant ethic'—only with such ethical support will the market economy not end up generating more resentments than it can bear."

> "What is impossible is anything that would subordinate procedures and the news cycle to some externally determined purpose."
