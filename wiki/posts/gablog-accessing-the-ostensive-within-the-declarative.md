## Summary

This essay traces how [[declarative]] language arose by deferring and distancing us from the [[ostensive]] and [[imperative]] dimensions of human interaction. The declarative creates a world of observed objects without direct appropriation, but the further it moves from ostensive-imperative reality, the more layers of mediation become necessary—a logic that grounds liberal culture. The author proposes recovering an ostensive dimension within declarative speech by marking the [[attentional]] and [[disciplinary]] space of utterance itself, healing the fundamental split between "what" is said and "where/when/who" says it.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Declarative]]**: Language that defers action and creates a world of objects observed without appropriation; born from prohibition on imperative action.
- **[[Ostensive]]**: Direct gesture toward a present object; the foundation of language that declarative culture simultaneously depends on and suppresses.
- **[[Imperative]]**: The demand or command that declarative language initially defers through negation.
- **[[Attentionality]]**: The disciplinary space of attention within which all linguistic reference is embedded and must be marked.
- **[[Post-literate]]**: A condition in which humanity moves beyond print's grip on declarative culture toward recognition of ostensive-imperative dimensions.
- **[[Upclining]]**: The performance or management of self-referential paradoxes within subjectivity.
- **[[Total sign]]**: The post-sacrificial human being whose utterance marks both content and the attentional scene of its utterance.

## Notable Passages

> "The declarative is born in terror of the imperative and, by extension, the ostensive, the latter of which it produces a virtual version of."

> "The attempt here is to embed an ostensive dimension in the declarative in the form of a marker of the disciplinary space of attentionality that all the references made possible in the declarative depend upon."

> "A linguistic problem requires a linguistic solution or, more precisely, deferral."

## Questions Raised

1. How can we practically mark [[attentionality]] within utterance without collapsing back into biographical or performative metadata?
2. Does recognizing the "Mobius strip obverse" between content and scene of utterance require a new grammar, or merely a change in consciousness?
3. If declarative culture inevitably generates suppressed ostensive-imperative returns, can we design institutions that accommodate rather than deny these eruptions?
