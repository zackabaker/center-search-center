## Summary

This essay diagnoses America's post-9/11 trajectory as a choice between two paths: either exposing and discrediting [[victimary discourse]] through defense of liberal principles, or accelerating societal unraveling. The author argues that the 2008 election of Obama—characterized as a symbolic gesture disconnected from serious policy—represented a capitulation that enabled the metastization of victimary thinking while simultaneously sparking the Tea Party's inward turn. The piece concludes with a call for theoretical clarity through radical simplification: understanding victimary thinking as a heretical deviation within liberal democracy itself.

## Key Concepts

- [[Victimary discourse]]: An unlimited system of claims-making that paradoxically produces the greatest number of victims by perpetuating grievance cycles
- [[Liberal democracy]]: Understood as itself a heretical form of Christian thought, vulnerable to victimary corruption
- [[Resentment]]: The emotional substrate that prevents clear thinking; must be sharpened rather than suppressed to achieve theoretical clarity
- [[Declarative culture]]: The capacity to speak truth and name reality outside habituated expectations
- [[American sovereignty]]: The question of whether America will pursue global responsibility or focus on self-preservation
- [[Hologram presidency]]: A leader whose legitimacy rests on symbolic abstraction rather than substantive vision

## Notable Passages

"The only viable response to the unlimited victimary claims inherent in the attack was to defend the victims of the victims, which could in turn only be done in the name of liberal principles."

"How do we wrench ourselves outside of the limitations imposed by our resentments? Maybe by sharpening those resentments and shaking off the habits of thought which normalize them."

"Victimary thinking is a heretical form of liberal democratic ideology; liberal democratic ideology is just a heretical form of Christianity; while Christianity is itself a Jewish heresy."

1. Can victimary thinking be theoretically discredited without simultaneously losing the moral resources that liberal democracy depends upon for legitimacy?

2. Is the Tea Party's inward turn actually a viable response, or does it simply cede the question of global order to tyrannies the author sees as inevitable?
