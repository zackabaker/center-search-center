## Summary

This essay traces how the Holocaust, legitimately understood as a warning against persecution, has been rhetorically weaponized into a framework that automatically treats any less-powerful group as morally justified victims and any more-powerful group as oppressors. The author argues that political correctness has inverted moral reasoning by assuming weakness correlates with righteousness, leading to the justification of violence against designated "Nazis"—a label applied to anyone opposing progressive orthodoxy. The piece questions whether this victimary logic actually protects the vulnerable or merely creates new forms of tyranny.

## Key Concepts

- [[Victimary culture]]: The contemporary assumption that less powerful groups are automatically persecuted and thus morally justified in their demands
- [[Scapegoating]]: The rhetorical mechanism by which societies identify a powerless group to bear collective blame
- [[Holocaust rhetoric]]: The deployment of Nazi/Holocaust language to frame current political opponents as equivalent to genocidal regimes
- [[Power inversion]]: The reversal of moral criteria such that weakness becomes evidence of righteousness
- [[Declarative culture]]: The establishment of truth through assertion rather than evidence or rational argument
- [[Mimetic desire]]: The underlying resentment that drives persecutory narratives across history

## Questions Raised

1. If the lesson of the Holocaust is accurate moral reasoning about persecution, how do we distinguish genuine oppression from rhetorical claims to victimhood without defaulting to either absolute relativism or to criteria that will inevitably be contested?

2. When weaker groups gain institutional power (academia, media, HR departments), does the original victimary logic still apply, or does it require continuous redefinition of who counts as "oppressed"?

3. Can [[Generative Anthropology|GA]]'s focus on the [[originary scene]] help us understand why humans are so susceptible to victimary narratives across historical periods?
