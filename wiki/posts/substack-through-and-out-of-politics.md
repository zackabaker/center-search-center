## Summary

The occupant of the [[center]] exercises power primarily through selecting successors who will themselves select successors—a recursive function inseparable from the center's [[originary ritual]] role. This successor-selection imperative is not merely normative but descriptive: all authority-holders unconsciously practice it, whether honestly or through institutional constraint. The author argues that understanding [[juridical order]] as fundamentally rooted in [[conquest]] and territorial distribution provides the key to redirecting institutional power toward "open source Messianism"—discovering and promoting personnel capable of restoring a juridical order undermined by contemporary vandalism of legal systems.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Center]]** – The site of distribution and ritual authority from which succession decisions emanate
- **[[Sovereign selection]]** – The recursive process by which each center-occupant selects the next occupant
- **[[Originary ritual]]** – The foundational ceremonial function from which political authority derives its continuous legitimacy
- **[[Juridical order]]** – A system of deference and adjudication that prevents vendetta-resolution through law
- **[[Conquest]]** – The foundational act (imperial or feudal) establishing territorial nomos and authority distribution
- **[[Vandalism]]** – The systematic dismantling of juridical institutions, turning law into vendetta machinery
- **[[Open source Messianism]]** – A strategy of identifying and promoting personnel capable of restoring juridical order

## Notable Passages

> "Selecting the successor in perpetuity is intrinsically bound up with the center as the site of distribution, in which it remains continuous with its originary ritual function."

> "The occupant of the center (perhaps I should just start referring to that role as 'the occupier') is the highest judge, which is in fact a very pervasive and, next to that of presiding over or being the object of sacrifice, perhaps the most ancient function of the monarch."

> "Conquest, which is to say war, lies at the heart of the juridical, because whether we are thinking in terms of imperial conquest or the ascension of one feudal lord through the subjection of the rest, it is through conquest that the original nomos distributing territory and authority emerges."
