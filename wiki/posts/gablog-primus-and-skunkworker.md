## Summary

This piece traces how the logic of equality-as-sameness, once grounded in Christian moral anthropology and protection from [[scapegoating]], becomes pathological in post-Christian liberal states where only the state remains as the shared [[center]]. When equality is measured against a central power rather than God, the imperative to discover and remedy "new inequalities" becomes infinite and self-generating. The author shows how this creates a permanent left-right cycle: the left perpetually uncovers overlooked differences to be equalized, while the right defends existing equalities—a dynamic that destabilizes the social order itself.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Equality]]** — sameness in some respect; requires a measure and implicit reduction to a center
- **[[Center]]** — the shared locus of measurement and authority; shifts from God to the state in modernity
- **[[Scapegoating]]** — mimetically-motivated violence masked as social stability; disclosed and proscribed by Christian revelation
- **[[Mimetic rivalry]]** — the logic beneath scapegoating; unchecked when equality loses its transcendent anchor
- **[[Victimary progressivism]]** — the left's perpetual discovery of new inequalities demanding state remedy
- **[[Liberal democracy]]** — post-Christian order attempting to maintain Christian protections without Christian ground
- **[[Moral equality]]** — the Christian concept of equal worth before God; unsustainable when secularized

## Notable Passages

> "Sameness implies some form of measurement so that the objects in question can be reduced to that measure... In social terms, this entails reduction to a single center."

> "The Christian revelation discredits scapegoating by displaying what we can anachronistically call the 'bad faith' of such means of maintaining social stability."

> "If equality is now equality in relation to the state as central power, and the state itself is driven by the imperative to ensure that more citizens, and more aspects of their life, are equalized, the benefit of the doubt will always be given to whomever can claim to have uncovered a previously neglected form of inequality."

## Questions Raised

1. If Christian moral equality was genuinely grounded in transcendent meaning, can secular liberal orders sustain any stable principle of equality, or are they necessarily driven toward infinite equalization and eventual collapse?

2. Does the perpetual discovery of "new inequalities" reflect genuine injustice, or is it a structural feature of state-centered equality that generates resentment as a form of power?

3. What form of [[center]] could ground equality without either invoking transcendence (Christianity) or unleashing the state's totalizing imperative to measure and equalize all social life?
