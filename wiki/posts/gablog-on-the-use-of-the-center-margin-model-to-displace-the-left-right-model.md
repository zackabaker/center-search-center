## Summary

This essay proposes replacing the left-right political axis with a [[center-margin]] framework grounded in generative anthropology. Gans argues that all human history can be understood as a series of experiments in determining what figure should occupy the center—each retrieval of the [[originary scene]] configuration opens new historical possibilities. Rather than viewing power as subordinate to abstract principles (freedom, equality), the absolutist project recognizes that power itself determines meaning. The [[center-margin model]] allows analysis of everything from macro-historical events to micro-social praxis by treating each as a sovereign imaginary positing a relation between center and margin.

## Key Concepts

- [[Center-margin model]]: Replaces left-right political axis; analyzes history as experiments in centering
- [[Originary scene]]: The aborted gesture of appropriation; retrieved under conditions created by previous retrievals
- [[Power]]: Either subordinated to higher principles (leading to endless struggle) or self-determining (absolutist move)
- [[Sovereignty]]: Any social praxis involves a sovereign imaginary positing center-margin relations
- [[Thisness]]: Wierzbicka prime; the sheer "this-ness" of the object at the center, extractable at expense of the name
- [[Anthropomorphization]]: Making the centered Object a source of commands, practices, and language for the community
- [[Mediation]]: Money as example of replacing ritual center; enables new distributive principles based on equality

## Notable Passages

> "Everything specific to this object goes into making up its name, and insofar as that name compels attention to and hence sustains the center the anthropomorphization of that Object will be the source of all commands, practices and even the language of the community."

> "The retrieval of the originary configuration involves the extraction of more 'thisness' at the expense of the name, which further means of sustaining attention other than those maintained by ritualized repetition."

> "Money creates a new and more indirect centered configuration under conditions where the ritual center has been usurped by the Big Man."

## Questions Raised

1. How does extracting "thisness" from the name practically function in contemporary institutions, and what replaces ritualized repetition in secular democracies?

2. If the left-right model obscures the deeper center-margin dynamics, how should we analyze actual political movements and their hidden centering gestures?

3. What determines whether a new center retrieves the originary scene more authentically, or merely opens destructive historical possibilities?
