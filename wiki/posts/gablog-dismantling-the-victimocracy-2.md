## Summary

This post examines post-victimary discourse through the lens of Vox Day's realist political analysis, distinguishing between white nationalist sentiment and libertarian anti-SJW strategy. The author argues that genuinely post-victimary thought abandons victimary hand-wringing and abstract liberal principles in favor of evolutionary realism, game theory, and acknowledgment of mimetic group preferences. While appreciating this discourse's clarity, the author notes it misses deeper questions about why Western civilization adopted victimary logic in the first place.

## Key Concepts

- [[victimocracy]] — The regime of victimary politics where marginalized identity groups claim moral authority through claimed suffering
- [[post-victimary discourse]] — Language that rejects victimary logic and speaks through probabilities, group preferences, and mimetic contagion rather than moral absolutes
- [[mimetic contagion]] — The spread of desires and decisions through imitation and social pressure within groups
- [[praxeology]] — Application of free market principles and rational action theory to all human behavior
- [[victimary hand-wringing]] — Moralistic appeals to guilt and protection that characterize victimocratic rhetoric
- [[declarative culture]] — The abstract principles (liberalism, democracy, Judeo-Christian morality) that legitimize but obscure actual power structures
