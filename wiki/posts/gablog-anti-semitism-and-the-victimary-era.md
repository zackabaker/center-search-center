## Summary

This paper situates contemporary anti-semitism within Generative Anthropology's framework, tracing it from the paradox of Jewish monotheism (universal revelation granted to a chosen people) through modern resentment toward Jewish "principles" of individual freedom and universal morality. The Holocaust marks the advent of the "victimary era," wherein the perpetrator/victim/bystander configuration becomes the primary deferral mechanism against genocidal violence. Yet this creates a paradox: Israel's nation-state appears to replicate the very "Nazism" the victimary system forbids, while universalizing victimary claims requires breaking the Jewish monopoly on Holocaust guilt—positioning Jews once again in structural contradiction.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary hypothesis]]: The first sign emerged in a mimetic crisis arrested by a gesture of aborted appropriation; representation defers violence.
- [[Mimetic desire]]: The self-destructive convergence of the group upon a common object, source of rivalry and resentment.
- [[Representation]]: The deferral of violence through language, ritual, belief, morality, and art—all intrinsically fragile.
- [[Monotheism paradox]]: Universal divine knowledge revealed to a single chosen people, generating resentment across religions.
- [[Victimary era]]: Post-Holocaust system in which the perpetrator/victim/bystander model becomes the primary cultural deferral mechanism.
- [[Jewish principle]]: In modernity, the association of Jews with individual freedom, universal morality, and the dissolution of traditional authority.
- [[Omnicidal potential]]: The Holocaust as revelation of humanity's capacity for total annihilation, foreclosing repetition in the nuclear age.

## Notable Passages

> "Representation, then, is the deferral of violence, as is, therefore, all of culture. History is the ongoing process of preserving and, where necessary and possible, replacing such means of deferral."

> "The spread of monotheism, already inscribed in its universalistic origin, could hardly take place other than through resentment towards those who both gave this God to humanity and 'selfishly' claimed an exclusive relation to Him."

> "This, of course, creates an incentive to make one's own grievance fit that model...the Jews are once again placed in a paradoxical position."

## Questions Raised

1. How can the victimary system accommodate competing claims to victim status without collapsing into relativism, or does its universalization necessarily require dismantling the very distinctions it was constructed to preserve?

2. If the [[Jewish principle]] of universal morality is both the origin of monotheism's resentment and the foundation of modern individual freedom, can these be separated, or are they structurally inseparable?

3. Does the nuclear age fundamentally alter the relationship between [[representation]] and [[mimetic desire]], making traditional deferral mechanisms obsolete?
