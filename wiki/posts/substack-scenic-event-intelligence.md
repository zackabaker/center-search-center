## Summary

This essay applies [[generative anthropology|GA]] to information theory by mapping the [[scene]]/[[event]] duality onto noise-filtering in communication systems. Gans argues that the [[originary scene]] establishes perfect but paradoxical information—everyone knows the others desire the central object, yet no one knows who will act. The [[aborted gesture]] resolves this by suspending appropriative desire, converting raw information into [[intelligence]]. The richest human exchange occurs when participants maintain full awareness of possible violence while remaining certain it won't occur. The essay concludes that scenes and events can only be bounded by other scenes and events, creating an infinite regress of framing.

## Key Concepts

- [[Scene]] — a bounded space filtering mimetic action toward a central object
- [[Event]] — the temporal unfolding profiled against the scene; what happens within or disrupts the scene
- [[Originary scene]] — the first human scene where the [[aborted gesture]] inaugurates language and culture
- [[Aborted gesture]] — the suspended appropriative move that converts potential violence into signification
- [[Intelligence]] — information enriched by awareness of all possible but suspended actions
- [[Mimesis]] — imitative desire oriented toward the central object and toward other desirers
- [[Perfect information]] — the paradoxical state where everyone knows everyone wants the same thing, creating maximum entropy
- [[Center]] — the focal point organizing the scene and concentrating attention

## Notable Passages

> "The aborted gesture of appropriation, then, restores that fleeting condition of perfect information in a more sustainable form."

> "The richest conversation, then, would be one in which both participants are completely aware—increasingly aware, as the conversation proceeds—of all that they might do to each other, while also being increasingly certain that they won't do any of it."

> "To close one event, then, is to open another—the event of closing the previous event. To identify the boundaries of the scene is to construct another scene within or surrounding that scene."

## Questions Raised

1. If every scene boundary requires a meta-scene to define it, do we not face infinite regress? At what level does the circle close, or does it?

2. How does this model account for scenes that *fail* to suspend appropriative violence—riots, wars, revolutions where the [[aborted gesture]] never takes hold?

3. Can we operationalize "awareness of all possible actions" as a measurable feature of communication quality, or does it remain a phenomenological ideal?
