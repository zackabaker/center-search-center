## Summary

This essay proposes that the [[victimary]] spell can be broken through genuine [[alliance]] between the powerful and the marginalized against shared enemies, establishing bounded spaces with shared [[sacrality]] where [[asymmetry]] becomes generative rather than resentful. The author diagnoses why this strategy—attempted in the "Bush Doctrine"—failed, and traces how progressive hegemony from 1932-1968 collapsed under pressure from the Left and subsequent cultural revolutions. The core problem is that contemporary elites articulate only empty phrases ("cleaning up the mess") rather than genuine visions of futurity.

## Key Concepts

- [[Victimary]]: A system organized around claims of victimhood that generates resentment rather than intellectual content
- [[Alliance]]: Asymmetrical but genuine partnership against common enemies, constitutive of lasting covenants
- [[Asymmetry]]: Inevitable human inequality that becomes productive when bounded by shared rules and objects of desire
- [[Sacrality]]: The shared sacred center that makes reciprocal exchange and desire-formation possible
- [[Resentment]]: The emotional mode underlying victimary claims, blocking access to genuine political vision
- [[Declarative culture]]: The ability to articulate where one actually sees things going, absent in contemporary progressivism

## Notable Passages

> "The establishment of symmetrical relationships is not meant to eliminate [asymmetries] but to establish arenas with a shared sacrality and prescribed objects of desire that are open to all."

> "Everything then, can be described as 'cleaning up the mess,' 'turning a corner,' 'restoring our image'—i.e., phrases devoid of information, even the involuntary kind one provides when stating any view."

> "They had seen us through a depression, and won the largest and most important war in history... and ushered in a new era of racial equality."

## Questions Raised

1. Why does the contemporary left seem constitutively unable to articulate positive visions of futurity, instead recycling negative framings ("undoing," "restoration")?

2. If asymmetry is inevitable and potentially generative, what specific institutional designs could properly bound it within shared [[sacrality]] without collapsing into either enforced equality or renewed resentment?

3. Can the alliance-model be recovered after the Bush Doctrine's failure, or has the victimary logic become so entrenched in media and universities that only crisis could dislodge it?
