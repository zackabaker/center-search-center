## Summary

Drawing on Thorstein Veblen's *Theory of the Leisure Class*, this post explores the tension between two economic dispositions: the predatory (associated with hunters, warriors, and modern financiers) and the artisanal (associated with workmanship and causal reasoning). The author argues that Veblen inverts GA's understanding of human origins—GA shows that [[invidious distinction]] is fundamental to all human society, while the [[instinct of workmanship]] and [[aesthetic]] refinement are secondary developments that channel and mitigate the violence inherent in status competition. The post suggests modern market economies reveal themselves as fundamentally predatory structures dressed in quasi-peaceful guise.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Invidious distinction]]**: Status differentiation between individuals that necessarily precedes and shapes all human community
- **[[Predatory disposition]]**: The hunter/warrior impulse manifest in market speculation, finance, and military conquest
- **[[Instinct of workmanship]]**: The drive toward practical causality and technical mastery; secondary to but potentially civilizing
- **[[Conspicuous leisure]]**: Display of useless activity as status marker; leisure class assertion of superiority
- **[[Originary gesture]]**: The founding sign-act that channels [[invidious distinction]] into communal form through aesthetic symmetry
- **[[Pecuniary interest]]**: Market-driven motivation rooted in conquest and status rather than functional utility

## Notable Passages

> "Exchanges on the market are indistinguishable from fraud, an essentially predatory relation to others—all lines separating fraudulent form legitimate exchange are essentially contingent and pragmatic."

> "All human existence is based on 'invidious distinction,' which enables us to reverse Veblen's hierarchy of the two economic types."

> "The gesture needs to 'propose' some symmetry or harmonization of the group in order to take hold, but is nevertheless secondary to the felt need to interrupt the imminent violence itself."

## Questions Raised

1. If modern finance is fundamentally predatory, can market economies ever be reformed toward genuine workmanship, or are they structurally incapable of mitigating their own violence?

2. How does the [[aesthetic]] function of the [[originary gesture]]—its need to propose symmetry—relate to the modern erosion of shared aesthetic standards and the rise of [[dedifferentiation]]?

3. What would an economy organized primarily around the [[instinct of workmanship]] actually look like, given that [[invidious distinction]] cannot be eliminated from human social life?
