## Summary

This post adapts Thomas Kuhn's model of scientific revolution to generative anthropology, arguing that paradigm shifts require replacing entire conceptual constellations organized around a given center. Whereas natural sciences achieve permanent displacement (geocentrism → heliocentrism), human discourse encounters [[epistemological obstacle]]s continuously because lived events demand thought before frameworks are fully available. The author proposes that everyday [[signifying]] mirrors scientific discovery: we are always already "held" by implicit assumptions before reflection crystallizes them. Finally, both scientific experiments and sacred rituals share a structure of enclosed space and organized practice designed to reveal truth through controlled event.

## Key Concepts

- [[Epistemological obstacle]]: Inherited, ungrounded concepts that structure inquiry while remaining invisible to it
- [[Center]] (or center displacement): The organizing principle around which a disciplinary or interpretive constellation arranges itself
- [[Originary scene]]: The primordial scene where the central object holds the assembled through its apparent power
- [[Necessary appearance]]: The inevitable gap between explicit knowledge and what must appear true (Arendt)
- [[Sacred ritual]]: A closed, precisely organized practice that generates revelatory events, structurally homologous to scientific experiment
- [[Signifying]]: Everyday acts of making meaning; sampling ongoing events with available but inadequate frames

## Notable Passages

> "What both levels of inquiry have in common is what Gaston Bachelard called 'epistemological obstacles'—concepts grounding a process of inquiry that are themselves ungrounded in anything other than inherited institutional and what we could call 'mythical' imperatives."

> "In human affairs, not everything can be made explicit—indeed, with everything we do make explicit, more implicit assumptions are generated. There is always what Hannah Arendt called a 'necessary appearance.'"

> "I'm always being 'held' in some way before 'reflection' kicks in and, in fact, reflection tightens the grip of whatever holds me."

## Questions Raised

1. If human discourse perpetually generates new epistemological obstacles with each explicit formulation, does GA itself risk becoming an obstacle to future thinking about human origin and meaning-making?

2. How does the [[originary scene]]'s "appearance" that the center holds the assembled differ structurally from Arendt's "necessary appearance" of the rising sun, and what does this difference reveal about the priority of the scenic?

3. Can the homology between scientific experiment and sacred ritual be tightened to explain why certain cultural crises or moments of [[center]] displacement trigger violent rather than peaceful reconfiguration?
