## Summary

This essay reframes absolutism not as a preference for autocratic government, but as an ontological principle: [[sovereignty]] must be conserved and localized in a determinate center. The author argues that all functional polities tacitly acknowledge this during crises, deferring to executive power. Rather than debating whether absolutism is "better," the real argument concerns institutional design—how to make sovereignty clear and secure while potentially allowing procedural constraints (elections, term limits) within the sovereign's self-imposed framework.

## Key Concepts

- [[Sovereignty is Conserved]] — the tautological maxim that power must reside somewhere determinate, not dispersed
- [[Absolute Assumption]] — Collingwood's term for foundational principles (like energy conservation) that are not preferences but structural necessities
- [[Center]] — the locus of decision-making authority that all institutions defer to in genuine emergency
- [[Institutional Design]] — the question of how to structure offices and procedures while respecting sovereignty's indivisibility
- [[Crisis Deferral]] — the tacit acknowledgment across all polities that executive authority supersedes other branches when existential stakes arise
- [[Self-Abnegation of the Sovereign]] — the paradoxical capacity of a sovereign to impose constraints on themselves (e.g., elections) without losing sovereignty

## Notable Passages

"Everyone really agrees with this, because everyone knows when we speak of 'the United States' speaking with 'Germany' we know this means Donald Trump, or someone appointed by Donald Trump, speaking with Angela Merkel."

"If someone is to be sovereign, it were best that sovereignty be clear and secure... if we introduce a rule that the players must vote on the starting line-up, then he isn't really the coach, and we are setting him to fail."

"If we would all defer to the executive in a crisis, we should make that explicit and gear all institutions to readiness to be helpful in serving the executive in a crisis."

## Questions Raised

1. How does the principle of [[sovereignty is conserved]] apply to genuinely decentralized systems (blockchain governance, distributed networks)? Does the principle merely relocate rather than eliminate the sovereign center?

2. If a sovereign can self-impose constraints (elections, term limits), what prevents gradual erosion of sovereignty through institutionalization—where procedural rules eventually supersede the sovereign's will?

3. Does the [[center]] necessarily require *visibility* to function, or can concealed sovereignty (deep state, shadow power) adequately conserve power without the legitimacy that transparent absolutism might provide?
