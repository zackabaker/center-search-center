## Summary

This post analyzes the 2016 US Presidential election as an unprecedented confrontation between [[victimary]] forces (uniting corporate/professional elites with welfare constituencies) and the emerging [[alt-right]], which operates through an ethics of aggressive ridicule rather than respectability. The author argues this asymmetrical struggle will reshape Western politics regardless of outcome, with the alt-right's short-term goal of becoming an undeniable part of public discourse preceding more ambitious long-term aims. The piece examines how the [[victimocracy]] became the ruling ideology and how anti-victimary forces are weaponizing the collapse of [[political correctness]]'s moral authority.

## Key Concepts

- [[victimocracy]]: Alliance of ruling elites and identity-grievance constituencies unified by victimary ideology
- [[alt-right]]: Disparate anti-victimary forces previously isolated, now coordinated through confrontation
- [[Cathedral]]: The unified mainstream culture (including conservative institutions) that enforces orthodox thought through stigma
- [[political correctness]]: A system of moral enforcement relying on magic-word accusations like "racist" to eliminate badthink
- [[declarative imperative]]: The victimocracy's guiding ideology or command structure
- [[ZFG ethics]]: Alt-right stance of ruthless disregard for respectability norms in political combat
- [[normalcy]]: The mobilizing ideal against victimary dominance, appealing to wage-earners and middle strata

## Notable Passages

> "The entire faith of the mainstream culture, again very much including conservatism, is that 'racist' will forever remain a magic word that makes all badthoughts and badthinkers go away. The most obvious strategy of the alt-right, then, is to make a mockery of this faith."

> "The alt-right, and in particular the up-and-comers among them, adhere to an ethics of 'ZFG,' an initialism which... They will gleefully and ruthlessly take what Daniel Greenfield considers the 'low road' in combatting political correctness."
