## Summary

This post retrieves "civilization" as a central analytical category to understand historical periodization and contemporary crisis. The author traces a trajectory from the Renaissance (completing the civilizing process) through modernity (reaping civilization's fruits while forgetting how they were won) to postmodernity (characterized by contradictory anti-civilizational resentments). The current moment represents a sharpening of these resentments—no longer playful but destructive—and demands either a genuine restoration of civilization or recognition of its collapse.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Civilization]]**: The systematic transcendence of violence and internalization of restraint that creates the conditions for human flourishing
- **[[Barbarism]]**: The perennial human tendency toward violence that civilization must continually suppress and distance itself from
- **[[The Civilizing Process]]**: The historical movement through which societies develop habits, manners, and moral frameworks that replace violent addiction with regulated conduct
- **[[Postmodernity]]**: A period characterized by contradictory resentments against civilization itself, both attacking it as disguised barbarism and defending suppressed barbarisms as authentic freedom
- **[[Normalization]]**: The 19th-century internalization of civilizing constraints as therapeutic and educational issues rather than moral imperatives
- **[[Anti-Civilizational Resentment]]**: The paradoxical rejection of Western civilization from within, logically contradictory but politically complementary
- **[[The Enlightenment Forgetting]]**: The projection of the modern subject as naturally pre-social, obscuring the arduous historical work of civilization

## Notable Passages

"Constitutive of a fully developed civilization is the distancing of its denizens from the systematic 'addiction' to violence civilization had to transcend, and the naturalizing of civilized habits."

"These two anti-civilizational resentments are logically contradictory but politically complementary."

"If civilization is restored and those resentments marginalized, we will have our new period; if civilization is destroyed, who will care?"

## Questions Raised

1. Can the [[civilizing process]] be consciously renewed, or does civilization require the forgetting of its own origins to remain stable?

2. What institutional or cultural forms might contain both the defense of civilization and acknowledgment of its perennial vulnerability to barbarism without collapsing into either naive progressivism or reactionary denial?

3. Is the current "sharpening of anti-civilizational pincer movements" reversible through argument alone, or does it require a fundamental reorientation of the [[attentional structure]] through which we organize meaning?
