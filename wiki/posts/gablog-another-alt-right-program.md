# Another Alt Right Program

## Summary

This post analyzes an Alt Right political manifesto, revealing that beneath its surface demands for cultural homogeneity and protection from globalism lies a fundamental (if unstated) demand for an extraordinarily powerful [[state]]. The author argues that all the manifesto's specific grievances—from immigration to pollution to sexual ethics—collapse into a single requirement: a [[sovereign]] entity capable of filtering the world and enforcing communal standards. The critical question becomes whether such a state can be "virtuous," and whether honest discourse about "the good" (in male-female relations, culture, etc.) remains possible within current [[declarative-culture]] constraints.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Sovereignty]]**: The state's absolute power to control borders, markets, and cultural boundaries
- **[[Globalism]]** vs. **[[Nationalism]]**: The antagonism between international corporate systems and nation-state autonomy
- **[[Declarative Culture]]**: The regime of political correctness that prevents honest discussion of standards for "health" and goodness
- **[[Virtue]]**: Standards of right conduct that an absolutist state might enforce
- **[[Political Correctness]]**: A system designed to prevent certain discussions (race, sex, tradition) from reaching productive articulation
- **[[The Good]]**: The dialectical momentum toward recognizing excellence and health once discourse permits it

## Notable Passages

> "All the evils of today's society could really be traced back to 'international corporate oppression'—as is evident from the very odd insistence on 'protection from globalist pollution.'"

> "Such a state would clearly also be powerful enough to force you to associate with anyone it wants, or to mix cultures, or control immigration according to its own sense of the necessary."

> "Once we are talking about the 'good,' the momentum of the dialectic itself will lead us to see as good..."

## Questions Raised

1. **Can a "virtuous absolutist state" exist, or is the demand for state power and the demand for distributed moral judgment inherently contradictory?** The author suggests that once we admit the possibility of discussing "health" in human relations, we've already conceded moral realism—but who enforces these standards without tyranny?

2. **Why does the Alt Right manifesto systematically avoid naming the [[state]], [[power]], and [[sovereignty]] it actually demands?** Is this evasion strategic, or does it reveal something deeper about how modern political discourse obscures its own imperatives?

3. **Can honest discussion of "the good" emerge within a system (political correctness) specifically designed to prevent it, or must [[declarative-culture]] be fundamentally transformed first?**
