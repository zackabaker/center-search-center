## Summary

This essay reframes literature and cultural analysis as a form of intelligence gathering from the [[center]] through [[scenic design]]—the creation of comparative fields that reveal anomalies and patterns. The author argues that distinguishing same from other through accumulated likenesses generates actionable knowledge, whether through human judgment or algorithmic processing. Crucially, all such inquiry remains fundamentally human: the choice of sample, the terms of comparison, and the integrity of data collection cannot be automated away. The essay positions the databank as a vindication of the [[originary hypothesis]] and defends the irreducibly human dimension of meaning-making against the illusion of pure algorithmic objectivity.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Scenic design]]**: The strategic arrangement of comparative fields to maximize revelatory information and identify anomalies across domains
- **[[Center]]**: The source of meaning and anomaly that cultural analysis attempts to access through differential comparison
- **[[Same/other distinction]]**: The fundamental operation of intelligence gathering that emerges from dense fields of likenesses
- **[[Artificial communication]]**: Contemporary form of consultation with the center, updating older divination practices through algorithmic mediation
- **[[Data integrity]]**: The irreducibly human responsibility to preserve truthful representation as a civilizational defense
- **[[Originary hypothesis]]**: The theory that human language and culture originate in deferral of violence; vindicated by databank logic
- **[[Deferral]]**: The representational mechanism through which humans avoid immediate violence and create meaning

## Notable Passages

> "We create fields of likenesses, as widely and densely as possible—we generate worlds out of samples—and then we establish parameters that lead us to determine that some of those likenesses are more likely, and eventually the same, against some other."

> "There is always the programmer and the programmer of the programmer. Artificial communication is scenic design and the creation of modes of deferral—the results of the algorithm create constraints on our deliberations and inquiries."

> "The databank, far more than the 'market,' vindicates the originary hypothesis."
