## Summary

This post argues that American voters' self-identification as "conservative" while electing leftist candidates reveals a fundamental disconnect from political reality. Rather than a "center-right" country, the U.S. exhibits a collective consciousness severed from reliable factual grounding—a phenomenon rooted in the [[victimary]] logic of scapegoating. Voters embrace irreconcilable positions simultaneously: they want traditional values while supporting progressive candidates, and they resist negative information that might disrupt the euphoria of their collective [[declarative]] commitments. The author suggests that contemporary leftism operates purely as negation of Bush-era policies rather than positive articulation of coherent goals.

## Key Concepts

- [[Victimary discourse]] — the all-pervasive social logic that threatens those who deviate from progressive categories with moral condemnation
- [[Scapegoating]] — the mechanism by which collective euphoria follows the successful targeting and deposition of blame
- [[Declarative culture]] — a mode of existence built on utterance and commitment rather than correspondence to reality
- [[Center and periphery]] — the originary spatial structure now inverted, where political reality lies on the periphery of collective consciousness
- [[Detachment from reality]] — the severing of reliable ties to political fact, making voters "unmuggable" by contradictory evidence
- [[Negation]] — the purely oppositional logic ("unlike Bush") that structures leftist policy rationale without positive content

## Notable Passages

> "What if the typical Obama supporter (voter?) is unmuggable? If my previous post is right, and the Obama cultic phenomenon is simply the converse of the unprecedented scapegoating directed towards Bush, then it would make sense to expect a basic detachment from reality to be part of the phenomenon."

> "The core rationale behind what they want to do and why, is it ever anything that couldn't be mapped out in terms of 'unlike Bush, I want…'?"

> "People want to be part of the euphoria promised by the transcendence that follows a successful scapegoating campaign, and the vagueness of the 'ideas' behind the campaign allows them to deposit whatever content they want into it."

1. Can a democratic system sustain itself when voters hold mutually contradictory commitments without experiencing cognitive dissonance or correction?
