## Summary

Eric Gans examines John McGinnis's argument that empirical social science will enable policy consensus by transcending partisan conflict through measurable outcomes. Gans sees this as a new mode of [[deferral]]—temporarily suspending appropriation of power while methodology works itself out. However, he notes that empiricism itself becomes a value-laden policy proposal that privileges transparency, decentralization, and liberty as information-generating mechanisms, ultimately revealing that quantification cannot escape the qualitative conflicts it claims to resolve.

## Key Concepts

- [[Deferral]]: Withholding appropriation of the sacred object (here, policy mechanisms) to allow mediating procedures to establish consensus
- [[Quantification]]: The attempt to render qualitative disputes resolvable through measurable empirical data
- [[Empiricism]]: Social science method claiming objectivity and freedom from partisan manipulation
- [[Declarative Culture]]: The culture that assumes language can establish truth independent of scenic presence
- [[Transparency]]: A value masquerading as a neutral organizing principle that privileges certain social structures
- [[Appropriation]]: The taking-up of the sacred/powerful object that deferral seeks to delay
- [[Information]]: The currency of empirical governance, privileging societies organized for data generation

## Notable Passages

> "We would simply be obliged to put our values to the test, accepting a common measure between opposing values."

> "The new empiricism is ultimately a policy proposal in its own right...those modes of organizing society that generate the most information are to be privileged."

> "McGinnis' call for a new empiricism...favors transparency, the replacement of 'committed' academic research with disinterested inquiry...and a high degree of liberty."

## Questions Raised

- If quantification always requires prior qualitative decisions about what to measure, can empiricism ever truly transcend value conflict, or does it merely displace contestation to earlier stages?
- Does the empiricist privileging of "information-generating" societies constitute a new form of soft totalitarianism—one that rewards transparency and decentralization while marginalizing non-quantifiable forms of knowledge?
- How does [[deferral]] through empirical methodology differ from traditional religious or legal procedures of [[deferral]], and does it actually stabilize consensus or merely obscure power relations?
