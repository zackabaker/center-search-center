## Summary

This essay distinguishes between the [[center]]—the immediate object of attention and consumption on the originary scene—and [[centrality]], the enduring memorial and institutional structures that persist in group memory and praxis. Gans traces how the minimal sign that prevents violence during the sparagmos becomes inscribed in memory, creating the [[Object]] that subtends all future iterations of the scene. The maximal difference between center and centrality marks advanced civilization but opens dangerous possibilities for degeneration; Gans illustrates this through the Iran nuclear deal, where mere "scribbles" gain world-historical weight through protocol and legitimacy structures.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Center]]**: The immediate focal point of collective attention and the consumed or contested object itself.
- **[[Centrality]]**: The enduring institutional and memorial structures that articulate the center to broader social order; the capacity to make distant events significant.
- **[[Object]]** (Lacanian): The imagined central object that sources communal being and becomes target of collective resentment.
- **[[Originary scene]]**: The first human event in which a sign prevents violent seizure of a central object.
- **[[Sparagmos]]**: The frenzied consumption of the central animal; maintained in minimal order by the sign.
- **[[Metalanguage]]**: The self-referential dimension of language; minimal metalanguage shows dependence on differential repetition rather than concealing it.
- **[[Imperium in imperio]]**: Hidden language structures that obscure power relations within seemingly neutral discourse.
- **[[Mimetic crisis]]**: The escalating rivalry for the central object that demands the restraining sign.

## Notable Passages

> "The Object, or centrality beyond center, would be nothing more than this memory and the maintenance of the unity of the repeated event."

> "The object here is some scribbles on a piece of paper; the Object is the alignment of power that follows the ascertaining of international 'legitimacy.'"

> "It's clear that the maximal difference between center and centrality, object and Object, is the mark of a more civilized order. We can also see all the potential for degeneration and de-civilization in this distance."

## Questions Raised

1. How does the transition from minimal to maximal difference between center and centrality occur historically, and what determines whether a civilization stabilizes this distance or degenerates across it?

2. If all Object-formation depends on memorial inscription of the sign, what happens when institutions fail to maintain this memory—does the object collapse back to mere center, or does something more volatile occur?

3. Can the metalinguistic honesty Gans advocates—showing rather than concealing dependence on differential repetition—scale to complex civilizational orders without undermining the legitimacy structures that encode centrality?
