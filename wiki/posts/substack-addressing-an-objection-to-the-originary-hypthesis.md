## Summary

This post examines Gregory Lobo's critique of the [[originary hypothesis]], particularly challenging Eric Gans's formulation that culture emerges functionally to control [[mimetic desire|mimetic violence]]. The author argues that positing an "extinction-level" crisis of violence requiring cultural solution imposes anachronistic functionalism onto human origins. Instead, mimetic violence may have served natural ecological equilibrium functions without requiring a conscious or collective solution. The critique calls for tighter language around how we theorize the emergence of [[representation]] and [[scenic deferral]].

## Key Concepts

- **[[Originary hypothesis]]** — GA's foundational claim about the scene of origin where language and culture first emerged
- **[[Mimetic desire]]** — the imitative wanting that intensifies rivalry among humans, requiring cultural mediation
- **[[Scenic deferral]]** — the collective use of signs to postpone or suspend mimetic violence through shared representation
- **[[Evolutionary functionalism]]** — the fallacy of explaining cultural features as solutions to pre-existing biological problems
- **[[Representation]]** — the domain of signs and symbolic culture that emerges at the human threshold
- **Individual organism vs. collective agency** — Lobo's emphasis on precise biological language over species-level shorthand

## Notable Passages

> "There is no reason to think that a particular instance of mimetic violence would ever, prior to culture, constitute an extinction level event."

> "What I want to suggest is that there simply is no scenario in which it makes sense to think of a hominin population being unable to control its mimetic violence yet needing to control it."

> "Gans certainly does sometimes speak the language of functionalism, which is an after the fact, we might say 'Whiggish' way of speaking about things."

## Questions Raised

1. If mimetic violence never reached "extinction-level," what *precisely* triggered the emergence of [[representation]] and [[scenic deferral]]? What is the originary event if not a crisis?

2. How can GA avoid evolutionary functionalism while still maintaining that [[language]] and culture are fundamentally solutions to mimetic conflict?

3. Does grounding the originary moment in ecological equilibrium rather than cultural necessity require reconceptualizing the role of [[the center]] in GA's scenic model?
