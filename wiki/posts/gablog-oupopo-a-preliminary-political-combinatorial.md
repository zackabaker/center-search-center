## Summary

This post adapts the Oulipo literary movement's proceduralist method to politics, proposing "oupopo" (oulipotics) as a framework for making political rules explicit and generative. The author argues that any activity—including politics—can be decomposed into discrete moves and rule-governed combinations, and that the political sphere should operate on maximum visibility where [[resentment]] becomes a discovery procedure for accessing the center. Politics becomes the art of making tacit conflicts explicit while proposing universalizable rules that don't a priori privilege any single resentment.

## Key Concepts

- [[Oulipo]] — French literary group that deliberately constrains creative production through explicit procedural rules
- [[Proceduralism]] — Method of breaking down activities into discrete moves and explicit rules for combination
- [[Oupopo]] — Political adaptation of Oulipo logic; politics as rule-governed combinatorial practice
- [[Public sphere]] — Following Arendt: the space where what is said and done is seen and heard by all; generative and infinite (unlike celebrity)
- [[Resentment]] — The fundamental political material that must be displayed openly and given formal expression
- [[Center]] — The originary locus that each resentment implicitly claims to speak from; made present through political articulation
- [[Partisan politics]] — The honest acknowledgment that politics is always an "us vs. them" configuration
- [[Discovery procedure]] — Politics as method for bringing hidden resentments and the actual terms of the center into view

## Notable Passages

"Politics is maximum visibility that solicits further exposure—the need politics meets is to have resentment displayed openly and in such a way that various ways of ensuring the reconciliation of one resentment with others can be tried out."

"The resentments themselves are changed in the process of giving them form—in particular, the claim of each resentment to speak from the center must be accentuated, and in this way the actual terms of the center are brought into view."

"An honest politics that avows its partisanship (its resentments) while at the same time proposing rules that would not a priori privilege any resentment over those of others."

## Questions Raised

1. Can procedural rule-generation in politics genuinely remain neutral with respect to resentments, or does the very act of making rules explicit necessarily privilege certain articulations over others?

2. How does the "globalization of the human conversation" (every non-intimate assembly virtually including all humans) change the texture of political resentment and its formalization?

3. What distinguishes a proceduralist politics from mere [[declarative culture]] that simply names problems without solving them—and does oupopo risk becoming another form of performative visibility without substantive reconciliation?
