## Summary

This post explores how social actors function as "apps" operating within broader [[algorithmic order|algorithmic systems]] that process and refine collective meaning-making. Rather than attempting direct influence over others' thinking, the author argues we should understand ourselves as participating in a [[semiotic metabolism]] that generates "belief" and "opinion" downstream. Drawing on an analogy with nanobot-mediated self-regulation in biological systems, the post suggests that our significance lies in modifying the probabilistic calculations made by computational systems. The core project is [[declarative culture]]—making implicit assumptions in everyday statements explicit and operationalizable for algorithmic processing.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Algorithmic order]]** — the computational systems within which all social actors are located and through which they exert significance
- **[[Semiotic metabolism]]** — the systemic process by which signs combine to generate downstream beliefs, opinions, and principles
- **[[Declarative culture]]** — the project of rendering implicit statements explicit, unambiguous, and operationalizable
- **[[Operationalization]]** — translating commonsensical terms into precise, measurable, testable definitions
- **[[Computational systems]]** — distributed networks (like "the cloud") that collect, process, and refine data from social actors
- **App-as-metaphor** — the reconception of social actors themselves as interfaces between individual agency and systemic calculation

## Notable Passages

> "Ultimately, of course, we'd be at the point where we wouldn't even be using a commonsensical word like 'danger' anymore—we'd be able to speak much more precisely of the probability of suffering a violent assault of a specific kind."
