## Summary

Thinking emerges as an internal conversation between imagined interlocutors—a capacity strengthened through reading and writing across multiple [[scene|scenes]] beyond institutional assessment. True thinking requires the ability to imagine unlikely claims and create novel scenes rather than merely rehearsing moves for prestige within established hierarchies. Wisdom literature teaches us to examine [[desire]] itself as a permanent gap between wanting and fulfillment, thereby converting desires into objects of thought rather than shutters of consciousness. This mode of thinking depends on the temporary breakdown of [[ritual]], legal, and [[disciplinary institutions|disciplinary]] frameworks, during which deferred [[violence]] becomes imaginable but need not be enacted on a victim. Ancient scribal training via wisdom literature models how thinking scales from obedience to heterodox questioning.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Thinking]]**: Internal dialogue with imagined interlocutors; quality depends on range of imaginable responses
- **[[Scene]]**: Ritual, juridical, disciplinary, or hypothetical contexts that shape what responses are legible
- **[[Desire]]**: The incommensurable gap between what we want and what satisfaction actually delivers
- **[[Deferral]]**: The temporary suspension of violence enabled when institutional frameworks are questioned
- **[[Wisdom literature]]**: Formulations that teach practitioners to make desires thinkable rather than imperative
- **[[Victim]]**: The scapegoat rushed into place when deferred violence becomes imminent
- **[[Disciplinary institutions]]**: Educational, legal, and ritual structures that restrict the range of imaginable thought

## Notable Passages

> "The quality of your thinking is then a result of the range and quality of the interlocutors you can imagine for yourself."

> "The very fact that you desire something means that it will not be what you were desiring. There's a kind of self-evident incommensurability between desire and fulfillment."

> "Once these scenes break down the violence they defer becomes imaginable and even imminent, and when you're thinking you may not be restoring the categories and institutions that have been shaken but you are at least resisting the rush to find a victim to blame."

## Questions Raised

1. How does the shift from institutional [[scene|scenes]] (school, court) to self-created imaginative scenes alter the [[violence]] that thinking defers—and what determines whether that deferred violence gets redirected toward a [[victim]]?

2. If ancient wisdom literature trained scribes to think by examining the gap between [[desire]] and fulfillment, what modern institutional equivalent (if any) performs this function, or has it been largely abandoned?

3. Does the ability to entertain "nonsensical claims for the sake of seeing what else you would have to say" require a prior experience of institutional breakdown, or can it be cultivated within stable hierarchies?
