## Summary

Drawing on Carl Schmitt's concept of [[nomos]] as an originary partition and covenant grounding communal judgment, this essay explores how [[sovereignty]]—ultimately exercised by a single decisive voice—becomes "uncertain" when formal and informal power diverge. The author maps contemporary political positions (left, center, conservative, alt-right, reactionary) as distinct responses to this crisis of sovereignty and its relationship to the [[nomos]]. The central tension is whether sovereignty can be restored, redistributed, or fundamentally reconceived without destroying the foundational order itself.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Nomos]]** – An originary division of land and tacit covenant grounding a community; the reference point against which all communal acts are judged legitimate or illegitimate.
- **[[Sovereignty]]** – The capacity to make decisive judgments in disputes and command organized force; logically singular though institutionally distributed across offices.
- **[[Formal and informal power]]** – The distinction between constitutionally designated authority and the actual power relations that emerge and operate in parallel.
- **[[Republican form]]** – A system distributing sovereignty across multiple offices to protect the [[nomos]], but vulnerable if the [[nomos]] itself becomes contested.
- **[[Uncertain sovereignty]]** – The condition when formal sovereign structures no longer command obedience and auxiliary powers step into the vacuum.

## Notable Passages

> "The sovereign is both inside and outside the system insofar as its decisions constitute the system."

> "If the nomos is not shared unquestioningly then there will eventually be situations where the troops are commanded independent of a declaration of war or one side in a dispute is favored but the decision is not enforced."

> "The 'center' obfuscates the disjunction between formal and informal power while assuming the nomos is more or less intact."

## Questions Raised

1. If [[nomos]] requires being "shared unquestioningly," is the move toward explicit, contestable foundations in modern democracy inherently destabilizing, or can it be regrounded in a new [[nomos]]?

2. Can the [[republican form]] survive indefinitely with distributed [[sovereignty]] if citizens actively dispute the legitimacy of the underlying order, or does uncertainty always invite actors to restore [[sovereignty]] through domination?

3. How does the distinction between formal and informal power relate to [[declarative culture]], and can the gap be closed through better institutional design or only through renewed consensus on the [[nomos]]?
