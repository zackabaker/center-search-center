## Summary

This post develops a sophisticated framework for analyzing institutional power by layering [[originary grammar]] (ostensive-imperative-declarative) onto the [[ritual-juridical-disciplinary]] triad. Rather than collapsing one level into another (as Marxist historicism did), the author proposes thinking of institutions as "grammatical stacks" with distinct but interfacing modes. Each institutional domain—ritual, juridical, disciplinary—operates according to a particular grammatical logic, and imperatives crossing between domains create reconfiguration pressures. The framework is tested through the example of market pricing, which is ultimately a [[juridical]] question informed by [[disciplinary]] inquiry into production conditions.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary Grammar]]: The three-part structure of human meaning-making: ostensive (pointing), imperative (commanding), declarative (stating what is)
- [[Grammatical Stack]]: A layered institutional arrangement where ritual, juridical, and disciplinary domains interface without direct "emanation"
- [[Ritual-Juridical-Disciplinary]]: The three primary institutional modes organizing society, each with distinct grammatical operations
- [[Declarative]]: The grammatical mode that determines which imperatives apply in particular cases; central to normative ordering
- [[Ostensive-Imperative-Declarative]]: The foundational grammatical triad underlying all human institutions
- [[Disciplinary]]: The mode producing new ostensive-imperative orders by drawing prior events into the declarative domain
- [[Juridical]]: The institutional domain that adjudicates through imperatives while gathering ostensive evidence, mediated by declaratives

## Questions Raised

1. How do institutional crises manifest when the grammatical stack becomes incoherent—when ritual, juridical, and disciplinary logics actively contradict rather than interface?

2. Can the market ever truly be "subsumed within the juridical" when pricing reflects power asymmetries that law cannot fully adjudicate, or does the framework require updating?

3. What happens to the grammatical stack when institutions lose consensus on which [[declarative]] utterances actually bind future behavior?
