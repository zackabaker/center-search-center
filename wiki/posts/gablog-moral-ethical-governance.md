## Summary

This post exposes liberalism's central contradiction: it claims moral neutrality while enforcing aggressive moral positions against perceived threats to its worldview. Rather than accept this paralysis, the author argues that ethical governance should unapologetically aim to make citizens better people through [[discipline]] and truth-seeking institutions. Drawing on [[generative anthropology]], the text proposes that rulers entrusted with moral authority—willing to absorb [[violent centering]]—can establish disciplinary spaces where truth and community cohesion are pursued free from vendetta logic and procedural rigidity.

## Key Concepts

- [[Liberalism's incoherence]] – The unstable claim to moral neutrality while practicing moral enforcement
- [[Violent centering]] – The originary moment of social order requiring one figure to bear collective tension
- [[Moral panics]] – Frenzied liberal responses to perceived transgressions of its secular theology
- [[Meaning gap]] – The distance between speaker's self-understanding and how others perceive them
- [[Disciplinary space]] – Institutions where truth and remedy are pursued by entrusted authorities outside vendetta logic
- [[Ethical governance]] – Rule aimed deliberately at improving citizens' virtue and character
- [[Victimary logic]] – The liberal displacement of authority onto claims of victimhood and procedure

## Notable Passages

> "If your theory of government is that the government is to remain neutral between different versions of the good life, then the full fury of that government must be brought to bear on whomever would put forth their practice of the good life as superior to others."

> "Those entrusted, to be more precise, must be those willing to draw some of the violent centering toward themselves, if necessary."

> "The point is not proceduralism, but disciplinary spaces where authoritative individuals are entrusted with search for the truth, the best remedy, and the preservation of the peace."
