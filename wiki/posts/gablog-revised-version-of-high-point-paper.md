## Summary

This paper examines how contemporary pressure on the category of "human" operates through two complementary processes: **analytic** dismantling (reducing humans to predictable aggregations of data and probabilities) and **synthetic** reconstruction (engineering bodies through prosthetics, drugs, and biotechnology). The author argues that rather than representing an unprecedented threat, these developments reveal the originary truth that humans are fundamentally [[prosthetic being|prosthetic beings]], constituted first by the human [[sign]]. However, the text questions whether deconstructive reassurance ("always already") serves as intellectual narcotic, obscuring the possibility that transhumanism might actually alter what makes us distinctly human: our self-threatening nature.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Analytic dismantling]]** — Breaking the human into predictable probabilities through data, genetics, and environmental history
- **[[Synthetic remantling]]** — Engineering improved human capabilities through prosthetics, biotechnology, and medical intervention
- **[[Prosthetic being]]** — The originary insight that humans are constitutively dependent on external tools and signs, not naturally given
- **[[Originary gesture]]** — The founding moment of human culture that simultaneously separates us from nature while modeling itself on natural constraints
- **[[Transhumanism]]** — The commitment to transcending natural human limitations through technological enhancement
- **[[Non-instinctual desire]]** — The distinctly human capacity for abstract wanting, freed from animal pecking order
- **[[Declarative culture]]** — Culture built on signs and linguistic declaration rather than instinct

## Notable Passages

> "The first prosthesis being the human sign"—a deconstructive inheritance revealing we were always already technological beings, not newly corrupted by devices.

> "Maybe this time really is different, and something fundamentally human is at stake"—questioning whether soothing historical parallels mask genuine epochal rupture.

> "The originary gesture aims at separating us from 'Nature' and simultaneously takes 'Nature' as a model"—capturing the paradox at culture's origin.

## Questions Raised

1. Does the deconstructive move—asserting humans were "always already" prosthetic—genuinely illuminate transhumanism, or does it domesticate what might be genuinely unprecedented ontological transformation?

2. If the defining feature of humanity is self-threat rather than rational potential, what happens to that definition when we engineer beings incapable of threatening themselves (through bio-constraint or superintelligence)?

3. How can we distinguish between natural constraints that originary humans *imitated* versus those they *transcended*—and does this distinction survive in an age of total redesign?
