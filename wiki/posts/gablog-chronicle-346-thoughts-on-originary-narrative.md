## Summary

This brief Chronicle announcement directs readers to an extended reflection on [[originary narrative]] as a foundational concept in generative anthropology. The piece builds on previous discussions and elaborates themes from "Originary Narrative" and *The End of Culture*, positioning originary thinking as central to understanding human cultural emergence. Rather than standing alone, this Chronicle serves as a pointer to deeper theoretical work on how narrative structures encode our fundamental relationship to meaning-making and the sacred.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary narrative]] — the ur-narrative of human cultural emergence centered on the aborted gesture of appropriation
- [[Generative anthropology]] — the discipline studying humanity's originary event and its cultural elaborations
- [[Culture]] — the system of deferral and representation emerging from the originary scene
- [[The sacred]] — what is protected from instrumental appropriation through representational distance
- [[Chronicle]] — GA's periodic synthetic treatment of contemporary events through originary perspective
