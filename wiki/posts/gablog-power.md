## Summary

Power is fundamentally a display of [[discipline]] that exceeds what observers believe themselves capable of. Rather than deriving from coercion or agreement, power emerges when someone resists temptations and controls impulses that others cannot master—making others defer to their superior insight. This principle applies equally to charismatic authority (exemplified by Moses), transgressive rebellion (Emerson), and intellectual founding, where the capacity to think outside shared assumptions opens new worlds for others to inhabit.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Discipline]]**: Self-mastery and impulse control demonstrated through action, generating deference from those who recognize their own comparative indiscipline
- **[[Charisma]]**: Originally obedience to a higher imperative through abstemiousness; now inverted to mean transgression against established norms, yet retaining its disciplinary essence
- **[[Acting-in-common]]**: Arendt's concept of power as coordinated action, distinct from mere strength or capacity for effects
- **[[Display]]**: The visible manifestation of discipline that impresses observers and initiates followership
- **[[Imperative]]**: A higher command or principle that the powerful one obeys, constraining their own will
- **[[Founding]]**: The intellectual act of controlling and setting aside shared assumptions to open new conceptual space

## Notable Passages

> "Power is simply a display of discipline greater than those impressed by that display consider themselves capable of."

> "The individual you see resisting temptations you give way to and controlling impulses you are overpowered by has power over you—you will defer to him because you know that your own indiscipline blinds you to cause and effect."

> "It takes discipline to take those founding assumptions and, one by one, control for commonsensical ways of accounting for things and replace them."

## Questions Raised

1. If power depends on displaying discipline that exceeds observers' self-perception, what happens when discipline becomes commodified or performative—when transgression itself is marketed as the norm?

2. How does the modern inversion of charisma (from abstemiousness to transgression) affect the [[imperative]] structure that originally grounded power, and can transgressive power sustain collective action?

3. In intellectual founding, who determines whether the new assumptions opened are genuine advances or merely alternative constraints?
