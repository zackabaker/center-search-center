## Summary

This piece proposes that addressing contemporary injustice requires understanding how to model [[thirdness]] through the [[technoscene]]—the global stack of sensing, computing, and simulating machinery. Rather than traditional "policy," intervention happens through protocol modifications upstream that reshape what feedback loops are possible downstream. Gans's originary grammar, particularly the distinction between [[demand]] and [[command]] in the emergence of the [[declarative]], offers a scalable model for reconverting the [[vendetta]] against the [[nomos]] into resolvable disputes centered on linguistic presence and the deferral effected by the originary [[gesture]].

## Key Concepts

- **[[Thirdness]]**: The mediating position that can intervene in justice through redesign of feedback structures
- **[[Technoscene]]**: The integrated stack of global sensing, measuring, computing, mapping and simulating systems
- **[[Victimary]]**: The conversion of juridical order into vendetta deployment against those threatening established succession
- **[[Declarative]]**: The linguistic form that freezes time by asserting absence, deferring demand through command
- **[[Originary gesture]]**: The founding deferent sign whose logic scales into grammar and social structures
- **[[Demand vs. Command]]**: Two modalities of the imperative—demand as consumer-end request, command as hierarchical order
- **[[Linguistic presence]]**: The maintenance of the present tense through iterative speech acts
- **[[Nomos]]**: The established order that the victimary vendetta attacks through hijacked juridical norms

## Notable Passages

> "Justice is an essential point of intervention here because...the 'victimary' is really the conversion of the juridical order into a means of deploying the vendetta at the level of the originary distribution."

> "The declarative sentence...does function to freeze time within language, iterating the deferral effected by the originary gesture."

> "Invoking a command is always available as a way of deflecting or refusing a demand: 'I'm sorry, I can't help you, those are the rules.'"

## Questions Raised

1. How exactly does originary grammar "scale up" through the technoscene without collapsing into technocratic determinism?
2. Can the distinction between demand and command actually be recovered and embedded in protocol design, or does the technoscene inherently flatten these modalities?
3. What would it mean for thirdness to "eventually become" the transformation it simulates—is this a description of emergence or a performative goal?
