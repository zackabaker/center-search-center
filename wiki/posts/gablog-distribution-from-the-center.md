# Distribution from the Center

## Summary

The center's primary function is **distribution**—determining what gets allocated and to whom. On the originary scene, the [[sign]] ensures minimal inclusion: all who participated in the sign must receive a share, establishing [[equivalence]] as foundational to humanization. Once a human occupies the center, distribution shifts from equal apportionment to merit-based allocation, creating secondary centers of power. The stability of this system depends on maintaining the distinction between the central authority and the [[subsistent center]]—a distinction that collapses with differentiation.

## Key Concepts

- [[Center]] — the locus from which all distribution emanates and is legitimated
- [[Sign]] — the mechanism mediating distribution and ensuring group inclusion on the originary scene
- [[Originary scene]] — the first human event where equal distribution reinforces the founding act of linguistic deferral
- [[Equivalence]] — the principle that all sign-issuers must be included in consumption, founding humanization
- [[Sacral kingship]] — the pre-differentiated state where the central authority and subsistent center remain unified
- [[Merit and loyalty]] — the criteria replacing equal distribution once a human center emerges
- [[Secondary centers]] — aristocrats and functionaries who become distribution nodes themselves
- [[Subsistent center]] — the ongoing sacred presence that legitimates the center's authority

## Notable Passages

> "The group itself must assume and insist that apportionment is determined by the center. What matters most is that inclusion in the group—even of a member despised or mocked—is beyond all question."

> "Once a human occupies the center, distribution is determined by the central authority. At this point, equal distribution is no longer a consideration."

> "The most terrible consequences of liberal individualism come from the destruction of this assumption that all individuals, before all else, have their entire existence within the group."
