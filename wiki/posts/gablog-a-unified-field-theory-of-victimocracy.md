## Summary

This essay derives the current political impasse from the originary opposition between [[center and periphery]]. While [[Political Correctness]] claims to defend egalitarianism by attacking "privilege," it actually denies [[firstness]]—the legitimate reward for merit and contribution—which is essential to a functioning moral order. The author argues that [[egalitarianism]] and [[firstness]] are interdependent: the sacred [[center]] establishes equality among the periphery *through* its power to defer conflict and allocate rewards fairly. PC's Manichean worldview treats all inequality as evidence of oppression, preventing constructive political engagement with real structural issues.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary Scene]]: The primordial scene of human culture where the first sign emerges, establishing the sacred center and peripheral equality
- [[Center and periphery]]: The spatial opposition on the originary scene; the center defers conflict through symbolic power while the periphery achieves equality before it
- [[Firstness]]: The legitimate priority of merit, contribution, and reward; the right of the center to allocate honors according to reciprocity
- [[Egalitarianism]]: The principle that all humans are equal in rights and dignity, made *possible* by the center's power to level conflict
- [[Political Correctness]]: A rhetoric that attacks "privilege" apotropaically, allowing power structures to operate unchecked while preventing merit-based reward
- [[Victimocracy]]: A system in which victimhood claims override merit and reciprocity, leveraging resentment as political currency
- [[Moral reciprocity]]: The principle that one deserves recompense proportional to one's contribution to the community

## Notable Passages

> "PC wants to exclude firstness by claiming that any inequality in material situation can only be due to an oppression that denies fundamental equality. This is a Manichean world-view, in which all the benefits of civilization, because they are not distributed equally, become evidence for evil conspiracy."

> "Firstness depends on egalitarianism, as the rights of the community, because a privileged position can be claimed and defended only in terms of its benefit to the group."

> "The genius of the left's attack on 'privilege' is that anyone can participate, no matter how successful, by simply acknowledging their 'unmerited' position with an apotropaic gesture designed to ward off criticism."
