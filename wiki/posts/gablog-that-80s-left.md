## Summary

This post distinguishes the 1960s Left—diverse, anti-establishment, and genuinely oppositional—from the consolidated "80s Left" that merged with liberal institutions and unified around the concept of "diversity." The 80s Left's defining innovation was the [[backlash]] framework: delegitimizing all resistance to its agenda as irrational reactionism rather than genuine political disagreement. The author argues this rhetorical strategy, which worked in academic settings, fundamentally fails at the scale of democratic governance.

## Key Concepts

- [[Backlash]]: The rhetorical delegitimization of resistance to progressive policy as inherent bigotry rather than principled opposition
- [[Declarative Culture]]: Rule through the authority of appointed "experts" who "care about" issues and thereby claim interpretive monopoly
- [[Victimocracy]]: A system where moral authority derives from claiming victim status and historical grievance
- [[Idiom]]: The characteristic linguistic and conceptual style through which a political movement speaks and thinks
- [[Institutional Power]]: The consolidation of a political movement within media, academia, and bureaucratic structures
- [[Diversity]]: The unifying doctrine of the 80s Left, presented as descriptive reality rather than contested value

## Notable Passages

"The 80s Left got all of reality covered: there are people 'caring' about everything now, and they can issue the authoritative word on the environment, race, peace, the UN, and so on."

"The backlash is by definition illegitimate, even when they control the Presidency and Congress—the 'mainstream' press should not even pay attention to their doings, except in the manner of a police blotter."

"What worked for graduate students bullying their less ideologically belligerent fellow students won't work for a country of 300 million (still) free individuals."

## Questions Raised

1. Does the distinction between 60s and 80s Left still hold after 2008, or has the consolidation deepened further into incoherence?

2. At what point does an idiom of resistance become so institutionalized that it can no longer perceive genuine opposition—and what would de-institutionalization require?

3. If the backlash framework requires defining opponents as irrational by definition, what prevents the Left from ever recognizing a legitimate political defeat?
