# Tribes of Terror

## Summary

This brief post references Stanley Kurtz's essay on tribal violence and terrorism, positioning it as "extremely informative" for understanding contemporary political formations. Though minimal in its own commentary, the post gestures toward how [[tribal]] social structures generate [[violence]] and terror, likely in tension with modern [[declarative culture]]. The endorsement suggests GA analysis should attend to how pre-modern or de-modernizing group identities produce [[mimetic]] conflict and extremism in current geopolitical contexts.

## Key Concepts

- [[Tribes]]: Pre-modern or de-modernizing kinship groups operating outside centralized sovereignty
- [[Violence]]: Mimetic escalation and sacrificial discharge within and between groups
- [[Declarative culture]]: Modern articulation of shared meaning through language and law
- [[Victimary]]: Identity constructed through claims of victimization and resentment
- [[Sovereignty]]: Monopoly on legitimate force and representation within a bounded political space
- [[Mimetic crisis]]: Breakdown of differentiated roles leading to undifferentiated rivalry
- [[Terror]]: Spectacular violence deployed to constitute group identity and intimidate rivals
