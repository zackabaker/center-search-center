## Summary

This piece examines the internal contradictions of contemporary queer theory and LGBT activism through a generative anthropology lens. The author argues that queer theory's claim that gender is purely performative and culturally constructed conflicts with the lived assertion that transitioning to another gender represents a fundamental transformation—which tacitly reinstates biological sexual difference. Rather than resolving this tension, the movement "locks down" inquiry, treating questioning as aggression. The text suggests that a more coherent position would acknowledge both performative variation *and* biological centering, but that contemporary left-wing cultural politics operates in "take no prisoners mode," indicating desperation rather than confident ideology.

## Key Concepts

- [[Performativity]]: Gender roles are enacted performances that stabilize and destabilize normative assumptions simultaneously
- [[Normative order]]: The structured expectations around gender that any performance both conforms to and deviates from
- [[Resentment]]: The driving force behind the need to delegitimize all biological grounding for sexual difference
- [[Cultural coherence]]: The requirement that a worldview's explicit claims align with its lived practices and celebrations
- [[Categorical essence]]: The biological or structural center around which cultural variations orbit
- [[Victimary politics]]: Framing all restriction as oppression, foreclosing reasonable disagreement

## Notable Passages

> "If the only differences between men and woman are culturally constructed, and if those differences exist only to perpetuate inequality, what does it mean to 'become a woman'—doesn't that claim that such transformation is liberating imply the differences are significant after all?"

> "The cultural vandals of the left are in a take no prisoners mode, which might itself be a sign of desperation—but desperate measures are sometimes successful."

> "Boys and girls and men and women will cluster around certain 'male' and 'female' characteristics, even if we may occasionally be surprised at what they turn out to be."

## Questions Raised

1. Can a theory that treats gender as purely performative coherently celebrate transition as a transformative act without reintroducing the very biological difference it denies?

2. Why does contemporary queer activism respond to logical challenges with institutional silencing rather than theoretical refinement—what does this reveal about the movement's actual confidence?

3. What would a generatively coherent account of gender acknowledge about both biological centering and performative variation?
