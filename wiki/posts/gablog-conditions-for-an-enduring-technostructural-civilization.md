## Summary

This text argues that liberalism's foundational flaw is systematic falsification of reality, achieved by imposing equality from above to demolish intermediate authorities. This destruction of reality corrodes sciences and institutional discourse, creating environments where truth-seeking invites "backlash." A sustainable civilization requires creating conditions where people are rewarded for disputable hypotheses within intellectual traditions, which demands solving the mimetic problem: enabling people to engage unfamiliar or threatening statements without preemptively denouncing them. Only by modeling behavior on [[central authority]] rather than mimetic rivalry can discourse escape the "who, whom" frame.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Mimesis]]** — The imitative desire that generates rivalry and the fundamental human problem all culture attempts to regulate
- **[[Violent centralization]]** — Communal expulsion and scapegoating mechanisms that resolve mimetic crises through collective violence
- **[[Central authority]]** — A stable reference point for deferring mimetic rivalry and enabling rational deliberation
- **[[Liberal falsification]]** — Systematic destruction of reality through top-down equality enforcement and demolition of intermediate authorities
- **[[Mimetic crisis]]** — Breakdown of differential mechanisms when imitative desire destabilizes social hierarchies
- **[[Intermediate layers of authority]]** — Traditional power structures that liberalism destroys to consolidate centralized control
- **[[Deferral]]** — The anthropological mechanism by which culture postpones or redirects mimetic violence

## Notable Passages

"The most destructive thing about liberalism is the systematic falsification it imposes on all of reality... you destroy reality, because reality can only be comprehended and apprehended from within positions of power and authority."

"If I am to look at what someone else says...and ask questions that allow that statement to be further fleshed out rather than denounce it as heresy, then I must have constructed a model of behavior for myself modeled on central authority rather than on some rampaging power agency."

"The most radical form of traditionalism is one that sees mimesis, mimetic rivalry, communal expulsion...as the problem with which all of human culture...is occupied."
