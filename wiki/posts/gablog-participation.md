## Summary

This post traces how [[ritual]] reproductions of the [[originary scene]] evolve into hierarchical social orders centered on the [[Big Man]]—a figure who monopolizes [[producer's desire]] while the community subsists on [[consumer's desire]]. The monotheistic revelation temporarily redistributes responsibility through resentment toward alphadom, but modern consumer society paradoxically reinstalls Big Man logic through corporations and institutions that denounce bigness while monopolizing productive power. The internet offers an emergent counter-tendency, though the post is cut off before fully developing this redemptive possibility.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary scene]]: The founding moment of human community, reproduced through ritual to defer violence and construct meaning
- [[Big Man]]: The asymmetrical figure who alone possesses [[producer's desire]] and controls distribution to the community
- [[Producer's desire]]: The capacity to confer meaning and create value; monopolized by elites but necessary for all flourishing
- [[Consumer's desire]]: The aggregate resentment and demand of the majority; morally valorized but dependent on hidden producer activity
- [[Declarative culture]]: The monotheistic turn that directs resentment toward alphadom and redistributes responsibility through the name of God
- [[Resentment]]: The emotional engine that both enables democratic leveling and perpetuates obsessive focus on hierarchy
- [[Aesthetic]]: The domain of mastery separated from ritual, modeled on the Big Man's monopoly of attention

## Notable Passages

> "Responsibility again devolves upon everyone, but it is a responsibility both enhanced and truncated: enhanced, because a far wider range of human intentions can now be comprehended and therefore demand recognition; truncated, because the resentment toward the Big Man (in all of us) locates morality in the consumer's desire."

> "Modern consumer society is the reign of Big Corporations that stay big by inciting our consumer resentment against all forms of bigness."

> "All forms of bigness today—corporations, the political parties, the state, the media, the educational system and academy—all function according to the same logic of the disavowal and denunciation of the producer's desire."

## Questions Raised

1. How does the internet's redistribution of attention actually break the Big Man logic, or does it merely create competing alphas in a flattened terrain?

2. If resentment is the emotional motor of both [[declarative culture]] and modern self-sabotage, can a society cultivate [[producer's desire]] without abandoning the moral gains of monotheistic leveling?

3. What would genuine participation in meaning-making look like—and is it compatible with the scale and mediation of contemporary institutions?
