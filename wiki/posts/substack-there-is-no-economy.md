## Summary

This brief post directs readers to a new article in *Anthropoetics* journal examining the claim that "there is no economy" in any pre-declarative or non-mediated sense. The piece suggests that what we call "the economy" is fundamentally a [[declarative culture|declarative construct]]—a linguistic and cultural imposition rather than a natural or self-evident domain. It challenges economists and theorists to reconsider whether economic relations exist independently of their representation within language and power structures.

## Key Concepts

- [[Declarative culture]]: The use of language to constitute reality rather than merely describe it
- [[Economy]]: Not a natural domain but a discursive and political construction
- [[Originary scene]]: The founding moment of human consciousness and social order
- [[Market]]: A representation and performance of exchange relations, not a brute fact
- [[Power]]: The capacity to define what counts as "economic" in the first place

## Questions Raised

1. If there is "no economy" in a pre-declarative sense, what precisely constitutes economic reality—is it purely linguistic/performative, or does it require institutional enforcement through power?

2. How does the insight that the economy is declaratively constructed change how we should theorize [[market]] behavior, value, and exchange?

3. Does recognizing the economy as a declarative construct suggest radical possibilities for its reimagination, or does it reveal its necessity as a mediating structure within any complex society?
