## Summary

This paper examines pressure on the category of "human" from two directions: analytically (breaking humans into predictable data aggregates) and synthetically (engineering enhanced or modified bodies). Rather than seeing these as unprecedented threats, the author argues that [[prosthesis|prosthetic being]] has always constituted humanity, with the [[sign]] itself as the originary prosthesis. The central question becomes whether technological transhumanism undermines the [[originary understanding]] of humans as self-threatening beings—or whether that understanding remains foundational regardless of enhancement. Ultimately, the author proposes that the [[originary gesture]] simultaneously separates us from Nature while taking Nature as a model.

## Key Concepts

- [[Prosthesis]]: The human condition fundamentally structured by supplementarity; sign-use is the originary prosthesis
- [[Analytic dismantling]]: Breaking the human into predictable aggregates via DNA, environment, and data mining
- [[Synthetic remantle]]: Engineering new bodily capabilities through technology, drugs, surgery, and biological manipulation
- [[Originary scene]]: The foundational moment of human emergence through the [[sign]] and community formation
- [[Transhumanism]]: The project to achieve posthuman enhancement via technological and medical advances
- [[Declarative culture]]: Culture constituted through linguistic acts that separate us from pure instinct
- [[Self-threat]]: The defining human danger: that we pose greater risk to ourselves than external nature does

## Notable Passages

"The first prosthesis being the human sign...what seems unprecedented has always already been with us. Maybe this time really is different, and something fundamentally human is at stake."

"Does a posthumanism theoretical perspective...obviate the need for that understanding?" — posing whether enhanced humanity can escape its self-threatening essence.

"The originary gesture aims at separating us from 'Nature' and simultaneously takes 'Nature' as a model for doing so."

## Questions Raised

1. If the [[sign]] is the originary prosthesis, does exponentially advancing technological prosthesis fundamentally alter what makes us human—or does it merely externalize what was always internal to our condition?

2. Can the originary definition of humanity as [[self-threat|self-threatening]] survive indefinite technological enhancement, or does posthuman optimization necessarily redefine the human away from [[resentment]] and danger?

3. How does the imitation of Nature function in the [[originary gesture]]—and what role might this mimesis play in evaluating whether transhumanism represents genuine posthumanism or merely amplified humanism?
