## Summary

This post argues that Western anti-progressive forces should adopt "conversion" as a model of political and moral communication to counter Islamic expansion among leftist constituencies. Rather than debate through competing declarative statements, the author suggests that genuine change requires indicating a new [[sacred object]] through an [[ostensive]] act. The proposal calls for a renewed missionary spirit in Christianity and Judaism—framed as both literal foreign policy and a thought experiment in civilizational self-confidence. The piece ultimately explores how serious conversation might authentically invite conversion through trust rather than coercion or obnoxiousness.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Sacred object]]**: The center of communal attention that binds a group; cannot be changed through declarative argument alone.
- **[[Ostensive]]**: The originary gesture of indicating something new; prerequisite for introducing a new mode of sacrality.
- **[[Declarative statement]]**: Propositional utterance that assumes shared sacrality; insufficient alone to produce genuine transformation.
- **[[Resentment]]**: The emotional fuel binding marginalized populations to totalitarian systems offering intensity and conformity.
- **[[Conversion]]**: A change in sacred allegiance requiring indication of a new center, not mere rational persuasion.
- **[[Civilizational confidence]]**: The cultural self-assurance needed to project an alternative sacred center to competing systems.
- **[[White Guilt]]**: The postmodern guilt framework that has hollowed out leftist Enlightenment principles and opened space for Islamic alignment.

## Notable Passages

> "If such change takes place, though, it is because the ostensive has entered somewhere, and if the ostensive has entered it means a new sacred object has been indicated."

> "Islam is undoubted the stronger, ascendent party and whatever secularist, Enlightenment scruples the Left once had are incompatible with postmodern White Guilt."

> "Conversations could at any point turn toward conversion if they attain the right level of seriousness and trust, not that it actually happens according to any preferred schedule."

## Questions Raised

1. How can we distinguish between genuine [[conversion]] (authentic shift in sacred allegiance) and mere strategic adoption of rhetoric that preserves underlying resentment?

2. If [[ostensive]] indication of a new [[sacred object]] is necessary for conversion, what specific objects or practices could Western civilization credibly offer to populations currently bound by Islamic or progressive resentment structures?

3. Does the proposal for "conversion" as political communication risk reproducing the very intolerance and social toxicity the author acknowledges, or can new social conventions genuinely permit this without dissolution of civil relations?
