## Summary

This post analyzes how modern "democratic socialism" has shifted from class-based to identity-based oppression narratives, attributing all statistical group disparities to systemic discrimination requiring government redistribution. The deeper paradox, drawing on Gans's thought, is that wealthy technological elites genuinely support this victimary program not out of pure hypocrisy but because it manages [[resentment]] in a way that actually sustains the modern economy. Yet this system contains an inherent tension: resentment production fuels [[declarative culture]] (media, education, social platforms) while material production still depends on stable conditions that victimary politics destabilizes.

## Key Concepts

- [[Victimocracy]] – governance through claims of systemic oppression against ascriptive groups requiring state remediation
- [[Systemic Discrimination]] – oppression embedded in structures rather than individual intent, justifying redistributive policy
- [[Resentment]] – the affective engine of modern economy, simultaneously necessary to manage and dangerous to unleash
- [[Declarative Culture]] – symbolic production (media, education, entertainment) as primary economic driver in information societies
- [[Technological Literacy]] – the new basis of economic competition, creating new inequalities and status hierarchies
- [[Democratic Socialism]] – left political program combining identity-based reparation with economic redistribution

## Notable Passages

"The 'socialist' program consists largely of public gestures which have little or no concrete effects; and when they do, usually make problems worse."

"Democratic socialism actually supports the economic system it purports to combat...The only way to explain how and why technological leaders really seem to believe in the battle."

"The economy actually depends upon the stimulation and production of resentment, which fuels a large part of our economy...It's not clear how the production of resentment can help supply the material goods upon which our lives depend."
