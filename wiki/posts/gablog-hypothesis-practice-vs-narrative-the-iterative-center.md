## Summary

This post distinguishes between two modes of understanding: the ritual/myth nexus (which sustains community through narrative explanation of exchange with a central being) and the practice/hypothesis nexus (which iterates the originary scene through collaborative refinement of perception). While myth provides post-hoc justification for ritual's unpredictable outcomes, practice generates its own internal hypotheses through the pursuit of ever-finer discrimination—seeing what normally goes undetected. The gap between what we expect and what we observe becomes the engine of intellectual and communal development.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary scene]] — the fundamental moment of human differentiation from animals, which practices iterate and refine
- [[Central being]] — the object around which ritual exchange structures community coherence
- [[Imperative exchange]] — the foundational reciprocal giving between community and center in ritual/myth
- [[Practice]] — iterative refinement of perception and action that generates ostensive signs visible only through participation
- [[Hypothesis]] — minimal possible differences detected within a practice, tested by its own unfolding
- [[Ostensive sign]] — something made visible and real only because of the practice that produces it
- [[Reciprocity]] — the higher form governing practice, distinct from the transactional exchange of ritual
- [[Scenic aptitude]] — capacity to see and discriminate, the gift the center provides through practice

## Notable Passages

"A practice aims at producing an ostensive sign: as a result of the practice we can all see something that is there only because of the practice, and we see insofar as we participate in the practice."

"Getting clearer about what you're looking for is more important than finding it in any instant."

"The hypothesis is the generation of minimal possible differences in the course of conducting the practice."

## Questions Raised

1. How does the transition from myth/ritual to practice/hypothesis relate to the emergence of what we call modernity or secular thought? Is practice-based knowing inherently post-mythological?

2. If hypothesis is "minimal possible difference," how do we distinguish between trivial discriminations and those that genuinely refine our grasp of something like courage or justice?

3. Can a society sustain itself on practice alone, or does it require some residual mythic narrative to hold together those who don't participate directly in its central practices?
