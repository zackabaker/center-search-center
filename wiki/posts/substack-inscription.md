## Summary

Gans argues that foundational GA concepts like "sacred," "religion," and "ritual" fail the test of [[minimality]] because they are culturally contingent 19th-century Western abstractions. He proposes **[[commemoration]]** as a more minimal, universal concept: every human community remembers and materializes memory through repeatable practices directed by the [[center]]. Commemoration is coextensive with humanity itself, and the entire human project consists of endlessly repeating the [[originary event]] in selective, skewed ways that allow communities to recognize patterns as "the same."

## Key Concepts

- **[[Minimality]]**: The standard that a concept be truly universal rather than culturally specific abstractions
- **[[Commemoration]]**: Repeatable, material practices through which communities commit events to collective memory
- **[[Originary event]]**: The foundational human scene whose repeated reenactment constitutes human culture
- **[[Center]]**: The directing force that commands attention to breaches and determines what enters collective memory
- **[[Scenic design]]**: The accumulated scaffolding of practices and institutions that enable repeated commemoration
- **[[Sameness]]**: The capacity to recognize patterns as identical across iterations, grounded in memory

## Notable Passages

> "Commemoration reminds us that memory is not, primarily, an internal matter, but one made material through repeatable practices."

> "The distilled thesis of GA is that we humans do nothing but repeat the same originary event over and over again, in countless ways."

> "Any attempt to repeat it will be necessarily skewed and selective, focused precisely on that which will enable us to say 'this is the same.'"

## Questions Raised

1. If commemoration is truly universal, why have some languages failed to abstract explicit terms for "memory" and "remember"—and what does this linguistic gap reveal about how we theorize universals?

2. How do we distinguish between successful commemorations that maintain "sameness" and failed ones that become unrecognizable—what is the threshold where repetition breaks down?

3. If human culture is endless repetition of the originary event through increasingly dense and selective scenic scaffolding, is there a limit to how distorted that repetition can become before it ceases to be the "same" event at all?
