## Summary

This post develops the concept of **[[singularized succession in perpetuity]]** as a framework for thinking about power, legitimacy, and futurity beyond liberal democracy. The author argues that true [[sovereignty]] is demonstrated when a ruler publicly selects their successor—a unifying event that reorganizes the entire social order around producing worthy candidates. By extending this into perpetuity (ensuring successors select successors indefinitely), the concept becomes fundamentally anti-liberal and incompatible with the "rule of law," grounding legitimacy instead in the [[center]] and [[nomos]] rather than juridical abstraction.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Sovereignty]]**: Power with a determinate locus, proven by the sovereign's capacity to select their successor
- **[[Singularized succession]]**: Selection of a successor as a public, celebratory event organizing the entire social order around mentorship and competition
- **[[Center]]**: The point of imperative preservation that transcends individual generations, the organizing principle of singularized succession
- **[[Nomos]]**: Originary distribution; the juridical grounded in pre-legal order rather than abstract law
- **[[Rule of law]]**: Liberal abstraction that cannot accommodate succession; exposed as anti-succession
- **[[Breeding]]**: Cultivation of traits, habits, and kinship networks across generations to produce qualified candidates
- **[[Futurity]]**: Political question of how present power arrangements bias the future, increasingly tractable through data security
- **[[Trans-scenic]]**: Operating across multiple scenes/generations; building perpetuity into succession's logic
