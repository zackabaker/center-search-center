# Media as Ritual

## Summary

Media functions as a post-sacral ritual that positions audiences as judges of transgression, perpetually commanding them to "take a position" on displayed events. Rather than occasional ceremonial displays of power (coronations, pageants), scaled media distributes scenes daily through technologies that abolish distance. This new mode of governance accelerates turnover at the center while centralizing control, requiring a fundamental recomposition of the self and necessitating consumerism as the compensatory exchange for loss of traditional social appurtenances.

## Key Concepts

- [[Ritual]] — Fundamentally commemoration and scene-setting; scaled up through media to distribute governance displays daily rather than on special occasions
- [[Center]] — The occupied seat of governance; now subject to systemic and ritually enacted deposition rather than stable authority
- [[Tele-]] — Distance-abolishing communication that abstracts scenes by singling out few features for vicarious participation
- [[Post-sacral]] — The condition after religion's organizational power has been displaced by media and financial systems
- [[The scene]] — The mediated, filtered representation that must be made inhabitable through decomposition and recomposition of the self
- [[Overton window]] — The bounded oscillations within which media-governed contributions remain confined
- [[Media-governing-financial complexes]] — Integrated systems of control that extract ongoing participation through ritualized judgment

## Notable Passages

> "The mass media, and now social media, do perpetually position us as judges on events involving some purported transgression, commanding us to 'take a position.'"

> "Media as ritual, keeping in mind that ritual always means distribution from the center, necessarily entails consumerism."

> "The new principle of governance is that turnover at the center be accelerated while the means of domination and control be increasingly centralized."
