## Summary

Writing originates in the installation of a disembodied voice—like God's Name to Moses—that enters and overrides competing voices in individual minds across time and space. The author synthesizes originary accounts of writing with [[originary technics]], arguing that writing perfects the [[declarative sentence]] as a metalinguistic system of authorized meanings and rule-governed discourse. This trajectory from imperative to declarative to programming represents the convergence of technical perfection, where the imperative eventually becomes replaced by reminders, aids, and scenically designed constraints that make certain actions the only conceivable ones.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Declarative sentence]]** – The founding utterance that reroutes a failed imperative and establishes meaning through exhaustive composition of interrelated parts
- **[[Originary technics]]** – The origin of all technical and institutional systems in the perfection of the imperative through ritual rehearsal and scenic design
- **[[Imperative]]** – The originary command whose failure generates both technics and the declarative mode
- **[[Metalanguage of literacy]]** – The discovery and systematization of language itself through writing, establishing authorized word use and rule-governed sequencing
- **[[Disembodied voice]]** – The invisible, identical voice that enters all minds equally when reading, modeled on God's Name
- **[[Scene]]** / **[[Scenic design]]** – The ritualized space where imperative action becomes the only conceivable option through aids, reminders, and constraints

## Notable Passages

> "Logic eventually leads to programming, which is, then, the full convergence of the perfection of the imperative."

## Questions Raised

1. If writing perfects the declarative by making language a systematized, rule-governed metalanguage, what happens to the creative or transgressive potential of utterance? Does the metalanguage of literacy necessarily domesticate language?

2. How does the "disembodied voice" of writing differ from other forms of mediated instruction (visual, gestural, digital)? Does each medium install a different kind of voice in the social ecology?

3. The text suggests ritual scenic design eventually makes certain actions "the only conceivable one"—doesn't this risk collapsing all difference into pure imperative, contradicting the originary split between imperative and declarative?
