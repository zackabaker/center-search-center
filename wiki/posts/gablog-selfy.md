## Summary

This essay asks whether the ubiquity of selfies—the compulsive documentation of one's image—paradoxically signals the *absence* of genuine selfhood in contemporary culture. Drawing on psychoanalytic and originary frameworks, the author explores how modern terminology (ego, consciousness, subjectivity, self, person) obscures rather than clarifies the nature of human interiority. Via Andrew Bartlett's distinction between "person" and "self," the piece suggests that authentic personhood emerges through erotic reciprocity and meaning-making, while the contemporary "self" has become denuded and anesthetic—all surface, no depth.

## Key Concepts

- [[Subjectivity]]: The constitutively embedded subject, phenomenologically situated in a world of objects and inter-subjective intentions; implicitly contains a taunt about subjection.
- [[Personhood]]: Achieved through lovability and reciprocal erotic centering; the capacity to create meaning with another as mutual sources of concern.
- [[Selfhood]]: A denuded, anesthetic entity lacking bodily vulnerability and meaning-making power; distinct from and inferior to personhood.
- [[Originary person]]: In Gans's schema, God was the originary person; human persons appropriate derived divinity through the private, erotic center.
- [[Consciousness]]: Marginalized in Freud's "Copernican turn"—positioned at the edge of obscure unconscious processes rather than at the center.
- [[Cultural mediation]]: The immersive parody of contemporary culture (the "Selfie" song) blurs boundaries between parody and celebration, obscuring authentic experience.

## Notable Passages

> "Everyone is taking selfies, but does that mean that no one is selfy, that is, self-like, anymore?"

> "To be a self is not quite yet to be a person. The self designates rather a denuded, anesthetic entity lacking both the concrete bodily vulnerability and the power to create meaning that belongs to the person."

> "All these terms...are used so widely and have been used for so long that it would be ridiculous to dismiss them as 'mystifications'... The task for originary thinking is explore the overlapping terrains covered by this sprawling vocabulary."

## Questions Raised

1. Can the distinction between "person" and "self" be sustained in a culture where erotic reciprocity has been replaced by algorithmic mediation and image curation?

2. Does the originary framework allow us to recover a notion of authentic selfhood, or does it suggest that the contemporary "self" is simply a new historical formation we must learn to think through rather than lament?

3. What would it mean to be "selfy" in an originary sense—to possess genuine interiority and meaning-making capacity—in an age of total mediatization?
