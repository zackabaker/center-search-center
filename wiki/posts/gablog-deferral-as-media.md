## Summary

This essay reconceives media not as sign-systems themselves but as the distinctive forms of [[deferral]] they enable. Taking the example of refraining from physical violence to illustrate how deferral opens cognitive and perceptual possibilities, the author argues that each new media form (writing, print, film, electronic) represents an advance in deferral capacity—yet each paradoxically attempts to model itself on the forms it displaces. The problem emerges when writing mimics speech, creating hypnotic identification with a remote speaker rather than prompting independent hypothesis-generation in the reader.

## Key Concepts

- [[Deferral]]: The spacing created by a sign that enables observation, reflection, and cognitive activity rather than immediate response
- [[Media]]: Not the sign-system itself, but the particular form of deferral that sign-system produces
- [[Sensorium]]: The ensemble of human perceptual capacities, modified and redistributed by each new media form
- [[Mimesis]]: The reflexive tendency of new media to model themselves on displaced forms, producing distorting effects
- [[Internal dialogue]]: The problematic assimilation of written text to speech, where the reader internalizes a remote speaker's voice
- [[Hypothesis-generation]]: The intellectual activity that emerges when deferral is recognized as such rather than disguised as presence
- [[Rhetorical combat]]: The competitive framing of discourse where a speaker must be defeated or internalized—an effect of mimetic modeling

## Notable Passages

"It is whatever posture and attitude I have replaced the potential blow with that generates this space of deferral in which looking, listening and thinking are possible."

"A single text 'speaking' to thousands or millions of people is taking up residence in their minds—it becomes their own voice. The experience and consequences are radically different, but it's still modeled as an 'internal dialogue.'"

"If writing were represented and performed more as what it is, a mapping of speaking and listening possibilities, it would not have these hypnotic effects."

## Questions Raised

1. Can we ever fully recognize a media form's deferral without being captured by its mimetic strategies—or does each era remain partially blind to its own medium's hypnotic effects?

2. If writing were transparently performed as "mapping of possibilities" rather than simulated speech, would this weaken its capacity to build shared culture across dispersed communities, or would it strengthen genuine consensus?

3. Does the advance in deferral capacity across media forms necessarily constitute moral or intellectual progress, or might some losses in memorization, embodied response, and face-to-face accountability be genuinely irreplaceable?
