## Summary

This post examines the logical tensions within Alt-Right nationalism, particularly Vox Day's foundational theses. The author argues that the Alt-Right simultaneously rejects [[equality]] as non-existent while claiming to oppose ethnic domination—a contradiction that collapses once groups actually coexist in shared territory. The text reveals how the movement privileges immediate political aims over rigorous philosophical consistency, relying on interpretive flexibility that mirrors the very egalitarian principle it claims to reject.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Equality]]**: The principle that people should be treated by the same standard; rejected by Alt-Right ideology yet implicitly reinvoked in claims against domination
- **[[Nationalism]]**: A doctrine of ethnic sovereignty that requires internal logical coherence between individual and group-level claims
- **[[Domination]]**: Rule of one group by another; the Alt-Right opposes it, but the text questions on what grounds if equality doesn't exist
- **[[Sovereignty]]**: The right of peoples to dwell unmolested in preferred native culture; assumes a framework for delimiting legitimate vs. illegitimate influence
- **[[Interpretive generosity]]**: The principle of reading differences as neutral rather than invidious—itself an egalitarian move
- **[[Excessive influence]]**: Vaguely defined concept masking the problem of how to measure legitimate vs. illegitimate group power

## Notable Passages

"The Alt Right rejects the idea of equality because it simply doesn't exist in reality. This implicitly rejects the notion of a more limited political or civic equality... then, on what ground can one 'oppose the rule or domination of any… group by another'?"

"Within the context of complete separation of groups, a live and let live policy can prevail... In the context of more than one group within a given territory, differences are given a maximally invidious reading."

"The immediate aims (and current practices) of the Alt Right are privileged over a rigorous and consistent account of the questions raised by those aims."

## Questions Raised

1. Can a coherent philosophy of nationalism exist that rejects [[equality]] at the individual level while refusing domination at the group level, once mixed populations are acknowledged?

2. Does the Alt-Right's interpretive strategy—calling differences "neutral" rather than hierarchical—smuggle back the very [[egalitarian]] logic it explicitly rejects?

3. What makes certain forms of group "influence" (talent, intelligence, work ethic) illegitimate while others (nepotism, tribalism) are uniquely condemned in the same breath?
