## Summary

This piece proposes that writer training must be grounded in tracing [[idiomatic]] traditions back to ritual, liturgical, and mythological foundations—understanding writing as data security and scribal practice. The author argues that authentic voice emerges not from individual "minds" but from the [[signifying center]], accessed through recognition of how [[imperative gaps]] are filled in asymmetrical exchanges. By studying how voices interrupt programmed scenes, we can move beyond metalinguistic reduction and access generative understanding rooted in [[scenic]] dynamics rather than controllable propositions.

## Key Concepts

- [[Voice of the center]]: The signifying center's utterance that tells us how to obey imperatives and fill gaps in our exchanges with the occupied center
- [[Imperative gap]]: The asymmetrical space in exchanges where meaning must be negotiated and filled through proper channeling of the center's voice
- [[Scenic interruption]]: When an alien voice from another scene breaks into one's current program, opening ritual or disciplinary anomalies
- [[Idiomatic tradition]]: Continuous lines of ritual, liturgical, mythological practice that distinguish minor differences and enable creative programming
- [[Post-sacral world]]: The modern condition requiring constant centering trials; solvable only through mastering traditions of succession and meaning-making
- [[Occupied center]]: The object of exchange that participants orient toward; distinct from the signifying center
- [[Writing pedagogy]]: Training in inscription as maintenance of tradition lines and meta-representation of oral-literate interfaces

## Notable Passages

> "The only thing to do is to create spaces for the training of writers, with writing understood as the maintenance of data security, as the participation in scribal-archivist-philological-programming traditions"

> "The voice of God always interrupts some program running in our 'head,' 'pre-empting' it...an interruption into one scene can only come from another scene."

> "We might even say that the entire scene is now organized around either expelling or 'naturalizing' that voice."
