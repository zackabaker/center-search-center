## Summary

Drawing on Peirce's pragmatist epistemology, this essay argues that meaning emerges through language's capacity to design future scenes populated by figures who will enact the implicit promise of an utterance. Every statement sketches a hypothetical scenario and commits the speaker to its eventual realization. Truth becomes the long-run coherence of a community of inquirers, and meaningfulness depends on how well an utterance folds together multiple overlapping disciplinary scenes while acknowledging the limits of its own awareness. This framework aims to resolve the concept of [[singularized succession in perpetuity]] as the successor to [[central imaginary]], grounding political obedience in the listener's ability to recognize whether imagined authority can actually satisfy the desires it claims to address.

## Key Concepts

- [[Ostensive truth]] – meaning located in a hypothetical future scene that validates present utterance through eventual recognition
- [[Central imaginary]] – the imagined political authority and scenic conditions required to satisfy a political desire
- [[Scene design]] – the implicit mapping of future conditions and populated figures initiated by any utterance
- [[Idiom]] – specialized disciplinary languages whose refinement requires perpetual learning and discrimination
- [[Singularized succession in perpetuity]] – the successor concept to sovereignty, emphasizing continuous commitment to scene-realization
- [[Community of inquirers]] – the distributed collective whose long-run coherence determines truth and meaning
- [[Data]] – linguistic utterances that encode promises about future scenes and their conditions

## Notable Passages

"You start to design a scene, however unlikely or distant, with every utterance even if you're doing no more than scouting out the location."

"The meaning of any utterance depends upon the indirect cooperation of all the other people saying things, which is to say sketching out the design of scenes that will more or less overlap with the ones undergoing development in your own utterance."

"A kind of economy is built into meaningfulness, then, because a severe selectivity is involved wherein you build into your statements criteria for the other disciplinary scenes you are willing to rely upon."

## Questions Raised

1. How does the distinction between [[central imaginary]] and [[singularized succession in perpetuity]] resolve the paradox that political authority claiming to satisfy desires may harbor entirely different intentions?

2. If meaning depends on infinite future convergence of disciplinary scenes, what criteria allow us to discriminate between genuinely convergent idioms and merely overlapping but ultimately incoherent ones?

3. How does treating language as data—utterances encoding scenic designs—change our understanding of algorithmic analysis and the possibility of machines "listening to the center"?
