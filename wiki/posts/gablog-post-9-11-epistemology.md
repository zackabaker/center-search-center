## Summary

This brief GABlog entry points to a Hoover Institution essay examining how post-9/11 discourse reveals patterns of [[scapegoating]] and systematic [[reality-avoidance]] in contemporary thought. The piece suggests that the aftermath of 9/11 precipitated a crisis in how we construct knowledge and meaning, leading to the prioritization of narrative coherence over empirical acknowledgment. Rather than confronting the actual conditions that generated the attacks, dominant culture retreated into blame-assignment and ideological self-protection.

## Key Concepts

- [[Scapegoating]] — The displacement of collective anxiety onto designated enemies rather than addressing underlying conditions
- [[Reality-avoidance]] — The systematic refusal to acknowledge facts that contradict preferred narratives
- [[Declarative culture]] — Culture constructed through assertion and consensus rather than grounded in shared originary experience
- [[Victimary]] — The rhetorical positioning of groups as inherent victims, foreclosing accountability
- [[Epistemology]] — The framework governing what counts as knowledge and truth in a given epoch
- [[Originary thinking]] — GA's method of grounding analysis in the scenic origin of human language and culture
