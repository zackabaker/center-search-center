## Summary

This essay traces how [[monotheism]] emerged as a new [[idiom]] born from the [[differend]]—Lyotard's concept of a violation that cannot be litigated. The author argues that the Hebrew Bible invented a revolutionary linguistic and imaginative register by positing a God-emperor above all worldly emperors, thereby resolving an impossible double bind: how to condemn imperial destruction while surviving it as divine grace. This founding move, premised on endless [[redemption]]-debt to the divine, remains embedded in all Western thought. The essay concludes that the modern demolition of [[sacred kingship]] has left us unable to ground legitimate authority except through appeals to imaginary higher powers, rendering contemporary governance incoherent.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Differend]]**: A violation that makes it impossible to litigate the violation itself; source of new [[idiom|idioms]]
- **[[Idiom]]**: A linguistic order or register forged to detect and remedy deviations from a governing imaginary principle
- **[[Monotheism]]**: The invention of a superior divine court of appeal that places emperors under judgment and creates infinite redemption-debt
- **[[Sacred kingship]]**: The ancient imperial system wherein the emperor cannot be adjudicated; demolished by monotheistic thought
- **[[Redemption]]**: Payment of incommensurable debt; in monotheism, ongoing devotion to God replacing human sacrifice
- **[[Center]]**: The privileged subject (Israel) wronged yet redeemed, offering the dispossessed masses a model of redemption
- **[[Legitimacy]]**: In post-sacred systems, grounded only in appeals to imaginary authorities (democracy, rights, constitution), which generate counter-authorities

## Notable Passages

> "Monotheism was the invention of a new idiom in the space of a differend, and this idiom required the articulation of the privileged subject wronged by and defiant toward the emperor."

> "In demolishing sacral kingship once and for all, the subjection of actual authorities to higher authorities provides, as the only path to legitimate authority, posturing before some imaginary authority."

> "All references to higher authority...can do is create more counter-authorities to the already existing ones—and these counter-authorities get incorporated in the form of massive excrescences in our burgeoning power structures."
