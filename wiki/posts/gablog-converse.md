## Summary

This post analyzes [[political correctness]] as fundamentally rooted in an ethical fiction: that all humanity is present and equal in every conversation, mirroring the conditions of the [[originary event]]. The author traces how this constraint—that any utterance predicating differences about categorical groups becomes immoral—creates an impossible conversational bind. By examining the logical contradictions of PC's egalitarian assumptions, the piece opens a generative analysis of how victimary morality enforces a universal symmetry that paradoxically prevents substantive discourse.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary event]]: The foundational moment of human culture where the sacred object and equal human status are established through the exchange of signs.
- [[Political correctness]]: An ethical regime that treats all conversations as if all humanity is present, forbidding utterances that predicate unfavorable attributes to categorical groups.
- [[Victimary]]: The moral and political order centered on claims of historical victimhood and universal human equality.
- [[Ascriptive category]]: Identity groups defined by unchangeable characteristics (race, ethnicity, etc.) rather than achievement.
- [[Declarative culture]]: A culture organized around speech acts and ethical declarations rather than material or hierarchical facts.
- [[Conversational symmetry]]: The assumption that all participants in discourse must maintain maximal favorable assumptions about one another's equal humanity.

## Notable Passages

> "The fundamental ethical posture of PC is the fiction that, as in the originary event, all humanity is present at every conversation, so that any utterance that can be suspected of predicating something unfavorable of a category of people is to be avoided."

> "Any suggestion of unequal competence is not merely impolite but immoral, the equivalent of denying someone the vote because he or she is less intelligent or well-educated than others."

> "What is there left to talk about in this universal conversation?" — capturing the performative contradiction at PC's core.

## Questions Raised

1. If [[conversational symmetry]] requires avoiding all predicates of difference, yet differences are unavoidable and even positive aspects (creativity, compassion) can be read as condescending, isn't the victimary regime logically incoherent rather than merely restrictive?

2. How does the historical emergence of this [[originary equality]] fiction—triggered by WWII and the Holocaust—differ generatively from earlier Christian-inflected claims of universal human worth, and what shifts in [[declarative culture]] made it enforceable?

3. Can substantive political discourse exist under victimary constraints, or does the regime necessarily dissolve into performative ritual?
