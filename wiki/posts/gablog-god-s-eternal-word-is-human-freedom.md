## Summary

This brief post endorses a post from the Covenant Zone blog exploring the relationship between divine transcendence and human freedom. The author suggests that understanding God's eternal word as fundamentally connected to human freedom is a crucial insight for originary thinkers. The endorsement implies that freedom—understood in relation to the sacred and the declarative—lies at the heart of generative anthropology's understanding of human uniqueness. This framing ties theological and anthropological concerns together around the question of what makes humans distinctly free.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary thinking]]: The GA approach to human origin and nature grounded in the scene of representation
- [[Human freedom]]: The capacity for declaration and symbolic deferral that distinguishes humanity
- [[Covenant]]: The sacred binding agreement between God and humans, foundational to Western civilization
- [[Declarative culture]]: Culture founded on the power of linguistic declaration and meaning-making
- [[Sacred word]]: Language as the mediator between the divine and human communities
