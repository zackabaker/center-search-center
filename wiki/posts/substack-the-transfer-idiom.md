## Summary

The author argues that traditional frameworks—doctrine, theory, philosophy, ideology—are fundamentally compromised by their dependence on literacy's [[metalanguage]], which fills scenes with interpretive verbs and nominal abstractions that serve bureaucratic power. Rather than these inherited metalanguages, we need [[declarative culture]] in performative forms: oaths, prayers, incantations, rituals that invoke trust and institute [[centered ordinality]]. The [[transfer idiom]], understood as [[idiomatic intelligence]], offers a better path for generating actionable hypotheses about hidden scenes of power without deferring to pre-populated positions.

## Key Concepts

- [[Metalanguage of literacy]]: The interpretive language (claimed, stated, suggested, assumed) that literacy generates to fill representational scenes; origin of philosophical and juridical discourse
- [[Declarative culture]]: Direct, performative utterances that transform and test trust; opposed to metalinguistic assertion of power
- [[Centered ordinality]]: The structural condition of unified attention and hierarchy established through performative declaration
- [[Idiomatic intelligence]]: Knowledge flowing through idioms rather than doctrines; suited to detecting hidden scenes and invisible power structures
- [[Transfer idiom]]: A translation of idiomatic intelligence; enables intervention in spaces between visible agency and backstage manipulation
- [[Originary hypothesis]]: The foundational GA claim about human origin and meaning-making
- [[Anthropomorphics]]: The extraction of human forms from GA's originary framework (author's prior work)

## Notable Passages

> "You cannot have a doctrine, theory, philosophy, principles, belief or ideology that is not mortgaged to the metalanguage of literacy, those scene-fillers that make us forget that inscription generates scenes even while taking place on one."

> "We need a higher degree of declarative functionality than any of the metalanguages... something directly performative and transformative, something invoking and testing trust, instituting centered ordinality, requiring a signature."

> "The available scenic hypotheses are inevitably arbitrary, based either on some pre-political 'anarchist' scene of equality or some concrete historical and therefore unrestorable mode."
