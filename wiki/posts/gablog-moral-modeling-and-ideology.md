## Summary

This post critiques Eric Gans's application of the [[moral model]] to justify distributionist ethics and resentment-management in modern society. The author argues that Gans's focus on horizontal (peripheral) equality neglects the [[center]], which is the true locus of moral obligation and coherence. By conflating moral equality with economic redistribution, contemporary ideology obscures genuine morality—doing right rather than managing competing self-interests. The author proposes that authentic moral thinking must remain centered, grounded in ritual and communal obligation rather than peripheral demands for compensation.

## Key Concepts

- [[Moral model]]: The originary scene as the template for all human morality, where all participants equally issue the sign and share the central object
- [[Center and periphery]]: The vertical distinction between the sacred center and the peripheral community; moral rules emerge from relation to center, not horizontal equality
- [[Originary scene]]: The hypothetical first human event where language and morality are born simultaneously through collective deferral
- [[Resentment]]: The demand for compensation by the disadvantaged, which Gans treats as a moral prompt but the author sees as mere self-interest
- [[Distributionism]]: The ideology that wealth and power inequalities require concession to resentful periphery members
- [[Social justice]]: A tautological concept that conflates justice with societal restructuring, masking an "extortion racket"

## Notable Passages

> "Morality" already implies obligations to each other; as moral beings, we are already "equal" with respect to having obligations. The "equal" adds only the suggestion that morality involves making people more equal in other respects—the source of immorality.

> "You can never develop a coherent moral thinking focusing on the periphery, or the horizontal. On the originary scene, all are equal in relation to the center, equal in having that relation to the center."

> "They are institutionalized an extortion racket"—on resentment-management systems masquerading as justice.

## Questions Raised

1. If the [[moral model]] requires vertical centering rather than horizontal distribution, how do we prevent the center from becoming tyrannical or self-serving without peripheral accountability mechanisms?

2. Can genuine moral behavior exist in modern mass societies that lack stable ritual centers, or does scale itself corrupt [[center and periphery]] relations?

3. Is the author's distinction between "self-interest" and "morality" sustainable, or does all moral action necessarily contain an element of self-preservation?
