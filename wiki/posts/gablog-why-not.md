# Why not

## Summary

This essay analyzes Chief Justice Roberts' Supreme Court decision upholding the Affordable Care Act as a masterwork of conceptual ambiguity regarding the distinction between "tax" and "mandate." Rather than simply celebrating or condemning the ruling, the author identifies embedded traps for both Left and Right, ultimately revealing how the decision exposes the citizenry's complicity in unchecked governmental expansion. Roberts' genius lies in weaponizing language itself—making the taxability of the mandate context-dependent and performatively undecidable.

## Key Concepts

- [[Declarative culture]] — Power exercised through linguistic redefinition and naming rather than force
- [[Sovereignty]] — The government's expanding reach into every domain of citizen choice and behavior
- [[Nonce categories]] — Definitions valid only for a specific moment or purpose, then discarded
- [[Tax vs. mandate distinction]] — The linguistic sleight-of-hand that Roberts exploits to collapse categorical certainty
- [[Performative contradiction]] — When speakers deny the very thing they are performing (calling a tax a non-tax)
- [[Government overreach]] — The extension of state control through incentive structures, bribes, and penalties
- [[Postmodern play]] — A rhetorical construction where meaning is deferred to the audience's interpretation

## Notable Passages

> "What we might actually have here is a 'nonce' tax. And there's even more!"

> "Roberts has written a little postmodern play, in which the audience has to perform the denouement."

> "The whole point is to tax without admitting you are doing so."

## Questions Raised

1. If Roberts deliberately constructed an undecidable linguistic trap exposing governmental overreach, does his ruling represent a critique of or complicity with the system he's formally upholding?

2. Once "tax" and "mandate" become interchangeable through context-dependent redefinition, what grounds remain for any stable legal or political language?

3. Is the public's acceptance of this linguistic game itself the real scandal—suggesting we've abandoned the demand for categorical clarity in exchange for procedural continuity?
