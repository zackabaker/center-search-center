## Summary

This essay radicalizes David Olson's theory of literacy by arguing that writing solves the problem of reporting absent speech through the invention of a "metalanguage of literacy"—verbs and nominalizations that supply context once provided through vocal/bodily enactment. The author extends this insight to suggest that all writing (and potentially all technics) involves a fundamental concealment: the infrastructure enabling representation is systematically hidden beneath an illusion of transparent presence. This concealment constitutes a kind of falsification inherent to how language and media operate.

## Key Concepts

- [[Metalanguage of literacy]]: The vocabulary (especially verbs like "imply") that allows written language to impute attitudes and context to reported speech without enactment.
- [[Scene of writing]]: The originary moment in which prose emerged to solve the problem of reporting speech across absent audiences.
- [[Infrastructure]]: The hidden mechanisms and media that enable representation but are systematically effaced by literate convention.
- [[Logocentrism]]: Derrida's concept of the philosophical assumption that speech is transparent presence; writing's concealment of its own mediating role.
- [[Classic prose]]: The stylistic ideal (via Thomas and Taylor) where writer and language become invisible, creating an illusion of direct access to represented scenes.
- [[Enactment]]: The original oral mode of reporting speech through vocal and bodily mimesis rather than linguistic description.
- [[Technics]]: Media and mechanisms of communication; the essay implies concealment may be constitutive of all technical systems, not just writing.

## Notable Passages

> "If we imagine an original scene of speech being reported here, we'd have to imagine the reporter indicating this 'implication' through his reporting of the speech."

> "Any writing that plays along with this without drawing any attention to it is to that extent a falsification. But the falsification lies less in the style or straightforward reportage than in the entire vocabulary, generating an entire theory of 'mind' and sociality, that conceals the media, or mechanisms, or infrastructure."

> "Couldn't it also be the case that a similar kind of concealment is constitutive of all technics?"

## Questions Raised

1. If all technics (not just writing) conceal their infrastructural operations, can transparency about these mechanisms ever be achieved, or is concealment intrinsic to how media function?

2. How does the "falsification" constitutive of literacy differ from other forms of human self-deception, and what are the ethical implications of deploying metalinguistic tools while remaining conscious of their infrastructural concealment?

3. Can a genuinely non-falsifying prose exist that exposes its own infrastructure without destroying the communicative power that the infrastructure provides?
