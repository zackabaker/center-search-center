## Summary

This post argues for a strategic approach to Islamic civilization that defers declaring total war while simultaneously forcing concrete tests of intent through cost-imposing policies. Rather than accepting the liberal fiction that only "extremists" distort Islam, the author proposes policies designed to generate empirical data about whether moderate Muslim leaders genuinely seek engagement or merely exploit consequence-free rhetoric. The strategy treats [[scenic politics]] as the arena where civilizational differences are worked out through mutual accountability.

## Key Concepts

- [[Scenic Politics]] – The domain where competing powers test resolve and intentions through performative actions and consequences
- [[Agonistic Reciprocity]] – Engagement with an opponent on mutually understood terms where actions carry real costs
- [[Deferral]] – Strategic delay in making final civilizational judgments while gathering evidence through action
- [[Declarative Culture]] – The verbal claims and rhetoric that must be tested against actual behavior and accountability
- [[Civilizational Conflict]] – Contest between fundamentally different orders that cannot be resolved through liberal accommodation
- [[Power and Accountability]] – The necessity of making speech costly to distinguish sincere from opportunistic utterance

## Notable Passages
