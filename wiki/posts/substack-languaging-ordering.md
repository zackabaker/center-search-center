## Summary

This piece argues for a radical self-referentiality in human orders—making them "as much about themselves as possible" by exposing their own infrastructures and construction. Drawing on Johanna Drucker's *Diagrammatic Writing* as a model, the author proposes that literacy, media, and social participation should foreground their own mechanisms rather than disappearing into use. This practice of revealing scenicity (rather than hiding it) consolidates rather than dissolves community, transforming everyone into both composers and composed-by of the social text.

## Key Concepts

- [[Idiomatic intelligence]]: Sociality that explicitly celebrates its own origin and specificity in a continuous festival of self-reference
- [[Self-referentiality]]: The deliberate exposure of construction methods, infrastructures, and inherited conventions that normally remain invisible
- [[Scenicity]]: The quality of being composed and constructed; revealing the scene's own composedness consolidates rather than paralyzes it
- [[Para-scene]]: The frame around direct participation—paratexts, infrastructures, and margins that make cultural objects legible
- [[Media invisibility]]: How immersion in media systems (like Twitter) makes the infrastructure disappear while appearing to be direct expression
- [[Declarative composure]]: Making the boundaries between writer/reader, sender/receiver, constructor/constructed explicit and provisional

## Notable Passages

> "Sociality is and should therefore explicitly be an ongoing festival commemorating its own origin, both that human order itself in all its specificity but that order as a refraction, or translation, or transcription of human origin itself."

> "You are consolidating rather than dissolving the scene by revealing its scenicity. The boundary between what's on the scene and what's outside of the scene is therefore posed as a question."

> "Anything outside of the scene is registered as such by the sensory mechanisms of the scene."

## Questions Raised

1. How does revealing media infrastructure differ from the modernist "suspension of disbelief" reversal—does it risk creating *knowing cynicism* rather than genuine shared scenicity?

2. If idiomatic intelligence requires attention to very specific "furniture" meaningful only to those using it, how does it scale beyond intimate communities without becoming either universalized abstraction or fragmented particularism?

3. Can the para-scene ever be fully transparent, or does every act of foregrounding one layer of construction simply create a new invisible infrastructure beneath it?
