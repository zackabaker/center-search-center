## Summary

Declarative sentences function as inquiries into the conditions under which imperatives can be fulfilled, allowing speakers to suspend multiple scenes—past, present, future—in productive tension. Literacy alone enables abstract reflection on declarative possibilities, transforming violent demands ("show me John") into negotiable queries that defer conflict through linguistic complexity. The articulation of interdependent scenes within declarative culture creates a dialectic between discursive order and power structures, where maintaining coherence across scenes becomes a form of cultural and political organization.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Declarative sentence]]** — An utterance that answers anticipated or actual imperatives by managing multiple suspended scenes simultaneously
- **[[Imperative]]** — The directive force underlying all language; what declaratives attempt to fulfill through inquiry into conditions
- **[[Ostensive sign]]** — The reference point at the center of each scene, marking what is being talked about and why
- **[[Scene]]** — A temporal-spatial unit (past, present, future, completed, ongoing) held in relation to other scenes through declarative articulation
- **[[Deferral]]** — The linguistic postponement of violent resolution through increased complexity and multi-scene reference
- **[[Ostensive-imperative articulation]]** — The foundational structure at the center of every scene, grounding declarative meaning
- **[[Literate culture]]** — A condition enabling abstract, sustained inquiry into declarative possibilities unavailable in oral cultures
- **[[Discursive order]]** — The system allowing articulation of suspended scenes; dialectically related to power structures

## Notable Passages

> "If declarative sentences are inquiries into imperatives, it is also through declarative sentences that we engage in inquiries into declarative sentences."

> "The very concept of a 'sentence' is only possible in a literate culture, which can isolate the sentence as a unit of speech, settle upon rules for using them properly, and devise various stylistic alternatives."

> "This is essentially what declarative and cultural complex entails: being able to keep various scenes, past, present and future, completed and still ongoing, within our power to change and beyond our power, etc., suspended simultaneously and interdependently."
