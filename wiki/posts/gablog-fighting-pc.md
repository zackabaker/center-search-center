## Summary

This essay critiques the inadequacy of simply "fighting" political correctness without diagnosing its structural roots. Drawing on David Gelernter's analysis, the author argues that PC is not an aberration but the logical outcome of civil rights law itself—specifically the shift from negative rights (the right to vote) to affirmative policing of discrimination through statistical burden-shifting. The author contends that conservative resistance fails because it seeks to preserve the "old-time American mainstream" that already contained the seeds of progressive metastasis. True opposition requires dismantling not just PC's excesses but the entire civil rights legal inheritance that makes it inevitable.

## Key Concepts

- [[Invasive Leftism]] – The systematic imposition of thought-control through institutional policy and discourse policing
- [[Victimocracy]] – A system where victimhood status becomes the basis for legal and social power
- [[Declarative Culture]] – The enforcement of approved speech and ideology through institutional decree rather than persuasion
- [[Civil Rights Inheritance]] – The legal framework (anti-discrimination law, affirmative action) that generates PC as its logical extension
- [[Burden-Shifting]] – The legal mechanism where statistical disparities become prima facie evidence of discrimination, requiring defendants to prove innocence
- [[Working Class Demonization]] – The targeting of wage-earners (especially white workers) while leaving investor and salaried classes unconstrained
- [[Blank Slatism]] – The ideology that denies innate differences and attributes all disparities to systemic oppression

## Notable Passages

"The truth is, you can't argue about abuses of the civil rights legal and political inheritance without being forced to disentangle what from that inheritance is worthy of preservation."

"Once you say that businesses can't discriminate on the basis of race, gender, etc., you must then answer the question: since, once it's illegal, no one will tell us that they are discriminating, how can we tell?"

"All of 'PC' follows from this arrangement. The lies, the hysterical denunciations, the atmosphere..."

## Questions Raised

1. If civil rights law necessarily generates PC through its burden-shifting mechanisms, can a rights-based framework survive without descending into victimocratic enforcement?

2. What alternative to both the "old-time American mainstream" *and* progressive victimocracy does GA propose for organizing political and legal life?

3. How do we distinguish between legitimate protection against genuine discrimination and the system of accusation that makes statistical disparities themselves proof of wrongdoing?
