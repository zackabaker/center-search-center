## Summary

Katz argues that composition pedagogy must differ fundamentally from traditional disciplines by making theoretical distinctions operationally visible in the classroom itself. Rather than teachers disseminating completed inquiry results, composition must collapse the theory-practice divide by having students read the same theoretical texts shaping pedagogy and by grounding all abstract distinctions in concrete textual practices students can identify and name. The core principle: pedagogical questions are "real" only when they leave discernible marks on student writing that students themselves can point to and discuss.

## Key Concepts

- [[Disciplinary inquiry]] – the systematic study of a field's methods and assumptions rather than its results alone
- [[Pedagogical transparency]] – making the teacher's theoretical assumptions and choices visible and debatable to students
- [[Scenic understanding]] – the ability to see distinctions enacted in actual textual forms and writing practices
- [[Generalized semiotics]] – expanding semiotic analysis beyond specialized domains into composition pedagogy itself
- [[Expressivist/constructivist debate]] – competing writing pedagogies that Katz argues both assume mental content external to language
- [[Minimal assumptions]] – reducing foundational pedagogical claims to their most defensible core
- [[Declarative culture]] – the culture created when students collaboratively define writing through their own inquiry

## Notable Passages

> "Our questions would be deemed 'real' insofar as they can be made visible in textual practices and assemblages of interpretive moves that students themselves can name."

> "If our elemental assumptions regarding writing are to be made transparent to the students, those assumptions must be as minimal as we can make them."

> "The real subject is writing, as writing is defined by students in their own terms through a systematic inquiry into their behavior as writers."

## Questions Raised

1. How can a teacher maintain genuine [[pedagogical transparency]] without abandoning necessary authority and design choices? Does making assumptions visible eliminate them or merely displace them?

2. What are the limits of making all theoretical distinctions "visible" in student writing? Are some aspects of language and thought necessarily tacit or can they all become scenic?

3. If composition pedagogy must be more minimal than other disciplines in its assumptions, what is the irreducible core that cannot be further reduced without ceasing to teach writing at all?
