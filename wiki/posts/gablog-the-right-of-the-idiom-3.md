## Summary

This post distinguishes between [[disciplinary space|disciplinary spaces]] and partisan "crowded" discourse by examining how attention is organized differently in each. Within a genuine discipline, participants share a focused object of study (like looking at the same painting together) and can use deictic language effectively; outside it, they misinterpret signs. By contrast, partisan crowds maintain perfect structural symmetry—each side understands the opponent's meaning equally well but with opposite valuation. The author proposes that [[language]] itself should be understood as the medium "from which we attend to each other's attending," drawing on Michael Polanyi's concept of [[tacit knowledge]].

## Key Concepts

- **[[Disciplinary space]]**: A bounded domain with evolved vocabulary, shared focus, and transitional terms that acculturate newcomers while maintaining coherence
- **[[Attentional structure]]**: The organized pattern of what participants "attend to" versus what they "attend from" in any given context
- **[[Deictic language]]**: Language of pointing and shifting (this, that, him, her) that gains power only within shared perceptual fields
- **[[Tacit knowledge]]**: What we attend *from* rather than *to*—the invisible substrate enabling conscious focus
- **[[Partisan discourse]]**: Crowd-organized talk oriented toward state power, structured by perfect but inverted semantic maps
- **[[Idiom]]**: The distinctive linguistic and conceptual vocabulary specific to a discipline or domain
- **[[Mimetic modeling]]**: The mutual understanding rivals achieve through opposition, even when they interpret events inversely

## Questions Raised

1. If partisan crowds possess perfectly inverted but isomorphic maps of meaning, what prevents disciplines from similarly polarizing into factions with opposite interpretations of the same phenomena?

2. How does the [[tacit knowledge|tacit dimension]] of [[idiom]] enable genuine innovation or correction within a discipline—or does it necessarily preserve inherited blindnesses?

3. Can a participant ever genuinely "enter" a [[disciplinary space]] without already possessing the attentional habits that discipline requires, or is acculturation always partial and incomplete?
