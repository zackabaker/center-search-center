## Summary

This post proposes a new institutional form centered on the [[juridical]] as the irreducibly human frame through which all events acquire meaning and order. Rather than treating law as secondary to ritual or discipline, the author argues the juridical extends the originary ritual form into imperial conditions and frames all inquiry—scientific, social, political—back toward adjudication. The proposal envisions a scalable platform that makes explicit and formalized what we already do tacitly: analyze all events through implicit claims, counter-claims, and potential tribunals, thereby transforming civilization itself into a continuous exercise in representing and judging human injuries and reciprocities.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Juridical]]** – The minimal condition for constituting events; the frame of potential claimants before a tribunal; the irreducibly human in boundary disputes with nature and technology
- **[[Ritual]]** – The originary form taken by events, antecedent to juridical framing
- **[[Discipline]]** – The experimental method that constructs events by controlling human construction itself; ultimately dependent on juridical scaffolding
- **[[Centering]]** – Placing a concept or institution at the core of analysis to reveal hidden structures
- **[[Resentment and Reciprocity]]** – The basic human relations implicitly governing all event-analysis and judgment
- **[[Originary Distribution]]** – The foundational imperial allocation of resources, responsibilities, and ordinality
- **[[Declarative Culture]]** – Making the tacit explicit through pedagogical platforms and technological means
- **[[Nomos]]** – The originary law or distribution that frames any social order, even hypothetical ones

## Notable Passages

> "Nothing 'happens' that could not be framed as including potential claimants before some tribunal. The juridical is secondary to the ritual, on one side, and dependent upon the disciplinary, on the other."

> "To understand and represent any event is to represent it as composed of implicitly juridical instances in which complainants and tribunal could be singled out, hypothetically and conceptually."

> "The best theoretical thinking always aims at making the tacit explicit, which is to say the creation of pedagogical platforms, which is always a technological matter."

## Questions Raised

1. How does centering the juridical avoid collapsing into an infinite regress of appeals and counter-judgments, or does the civilizational exercise *require* that endless opening?

2. What would distinguish this formalized juridical platform from existing systems of discourse (journalism, academia, courts) that already implicitly frame events through claim and counter-claim?

3. How does the technological instantiation of this juridical inquiry preserve the human element it claims to center, or does the platform itself become the new tribunal?
