## Summary

This post reframes [[sovereignty]] as fundamentally grounded in [[conquest]] rather than democratic consent, arguing that all legitimate rule derives from the ability to acquire and maintain power through force (violent or otherwise). The author shows how [[absolutist]] regimes organize subjects hierarchically according to their contribution to conquest and its preservation, eventually achieving patriotic buy-in through prolonged occupation. The framework illuminates both contemporary political struggles (like Trump's potential counter-coup against institutional resistance) and why [[liberalism]] systematically conceals its own reliance on conquest by disguising loyalty as "good citizenship" and erasing visible hierarchies.

## Key Concepts

- [[Sovereignty]]: The right to rule based on demonstrated capacity to acquire and defend power against rivals
- [[Conquest]]: The foundation of legitimate authority, ranging from violent seizure to consensual takeover of failing regimes
- [[Loyalty]]: The organizing principle of absolutist regimes, incompatible with liberal legal equality but essential for regime survival
- [[Patriotism]]: The gradual fusion of conqueror and conquered populations into shared identification with the regime
- [[Absolutism]]: A political order explicitly structured around conquest and the hierarchical distribution of power based on fidelity
- [[Liberalism]]: An ideology that conceals rather than eliminates the principle of conquest, disguising it as neutral administration
- [[Fourth Generation Warfare]]: Asymmetric conflict where weaker actors leverage internal divisions in stronger societies

## Notable Passages

> "A sovereign has a right to rule based on his ability to maintain secure sovereignty—this means that he has acquired this ability and right through his own conquest, or by inheriting the results of a previous conquest."

> "Liberalism does not and could not eliminate this principle of sovereignty—it just redefines 'loyalty' in terms of constantly shifting norms of 'good citizenship.'"

> "No regime can survive if people are not rewarded in accord with their loyalty, which introduces a principle of differentiation incompatible with civil and legal equality."
