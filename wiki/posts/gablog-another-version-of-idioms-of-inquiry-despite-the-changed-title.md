## Summary

This post explores how [[Generative Anthropology]]'s [[originary hypothesis]] functions as a novel "idiom of inquiry"—a way of thinking rather than merely a historical claim. Gans's approach reframes the question of language origin from archaeological speculation into a rigorous methodology for understanding the [[declarative]] structure of human culture. The piece argues that GA's central contribution lies not in proving *how* language began, but in demonstrating *how to think* about human consciousness, meaning-making, and social organization through originary reasoning. This idiom reshapes what counts as evidence, reasoning, and relevance in humanistic inquiry.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary hypothesis]]: The founding model of human language emerging from a scene of collective attention and deferral around a central object
- [[Idioms of inquiry]]: Distinctive methodological frameworks that determine what questions can be asked and how evidence is evaluated
- [[Declarative culture]]: The human capacity to speak *about* things in their absence, enabling abstraction and shared meaning
- [[Center]]: The locus of attention and authority around which human communities organize themselves
- [[Mimetic crisis]]: The conflictual moment when desire becomes contagious and threatens collective order
- [[Deferral]]: The founding gesture postponing violence through shared focus on the sacred center

## Notable Passages

> "GA is not claiming to have discovered how language began, but rather proposing a new way of thinking about human culture and consciousness."

> "The originary hypothesis makes visible the connection between linguistic structure and the perpetual problem of human resentment and social order."

> "To think in GA terms is to make the scene of origin present to inquiry, treating it as a permanent structural feature of human interaction."
