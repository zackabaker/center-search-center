## Summary

This post interrogates the difficulty of measuring [[victimary thinking]]'s actual power in society, especially following claims that Obama's election weakened it. The author asks whether victimary resentment responds to real conditions or generates its own perpetually, and whether stable equilibrium between the victimary and "the normal" is even possible. The deeper problem is epistemological: our human capacity to predict social dynamics or detect shifts in ideological dominance is severely constrained by luck, hindsight bias, and the circularity of acting on faith in our own forecasts.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Victimary thinking]]** – ideology grounded in historical grievance, resentment, and claims of victimhood; may be parasitic or self-perpetuating
- **[[Originary tension]]** – the permanent structural conflict between victimary and "normal" modes of thought, neither wanting to eliminate the other
- **[[Resentment]]** – the emotional and psychological fuel that may drive victimary logic toward destruction or merely sustain its parasitic position
- **[[Measurement]]** – the epistemological problem of determining whether ideological power is rising, falling, or stable
- **[[Prediction]]** – human attempts to model social futures, always threatened by hindsight bias and self-fulfilling prophecy
- **[[The normal]]** – whatever stands opposite the victimary; unclear whether it represents "freedom," the "originary," or mere convention

## Notable Passages

> "To what extent are victimary resentments grounded in real events and conditions (and could therefore respond intelligently to changes in those conditions); to what extent does it produce its own events and conditions (in which case it is in principle insatiable)?"

> "The failure of some of my own prognostications has led me (out of a leveling resentment?) to conclude that our capacities, as humans, in this regard, are minimal."

> "All that we can really 'predict' is some cessation of the rivalries and crisis that most bear down upon us at present… And, then, we are strongly predisposed to see our prediction as correct, because we can only act upon faith."

## Questions Raised

1. Is there a principled way to distinguish between victimary resentment that responds to real material conditions versus resentment that self-perpetuates regardless of changing conditions?

2. If we can only reliably "predict" the cessation of present crises, and we validate that prediction through faith in our own imaginative models, how can [[generative anthropology]] ever claim genuine predictive or diagnostic power?

3. Can the victimary and "the normal" coexist in stable equilibrium, or does the inner logic of [[resentment]] necessarily drive toward either parasitism or annihilation?
