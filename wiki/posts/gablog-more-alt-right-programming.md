## Summary

This post analyzes a seven-point Alt-Right program, focusing particularly on its explicit rejection of democracy and universal franchise in favor of hierarchical, elitist rule. Gans argues that while points 2-3 address familiar racialist claims, points 5-6 represent a more intellectually honest engagement with the implications of their project—namely, that their vision requires strongman governance. The post traces how victimary progressivism's decades-long accusations against whites have paradoxically generated white racial consciousness, making the Alt-Right a chickens-coming-home-to-roost phenomenon. However, Gans identifies conceptual problems, particularly the conflation of tribal and racial categories.

## Key Concepts

- [[Victimary politics]]: The system wherein only designated victim groups are permitted collective interests while accusers claim neutrality
- [[Regime]]: The structure of governance and rule, including questions of franchise and who exercises power
- [[Hierarchy]]: Recognition of human differences as constitutive of political and social order, rejected by liberal democracy
- [[Tribalism]]: Internal social structures with kinship rules and retaliation ethos, distinct from but conflated with race
- [[Anarcho-tyranny]]: The simultaneous weakening of responsible people while unleashing chaos among the incompetent
- [[Declarative culture]]: The power to name and enforce what may be said about identity and group conflict
- [[Resentment]]: The generative emotion underlying white identity politics as response to sustained accusation

## Notable Passages

> "The recognition on the Alt-Right that much of what they want will require some kind of strongman or elitist rule demonstrates a more comprehensive awareness of the implications of their project than I, at least, have seen so far."

> "The stupidity of imagining that you can accuse whites constantly, for decades, of being an oppressor race, without whites beginning to think, at some point, well let's act like one, then, is simply staggering."

> "Tribes are nothing like races: tribes are internally structured social relationships, with strict kinship rules and an ethos of retaliation."

## Questions Raised

1. If the Alt-Right's program genuinely requires explicit hierarchical rule, can it sustain a "big tent" coalition, or does candor about elitism inevitably fracture populist movements?

2. What is the conceptual relationship between tribal organization (kinship-based, retaliatory) and race-based collective action, and why does the program conflate them?

3. Has victimary progressivism's discursive monopoly on naming group interests actually *created* the white identity it claims to merely describe, or was it latent?
