## Summary

This post analyzes two competing anthropologies revealed in Obama's and Palin's responses to the Tucson shootings. Obama proposes that civility (carefully monitored elite speech) can interrupt the continuum between speech and violence, while Palin argues that vigorous, unregulated speech—even violent metaphors—transcends violence through debate and argument. The text exposes how each figure embodies a fundamentally different theory of human meaning-making and social order.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Speech and violence continuum]]**: Obama's implicit assumption that certain speech patterns lead inevitably toward scapegoating and physical violence
- **[[Civility]]**: Not politeness, but elite-managed discourse designed to constrain dangerous speech patterns
- **[[Passionate speech]]**: Palin's counter-anthropology where vigorous, even violent-metaphorical language prevents actual violence through debate
- **[[Scapegoating]]**: The mechanism by which unmarked speech can initiate descent into collective violence
- **[[Declarative culture]]**: The implicit power to define acceptable speech and thought through monitored frameworks
- **[[Foundational liberalism]]**: The minimal rule: condemn violence, allow all other speech
- **[[Circle of concern]]**: Obama's framework for expanding moral empathy vs. respecting existing boundaries and duties

## Notable Passages

"For Obama, speech and violence lie in a continuum, and only carefully composed and tightly monitored speech can be removed from a vicious circle of speech in which marking others in virtually any way initiates the descent into scapegoating itself."

"For Palin, speech, vigorous, unregulated, 'passionate' speech, unafraid of being 'mocked' by the guardians of 'civility,' is the antidote to violence."

"Her only rule for 'civility' is the founding liberal one: 'we must condemn violence if our Republic is to endure.'"
