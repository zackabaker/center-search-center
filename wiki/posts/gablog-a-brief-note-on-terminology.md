## Summary

This post addresses terminological imprecision in generative anthropology, arguing that "ostensive" inadequately captures the nature of the [[originary sign]]. The author shows how "ostensive" misleadingly suggests GA is vulnerable to Wittgenstein's critique of Augustine's language model, when GA actually aligns with Wittgensteinian insights about language as rule-governed and scene-dependent. Since the [[originary sign]] is simultaneously ostensive, [[iconic]], and [[constitutive]], the post proposes a neologism—"iconstitensive"—that captures all three dimensions while avoiding conceptual confusion.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary sign]] – the first human sign that defers violence through representation and establishes the conditions for all subsequent language
- [[Ostensive]] – pointing to an object; problematically narrow when applied to language origin since it suggests naive referentialism
- [[Iconic]] – representing through similarity or resemblance; captures how the sign imitates the act of appropriation it aborts
- [[Constitutive]] – establishing the very conditions under which meaning-making becomes possible
- [[Scene]] – the complex configuration of participants and rules within which signs acquire meaning
- [[Deferral of violence]] – the fundamental human operation enabled by the originary sign
- [[Rule-governed activity]] – Wittgensteinian insight that language operates through mostly tacit, culturally embedded rules rather than direct designation

## Notable Passages

> "Language is a tacit, open-ended, rule governed activity for GA no less than for any Wittgensteinian derived mode of reflection or inquiry. We just insist that the rules have an origin in the urgency with which human beings must defer the ever-present potentiality of violence."

> "The originary sign is iconic and ostensive, because it is constitutive—that is, it establishes the very conditions under which iconic and ostensive signs are possible."

> "The stuttering 'ostensive' one might hear in 'iconstitensive' reflects, we might say, the hesitation that is also an essential ingredient of the originary sign."

## Questions Raised

1. Does the neologism "iconstitensive" risk obscuring the very distinctions it attempts to preserve, or does linguistic compression enhance our grasp of paradox?

2. How does the scene-dependency of meaning challenge attempts to formalize or systematize linguistic rules, and what implications does this hold for computational models of language?

3. If the originary sign's hesitation is essential to its nature, how do we account for the naturalization of linguistic signs in ordinary use?
