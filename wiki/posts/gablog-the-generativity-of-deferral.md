## Summary

This post explores how [[deferral]]—the restraint that emerges on the [[originary scene]]—generates human culture rather than paralyzing it. Against the objection that infinite deferral would be unsustainable, the author argues that deferral opens new worlds of collaboration and meaning-making. The central puzzle concerns how the community moves from collective restraint around the sacred object to its actual consumption without devolving into renewed violence. The author proposes that the [[sign]] itself functions to redirect aggression toward the object and maintain acceptable distribution, thereby grounding ritual and culture in [[linguistic]] control over [[resentment]].

## Key Concepts

- [[Deferral]]: The restraint emerging from recognition of the [[central object]], enabling culture rather than blocking appetite
- [[Originary scene]]: The founding moment of human community where simultaneous desire generates collective restraint and divinization
- [[Central object]]: The focus of converging appetite whose refusal of itself generates [[resentment]] and the need for deferral
- [[Sign]]: The linguistic marker formed in deferral that redirects aggression and maintains group cohesion during consumption
- [[Sparagmos]]: The violent, resentful tearing-apart of the central object following the moment of restraint
- [[Ritual]]: The repetition of the originary scene using remains, stabilized by the sign's capacity to manage intra-group violence
- [[Divinization]]: The transfiguration of the object through deferral, rendering it sacred and worthy of restraint
- [[Resentment]]: The aggressive response to the object's apparent refusal, driving both the sparagmos and the need for linguistic management

## Notable Passages

> "The immediate effect of deferral is not an intolerable feeling of privation...rather, the effect is of a new world opening up. Threats and rivals become collaborators and potential friends; the source of desire is transfigured."

> "Whenever the aggression toward the central object threatens to spill over into renewed intra-group aggression the members of the group would 'flash' the sign, or in some manner signify a reversion to the restrained and pacific posture modeled on the originary scene."

> "If the telos of humanity...is to bring about such conversions, aren't those who adopt that telos as their own at the mercy of those who defer only so much as is necessary?"

## Questions Raised

1. How exactly does the sign "flash" or emerge at the moment when sparagmic violence threatens to become intra-group aggression? Is this conscious, habitual, or structural?

2. Does accepting deferral as humanity's highest telos leave those committed to it vulnerable to those who maximize short-term advantage through minimal restraint?

3. What determines "acceptable" distribution of the consumed object without creating new resentments that destabilize the ritual?
