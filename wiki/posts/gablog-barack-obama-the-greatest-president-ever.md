# Barack Obama, the Greatest President Ever!

## Summary

This post examines Obama's post-election position through a [[generative anthropology|GA]] lens, arguing that he enjoys a period of "transcendence" enabled by scapegoating of his predecessor. The author explores whether [[scapegoating]] can genuinely discharge mimetic tensions and reduce social friction, while questioning whether Obama will remain beholden to his leftist base or pivot toward genuine independence. The piece speculates on how Obama's [[resentment|resentments]] and political idiom might constrain or enable his decision-making as he faces real policy choices.

## Key Concepts

- [[Scapegoating]] — the practice of discharging collective mimetic tensions through blame projected onto a single figure or group
- [[Victimary]] — relating to the logic of victimhood and its deployment in contemporary political culture
- [[Transcendence]] — a leader's temporary elevation above factional pressure, enabled by prior scapegoating
- [[Mimetic tension]] — the accumulation of rivalrous desire and anxiety in a social order
- [[Declarative culture]] — the realm of publicly stated commitments and promises, often insincere or inconsistent
- [[Resentment]] — the hidden driver of political motivation, difficult to read or predict in individuals
- [[Political idiom]] — the characteristic language and conceptual framework through which a figure operates politically

## Notable Passages

> "Maybe sometimes the mimetic tensions are genuinely 'discharged,' leading to a period of significantly reduced tensions. This wouldn't make scapegoating any more just or reasonable—but it would be a fact that we would have to acknowledge as theorists seeking the truth."

> "Obama's entire political idiom is a hybrid of the leftist activist group and faculty lounge. It is not unreasonable to imagine that he might realize fairly quickly how exhausted and inadequate that idiom is to his purposes."

> "He will have to prove himself by making difficult and unpopular positions—only when he does this will these favorable conditions become favorable conditions."

## Questions Raised

1. Can [[scapegoating]] genuinely and predictably discharge [[mimetic tension]], or is its efficacy always uncertain and contingent on unmeasurable social conditions?

2. What would force Obama (or any leader) to abandon a successful [[political idiom]], even if it were "exhausted"—does transcendence itself insulate against this realization?

3. How does a leader navigate the transition from benefiting from scapegoating (of a predecessor) to claiming sovereignty independent of that mechanism?
