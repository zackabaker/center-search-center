## Summary

The author traces the historical transition from [[ritual]] to [[juridical scene]] to [[scientific scene]], arguing that contemporary conflicts over the center stem from this progression. Rather than seeing these scenes as superseding one another, they remain "reciprocally imbricated"—the juridical invited scientific intervention, and technology now functions as a mode of governance through surveillance and constraint. The key distinction is that the [[disciplinary space]] operates through a paradoxical deferral: truth emerges from community agreement, but each truth launches new inquiry, requiring a protective politics that resists leveraging scientific authority for immediate political ends.

## Key Concepts

- [[Scenic design]]: The structured arrangement of space and activity to determine outcomes through controlled experimentation
- [[Disciplinary space]]: A community of inquirers organized around artificially created scenes that exclude all but relevant variables
- [[Juridical scene]]: The institutional apparatus for representing and resolving conflicts through centralized, measured force
- [[Scientific scene]]: The mode of inquiry grounded in long-run consensus among practitioners, perpetually deferring final answers
- [[Technology as governance]]: Systems of surveillance, record-keeping, and social constraint that simultaneously constitute new forms of social interaction
- [[Deferral of power]]: The refusal to exercise authority based on current knowledge, protecting the space of inquiry itself
- [[Reciprocal imbrication]]: The persistent coexistence and mutual infiltration of ritual, juridical, and scientific modes

## Notable Passages

> "We couldn't turn back from this even if we wanted, because the juridical form, with its standards of proof and always problematic notions of intentionality, invites from the beginning these extra-juridical interventions."

> "Once a scientist says 'science dictates that we now impose...' he is no longer a scientist."

> "The deferral, then, is the point, and this also means a deferral of power and a refusal of invitations and opportunities to exercise it."

## Questions Raised

1. How can we model political spaces on disciplinary ones without collapsing the distinction between the temporality of scientific inquiry and the urgency of political decision-making?

2. What safeguards prevent the "protective politics" needed to maintain disciplinary space from itself becoming a form of power that corrupts the disinterestedness it aims to protect?

3. If technology inevitably functions as governance, can we distinguish between legitimate and illegitimate forms of surveillance and constraint, or is the question itself a category error?
