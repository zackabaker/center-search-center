## Summary

This essay explores "redemption" as a conceptual bridge between economic, political, and religious meaning-systems, proposing it as a framework for understanding the [[anyown]]—the figure whose ownership rights are latent but manifest in market economies. The author argues that any serious critique of the welfare state must grapple with how dispersed governmental functions could be performed by private enterprise, using education and consumer protection as instructive test cases. Rather than naive libertarian fantasies, such thought experiments reveal how [[egalitarian distribution]] and [[gift economy]] logics persist within capitalist systems, and how the anyown's rights claim reverberates through the entire [[rights economy]].

## Key Concepts

- **[[Anyown]]**: The bearer of ownership rights rooted in egalitarian and gift economies but manifest in market systems; the basis for a redemptive politics.
- **[[Redemption]]**: To retrieve something lost through payment or restitution; applies equally to sin, slavery, honor, and economic exchange.
- **[[Rights Economy]]**: The interconnected system in which asserting one right automatically modifies the value and status of all others.
- **[[Egalitarian Distribution]]**: The pre-market logic of equal sharing that grounds legitimate ownership claims.
- **[[Gift Economy]]**: Non-market reciprocity that underlies and validates market exchange.
- **[[Welfare State]]**: The apparatus of government function whose dismantling raises essential questions about who performs its tasks.

## Questions Raised

1. How can the concept of [[redemption]] ground a politically viable alternative to both welfare-state expansion and crude libertarian privatization?

2. If the [[anyown]]'s rights claims necessarily reverberate through the entire [[rights economy]], what mechanisms prevent cascading instability or zero-sum conflict?

3. What distinguishes legitimate private certification schemes (education accreditation, consumer protection) from new forms of hidden power concentration?
