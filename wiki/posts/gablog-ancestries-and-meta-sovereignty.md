## Summary

This post grounds political theory in the [[originary hypothesis]], arguing that humans emerge through language as a mechanism for deferring the violence inherent in [[mimetic desire]]. Rather than appeal to static "human nature," the author proposes "anthropomorphics"—recognizing that human practice is fundamentally emergent, traditional, and historically contingent. Communities separated from one another will develop incommensurable languages, religions, and codes within generations, yet share a common mimetic basis. Understanding others requires engaging their traditions rather than imposing external templates.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Originary hypothesis]]**: Language originates in a singular event resolving the violence of convergent mimetic desire
- **[[Mimetic desire]]**: We desire what others desire, creating both predictability and incommensurable conflict
- **[[Anthropomorphics]]**: Human nature understood as emergent event rather than static essence
- **[[Violence deferral]]**: Language as the primary mechanism for postponing or redirecting mimetic conflict
- **[[Tradition]]**: The particular, discovered ways communities resolve mimetic tensions; always pre-existing any individual
- **[[Anthropomorphism]]**: Projecting human intention onto the other; necessary for understanding across communities
- **[[Incommensurable desire]]**: When two parties' desires converge on a single object neither can possess jointly
- **[[Meta-sovereignty]]**: The sovereign capacity to establish and maintain the systems that defer violence

## Notable Passages

> "Humanity continues to exist insofar as we find ways of deferring the violence intrinsic to incommensurable desires—that is what language is."

> "Mimesis is an excellent foundation for talking about the human because it provides a minimal version of human sameness that immediately opens onto a vista of human difference."

> "We engage with others within traditions of engaging with others, which might indeed be shared across communities."

## Questions Raised

1. If tradition is always pre-existing and unavoidable, what becomes of authentic innovation or genuine originality in human action?

2. How do we adjudicate between competing traditions when they make incompatible claims about deferring violence—especially when one tradition's deferral mechanism is another's provocation?

3. Does [[meta-sovereignty]]—the capacity to establish deferral systems—require explicit awareness of the mimetic mechanism, or can it operate tacitly within tradition?
