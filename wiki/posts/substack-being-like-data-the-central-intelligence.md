## Summary

The author argues that all state functions ultimately reduce to [[intelligence]]—the gathering, curation, and deployment of information about present and potential capabilities. Rather than resist the increasing dominance of national security apparatuses in governance, he suggests we should accept this reality and focus on what matters most: the integrity of information systems themselves. In a post-sacrificial order, repairing the [[center]] means perfecting the curation of intelligence across all agencies, which would paradoxically strengthen governance by depersonalizing power.

## Key Concepts

- [[Intelligence]]: The gathering and curation of information about capabilities, threats, and resources; the irreducible function of the state
- [[Center]]: The locus of power that must be repaired through proper intelligence curation, not restored through fantasy
- [[Post-sacrificial]]: The historical condition after traditional sacrifice loses binding force, requiring new modes of centralization
- [[Media]], [[Academic]], [[NGO]] and [[Corporate proxies]]: Channels through which intelligence flows to and from the security apparatus
- [[Agent provocateurs]]: Possibly embedded in intelligence agencies, generating controllable social responses through "woke" culture
- [[Information systems]]: The integrity of which is both most damaged and most crucial to repair

## Notable Passages

> "The most essential function of the state can be continually abstracted from all of these agencies precisely to the extent that these agencies only want to perfect their practices, which entails giving off intelligence beyond their own gathering capabilities."

> "The behind the scenes is becoming the scene. The most common initial response to these revelations is to resist this transformation...I'm suggesting something very different—accepting that the repair of the center involves the further reduction of all governance to the curation of intelligence."

> "We can become better consumers of the intelligence that the central intelligence disseminates in whatever disguised or distorted form, better generators of intelligence that might work."

## Questions Raised

1. If accepting central intelligence curation as inevitable, how do we distinguish between legitimate information integrity and totalitarian control of narrative?

2. Can ordinary citizens meaningfully participate in generating "intelligence that might work" if the system itself is designed to absorb all dissent as data?

3. What would a genuinely repaired center look like operationally, and how would we recognize it from within the very information systems it controls?
