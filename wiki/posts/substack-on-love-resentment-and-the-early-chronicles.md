## Summary

This essay recuperates [[love]] as a foundational GA concept, arguing that it has been underdeveloped compared to [[resentment]] in Generative Anthropic theory. Drawing from Gans's early *Chronicles of Love & Resentment*, the author demonstrates that GA's critical distinction from Girardian mimetic theory is precisely its claim that [[love]] precedes and enables [[resentment]], not vice versa. The piece traces how early Chronicles articulate love as originary and unarticulate (like [[ostensive]] meaning), while resentment is derivative, mimetic, and sharply communicable through the [[declarative]]. Yet the author notes a troubling trajectory: over time, resentment has come to preoccupy GA theorists more than love, possibly because love resists articulation.

## Key Concepts

- [[Love]] – The originary deferral of violence; the most profound human experience, preceding and grounding [[resentment]]; akin to [[ostensive]] meaning
- [[Resentment]] – A derivative passion rooted in mimetic desire; parasitic on love; sharply articulable and thus dangerous in its communicability
- [[Originary]] – Pertaining to the founding act; love is the most originary of human experiences
- [[Ostensive]] – The gesture of designation; love defines new significance through ostensive meaning
- [[Declarative]] – Resentment operates through declarative meaning; it parasitically reworks old significances
- [[Mimesis]] – The mechanism driving both love and resentment; mimesis is driven by love, generating resentment secondarily
- [[Deferral of violence]] – The minimal definition of love; the first human act and foundation of solidarity

## Notable Passages

> "The latter begins with resentment whereas GA begins with love. Human violence is as violent as it is only because the first human act is the deferral of violence."

> "Love is to the ostensive as resentment is to the declarative. Love defines a new significance, whereas resentment is parasitic on an old one."

> "The resenter is a child who breaks his toys knowing his parents are there to pick up the pieces. And thus it can be articulated with great sharpness. Its danger lies precisely in this ease of communication."

## Questions Raised

1. If [[love]] is truly originary and ostensive (like language's founding gesture), why is it inherently resistant to articulation, and does this resistance doom it to theoretical marginalization?

2. Does the increasing preoccupation with [[resentment]] over [[love]] in later GA work reflect a genuine theoretical necessity, or a cultural-historical shift toward victimary politics that GA was meant to diagnose?

3. If [[mimesis]] is driven by love, as the early Chronicles suggest, does this make love pre-human or trans-human—and what are the implications for understanding [[violence]] and human [[solidarity]]?
