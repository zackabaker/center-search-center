## Summary

Gans argues that paradox is constitutive of human representation and power itself. Representation necessarily contains the paradox of self-reference—we construct the reality we represent while that reality must precede us. This paradox is tied to representation's ethical function: deferring violence through shared attention. Power similarly embodies paradox: it exists only insofar as others acknowledge its pre-existence, making it both foundational and provisional. The center generates power through the paradoxical elaboration of eccentric circles, and civilization emerges when this power becomes formalized and monopolized by a single occupant.

## Key Concepts

- [[Paradox]]: The constitutive self-reference embedded in all human representation and power structures
- [[Representation]]: The act of conferring significance on reality while alluding to the violence deferred through shared attention
- [[Center]]: The locus of power that is both a location and its occupant; simultaneously a priori and provisional
- [[Centered ordinality]]: When someone re-presents an object in a novel form, transmitting a new standardized way of addressing it
- [[Mimesis]]: The paradoxical structure where the model becomes the rival, fundamental to human desire
- [[Sovereignty]]: The paradoxical power possessed only insofar as its possession is acknowledged as preceding acknowledgement
- [[Sacred]]: The reconciliation of communities to the paradoxical nature of the human itself
- [[Deferral]]: The ethical function of representation in forestalling violence through cultural elaboration

## Notable Passages

> "Paradox is constitutive of power and sovereignty as well, a point of supreme importance for absolutism. Power is located at the center—whoever occupies the center is powerful."

> "The paradox of power is that it is possessed insofar as others acknowledge that possession as preceding their acknowledgement. Power is both a priori and provisional, a location and its occupant."

> "The elaboration of a paradox is something like the continual generation of eccentric circles."
