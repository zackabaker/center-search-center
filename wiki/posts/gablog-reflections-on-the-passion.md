## Summary

This reflection uses Vonnegut's alien reinterpretation of the Passion narrative to expose the logic of scapegoating and victimization. By observing that the crucifiers "picked the wrong person" (one with divine connections), the aliens reveal Girard's insight: [[scapegoat mechanism|scapegoats]] work only when victims lack powerful allies. The aliens' proposed revision—that anyone could be divinely elected—points toward an egalitarian ethics where no victim can be safely sacrificed, while also suggesting continuity with earliest Christian theology's adoption narrative over incarnational Christology.

## Key Concepts

- [[Scapegoat mechanism]]: Violence against a powerless victim to achieve social unity, enabled by the victim's isolation
- [[Sacred violence]]: Ritualized killing legitimated by its supposed cosmic necessity or divine sanction
- [[Mimetic desire]]: The anthropological root of rivalry and resentment driving communities toward collective violence
- [[Adoption Christology]]: Early Christian theology holding Jesus elected/raised to Sonship rather than eternally incarnate
- [[Victimary logic]]: The ethical inversion that makes victims the measure of moral worth and claims
- [[Girardian analysis]]: René Girard's anthropological theory of human culture founded on the [[scapegoat mechanism]]

## Notable Passages

> "The people who crucified Jesus picked the wrong person to crucify, since Jesus's dad, unbeknownst to them, is the most powerful being in the universe."

> "The scapegoat victim is usually someone without any powerful connections, in order to avoid this kind of retaliation."

> "Don't crucify anyone, because anyone could be chosen as God's Son."
