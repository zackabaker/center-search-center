## Summary

This post analyzes the "high-low alliance"—the strategic partnership between billionaires, corporations, and foundations with marginalized groups against the middle class—as a coherent political phenomenon rooted in modern power structures. The author argues that this alliance emerges because [[liberalism]] and [[capitalism]] fragmented unified [[sovereignty]] into competing power centers, forcing economic and cultural elites to operate indirectly through disorder and victimary movements. A truly [[absolutist]] politics would reunify economic, cultural, and political power under singular [[sovereign]] responsibility, eliminating the need for elite manipulation of the victimized.

## Key Concepts

- [[High-Low Alliance]]: Coalition of wealth/power with the marginalized against the middle class; another term for the victimocracy
- [[Victimocracy]]: Political system organized around victimhood claims; the beneficiary structure of the high-low arrangement
- [[Sovereignty]]: Unified locus of power; historically absolute in monarchy, now fragmented and indirect in liberal regimes
- [[Absolutism]]: Political order where economic, cultural, and political power are explicitly unified under single authority
- [[Liberalism]]: Ideology masking property fragmentation and power diffusion; creates separation between formal and actual power
- [[Power]]: In this context, coherent authority requiring direct responsibility; contrasted with modern elite manipulation
- [[Disorder]]: Strategic tool wielded by elites to justify centralization; mechanism of indirect power accumulation

## Notable Passages

"Real economic and cultural power is not directly recognized as political power, and so economic and cultural power has to operate indirectly to reach its actual level of power."

"A more absolutist approach would eliminate the difference between economic, cultural and political power (making it all directly political), thereby making power coherent and also requiring that those with power also take responsibility."

"It was the rise of modern property, liberalism and capitalism that multifurcated property, thereby producing the competing power centers."

## Questions Raised

1. Can the distinction between intended and unintended consequences of elite intervention be maintained without attributing omniscience to power centers—or does the high-low framework require some form of coordinated intentionality?

2. If modern sovereignty requires direct unification of economic, cultural, and political power, what institutional forms could actually achieve this in a technologically complex society without reproducing the tyrannies of absolute monarchy?

3. Does the high-low alliance target the middle class specifically because it represents genuine competing sovereignty, or merely because it is the least useful constituency for either pole?
