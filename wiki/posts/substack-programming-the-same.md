## Summary

This essay reframes human history not as departure from the originary scene but as continuous attempts to improve our relation to it through increasingly complex proxies and deferral systems. The author argues that all human orders are fundamentally "total"—we are always leveraged to the center through ritual gestures and infrastructure. Technology breaks sacrality by allowing the center to instrumentalize humans as "pieces," while money, once severed from sacred grounding, becomes a metric for securing revenue flows rather than maintaining durable shared meaning.

## Key Concepts

- [[Originary scene]] – The foundational human moment of collective attention to a central object, never transcended but continually managed
- [[Deictic]] – The fundamental problem of indicating "the same" central thing to all observers; more precise than "deistic"
- [[Center]] – Not transcendent but immanent; the locus of intelligence and authorization that all human action addresses
- [[Ritual]] – Any gesture establishing a "durable same" across a field of likenesses; reframed beyond religious/secular divide
- [[Sacred/Sacrality]] – Framework in which humans retain integrity; breakdown under technology's instrumentalization
- [[Proxy]] – Mediated representations and infrastructures that defer access to the center while claiming to provide intelligence
- [[Scenic design]] – Configuration of humans as both operators and props, with varying degrees of puppetization
- [[Infra-referentiality]] – The need for continuous intervention in infrastructural conditions to make past utterances remain "the same"

## Notable Passages

> "Humans have never left the originary scene; rather, humanity has been an ongoing attempt to better the terms of that scene which, in fact, has never been 'quite right,' having been improvised from the start."

> "Everything move we make is a ritual gesture aimed at calling into being a particular array of gestures deriving intelligence from the center."

> "Technology is a breakdown of sacrality, or ritual, insofar as it allows the occupant of the center to treat human beings outside of any sacred framework, 'untented,' as 'pieces' or parts of some all."

## Questions Raised

1. How does "infra-referentiality"—the need to continuously intervene in infrastructure to keep past utterances "the same"—differ from or extend Derridean notions of undecidability and iterability?

2. If all human order is inherently "total" and leveraged, what would constitute resistance or genuine freedom, or is such resistance only conceivable within the system itself?

3. Can deictic precision—clarifying what "the same" center means—be achieved through new declarative idioms, or does every new language-game inevitably reproduce the same centering problem at a higher level of complexity?
