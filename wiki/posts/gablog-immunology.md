## Summary

This post argues that modern politics has shifted from [[declarative]] discourse (based on answerable propositions) to [[ostensive]] gestures (pointing and shrieking), rendering traditional political vocabulary obsolete. The victimary movement creates a two-tiered citizenship where speech directed at the powerful is categorically different from speech about the vulnerable, making rational debate impossible. Rather than seeking better arguments, we need political and cultural [[immunology]]—a vaccinal resistance to victimary contagion—to develop the immunity necessary for civilizational recovery.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Ostensive]]** — Speech that points and designates rather than making answerable claims; the primitive move replacing declarative argument
- **[[Declarative culture]]** — Political order premised on rational discourse and verifiable propositions about reality
- **[[Victimary]]** — Movement organized around frozen hierarchies of victimhood that immunizes certain groups from criticism while exposing others to uninhibited attack
- **[[Victimary immunity]]** — Protection granted to those who affirm the movement's binaries (confessing privilege, accusing oppression) 
- **[[Civilizational health]]** — The capacity to maintain norms of civility while resisting uncivil behavior designed to test those norms
- **[[Political immunology]]** — Cultural vaccines that build resistance to victimary logic rather than engaging it argumentatively

## Notable Passages

"Any statement, whether it be the sheer recitation of facts and statistics, detailed descriptions of events, attempts to adjudicate conflicts, moral criticism—must be qualitatively different when addressing the powerful, on the one hand, and the most vulnerable, on the other."

"We don't need better arguments against the victimary—we need the political and cultural equivalent of vaccinations. Once sufficient immunity has been developed, the possibilities of fighting to eradicate the victimary plague will suggest themselves."

"Our immune deficiency comes in two forms. First, as civilized beings we are unequipped to deal with overtly and ostentatiously uncivil behavior."
