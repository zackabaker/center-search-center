## Summary

This essay imagines a future biological "app"—a technological interface that would place individual human bodies in real-time relation to a global database of medical and biological research, enabling perfect metabolic self-regulation. The author argues that current medicine is fundamentally ad hoc, treating breakdowns after they occur through external intervention. A true totalizing approach would engineer the human organism itself to anticipate and prevent dysfunction through integrated biological feedback. While such technology seems inevitable and unquestionably beneficial, the essay hints at complications that emerge when we consider what it means to surrender organic autonomy to algorithmic control.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Metabolic order]]** — The body's natural self-regulating biochemical processes; the baseline against which medical intervention is measured
- **[[External intervention]]** — Medicine's current reliance on outside causes (drugs, surgery) to suppress or enhance specific processes
- **[[Totalizing engineering]]** — A hypothetical approach that transcends the natural/artificial distinction and makes all bodily functions self-correcting
- **[[Biological app]]** — A technological interface linking individual physiology to a global database of medical knowledge and bodily data
- **[[Declarative culture]]** — The broader cultural shift toward explicit, externalized, algorithmic control of human processes
- **[[Sensors and counteraction]]** — Anticipatory mechanisms that detect dysfunction before it manifests and trigger automatic regulatory response
- **[[Cloud integration]]** — The submission of individual bodily autonomy to networked, continually updated information systems

## Notable Passages

"In other words, there is, in current medical research and practice, no totalizing engineering approach to human health, an approach that would transcend the natural/artificial distinction and make the organic metabolisms self-regulating even in response to breakdowns in normal metabolic operations."

"An app is an interface that creates a relation between a user and the cloud. The kind of biological app...would place the individual human body in relation to the entire, continually updated, database of biological, chemical and medical research produced by global research."

"It's hard to see how one could be against such developments—both in the sense that it's hard not to see it as a tremendous improvement in human well-being and in the sense that it's hard to imagine what could stop it."
