## Summary

This essay proposes that idioms function as "samples" that can serve as a new form of currency, organizing wealth through promises and succession rather than traditional money. Drawing on Eric Gans's theory of linguistic innovation, the author argues that new linguistic forms emerge not through intentional creation but through the maintenance of [[linguistic presence]] in the face of "inappropriate" usage. This model resolves the contemporary debate about authorial intention, particularly urgent in the age of AI-generated text, by showing how meaning and value emerge from the scene itself rather than from individual intentionality.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Linguistic presence]]** – The continued possibility of sign exchange that prevents mimetic crisis and enables cultural continuity
- **[[Idiom]]** – Distinct parts of language better responded to than described; the basic unit of cultural sampling
- **[[Intention]]** – Not individual will, but the structural imperative to maintain the scene through appropriate speech acts
- **[[Derivative economy]]** – Wealth organized through promises and options rather than stored value; futures saturate the field
- **[[Speech forms]]** – Repeatable linguistic acts that emerge from inappropriate usage of existing forms, per Gans's originary hypothesis
- **[[Mistakenness]]** – The productive error that generates new linguistic forms while maintaining presence
- **[[Presence as sample]]** – Each scene thematizes distance from the originary while affirming its sameness through repetition

## Notable Passages

> "That we will have always been on the same scene is in this way 'thematized' by being made into currency—value is conferred by the prospective stacking of scenes that provides a measure of the distance from the originary scene."

> "Finding a way to maintain linguistic presence in the face of inappropriate usage creates the new form, i.e., a repeatable speech act, without anyone having intended to create that act."

> "Text produced by no one and yet which we read as if produced intentionally"—the paradox of AI writing solved through the scene's generative logic rather than authorial will.

## Questions Raised

1. If linguistic presence, not individual intention, generates new forms, what role remains for conscious innovation or artistic creation in the digital age?

2. How does a [[derivative economy]] organized through idiom-sampling prevent the mimetic crises that plague traditional hierarchical systems?

3. Can this model of presence-maintenance account for the deliberate transgression of idioms by avant-garde or revolutionary linguistic movements?
