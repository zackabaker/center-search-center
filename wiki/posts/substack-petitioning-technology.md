## Summary

Prayer represents the originary form of human discourse—the paradigmatic articulation of all four grammatical modes (ostensive, imperative, interrogative, declarative). Rather than deriving from economic or political vocabularies, all institutional discourses are themselves translations of the fundamental act of petitioning the center. Prayer is intrinsically technical and performative: it is the first form of self-design, and refinements in prayer practice across history represent humanity's long learning curve in addressing the impossible questions that arise from crises in social relations.

## Key Concepts

- [[Prayer]] – The originary utterance and paradigmatic articulation of all grammatical modes; fundamentally performative petition to the center
- [[Center]] – The sacred focal point that all prayer addresses; the source from which authority and meaning radiate
- [[Declarative]] – One of four grammatical modes; the form that maps reality and makes claims about shared understanding
- [[Originary scene]] – The founding moment of human culture where the first sign was issued as petition for salvation
- [[Impossible question]] – A question that presupposes the very center it asks about; demands a "prayerful" reframing rather than direct answer
- [[Petition]] – The fundamental human act of addressing a center with requests that simultaneously constitute the petitioner
- [[Self-design]] – The way ritual and prayer remake the self through disciplinary practice and refined utterance

## Questions Raised

1. If all institutional discourse derives from prayer rather than vice versa, how do we account for the apparent secularization of modern language—or is modern discourse still implicitly "prayerful" in structure?

2. What happens to the performative power of prayer when the center itself becomes uncertain or fragmented—i.e., when the addressee of petition is no longer shared?

3. How do we distinguish between prayers that have "failed" and genuine refinements toward more adequate prayer forms across history?
