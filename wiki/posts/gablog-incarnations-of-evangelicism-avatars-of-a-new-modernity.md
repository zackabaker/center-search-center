## Summary

This brief post gestures toward a genealogy of evangelicism as a recurring modality within modernity itself. Rather than treating evangelicism as merely religious phenomenon, the archive suggests it represents a structural pattern—an "avatar" or incarnation—through which contemporary culture expresses declarative force and resentment. The post points readers toward related analyses of how victimary progressivism and secular ideologies replicate evangelical fervor in new forms, inviting examination of what evangelicism *is* as a mode of conviction and persuasion across different historical moments.

## Key Concepts

- [[Evangelicism]] – A mode of declarative persuasion that appears across religious and secular contexts; the compulsive need to convert others to one's worldview
- [[Declarative culture]] – Social orders organized around the public declaration and reinforcement of shared meaning rather than pragmatic exchange
- [[Resentment]] – The affective motor driving repetitive claims to victimhood and demands for recognition
- [[Modernity]] – The ongoing condition in which traditional hierarchies dissolve, requiring constant renegotiation of authority and meaning
- [[Avatars]] – Successive embodiments or incarnations of a structural principle across different domains
- [[Victimary progressivism]] – Contemporary evangelicism dressed in the language of justice and marginalization

## Notable Passages

> Evangelicism: not confined to religion, but a structural modality of modernity itself.

## Questions Raised

- What distinguishes evangelicism as a *structural* feature of modernity from evangelicism as religious doctrine? Is the form identical or merely analogous?
- How does the shift from religious to secular evangelicism change the mechanisms of conversion and the affective stakes of resistance?
- Can a post-evangelical modernity exist, or is the compulsion to declare meaning and convert others intrinsic to the logic of [[declarative culture]] itself?
