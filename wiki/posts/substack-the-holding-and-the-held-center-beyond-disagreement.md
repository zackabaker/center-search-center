## Summary

This essay resolves a spatial ambiguity in [[center]] theory by distinguishing between the local center that captures immediate attention and the [[social center|occupied/signifying center]] that structures all attention implicitly. The author argues that human agency—the capacity to notice, frame, and share what we attend to—demonstrates our reliance on the signifying center even as we focus elsewhere. The healthiest institutional orders are those where disciplinary practices are so evolved that the center operates silently, noticed only through violations, while paradoxically requiring explicit ceremonial organization of succession and public attention to maintain legitimacy.

## Key Concepts

- **[[center]]** — The locus of human attention; always shifting locally but anchored socially in the occupied/signifying center
- **[[occupied center]]** — The material or designated focal point of collective attention at any moment
- **[[signifying center]]** — The social order that structures and enables all attention-giving; most powerful when unnoticed
- **[[idiomatic relation]]** — The degree to which an individual creatively "holds" something in attention, noticing novel features and imagining how to frame it for others
- **[[imperative gap]]** — The distance between what the center demands and what disciplinary practice permits; closed in ideal conditions
- **[[singularized succession]]** — A model of authority wherein leaders possess sole right to appoint successors through public ceremony and testing
- **[[discipline]]** — Evolved practices within a field that practitioners defend fiercely through resentment directed toward violations

## Notable Passages

> "The occupied/signifying center, then, is more powerful and all-encompassing the more it goes unnoticed precisely because it provides you with the space within which you can construct a disciplinary scene."

> "Those researchers would never have to give it a second thought—but they would, though, notice immediately, feel revulsion at, and respond forcefully to, any violation of the evolved practices of the disciplinary space in question."

> "The center, then, would be silent and present only in the way its being embedded in our practices turns us into fervent guardians of those practices."

## Questions Raised

1. How does the tension between the "silent center" (embedded in disciplinary practice) and the ceremonial [[singularized succession]] model actually resolve? Does public spectacle around succession inevitably politicize and distort the disciplines it aims to legitimize?

2. Can the [[imperative gap]] ever truly close, or is some degree of friction between center and discipline constitutive of human order? What distinguishes productive resentment (donating to the center) from destructive resentment (attacking the disciplines)?

3. How does an individual's [[idiomatic relation]] to local objects interact with their unconscious reliance on the signifying center—are these complementary or fundamentally at odds?
