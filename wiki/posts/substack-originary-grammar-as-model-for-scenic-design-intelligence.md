## Summary

Language structure offers a model for understanding how technology expands and reconfigures [[center-periphery]] relations. [[Technics]] originates as scenic design—the deliberate construction of ritual scenes to situate communities relative to the [[signifying center]]. As the human occupies the center, a process of sacralization and desacralization unfolds, with technology extending this dynamic through unidirectional communication. The key insight is that technological innovation requires [[de-anthropomorphization]]—abstracting human processes into imperative sequences that can be distributed across non-human agencies—before recomposing them into new forms.

## Key Concepts

- [[Scenic design]]: The deliberate construction of scenes to facilitate exchange with the center and maintain community positioning
- [[Technics]]: The human capacity for creating expanded scenes that enhance center-periphery communication
- [[Center-periphery relations]]: The fundamental structure of human meaning-making and social organization
- [[Signifying center]]: The locus of imperative authority that issues commands to the periphery
- [[Imperative]]: The gap-filling command structure that sustains center-periphery exchange
- [[De-anthropomorphization]]: The abstraction of human action into measurable, decomposable elements for technological scaling
- [[Diacritical imagination]]: The capacity to alienate a model from its representation, enabling transformation

## Notable Passages

"Scenic design, then, aims at constructing scenes such that whoever is placed at the center is connected with what is an increasingly exclusive and direct imperative relation with all on the periphery."

"Technology is the creation of expanded scenes enhancing unidirectional center-periphery communication."

"You are able to 'tell' the pieces of wood and metal where to stand, how to support each other"—demonstrating how imperative grammar extends to material composition itself.

## Questions Raised

1. How does the de-anthropomorphization required for technological scaling differ from the original [[diacritical gesture]] that constituted language itself?

2. If imperative sequences can be distributed across non-human agencies, what happens to the [[signifying center]]'s authority when it is no longer uniquely human?

3. Can the grammar of technological design ultimately preserve meaningful human agency, or does the abstraction required for scaling inevitably erode the scenic conditions that made human meaning possible?
