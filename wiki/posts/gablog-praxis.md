## Summary

This post argues that GA theorists must move beyond evaluating liberal mechanisms and instead **embody paradox** through direct [[scenic participation]]. Rather than merely speaking about asymmetry and transcendence, one must inhabit paradoxical positions that existing discourse cannot accommodate. The author responds to a critique suggesting GA risks reproducing liberalism's obsession with sovereignty, proposing instead that real praxis emerges from surfacing and enacting the internal contradictions already present in opposing discourses—particularly victimary positions that inadvertently confess their own inadequacy.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Praxis]]** — Embodied action that transcends mere theoretical critique through direct participation in social discourse
- **[[Paradox]]** — Asymmetry and first-ness that cannot be flattened into static positions; the generative tension underlying GA
- **[[Scenic participation]]** — Direct engagement in social scenes rather than abstract evaluation of mechanisms
- **[[Victimary discourse]]** — Rhetorical positions that inadvertently confess the inadequacy of their own subjects through their defensive scaffolding
- **[[Center]]** — Unifying point that can be deepened and complexified by tracing historical imperatives backward
- **[[Embodiment]]** — Speaking and enacting theory simultaneously rather than merely predicating it

## Notable Passages

> "Don't speak about asymmetry, embody it to generate praxis."

> "Participation is first of all participating in another's language. If you surface the paradoxes constituting the other's discourse, then you're embodying paradox."

> "You can learn to simply read this off the other's discourse and enact it, without making any overt argument of your own."

## Questions Raised

1. How does one distinguish between genuinely embodying paradox (enacting it) versus performatively claiming to embody it—i.e., what prevents this praxis from becoming another layer of abstraction?

2. If victimary discourse already contains its own confessions of inadequacy, why hasn't this immanent critique already disabled the discourse? What maintains its social power despite its internal contradictions?

3. How does this approach to praxis scale from individual discourse encounters to meaningful political or civilizational transformation?
