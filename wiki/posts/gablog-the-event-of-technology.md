## Summary

This essay traces how [[technology]] emerges historically from the breakdown of [[sacred center|sacred centers]]. Gans argues that early human communities operated within [[imperative exchange]]—a ritualized frame where all production was attributed to mythical agents and accompanied by ritual obligation. Technology proper arises only when humans become visible as [[implements]] themselves, severable from sacred obligation. The text suggests that desacralization of power creates a paradox: power cannot truly be desacralized because new mythicized centers continually replace the old, each inherently divisive and prone to escalation.

## Key Concepts

- **[[Sacred center]]**: The originary locus of ritual and meaning-attribution; the site from which all new cooperative modes are narratively derived and authorized.
- **[[Imperative exchange]]**: The reciprocal frame binding humans to mythical agents—gifts from the gods come with imperatives to use them honorably; humans issue prayers in return.
- **[[Desacralization]]**: The puncturing of sacred authority, which paradoxically generates new mythicized centers ("common good," "rule of law," "GDP") as masks for unquestioned power.
- **[[Implements]]**: Tools and, crucially, humans themselves—when visible as implements rather than moral agents, they become available for technological rationalization.
- **[[Declarative culture]]**: The linguistic drift toward attributing actions to mythical agents; enables mythologization of ritual through narrative.
- **[[Originary scene]]**: The primordial event establishing human language and ritual; reproduced precisely in early communities through ritual constraint.

## Notable Passages

> "You would in turn be obliged to that mythical agent, and would give to it some part of the fruits of your labor... The gift the god has given you comes with an imperative: in one form or another, that imperative would be to use it in such a way as to honor the donor."

> "The answer must be: when it becomes possible to see other human being as implements. The divine kings, commanding hundreds of thousands, even millions, in their slave war and labor armies, would first [enable this vision]."

> "Desacralization of power, though, is possible because there is a difference between the ritual center and activities engaged in outside the center."

## Questions Raised

- If desacralization necessarily generates new mythicized centers, can modernity ever escape the cycle of authority-creation and resentment toward it? Or is perpetual oscillation the condition of complex societies?
- What exactly marks the threshold where humans become visible as implements rather than moral agents? Does this require a specific technological or organizational infrastructure, or is it fundamentally a shift in representational consciousness?
- How does the shift from [[imperative exchange]] to technological rationality transform the [[attentional structure]] of communities—what changes in how people experience obligation and legitimacy?
