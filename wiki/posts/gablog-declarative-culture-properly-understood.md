## Summary

This essay distinguishes between [[ostensive]], [[imperative]], and [[declarative]] modes of communication, arguing that a properly functioning [[declarative culture]] should *support* rather than undermine the implicit [[ostensive-imperative world]]. The problem with contemporary "ruthless criticism" is that it uses declaratives to delegitimize authority by appealing to imaginary super-sovereignties (justice, equality, nature) while refusing to examine the desires and resentments driving that critique. True declarative sophistication requires designing entire institutional orders, not merely consuming end-states—a level of thinking systematically discouraged in mass democracy.

## Key Concepts

- [[Declarative culture]]: The explicit articulation of what remains implicit; the domain of disciplines and critical examination
- [[Ostensive-imperative world]]: The scenic order that "works" precisely because it goes unnoticed and unremarked upon
- [[Scenic configuration]]: The underlying structural whole presupposed by ostensive and imperative utterances
- [[Super-sovereignty]]: An imagined external standard (nature, justice, equality) against which actual authority is measured and found wanting
- [[Rogue imperative order]]: Competing power disguised as neutral declarative critique
- [[Designer's perspective]]: Thinking that integrates feedback across institutional levels, as opposed to consumer perspective

## Notable Passages

"Ostensives and imperatives 'work' because a whole scenic configuration is already in place and goes unnoticed and unremarked upon. Not noticing and remarking upon this configuration is a precondition for the operation of ostensives and imperatives."

"The declarative culture inhabiting the cloud-cuckoo land of super-sovereignty...is really more the outgrowth of a competing, rogue, imperative order than a properly declarative one."

"Everyone is encouraged to play at being president, with immediately implementable opinions; no one is encouraged to think and operate at the level at which one's feedback might be help."

## Questions Raised

1. How can postliberal or autocratic thought remain "ruthlessly critical" of contemporary order while genuinely committed to restoring implicitness—doesn't explicit critique necessarily poison the implicit?

2. What institutional structures would encourage citizens to think at the "designer's level" rather than the consumer level when evaluating complex systems like healthcare?

3. Is there a declarative culture sophisticated enough to examine its own resentments without dissolving into either complicity or rogue imperativism?
