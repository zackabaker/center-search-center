# The Pointman

## Definition

The **pointman** is the designated occupant of the [[center]] at any given moment — the figure upon whom the community's imperative attention converges and from whom imperatives legitimately flow. The term names a functional role within [[center study]] without presupposing the full weight of sovereignty theory: the pointman is whoever, at a determinate moment, bears the community's credit and absorbs its resentment. Katz has since noted that the classical term *[[dictator]]* — stripped of its modern pejorative sense — captures the same function with greater precision (see [[Rotating Dictatorship]]).

---

## Originary Derivation

On the [[originary scene]], the community's attention converges on the central object, and the aborted gesture of appropriation — the [[ostensive sign]] — constitutes both the object and the group simultaneously. There is, however, an implicit asymmetry latent in this symmetry: any deviation from perfect simultaneity means that *some* participant's gesture precedes the others', making that participant, however fleetingly, the one whose sign the others imitate. This proto-pointman does not *command* the deferral of violence — he is rather its first instance, the one whose posture the group reads as the model for its own.

From this originary asymmetry, the pointman's role generalizes as the community requires coordinated action beyond the scene of the sacred meal. Someone must issue the first imperative — "take," "go," "stop" — and someone must be positioned such that their imperative is the one others obey. The pointman thus emerges wherever [[imperative exchange]] is asymmetric: where one party's imperative has structural priority. This is not tyranny but a minimal condition of collective action; without a pointman, every imperative cancels every other imperative in a symmetrical standoff that reproduces the originary crisis without its resolution.

The pointman's authority is therefore *on loan* from the community. He occupies the center insofar as the community's [[credit]] flows through him. When that credit is exhausted — when resentment accumulates faster than his imperatives can be vindicated by results — the center becomes available for succession. This dynamic links the pointman directly to the [[originary debt]] structure: the community extends credit in advance of performance, and the pointman's legitimacy is the ongoing renegotiation of that debt.

## Development in the Archive

In [[The Pointman]], Katz introduces the term explicitly to avoid the cumbersome phrase "occupant of the center," situating the concept in relation to the [[Axial Age]] transition from ritual to rationality. The pointman concept allows center study to track governance without importing the heavy metaphysical commitments of sovereignty theory: what matters is not who *is* the ruler in some essential sense but who, functionally, is positioned such that their imperatives organize collective deferral at a given moment.

In [[Rotating Dictatorship]], Katz revisits and partially supersedes the term, arguing that *dictator* — in its Roman, pre-modern sense of a temporary appointment for emergency purposes — already captures what "pointman" was meant to name, and does so with the additional virtue of encoding *temporality and rotation* into the concept. The dictator is appointed, performs a task, and relinquishes power; this structure formalizes what the pointman concept implies but leaves implicit. The shift from pointman to rotating dictator marks a maturation of the concept toward a positive theory of legitimate succession rather than merely a placeholder label.

[[Credit and Succession]] and [[Notes on Governance and Center Study Politics]] develop the relational infrastructure around the pointman: the community's obedience or disobedience is modeled through the idiom of *credit extension*. The pointman's imperatives are obeyed insofar as the community judges him creditworthy — insofar as past imperatives have been vindicated and the outside option (exit from the arrangement) remains less attractive than continued coordination. Colin Drumm's concepts of the [[outside spread]] and [[outside option]], as Katz deploys them, give the pointman's tenure a quasi-financial structure: the center holds as long as the spread between coordinated and uncoordinated outcomes favors the incumbent.

---

## Key Tensions

1. **Designation vs. emergence**: The originary scene produces a pointman through imitation without designation. Any theory of formal appointment (elections, dictatorships) must account for how explicit designation maps onto — or distorts — this originary asymmetry.

2. **Credit and resentment as co-constitutive**: The same act of centering that extends credit to the pointman generates resentment at his singularity. It remains an open question in the archive whether these can be balanced or whether succession is always also a partial explosion of accumulated resentment.

3. **Pointman vs. rotating dictator**: The terminological shift raises a substantive question — does the *rotation* belong to the concept essentially, or is a permanent pointman coherent within GA's framework?
