# Scenic Design

## Definition

Scenic design refers to the deliberate arrangement of elements within a scene—signs, bodies, objects, media, and spatial relations—so as to direct and sustain attention toward a center. It is not merely decoration or representation but the active shaping of the conditions under which [[deferral]] of appetitive action becomes possible and meaningful. In GA terms, all design is implicitly scenic insofar as it reproduces, at some level of abstraction, the structure of the [[originary scene]].

---

## Originary Derivation

On the [[originary scene]], the proto-human group arrests its movement toward the central object and emits the aborted gesture of appropriation as a sign. The scene is not simply given—it is *produced* by this act: the participants constitute a periphery, the object is elevated to a [[center]], and the sign circulates among participants as shared attention. The scene has, in other words, a *design*, even if no one designed it. The arrangement of center and periphery, the mutual visibility of participants, the object's sacral framing—these are the minimal conditions of human meaning.

Scenic design in the fuller sense emerges when that originary structure is consciously reproduced, varied, or elaborated. The [[ostensive]] sign already implies a minimal design: something is framed as worthy of attention. When ritual institutionalizes the scene, it codifies scenic arrangements—spatial orientations, temporal rhythms, bodily dispositions—that reproduce the originary deferral reliably. As Katz argues in [[Transposing the Scene]], the conversion of ritual/mythical nexus into *practices* is precisely a history of transposing the scenic template across different media, materials, and social configurations.

The [[aesthetic]] dimension of scenic design is grounded in what Bouvard calls the oscillation between sign and object. In [[The Aesthetic as Liminally Scenic]], this oscillation—attending to the sign *as* sign rather than passing through it to the referent—is the condition of aesthetic experience. Scenic design exploits this liminality: it arrays elements so that the viewer is held at the threshold, neither purely absorbed in the content nor wholly alienated from it. Design is thus the engineering of productive ambiguity between sign-function and thing-presence.

---

## Development in the Archive

Katz's [[Scenic Design Practices]] extends this framework into media theory, arguing that with computing as "metamedia," the distinction between media and technology collapses. Every medium is now legible as a layer of scenic construction—speech, gesture, writing, and digital interface all participate in arranging attention. This dissolves any naive hierarchy that would privilege "content" over "form": the medium *is* a scenic arrangement, and designing it is designing the conditions of human attention and therefore of sociality itself.

The concept connects to Katz's notion of [[conversivity]] in [[Conversivity]], where Hamlet's deployment of the play-within-a-play illustrates scenic design as a *diagnostic* practice: one arranges a scene so that it elicits responses that reveal the configuration of desires and guilts already operative in participants. Scenic design here becomes not merely expressive but *epistemically* productive—it generates knowledge about the scene's participants by placing them within a controlled scenic frame.

In [[Declarative Culture, Properly Understood]] and [[Accessing the Ostensive within the Declarative]], Bouvard tracks how declarative culture tends to suppress its own scenic presuppositions. The declarative sentence works by making explicit what ostensives and imperatives leave implicit—but in doing so it conceals the scenic infrastructure that makes it possible. Scenic design, from this angle, is the practice of *rendering visible* that infrastructure: of attending to the frame rather than taking it for granted. This connects to the political stakes of scenic awareness, developed in [[Psychogeography]] and [[Praxis]], where the arrangement of social space is itself a form of power.

## Key Tensions

1. **Intentionality vs. Emergence**: If the originary scene produced its design without a designer, at what point does scenic arrangement become deliberate "design"? GA must account for the passage from emergent scenic structure to reflective scenic practice without projecting modern intentionality backward.

2. **Design and Manipulation**: Scenic design that shapes attention can serve deferral and communal meaning-making, but it can equally serve mimetic rivalry and scapegoating. The same scenic competence that sustains ritual community may engineer propaganda. GA needs a criterion for distinguishing scenic design that honors the center from that which exploits it.

3. **Medium and Transcendence**: If all media are scenic arrangements, does the collapse of medium into technology (per [[Scenic Design Practices]]) democratize scenic design or flatten the differential between scenes that *open* toward transcendence and those that merely circulate appetite?
