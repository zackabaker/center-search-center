# Attentionality

## Definition

Attentionality names the primordial, shared orientation toward a central object that constitutes the originary scene prior to and as the condition of any individualized purposive stance. Where *intentionality* (in the phenomenological tradition) designates the directed "aboutness" of a single consciousness, attentionality designates the irreducibly *joint* directedness of multiple participants toward a [[The Center|center]]—a directedness that is ostensive, scenic, and ethical before it is cognitive or propositional. As Katz argues, attention must precede intention; intention is what attentionality becomes once it is individualized and absorbed into the [[Declarative Sign|declarative]].

---

## Originary Derivation

On the [[Originary Scene]], the emission of the [[Ostensive Sign|ostensive sign]]—the aborted gesture of appropriation converted into designation—is not the act of a solitary intending mind but the simultaneous, mutually reinforcing orientation of all participants toward the central object. The sign works because *everyone attends together*; the center holds the group's attention before any individual can frame a proposition about it. Attentionality is thus the anthropological name for this pre-propositional, jointly scenic structure: the being-toward-the-center that makes [[Deferral|deferral]] of violence possible in the first place.

The move from attentionality to intentionality maps onto the move from the [[Ostensive Sign]] to the [[Declarative Sign]]. As Katz argues in [[Accessing the Ostensive within the Declarative]], "it is in the nature of the declarative to both supplant and appropriate the ostensive." When the declarative emerges by negating a failed [[Imperative Sign|imperative]], the joint scenic orientation is re-encoded as a predication made by a speaker *about* an object—individual intentional stance displaces collective attentional stance. Intentionality is therefore a *derivative* and *individualized* sedimentation of the more originary attentional structure.

This derivation carries an ethical weight. Because attentionality is constitutive of the human as such—the scene *is* the gathering of attention around a center—any practice, technology, or discourse that systematically fragments or redirects shared attention strikes at the originary ethical bond. The [[Originary Scene]] is not merely an epistemological starting point but a normative one: to be human is to be capable of joint attention, and to sustain that capacity is an ongoing ethical task.

## Development in the Archive

In [[The Worlding Event]], Katz gives the concept its most explicit programmatic statement, arguing that attentionality enjoys priority over intentionality because "attention must precede intention," and that intention "individualizes what is 'joint' in attention, making it more of a declarative than an ostensive concept." He traces the emergence of intentionality *from* attentionality as a historical and structural derivation, grounding the entire phenomenological tradition in something it cannot itself account for without the originary hypothesis.

[[Attentionality and Originary Ethics]] extends the concept toward a normative framework Katz calls *upclining*—the practice of elevating the quality and sustainability of joint attention within a given scene or disciplinary space. Here attentionality becomes not only a descriptive anthropological category but a criterion for ethical and pedagogical evaluation: practices are better or worse insofar as they support or degrade the capacity for genuine shared orientation toward a center.

The concept receives oblique elaboration in [[The Right of the Idiom, 3]], where disciplinary spaces are analyzed as evolved structures for "rerouting the attentions of the others within the space"—attention management as the practical core of any institution. [[Selfy]] approaches attentionality from the pathological end: the selfie culture symptomatizes a collapse of joint attentionality into competitive self-display, an attention-span of "approximately 3 seconds" marking the erosion of scenic coherence. [[Figuring Things Out]] links attentionality to technics, arguing that problem-solving is fundamentally scenic—constructing and maintaining the scenes upon which joint attention can be brought to bear.

## Key Tensions

1. **Attentionality vs. Intentionality as accounts of language**: If attentionality is truly prior, does this require revising speech-act theory and phenomenological accounts of meaning wholesale, or can intentionality be retained as a useful *secondary* framework once its derivative status is acknowledged?

2. **Joint vs. asymmetric attention**: The originary scene posits simultaneous, symmetrical co-orientation, but most real scenes involve asymmetries of salience and power. How does attentionality theorize *directed* or *managed* attention (as in pedagogy or media) without collapsing into a theory of manipulation?

3. **Degradation and restoration**: If attentionality can be eroded (by selfie culture, algorithmic fragmentation, etc.), what are the originary criteria for distinguishing legitimate *transformation* of attentional forms from genuine *decline*?
